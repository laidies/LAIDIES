The First Four

◆

1815–1852

Ada Lovelace

Keeper of the First Algorithm

Wrote the first algorithm — for a machine that wouldn't exist for a century. The credit went to the man whose machine it was.

◆

1906–1992

Grace Hopper

Keeper of the Compiler

Built one of the first compilers — so we could tell machines what to do in words, not raw code. No LLMs without her.

◆

1914–2000

Hedy Lamarr

Keeper of the Airwaves

Co-invented frequency-hopping (1942) — the idea in the family tree of Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. A movie star no one believed was an inventor.

◆

1935–2007

Karen Spärck Jones

Keeper of the Index

Invented IDF (1972) — the math under every search box, and the AI that looks things up. "Computing is too important to be left to men."

Recorded history has an honesty problem. The historical AI foundation was built while women of color were systematically kept out of the field. The lineage above (Ada → Grace, Hedy, Karen) is real, and it's also incomplete for reasons that had nothing to do with the women who were missing. The Keepers building it now — Gebru, Buolamwini, Li in the 8 below — are correcting the record in real time.

✦  In memoriam  ✦

Alan Turing
1912–1954

This wing is the women's. But it rests on a foundation, and one of its architects was Alan Turing — who imagined the machine itself, and the test we still use to ask whether it thinks. In 1952, the country he had helped save prosecuted him for being gay and chemically castrated him — the price of staying out of prison. He was dead within two years; it took Britain half a century to apologize. He is not a MAiVEN — he is the reason so many of them had a machine to build on, and a reminder that brilliance has been erased here for who you love as much as what you are. We keep his name lit, too.
