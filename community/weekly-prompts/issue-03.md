# Weekly Community Prompt: Issue 03 - The Burn Book Problem

Source file: `content/episodes/issue-03.json`

## Primary Thread

What is the most confident wrong AI answer you have seen?

No shame. No gotcha energy. Episode 3 is about the moment when the answer sounds polished enough to use, and then one detail starts tugging at the corner of the story.

Share the lesson, not confidential details:

- What kind of answer was it? A summary, policy question, client note, research memo, meeting recap, vendor comparison, or something else?
- What made it sound believable at first?
- What was the Chutney detail: the date, link, quote, number, policy, source, or context clue that did not hold up?
- How did you check it?
- What will you ask for sooner next time?

The room is not here to laugh at anyone for trusting a useful tool. We are here to make each other harder to embarrass.

## Try-On Debrief

Pick low-risk source notes or one AI answer you might actually use at work.

Run Prompt Like Elle:

1. What can I safely reuse as wording or structure?
2. Which claims depend on a source, date, number, quote, policy, jurisdiction, or domain rule?
3. What assumptions did AI make or smooth over?
4. Which ordinary-looking detail would make the answer fall apart if wrong?
5. What receipt should I check before I use it?

Then verify three things yourself: one fact, one source, and one claim that would matter if it were wrong.

Bring back the suspicious part, the corrected version, or the source-check move you are stealing for next time.

## Room Nudge

No screenshots with company, client, coworker, customer, policy, legal, HR, financial, private, or sensitive details. Redact generously. We want the receipt-checking lesson, not the workplace tea.

Post the version before it turns into a second job in your head. Someone else is probably stuck at the same locker.
