# The LAiDIES social content ladder

**Status:** canonical weekly social rule  
**Companion:** `social/SOCIAL-GROWTH-OPERATING-RULES.md`  
**Weekly template:** `social/episodes/episode-social-content-ladder-template.md`

Social does not have one job. It must attract someone who has never heard of
LAiDIES, reward her attention before asking for a click, show her why the
lesson matters, invite her into the room and give her a reason to return.

The website is the experience. Social opens different doors into it.

## The seven jobs

The ladder is a **job inventory**, not an instruction to make seven unrelated
posts every week. One excellent Reel can do more than one job, and one strong
idea can be adapted across formats. Every job must be considered; weak filler
must not be made simply to fill a rung.

| Job | What the person should feel or do | Useful forms |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Stop** | “Wait—this is for me.” | Nostalgia recognition, work truth, sharp visual, specific joke |
| **2. Wonder** | “What could those two things possibly have in common?” | Reference-to-AI connection mystery |
| **3. Learn** | “That finally makes sense.” | One useful concept, contrast, checklist or tiny demonstration |
| **4. Send/save** | “My friend/team needs this” or “I will need this later.” | Carousel, workplace script, cheat sheet, before/after |
| **5. Join** | “I have an answer to this.” | Poll, question, comment prompt, remix, Room contribution |
| **6. Visit/use** | “I want to see, read or try that.” | Exact episode, class, quiz, game, card, book or KSVL CTA |
| **7. Return** | “I know what happens here every Wednesday.” | Running series, next-week hook, callback, collectible, ritual |

## The connection-mystery hook

This is a signature LAiDIES doorway:

> What do **[character / movie / object / cultural moment]** and
> **[specific AI concept or workplace problem]** have in common?
> Find out in this week’s episode of LAiDIES.

It works only when the connection is load-bearing. The episode must actually
answer the question, and the reference must make the AI idea easier to
understand or remember.

For Episode 5, possible structures include:

- What do the September issue and a new AI model release have in common?
- What do a supermodel’s new campaign and an updated AI model have in common?
- Why would you never wear haute couture to the beach—and why should you stop
  using the most powerful AI mode for every tiny job?

These are working structures, not final copy. The final version follows the
ruled Episode 5 lesson.

## Reach-worthy content

No one can promise virality. LAiDIES can deliberately make content that has a
credible reason to travel.

A reach candidate should have at least two of these:

- **recognition:** a Rewind Era image, phrase or behaviour people know
  immediately;
- **identity:** a precise “this is me / my group chat / my office” truth;
- **surprise:** an unexpected but defensible bridge between pop culture and AI;
- **utility:** a result worth saving or sending;
- **participation:** an easy opinion, choice, reveal, test or remix;
- **performance:** a visual or audio beat that works even before the caption.

Reach without a LAiDIES connection may gain views and teach the algorithm the
wrong audience. Teaching without a strong entrance may be useful and unseen.
The goal is both.

## The weekly mix

Each episode’s social ladder must consider:

1. one connection-mystery hook;
2. one standalone piece of useful teaching;
3. one high-reach reference/identity or source-linked quote/lyric idea;
4. one save/send object, often a useful hook people want on repeat;
5. one interaction or community prompt;
6. one exact website-feature route; and
7. one reason to come back.

These can resolve into the sustainable 3–5 weekly feed posts plus Stories
defined in the growth operating rules. The content ladder decides the jobs;
the platform plan decides how many objects carry them.

## The site-promise gate

No social post may promise a site experience merely because its page or asset
exists in the repository.

Before scheduling a post with a site CTA, record:

- the exact public URL or anchor;
- the exact action promised in the post;
- the success state the visitor should reach;
- the honest failure/empty state if something goes wrong;
- desktop and mobile proof;
- logged-out/new-visitor proof where relevant;
- analytics event and UTM;
- owner and publish status.

Examples:

- “Take the quiz” means the quiz loads, accepts answers, explains the result
  and offers a sensible next action.
- “Watch the episode” means the correct video plays, captions work and the
  page does not imply a missing feature is available.
- “Collect this week’s charm” means the charm is visible, collectible,
  persistent and represented honestly in the Closet/Card.
- “Search the topic” means the current episode and related resources are in
  both indexes and return a useful result.
- “Join the Room” means the public/member boundary is understandable and the
  person is not dropped into a dead end.
- “Earn butterfly clips” means the action credits the stated amount once and
  the same balance appears in the Closet.
- “Spend your clips” means the real reward exists and arrives in the exact
  shelf/download/collection promised.
- “Hear/read this line” means the quote card opens the correct song, scene or
  episode—not a generic homepage.

If the promise fails, fix the destination or change the CTA. Do not knowingly
send traffic to a broken or aspirational experience.

## Measure the journey, not only the post

The Friday review follows:

```text
reach → useful attention → profile visit → site click
      → promised action started → promised action completed → return
```

Platform views and likes alone cannot say whether social is building
LAiDIES. Each post has one primary signal, but the batch is evaluated against
the whole journey.

Record:

- reach, watch time or dwell;
- follows and profile visits;
- saves, sends, replies and comments;
- link clicks by post/UTM;
- landing success/error rate;
- quiz/game/class/article starts and completions;
- newsletter or community conversion where applicable; and
- return visits to the next Wednesday release.

## Stability after the reveal

After the SUNNYVAiLE reveal, the audience should learn one stable place.
Weekly novelty should come from episodes, references, lessons, outfits, songs,
charms and Town events—not repeated renaming or rebuilding of the navigation,
locations and core product promise.

Major feature or information-architecture changes go through the reveal
stability rule in
`operations/launch/sunnyvaile-public-reveal-readiness.md`.
