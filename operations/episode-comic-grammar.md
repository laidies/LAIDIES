# Episode Comic Grammar — transitions + the 4 formats (Ali, 2026-07-18)

The connective tissue of every episode, in comic language. Grounded in
`operations/reference/comic-storytelling/README.md` (McCloud transitions, flashback conventions, camera moves).
The four formats come from [[episode-style-comic-popart-direction]]'s menu.

## 🎨 VIBRANCY + 90s PALETTE (Ali, 2026-07-19) — applies to EVERY comic image
Keep the comic renders **VIBRANT and saturated** in the **Y2K/90s palette — pinks, teals, blues** (plus
plum, gold, cream accents; the [[brand-palette-and-type-lock]] colors). Bright/present/town beats lean
hard into that vibrant Y2K color; history/serious beats stay moodier (palette follows scene) but still
sit in the palette — NOT washed out, NOT muted/dusty (Ali dislikes muted grading). The whole show should
read as a vibrant Y2K comic book.

## 🎞 PROPER = the RESEARCHED COMIC-BOOK TRANSITIONS (Ali, 2026-07-19)
Every scene-to-scene move uses a **comic-book transition from the research** (`operations/reference/comic-
storytelling/README.md`) — NOT a generic video dissolve. Pick the technique that fits the beat:
- **Scene-to-scene + caption box** (jump across time/space) — the default for moving between women/eras
  (pair with the `PLACE, YEAR` time-jump card).
- **Page-turn** — the "we're going back / forward in time" device (the flashback beat).
- **Flash-cut / whip** — an abrupt hard jump for shock or speed.
- **Montage** (3–5 small panels) — compress time (the AI-winter years, the Y2K era).
- **Aspect-to-aspect** — drift across details of one place/mood (quiet establishing beats).
- **Push-in / pull-out** — camera moves that build to a realization or reveal scale.
Each transition = its own FRAME SEQUENCE (`comic-animation-frame-spec.md`). Recurring transition TYPES stay
consistent across episodes (same technique for the same kind of move).

## THE 4 FORMATS — deploy across the episode (rhythm, not 8 identical scenes)
1. **Full scene** — a character in her world (each historical woman's beat; the heroine present-day).
2. **Full-page comic** — a big dramatic/pivotal beat (a reveal, a breakthrough), used sparingly.
3. **Text-only frame** — a key term held on screen as a comic card (e.g. `COMPILER`, `THE FIRST BUG`, `IMAGENET`).
4. **Emphasis frame** — a punchline/reaction word-burst (e.g. `SHE WROTE THE FIRST PROGRAM.` / an `AS IF!`-style hit).

## TIME-JUMP TRANSITION (present → a historical woman) — the reusable device
Every jump back to a Founding Mother uses the SAME treatment so it reads as grammar (consistency = legibility):
- **Scene-to-scene transition** (McCloud) — the jump across time/space.
- **+ Caption box** = place + year (the clearest signal): `LONDON, 1843` (Ada) · `PHILADELPHIA, 1946` (ENIAC)
  · Grace's era, etc. Distinct caption style, same every time.
- **+ Tone/light shift** = the PAST reads aged / cooler / moodier; the PRESENT is brighter. (House rule:
  **color is the primary cue, caption the backup** — cheapest, hardest to misread.) Ties to LIGHT-AS-ARC
  (dark past → illuminated present) in [[episode-style-comic-popart-direction]].
- **+ optional page-turn / heavier panel border** for the "we're going back" beat.
- **RETURN to present** = reverse the signals — snap to brighter color, clean border.

**"Something there" for the Ada jump** = a TIME-JUMP transition FRAME: a comic panel establishing Ada's world
(Victorian London, the Analytical Engine), caption box `LONDON, 1843`, aged/period tone, comic style — the
bridge INTO her scene. Same pattern for every other woman.

## CAMERA MOVES within scenes (from the playbook)
- **Push-in** (3 successively closer panels) → build to the heroine's realization.
- **Pull-out** → reveal scale (e.g. the wall of ImageNet thumbnails for Fei-Fei).
- **Montage** (small panels) → compress time (the "AI winter" years passing).
- **Splash / bleed** → the single biggest beat of the episode only.

## "PREVIOUSLY ON…" = one comic STRIP
A single horizontal multi-panel strip of key prior-episode moments, locked comic style + caption boxes.
ONE image (no motion/narration-sync) → fast, cheap, on-style. Doubles as a cleaner episode open than a hard cut.

## Why this matters (production)
Text-only frames, emphasis frames, transition cards, and the recap strip are all **single images** — no
motion/narration-sync rebuild. So the comic grammar is not just style; it's a **cheaper way to carry story**
between the expensive full-scene/video beats. Directly eases the [[weekly-production-machine]] load.
