## What the item contains

The carousel reframes a project premortem as an AI-assisted stress test. Its five
steps ask the model to:

1. imagine a described plan has failed and generate plausible causal paths;
2. separate the most likely failure from the most damaging one and identify the
   assumption on which the plan depends;
3. redesign the plan around the major failure modes and define go, adjust and
   abandon checks;
4. attack the plan from a competitor's perspective; and
5. attach specific measurable early-warning signals and response thresholds.

This is a useful sequence. The source's framing is not evidence that Claude can
predict the future or determine which failure is actually most likely.

The premortem is an established group decision method, not a Claude feature.
Claude can help generate candidate risks when given sufficient context and a
clear output shape; it does not make those risks independent evidence.

## Strongest objection

The carousel asks the model to rank the "most likely" failures without supplying
base rates, operating evidence or knowledgeable dissenters. A fluent ranking can
therefore create false certainty and displace the people closest to the work.
Treat every generated failure, probability and competitor move as a hypothesis
to investigate. Do not paste confidential plans, personal data or unreleased
commercial information into an unapproved consumer AI account.
