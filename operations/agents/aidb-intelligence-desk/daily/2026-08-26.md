## New AIDB edition — what the top AI users are doing differently

### AIDB's attributed argument

AIDB argues that the practical gap is moving beyond chat and prompt craft. In its framing, advanced organizations increasingly delegate systems-level work to agents that have context, tools, reusable skills and permissions. It describes a ladder from generation, to synthesis, to execution, to maintenance; it also predicts that shared or “multiplayer” AI workflows will become more important. A legal example separates agent handling of coverage and coordination from human ownership of risk judgment and accountability.

The argument also says social and organizational inertia matter: people retain old computer habits even when new systems can do more. AIDB acknowledges displacement risk and the need to support affected workers. It correctly notes that OpenAI's 64% figure concerns output tokens, not sessions.

### Verified source facts and limits

OpenAI's August 12 Enterprise Signals report defines frontier firms as the top 10% of monthly output tokens per active user and typical firms as the 45th–55th percentile. It reports an 8.3× June gap, up from 2.6× in January; 64% of combined Codex and ChatGPT enterprise output tokens came from Codex; weekly skills use was 19% versus 3%, and plugins 21% versus 9%. OpenAI also reports rapid Codex-user growth outside engineering.

Those are OpenAI's enterprise-customer measures, not evidence about the general workforce or a direct measure of productivity, quality or business value. OpenAI explicitly calls tokens an imperfect proxy. Agent tasks naturally generate more output. AIDB's stronger formulations—such as treating the difference as wholly agents—are interpretation, not established fact. A niche independent critique makes the same metric warning; stronger independent outcome evidence is still missing.

### LAiDIES reader frame

The useful shift is not “write a longer prompt.” It is: useful delegated work needs a defined task, relevant context, tools, bounded permissions, a way to verify the result and a human who owns the decision. The reader question is not whether she uses enough tokens; it is whether the system produces an acceptable outcome without hiding errors, increasing review burden or surrendering judgment.

AIDB assumes familiarity with agents, Codex, plugins, skills, tokens, tool access and enterprise deployment. LAiDIES must explain those mechanisms in ordinary language and restore the work and power questions: who sets the task, who grants access, who checks the evidence, who benefits and who remains accountable.

Anthropic's August 25 announcement says chat and Cowork now share one memory; users can inspect, edit or delete Topics, pause or reset memory, and control whether sensitive topics are included. Memory is on by default for Free, Pro and Max across web, desktop and mobile; Team and Enterprise availability is admin-controlled and off for individuals until enabled.

This is a genuine reader-relevant product change, but not a recommendation to give Claude everything. It is retained as a Concept / AI Fundamentals / internal-practice watch.
