## Reader question

**Which part of my work should I give AI, and which part should stay mine?**

LAiDIES already teaches how to start safely, brief a job and manage a coherent
assignment. It does not yet give one explicit selection step that helps a
reader choose the job before those lessons begin.

## Trigger and source boundary

The 2026-08-14 AI Daily Brief edition proposed a five-factor “AI Deputization
Audit”: frequency/value, teachability, checkability, stakes and how much the
quality depends on the person. It then assigned 0–10 totals to three bands.
The exact original item is [the AIDB edition](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-14).

This is a source-resolved commentary framework, not a validated assessment.
The edition supplies no study, dataset or independent evidence for its score,
thresholds or assertion that most knowledge work falls in the middle band.
Do not publish the numbers or describe the method as current best practice.

## Useful contribution and critical omissions

The framework earns consideration because it compares expected value with the
cost of checking, and asks whether the work genuinely depends on the reader's
judgment. Those questions are useful before someone spends time briefing or
building an AI workflow.

It is not sufficient on its own:

- frequency and teachability do not establish permission to expose the data;
- “low stakes” can hide cumulative, reputational or downstream consequences;
- easy output checking does not include setup, correction and maintenance;
- reversibility and rollback are not explicit;
- relationship, professional and decision authority need more than a single
  “needs to be you” score; and
- catchy labels can imply more autonomy and responsibility than the system
  actually has.

## Duplication and relationship impact

- Episode 01 owns the emotionally safe, low-risk first task.
- Briefing 101 owns job, context, boundaries, output and acceptance criteria.
- Masterclass Lesson 04 owns coherent assignment management, intervention and
  checkpoints.
- ODC-101 owns the difference between a capability demonstration and a whole
  operating workflow, including tests and upkeep.
- The July AIDB synthesis already recommends improving the end-to-end workflow
  rather than scattering AI across isolated tasks.

The missing job is a short bridge among those treatments. Do not create a
second first-use lesson, a duplicate delegation class or a branded audit from
the scout.

## Smallest useful owner decision

Return `ACCEPT`, `MERGE`, `PARK` or `DECLINE` for one bounded extension that:

1. starts with a recurring work task the learner is actually authorized to
   use;
2. compares likely benefit with total setup, checking and correction effort;
3. separately checks reversibility, stakes and downstream consequences;
4. names privacy, confidentiality, policy and tool-permission boundaries;
5. identifies the judgment, relationship or accountable decision that remains
   human-owned; and
6. routes the selected task into the existing Briefing 101 or managed-work
   practice rather than ending at a score.

Before any public treatment, test the decision aid on at least one ordinary
low-risk task and one deceptive counterexample: frequent, teachable and
checkable, but unauthorized because of its data or workplace boundary. Success
means the learner rejects the counterexample and can explain why without
needing the AIDB labels.
