## The pack's promise

In about 20 minutes, a beginner should be able to explain what generative AI
is, choose one safe first task, try it without handing over sensitive
information, notice what still requires human judgment and decide one useful
next move.

The learner should leave with evidence of a first rep—not a folder of things
they meant to read.

## The five jobs

| Job | Component | Time | Learner leaves with |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `REVIEW` | Study Sheet | 3–5 min | The episode's mental map |
| `PRACTISE` | Try-On | 10 min | One completed low-risk AI rep |
| `REMEMBER` | Trading Cards | 3–5 min | Six visual memory anchors |
| `KEEP` | Cheat Sheet | In the moment | A one-page decision and review aid |
| `CHECK` | Pop Quiz next door | 4–5 min | Applied feedback, not slogan recall |

Four components belong inside the pack: Review, Practise, Remember and Keep.
The Quiz sits next door and returns the learner to the pack or episode.

## Proposed Episode 01 pack menu

### 1. REVIEW — “Open the Tab: The First-Rep Map”

One screen and one printable page.

**Top line:**  
*I do not need to understand everything to try one useful thing.*

**What Episode 01 explicitly teaches:**

1. **Generative AI makes a new first pass.** It is not a search result and it
   is not automatically a source.
2. **The app and the model are different layers.** ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini
   are the places a beginner works; a model is the engine underneath.
3. **Fast and polished is not the same as true or ready.** The user still owns
   context, checking, taste, judgment and the decision to use the result.
4. **Human judgment.** The first draft can be fast and useful, while the user
   still decides what fits, what is supported, what needs changing and whether
   the result should be used. The episode's “80% right / 20% wrong” is one
   email anecdote—not a general performance ratio.

**Study Pack synthesis—not a named framework from the episode:**

`LOW-RISK TASK → GIVE RELEVANT CONTEXT → GET A FIRST PASS → CHECK IT →
APPLY HUMAN JUDGMENT → USE / REVISE / STOP`

This map organizes the episode's story and Try-On into a repeatable path. It
must be labelled as the Study Pack's recap, not quoted or presented as a
framework named in the lesson.

### Visual learning set

Ali explicitly approves visuals and diagrams that make the lesson easier to
understand. Episode 01 should use a small, purposeful set:

1. **NEW FIRST PASS, NOT A SOURCE**  
   A two-lane diagram: search points toward existing pages/sources; generative
   AI produces a new draft that still proceeds through `CHECK`.
2. **THE APP / THE MODEL**  
   The episode's magazine/editor-in-chief analogy: the app is the magazine the
   learner opens; the model is the engine/editorial brain underneath. Keep the
   technical claim bounded and do not imply each product has only one model.
3. **CONFIDENCE ≠ CORRECTNESS**  
   Two equally polished answer cards, one supported and one invented, with the
   difference revealed by receipts rather than visual confidence.
4. **YOUR JUDGMENT LAYER**  
   A transparent inspection overlay marking `FITS`, `CHANGE`, `CHECK` and
   `DO NOT USE`. This replaces the fixed “Human 20%” graphic.
5. **THE FIRST-REP PATH**  
   `LOW-RISK TASK → CONTEXT → FIRST PASS → CHECK → JUDGMENT → NEXT MOVE`.
   Label it **STUDY PACK MAP** so it is clearly a learning synthesis.
6. **SAME JOB, DIFFERENT DRAFTS**  
   The same task ticket entering the available tools and returning different
   drafts, followed by `WINNER FOR THIS TASK`—not a permanent model ranking.

These are teaching visuals, not decoration. Each must answer one learner
question, contain a short “What this shows” caption, use editable verified
labels, include useful alt text or an adjacent text equivalent and remain
legible in mobile and one-page print form.

**Safety rail:**

The episode explicitly says to begin with one useful, low-risk task. It does
not teach the full five-part risk filter below. The pack may add that filter as
clearly labelled practical safety scaffolding:

- low stakes;
- reversible;
- no sensitive or restricted information;
- easy for the learner to judge; and
- useful even if the result needs editing.

**Good first tasks:**

- turn non-confidential notes into a checklist;
- rewrite a low-stakes email draft;
- explain an unfamiliar concept before a meeting; or
- generate questions for a conversation.

**Pack-added safety examples—not claims that the episode lists them:**

- medical, legal, financial or employment decisions;
- confidential client, employee or company data;
- factual claims the learner cannot verify; or
- sending/publishing the output without review.

## 2. PRACTISE — “The Ten-Minute First Rep”

This replaces Episode 01's current vague-versus-specific exercise, which
belongs to Episode 02.

### Step 1 — Pick the task

Choose one real, low-risk task that has been sitting around:

- a short email draft;
- meeting notes that need structure; or
- a concept you need explained plainly.

### Step 2 — Bring a safe brief

Use this starter:

> Help me make a first pass at **[task]**. It is for **[person/audience]**.
> Use only this information: **[non-sensitive facts or source material]**.
> I need **[short deliverable]**. Do not invent missing details; mark what I
> need to supply or verify.

The pack should explain that this is a safe starting rail, not a magic phrase
or the full Episode 02 briefing lesson.

### Step 3 — Run the same task

Try the exact same task and source material in the tools available to the
learner. The episode's full comparison uses ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini; the
pack can offer a one-tool first-rep path plus an optional two/three-tool
comparison so lack of an account never blocks the lesson.

### Step 4 — Use the evidence tray

Capture only short, non-sensitive excerpts and mark:

- **KEEP:** one useful sentence, idea or structure;
- **CHANGE:** one thing only the learner's judgment can fix;
- **CHECK:** one claim, assumption or missing fact; and
- **WINNER FOR THIS TASK:** which result was most useful today, without
  declaring a permanent best tool.

### Step 5 — Finish

The learner writes:

> AI saved me ______. I changed ______. I still need to verify ______. My next
> safe rep is ______.

Local notes need a visible clear/delete control and must not be presented as
private if they are stored in the browser.

## 3. REMEMBER — Episode 01 Trading Cards

Six flippable flash cards in Ali's locked 1990s pop-art style: purples,
vibrant pinks, electric/cobalt blues and sunshine yellow; heavy black ink,
white/cream, Ben-Day halftone and one dominant image.

### Card 1

**Front:** `GENERATIVE AI` + an original “new first pass” visual  
**Back:** Generative AI creates new text, images, code or other material from
patterns and your instructions. It can help make a first pass; it is not
automatically a source.

### Card 2

**Front:** `THE APP / THE MODEL` + magazine/editor visual  
**Back:** The app is where you work. The model is the engine underneath.
Different apps and models can respond differently to the same job.

### Card 3

**Front:** `MISSING CONTEXT` + wrong-meeting outfit visual  
**Back:** AI does not know the situation, people, rules or source material
unless that information is available in the current product and task.

### Card 4

**Front:** `CONFIDENTLY WRONG` + unsupported Burn-Book-style claim visual  
**Back:** A polished answer can still contain invented or unsupported details.
Check claims that matter before they borrow your name.

### Card 5

**Front:** `YOUR JUDGMENT` + “only you can see” inspection/edit visual  
**Back:** Context, taste, evidence, responsibility and the final decision stay
with you. Sometimes the result needs a light pass; sometimes it should not be
used at all. There is no fixed human percentage.

### Card 6

**Front:** `OPEN THE TAB` + one small task-ticket visual  
**Back:** Start with one low-risk, reversible task you can judge. A useful
ten-minute rep beats waiting for the perfect weekend.

Backs require final source/current-guidance reconciliation before release.
The front term remains editable and verified text. Collection is optional
after learning; it is not the learning gate.

## 4. KEEP — “Should I Use AI for This?” desk card

One printable page that works after the episode.

### Green light

- low stakes;
- reversible;
- no sensitive data;
- first draft, structure or brainstorm;
- user can judge the result.

### Yellow light

- factual claims;
- unfamiliar subject;
- current product/policy information;
- important tone or audience;
- anything that will be shared under the user's name.

**Move:** provide sources, verify, edit and ask for human review when needed.

### Red light

- restricted/confidential information;
- high-stakes professional decisions;
- irreversible action;
- output the user cannot assess; or
- automatic sending/publishing without a suitable review step.

### The three-box output check

`KEEP` — useful enough to retain  
`CHANGE` — needs human context or taste  
`CHECK` — needs evidence or a reliable source

This replaces the current four-page printable's overlapping lesson, exercise,
prompt library and reference jobs.

## 5. CHECK — Episode 01 Pop Quiz

Use five or six scenario decisions rather than twelve mostly recall-led items.
Example:

> You have confidential employee notes and need a performance decision by
> noon. What is the best first move?

- Paste the notes into any free chatbot and ask for the decision.
- Remove names and ask for a final decision anyway.
- Use an approved internal process/tool and keep the accountable human
  decision-maker involved. **← best**
- Ask three public tools and choose the majority answer.

Feedback should explain the decision, link to the relevant Study Sheet or
Cheat Sheet section and return to the exact Episode 01 pack.

Other question jobs:

- choose the safest first task;
- distinguish a generated first pass from a search/source;
- identify what still requires human judgment;
- spot a claim that requires checking; and
- choose a useful comparison without declaring a forever-best model.

## Freshness decision — retire “The Human 20%” as a general concept

**Decision:** `UPDATE EXISTING`

The episode's 80/20 line is valid only as the narrator's description of one
email draft. It is not supported as a stable percentage of AI versus human
work, and it should not become a flash-card definition, workflow stage or
promise about current models.

Current primary evidence still supports human review:

- OpenAI's Terms of Use, effective 2026-01-01, say output may be inaccurate
  and must be evaluated for accuracy and appropriateness, including human
  review where appropriate:
  `https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/`
- Google's current Gemini Agents guidance says to verify generated outputs
  before deployment, especially where work changes data or interacts with
  external systems:
  `https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/agents`
- OpenAI's current real-world task evaluations use task-specific expert
  grading rather than a universal human/AI work split:
  `https://openai.com/index/gdpval/`

Recommended treatment:

- keep “80% right; the other 20% wrong in ways only I could see” inside the
  personal Episode 01 story if it is clearly understood as that one result;
- replace universal downstream instructions such as “in every draft, notice
  the twenty percent” with “inspect what only you can judge, verify or decide”;
- replace the proposed `THE HUMAN 20%` card with `YOUR JUDGMENT`; and
- never imply that current models reliably complete 80% of every task or that
  humans always contribute exactly 20%.
