## What the original establishes

Atlassian reports a controlled experiment conducted 2026-03-30 to 2026-04-07
with 961 full-time U.S. knowledge workers. Participants evaluated the same
hypothetical colleague and identical business-proposal email; the treatment
changed only the note describing whether and why AI was used. Atlassian reports
that AI disclosure produced a substantially higher laziness rating and a
24-percentage-point lower likelihood of recommendation for a high-visibility
project. A team/client-focused explanation improved effort and recommendation
ratings relative to a personal-efficiency explanation, but both remained below
the no-disclosure control. A separate 1,006-person U.S. knowledge-worker pulse
survey measured adoption and disclosure attitudes.

## Limits that must survive any treatment

- This is one vendor research programme, not a universal workplace law.
- The experiment used a hypothetical colleague and one written-email task; it
  did not observe actual promotions, compensation or job loss.
- The population was U.S. full-time knowledge workers. Do not generalize the
  effect size to other countries, frontline work, every profession or every
  workplace culture.
- “10x lazier” is Atlassian's reported rating comparison, not evidence that
  AI users are lazy or that every disclosure is penalized.
- The study does not authorize concealing AI use when an employer, client,
  profession, contract or law requires disclosure.
- Atlassian's conclusion that culture is the fix is an interpretation supported
  by its moderation analysis, not a guarantee that a particular worker can
  neutralize the penalty by changing her wording.

## Smallest useful owner decision

Rule whether this earns **one** low-risk Career / Work-Life service treatment,
not a course, episode rewrite, NewsStand story and Daily Tip simultaneously.
The distinct job would be to help a reader separate three questions before she
speaks:

1. **Must I disclose?** Follow the applicable workplace, client, professional
   and legal rules; this research does not answer that question.
2. **What did AI actually do?** Describe the bounded contribution accurately,
   without implying that the tool authored or verified work it did not.
3. **What outcome did I remain responsible for?** Name the checking, judgement
   and team/client value that remained human-owned.

The source supports testing a team/client-value framing as a dated practical
option. It does **not** support telling readers to hide AI use or promising that
one sentence removes bias.
