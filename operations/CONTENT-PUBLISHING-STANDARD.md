# LAiDIES content publishing standard

**Status:** standing production gate  
**Applies to:** episodes, LIBRAiRY books, classes, interactive learning tools,
games and practice activities, reference pages, NewsStand features, printables,
quizzes and public AI guidance  
**Owner:** Codex prepares and verifies; Ali owns final voice/taste approval for
new public teaching formats

## The rule

Content is not publishable because it is written, long, attractive, linked or
technically rendered.

It is publishable only when a smart reader who is new to the subject can:

1. understand the promised idea;
2. picture it through an accurate LAiDIES analogy;
3. explain the important distinction back in her own words;
4. identify the important nuance, uncertainty or live disagreement;
5. distinguish demonstrated present capability from forecast future
   capability;
6. see what it means for her and for society;
7. take one useful next action; and
8. explain it accurately to a friend using an example or analogy whose limit
   she understands.

If the reader still has to ask “what is this actually saying?”, the content has
not passed.

## The two-minute reverse brief

Before writing, record:

- **Reader:** who is arriving and what may she already know?
- **Question:** what exact question is she trying to answer?
- **Promise:** what will she understand or be able to do after this?
- **Place in the system:** is this a quick definition, a concept lesson, an
  episode, a reference answer, a procedure, an interactive tool, a practice
  activity or a game?
- **Canonical source:** which episode, approved analogy, fact record or product
  decision owns the substance?
- **Not this:** what neighbouring topic must this page not accidentally reteach?
- **Admission rule:** for a glossary, taxonomy, directory or other collection,
  what exact test must every included item pass?
- **Continuation:** where does the reader go next?

No prose begins until these fields agree. For a collection, every proposed
member must pass the admission rule before its individual accuracy or
usefulness is scored.

## LAiDIES teaching unit

Every teaching unit uses the parts that fit its job. A quick Vocab entry may
use one or two sentences per part; a Concepts lesson may use a full scene.

1. **Plain-English answer** — accurate, specific and free of unexplained
   jargon.
2. **The analogy that unlocks it** — drawn from the approved LAiDIES canon,
   mapped to the real mechanism rather than added as decoration.
3. **Where the analogy stops** — state the limit when extending it would become
   inaccurate.
4. **Why the reader cares** — the decision, risk, shortcut or workplace
   consequence this changes.
5. **Concrete example** — show the idea operating on a recognisable task.
6. **Try it or check it** — one proportionate interaction, comparison or
   verification move.
7. **Continue the story** — link to the exact episode, book section, printable,
   quiz or primary receipt that deepens it; the visible label names both the
   destination and the specific learning payoff before the reader leaves.

For a fast-moving or contested concept, the unit must also show:

- **Where we are now** — capabilities demonstrated by current systems, not an
  inherited description of an earlier generation;
- **What remains unresolved** — the actual missing evidence, disputed
  threshold or forecast rather than a fabricated categorical boundary;
- **What could change** — a clearly labelled scenario, forecast or implication,
  not a present fact; and
- **Why the distinction matters** — consequences for the reader, work,
  institutions and society in proportion to the concept.

## Interactive learning tools

An interactive tool may do the work for the user. It does not have to turn the
interaction into an exercise, require revision or prove mastery.

When a tool also claims educational value, the teaching comes from making its
diagnosis, assumptions and transformation visible. It still needs:

- a precise input promise and supported task range;
- useful output without mandatory user labour;
- task/risk routing appropriate to different kinds of request;
- clear separation of supplied facts, assumptions, placeholders and unknowns;
- an enforced success/safety/error response contract;
- current privacy, retention, provider, cost and capability disclosure;
- failure states that do not masquerade as successful use or earn rewards; and
- live evaluation across the variety of questions the public promise invites.

## Games and practice activities

Games do not need to reteach the explanation. Their educational job is to make
the learner retrieve, notice, choose or perform a behaviour; experience a
consequence; receive explanatory feedback; and improve or transfer the skill.

Every game or activity that claims learning, progress or skill requires:

- one named target behaviour and its canonical prior lesson;
- a mechanic whose success actually requires that behaviour;
- a sourced and appropriately nuanced correct/evidence model;
- feedback that explains why a choice worked or failed;
- replay variation that cannot be passed by memorizing one answer;
- a bridge from the fictional interaction to a real decision or task;
- an explicit limit on what the result proves; and
- rewards tied to observed behaviour, not visiting, clicking or exhausting the
  available choices.

Delight, atmosphere, confidence and return behaviour are legitimate product
jobs. An activity with one of those jobs does not need a false educational
claim.

## Book boundaries

### Vocab 101

Answers: **“What does that word mean in LAiDIES?”**

- a quick-reference companion to terms already introduced in the series;
- arranged alphabetically by term, never by episode, source or difficulty;
- may receive durable terms from episodes, classes and reference lessons;
- admits a term only when AI gives it a specialized meaning, readers encounter
  it as AI jargon, or it names an AI-specific mechanism or state;
- uses the same canonical analogy as the lesson that introduced it;
- says why the word matters;
- links back to the exact episode, class or reference lesson that taught it;
- does not become a technical chapter or introduce an unrelated taxonomy.

Time-sensitive NewsStand language begins as dated **Current Vocab** beside the
story. It graduates into Vocab 101 only when the term is durable, useful beyond
one news cycle and has a complete LAiDIES teaching entry. General computer
literacy is a separate book if the reader need is proven; it is not mixed into
Vocab 101.

### Concepts 101

Answers: **“What is actually happening, and why?”**

- builds a mental model of the mechanism;
- relates several terms to one another;
- shows consequences, limits and failure modes;
- includes a practical example or Try-On;
- does not repeat the Vocab entries at greater length without adding a model.

### Briefing / Setup / Accounts 101

Answers: **“What do I do?”**

- begins with a real user job;
- provides a procedure or decision rule;
- demonstrates it on a realistic example;
- distinguishes durable principles from current product steps;
- links product-specific instructions to dated official sources.

### Reference books

Answers: **“What is true, according to what evidence, as of when?”**

- separates established evidence, inference, dispute and unknowns;
- uses primary sources wherever available;
- dates changing claims and records a recheck trigger;
- gives a direct answer before the receipts;
- does not use citations as decoration.

## Automatic rejection conditions

Any one of these returns the item to **DRAFT**:

- generic filler that could appear on any AI education site;
- a dictionary definition with no practical meaning;
- a cute analogy that does not map accurately to the mechanism;
- a present-versus-future comparison that assigns the future system a
  capability current systems already demonstrate;
- a difference in reliability, breadth, consistency, cost, scale or deployment
  taught as though it were a categorical difference in capability;
- a claim, comparison, price, feature or policy without a current source;
- duplicated teaching that conflicts with or adds nothing to its canonical
  episode/book;
- a glossary, taxonomy or collection member included merely because it is
  related to the topic, without passing the collection's admission rule;
- a placeholder, “coming soon” body or a book represented by a few embedded
  sentences;
- a heading or control that promises an interaction the page does not provide;
- an interactive tool that invents facts, sources or missing user context in
  order to complete its promised output;
- a tool failure, fallback or safety response counted as ordinary successful
  use or progress;
- a broad tool-capability claim supported only by one narrow happy-path test;
- a learning game that can be won without using the claimed skill;
- a binary game verdict that erases material uncertainty, partial truth or
  insufficient evidence;
- a learning reward granted for presence, clicks or option exhaustion rather
  than the behaviour named by the reward;
- links that lead somewhere other than the promised lesson;
- continuation labels that hide the destination or name only a generic
  container such as “read the episode”;
- an unexplained term used to define another unexplained term;
- a wall of prose without visible reading structure;
- a layout that hides, crops or competes with the teaching;
- a test that proves only that the DOM rendered.

## Editorial scorecard

Score each item `0` (fails), `1` (partial) or `2` (strong).

| Test | What passes |
|---|---|
| Reader promise | One clear question and one useful outcome |
| Canon fidelity | Matches approved episode, analogy and terminology |
| Accuracy | Claims are correct, scoped and sourced where needed |
| Clarity | A smart newcomer can follow it on first read |
| Usefulness | Changes a decision, action or understanding |
| LAiDIES teaching | Analogy unlocks the mechanism; it is not decoration |
| Information design | Headings, sequence and interactions support reading |
| Continuation | Exact links work; visible labels disclose the destination and specific learning payoff |
| Distinct job | Does not duplicate or blur a neighbouring book |
| Experience QA | Read and operated in the actual desktop/mobile interface |

**Pass:** at least 17/20, with no zero.  
**Hold:** any automatic rejection condition, factual integrity issue or broken
reader journey overrides the numeric score.

The score is a forcing function, not a substitute for judgment. A technically
passing item can still be held when it is boring, muddled or off voice.

## Required evidence packet

Before a public item can be called approved:

- completed reverse brief;
- canonical sources and receipts;
- scorecard with comments;
- desktop and mobile reading pass;
- all promised interactions used, not merely inspected;
- internal and external links checked;
- factual/current claims rechecked on the review date;
- duplication check against adjacent episodes/books;
- for an interactive tool: supported/unsupported task boundary, varied-input
  evaluation set, response contract, safety routes, failure/timeout behaviour
  and privacy/retention evidence;
- for a game or practice activity: target behaviour, mechanic-alignment test,
  answer/evidence key, feedback examples, replay/transfer proof and reward
  trigger;
- for a glossary, taxonomy, directory or collection: the admission rule and
  evidence that every included item passes it;
- explicit list of what remains unknown or intentionally out of scope;
- Ali approval when the item establishes a new public voice, analogy or
  experience pattern.

## Status path

`DRAFT` → `EDITORIAL REVIEW` → `CONTENT VERIFIED` → `BUILT LOCALLY` →
`EXPERIENCE VERIFIED` → `APPROVED` → `DEPLOYED` → `VERIFIED PUBLICLY`

No stage may be inferred from a later-looking file. A rendered HTML file can
still be a DRAFT. A deployed page can still fail the publishing standard and
must then be corrected or withdrawn.

## Operating rule for AI collaborators

Before adding or rewriting public teaching:

1. read this standard;
2. read the canonical episode/source and the closest approved example;
3. complete the reverse brief;
4. write one representative unit;
5. test it against the scorecard before scaling the pattern;
6. read it in the real interface;
7. record the evidence and honest status.

Do not fill a page merely because a layout has an empty slot.
