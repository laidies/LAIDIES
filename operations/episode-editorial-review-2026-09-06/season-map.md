# Season 1 — proposed story and learning sequence

Status: PROPOSED, Ali decision required for Episodes7–24. These are planning labels and episode situations, not approved public titles or publishable treatments. No older provisional map is silently promoted. First six episode jobs and titles remain settled; Episode5/6 prose production status is separate.

## Governing outcome
A capable woman finds an inviting way to begin, learns to get useful work from AI, becomes dependable enough that others rely on her work, builds a bounded helper and a useful complementary set, then supervises them and helps another woman begin. Competence becomes visible through decisions and consequences, not a narrator repeatedly announcing confidence. The season delivers real AI literacy as well as task skill: training, context, retrieval, tools, uncertainty, evaluation, power and accountability must connect.

## Story continuity
Keep the existing heroine, Steve (foil, never a cartoon villain), the tone-sensitive stakeholder, girlfriends and town natives. Carry selected work forward: the avoided email becomes a repeatable client-update method; an apparently tiny source/permission error later matters when other people rely on the output. The town supplies encounters and pressure-testing; it is not a list of locations to insert mechanically. Some episodes should concern personal life and civic judgment as well as work. Do not invent a second founder or make the heroine the sole builder of the already-existing town.

Town appearances follow story need. Patron Saints recur in their locked roles; they do not have to own one episode each. A named persona is not automatically an agent. Courses are optional depth, never prerequisites required to understand or finish the episode's bounded move.

## Why this differs from the two old maps
- Privacy/boundaries come before file-heavy use and saved context; basic safe-input advice is still required in Episode1.
- An explicit training/context/retrieval mental model prevents a season of tool tricks with no understanding; Episode9 deepens the short mechanism already introduced earlier.
- Summary, email and deck making are examples or practical labs, not automatically separate episodes. Their story earns a place only if it advances a distinct capability or character consequence.
- A visible sharing/handoff turn at16 pays off the early recognition/participation stakes.
- Agents have a build/test/permissions/supervision progression. Episodes17–19 do not stop at a cast of named personas, and21 makes continued operation part of the payoff.
- Bias and accountability appear explicitly at20, but safe use, stakeholder consequences and representation are seeded earlier; they are not a last-minute ethics appendix.
- The finale pays forward a practical skill. It does not grade the heroine's worth by speed or by replacing other workers.

## Boundaries carried into future briefs
- Episode3: recognize and independently check claims. Episode11: answer a bounded question from the right supplied document/version. Episode12: research across independent sources and reconcile disagreement. Do not replay the same checking lesson three times.
- Episode9 deepens the learned-pattern versus retrieved-source distinction. It is not a second general introduction or hallucination episode.
- Episode15: save a solo method. Episode16: make it usable by another human. Episode19: coordinate complementary helpers without losing source fidelity.
- Episode17 remains draft-only with supplied permitted material; Episode18 deliberately introduces expanded access and permissions. A helper cannot acquire side effects by implication.

Reuse existing High inventory and its locally building masterclass before commissioning anything. Each needs demonstration, practice, feedback and transfer. No course is claimed filmed, published or required to follow the season.
