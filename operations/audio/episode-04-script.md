## ① Cold open

It's ten-forty on a Tuesday, and someone has just said the four most unhelpful words in the modern workplace. [dry] "Just use AI for it." ... Cool. Great. Which one? [flat] Because I have six tabs open and they are all confidently beige rectangles wearing the same name tag. ChatGPT. Claude. Copilot. Gemini. Something my company announced in a town hall that I have personally never been able to find. [beat] "Just use AI" — as if AI is one obedient little intern, and not an entire mall directory of tools all pretending to be the same store.

[thoughtful] And I couldn't help but wonder... if "just use AI" is supposed to be the easy part, why does it feel like getting dropped in a food court with no "you are here"?

## ④ The story (in town)

[warm] So I did the thing I do now — I took the confusion to town. And this week that meant a building I'd walked past a hundred times without going in: the LIBRAiRY, up on the quiet end of Main. [dry] Not the fun stretch. No cocktails, no karaoke, no one doing Britney at eleven p.m. The reference shelf. The place you go when you need to actually look something up instead of vibe your way through it. [warm] And on the desk, there's a slim book with an extremely dramatic title: the SLAiYER Handbook. Chapter One opens with the exact sentence I needed, printed like a dare: [pointed] "use AI" is not an instruction.

Here's what it sorts out in about a page. [measured] AI isn't one thing you're bad at. It's four different things wearing one word — and the fastest way to keep them straight is to picture the kind of show this whole town was raised on. A group of friends fighting the forces of darkness, each one with a different job.

[warm] Generative AI is the witch. You hand her words, and she conjures a thing out of them — a paragraph, an image, a slide, some code. Input intention, output creation. [dry] That's what most people mean when they say "AI" right now: you ask, it makes.

The reasoning model is the Watcher — the one back in the library who reads the whole situation and thinks it all the way through before anyone moves. [measured] Slower on purpose, and better at exactly what blurting is bad at: the math, the logic, the plan with six moving parts. [smirk] That's your episode title, by the way. A Slayer needs a Watcher because the Watcher is the one who thinks first.

Agentic AI is the Slayer sent out on patrol. You don't tell her how to handle every vampire — you send her out, and she goes and does it. [pointed] It's the leap from telling you how to book the flight to going and booking the flight — clicking, filling, sending, in your actual accounts. Thrilling, and also exactly where the stakes change, because a thing that can act is only ever as safe as the permissions you handed it.

And AGI is the prophesied one everybody's waiting for — the all-powerful figure spoken about in the old texts, who could handle basically any job a person can. [dry] Hold onto this one: nothing you can sign into today is that. When a keynote says "AGI," they mean the thing people are either selling tickets to or losing sleep over — not a product with a login.

[knowing] And the twist the Handbook makes you underline: these aren't four separate species you choose between. They're layers. One tool can conjure like a witch, switch on the Watcher for a hard problem, and start running Slayer errands — all in the same afternoon. Knowing the words just means that when someone says one at you, you know which power they're actually talking about.

[warm] And suddenly the mall stops being overwhelming and turns into a directory. The big general assistants — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot — the all-rounders, where most everyday work happens. The specialists — Perplexity when you need research with its sources, NotebookLM for the pile of documents you already have, the note-taker sitting in your meetings — each one job, done well. Visual tools for when the thing needs to be seen instead of read. And the power tools, the ones that reach into real files and live websites, which we handle with oven mitts. [pointed] The point was never to collect them like lip glosses. It's to stop shoving one tool at every job and then feeling dumb when it can't do all five.

[warm] And that, it turned out, is the entire job of the Handbook. Every Slayer needs a Watcher — the one who tells you what you're walking into, before you walk in. [dry] Because "just use AI" is walking into the Hellmouth with a tote bag, a nice attitude, and absolutely no idea what's down there.

## ⑤ Cocktail party explanation

[deliberate] Which brings me to the cocktail party version — the line you can actually say out loud when someone at happy hour goes, "wait, which AI am I even supposed to use?" Here it is: AI isn't one thing. It's a cast. [dry] And the skill everyone skips is not hunting for the single best tool — it's knowing which one to call for which job. [warm] You would never ask Legal to fix the printer or IT to review a contract. Same energy. Match the tool to the task, and about half of feeling behind just... evaporates.
