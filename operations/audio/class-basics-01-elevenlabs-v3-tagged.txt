=== HOST VOICE (Jessica) ===

[warm] So you signed up, and it opened, and... it's a box. That's it. A box with a cursor blinking in it, and no instructions anywhere. [dry] If your first thought was "and now what" — that is not you missing something. There is genuinely nothing there telling you.

[warm] So let's name the furniture, because it's hard to use a room when nothing in it has a name. Down here in the middle is where you type. Over on the left, at the top, is a button that says New chat — that starts a fresh one. Underneath, your past conversations stack up, and you can open any of them again. And your own name, down at the bottom, is the way into your settings — where nearly everything else we'll ever cover actually lives.

[measured] Four things are the spine. Type here. Your history on the left. One button for a new chat. Your name for the settings. [warm] There's more on that left side — a search, a couple of things we'll meet later — but those four are the ones you need on day one. And here's the useful part: on your phone, these same four things exist, just tucked in slightly different spots — the settings behind a little gear, and so on. [pointed] So learn them as four things, not four positions, and you'll find them on anything.

[thoughtful] Now, the thing that surprised me. There's more behind that settings door than you'd expect. Not just your name and your billing — whole sections for your memory, for connecting other things, for controls most people never open. [warm] You don't need any of it today. I'm showing you so you know it's there — so when someone says "just turn on the thing," you know there's a place where the things are. [dry] That is most of the difference between people who find these tools easy and people who don't. Not cleverness. Knowing there's a menu.

[measured] Okay. Before you type anything — there are two things here, and they are not the same thing. This matters more than it sounds like it does. Everything you can click is one of them: the box, the history, the settings, your account, the files you put in. All of that is one thing. [warm] And somewhere inside it, there's a part that does nothing at all except produce words, one after another. That's the other one.

[pointed] The part you click is the tool. The part that produces the words is called the model. You'll meet that word constantly — which model are you on, this model's better at that — and it's worth knowing exactly what it means. [deliberate] The tool is what you set up. The model is what writes. Keep those two apart, and nearly everything else stops being confusing.

[measured] So what does the model actually do? When you press enter, it looks at everything in front of it, and it produces the words most likely to come next. That's the mechanism. Not looking anything up, not thinking it over — producing the most likely continuation of what it was handed.

[warm] Which explains something you're going to run into constantly. Ask a hundred different people to describe a chair, and you get: four legs, wooden, nothing you'd remember. [dry] Not one of them is wrong. The average is just... unmemorable. If you give it almost nothing to go on, the most likely answer is the most average one — and that's what lands in front of you. [pointed] It's not being lazy. That flat, could-be-anyone answer is what "most likely" looks like when nothing has narrowed it down.

[knowing] Which brings us to the first thing everybody types — and it's the one I'd skip. "What can you do?" [dry] And back comes a list. A perfectly accurate, completely useless list. You've learned nothing about whether this is any good, because you didn't give it anything to be good at. [wry] This is the moment most people write this off.

[warm] So try this instead. Take one real thing off your actual plate today — something small, and something you wouldn't mind getting back a little wrong. And then tell it who it's for, and what it needs to do. [measured] Watch what comes back. Not perfect — you'd still change it — but that's a starting point instead of a blank page. [pointed] Same tool. Same minute. The only difference is that this one had something to work with.

[measured] So. The box, the history, New chat, the settings. The tool is what you click; the model is what writes. And it produces the most likely next words for whatever you put in front of it. [warm] That's the map. Everything else this term is just rooms off that one menu.

[warm] Give it one real thing today. Not a test — something off your actual list. [dry] And if it hands you a throw pillow... now you know why.
