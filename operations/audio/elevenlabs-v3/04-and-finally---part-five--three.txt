Word one: generative AI. Think of it as having a Carrie Bradshaw in your laptop. Not one that finds you articles to read — that's Google. One that actually writes the column for you. Generative AI creates new things — text, images, code, summaries, first drafts — instead of just searching or sorting what already exists. These days, when someone at the office says "AI"? This is what they mean.

Word two: model. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are products — the apps you open. The model is the trained brain powering each one. It's like how Vogue, Elle, and Harper's Bazaar are all fashion magazines — but each one has a different editor-in-chief, with different taste, different judgment, different style. Give the same brief to all three, and you'll get wildly different results back. That's the model difference.

And word three: hallucination. That's when AI produces something that sounds confident and polished — and is factually wrong, or completely made up. It's not lying; lying requires intent. It just has no built-in "hold on — do we have receipts for this?" mechanism.
