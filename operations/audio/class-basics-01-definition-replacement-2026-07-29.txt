[measured] Okay. Before you type anything, let’s meet the cast. There is the product you open, the model doing the core processing, the context available for this task, and any tools the product can use. They work together, but they are not the same thing.

[warm] Chat G-P-T, Claude, and Gemini are products. The product is the whole experience you meet: the screen, your account, settings, conversation history, permissions, limits, and features.

[measured] A model is the trained component that turns an input into an output. That output might be text, an image, audio, code, a prediction, or a choice about what to do next.

[pointed] The model is not the whole product. You can use a model through an app, an A-P-I, or a coding tool. One product can offer several models, switch between them, or combine them with search, files, memory, code, and other tools.

[measured] Context is what the system has on its desk for this task: your request, relevant conversation, instructions, files, and selected results from any tools it used. What you never put on the desk may never reach the model.

[warm] Here is the map. Your request enters the product. The product assembles the instructions and context. A model processes that input. If the product allows it, a tool — like search — can bring back more material before the response is finished.

[pointed] That is why “it just writes the next word” is too small a map for the product in front of you. Prediction is important to how language models work. But the experience you are using may also include images, live search, files, tools, and several passes through more than one component.

[measured] Remember the cast: model capability, product features, available context, and what your account actually includes. Any one of them can change what comes back.
