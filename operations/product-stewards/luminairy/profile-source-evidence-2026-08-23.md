## MAiVENS — Foundresses

### Ada Lovelace

- Supported fact: her 1843 Notes on the Analytical Engine include an algorithm
  for calculating Bernoulli numbers.
- Scope: do not use the unqualified phrase “first programmer.”
- Source: [Bodleian Libraries — Ada Lovelace](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/libraries/weston/collections/ada-lovelace)
- LAiDIES lesson: a useful program is a precise sequence, not a magic answer.

### Grace Hopper

- Supported fact: Hopper worked on the Mark I and developed early compiler work;
  she later helped develop and promote COBOL.
- Source: [U.S. Naval History — Grace Hopper](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/people/namesakes/grace-hopper.html)
- LAiDIES lesson: clearer instructions make powerful systems more usable.

### Hedy Lamarr

- Supported fact: Lamarr and George Antheil patented a frequency-hopping
  communication system.
- Scope: do not say that Lamarr “invented Wi-Fi.”
- Sources: [Hedy Lamarr official biography](https://www.hedylamarr.com/about/biography/) · [U.S. Patent 2,292,387](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2292387A/en)
- LAiDIES lesson: an idea can take a long, indirect route into later technology.

### Karen Spärck Jones

- Supported fact: Cambridge records her as a pioneer in information retrieval
  and natural-language processing; her work included inverse document frequency.
- Source: [University of Cambridge — Karen Spärck Jones](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/misc/obituaries/sparck-jones/)
- LAiDIES lesson: search improves when a system weighs what distinguishes a term,
  not merely whether it appears.
