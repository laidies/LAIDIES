# Ali Homepage walkthrough — 2026-08-22

**Surface reviewed:** live `https://laidies.ai/`
**Authority:** Ali's desired visitor outcome and qualitative direction for the

## What the Homepage must do

The Homepage is the threshold to LAiDIES and SUNNYVAiLE. A first-time visitor
must quickly understand:

- LAiDIES teaches women how AI works, how to use it and how to think about it;
- SUNNYVAiLE is the learning town through which those experiences connect;
- the Rewind Era and pop-culture memory are teaching devices, not decoration;
- she can immediately choose a useful next action without first learning the
  town map; and
- joining is optional. Public learning and the Wednesday Postcard must not be
  confused with creating a Resident Card.

The existing masthead image and displayed copy are locked together. Preserve
the exact `assets/sunnyvaile-streets/main-street-dusk.webp` image and the exact
current headline, strapline, explanatory paragraph and “Be kind, rewind” line
from `index.html`; do not redraw the street, replace its signs, invent a new
tagline or paraphrase the explanation. Improvements may change responsive
composition and give **SUNNYVAiLE** more visual emphasis without changing those
words or image bytes.

Masthead lettering uses the image's own purple, pink and teal/cyan family;
mint is not a masthead accent. The shared header may use the gradient treatment
Ali liked, or an equally cohesive pop-art background, but its navigation remains
the canonical town-wide utility rather than a Homepage contents menu.

The redesign inherits the established LAiDIES identity rules. The public name
is always written **LAiDIES** with that exact casing; the `Ai` is a deliberate
contrasting colour unit, never dropped, separated or restyled into a different
wordmark. In the live-text header treatment, preserve the canonical teal `Ai`
and the square tittle over the `i`; only that tittle cycles through the six
established accent colours, and reduced-motion visitors receive the static pink
tittle. New layouts may recompose the surrounding header, but they do not
silently replace these known identity behaviours.

The comprehension sequence is non-negotiable:

1. **LAiDIES teaches AI for women without requiring a technical background.**
2. **SUNNYVAiLE organizes the different ways to learn and use that knowledge.**
3. **Each building has a familiar job:** the LIBRAiRY explains durable concepts,
   the NewsStand keeps them current, Chick Flicks teaches through stories,
   SUNNYVAiLE High will teach practical application, KSVL reinforces ideas
   through music, and the other buildings host practice, tools and community.
4. **The Rewind Era makes unfamiliar ideas easier to understand and remember.**

A newcomer must be able to explain that relationship after the first screen.
If she instead asks “Why is there a town and what does this have to do with
AI?”, the Homepage has failed regardless of how attractive the artwork is.

## What already works and must survive

- The masthead's plain-English explanation of LAiDIES.
- The idea behind **What brought you to town today?**
- The Wednesday in SUNNYVAiLE ritual and episode route.
- The town/district metaphor as a discovery layer.
- The Resident Card/Closet explanation as the reason to save and continue.
- KSVL, LUMINAiRY, Miss Jeeves and the Rewind-era references as distinctive
  parts of the world.

These ingredients should be edited into one hierarchy, not discarded.

## What is wrong on the live Homepage

### 1. Everything has similar weight

The page contains many valid ideas, but it presents them as a long sequence of
boxes and sections. A visitor has to assemble the product model herself. The
problem is hierarchy and grouping, not lack of content.

### 2. The current town is buried

The Homepage does not make it easy to see what is happening in SUNNYVAiLE now.
Current admitted NewsStand material should have a prominent editorial home:
news, the Daily explainer or concept, Paige's practical AI tip, the career/
work-life tip, Mme CLAi-O's reading, a featured Big Picture/Tribune story and
other governed Daily services. Unavailable items show an honest empty state;
they are never filled with invented material.

The Homepage is a preview and route into the full NewsStand, not a replacement
for the newspaper.

### 3. The ways to use LAiDIES are not grouped clearly

The primary choices should be organized by visitor job, with plain descriptions
of the result:

- **Ask or find something:** Miss Jeeves gives a quick grounded answer and
  points to exact useful internal and trusted external sources.
- **Learn and understand:** LIBRAiRY books and sections, Episodes and future
  Classes.
- **Keep current and think bigger:** NewsStand current editions, topics,
  features and archive.
- **Practise or try something:** tools and activities.
- **Build a skill with a game:** games that name the skill they reinforce, not
  merely that they are fun.
- **Explore women in AI:** LUMINAiRY, Mavens, Trailblazers and Patron Saints.
- **Enter the world:** KSVL, Mall/Rewind references, town buildings and the
  Wednesday route.

### 4. The unique town concept needs a short explanation

The Homepage should make the unusual town model understandable without
overloading the first visit. The Visitor's Centre can own the deeper welcome,
tour and explanation of how LAiDIES works. The Homepage owns the quick click:
what the town is, why it helps and which action to take now.

Returning visitors should not be forced through the full first-visit
orientation again. Current admitted content and a truthful continuation cue
should become more prominent for them.

### 5. Town navigation overpromises and adds clicks

District cards currently look like grouped navigation, but do not reliably
reveal the named buildings. For example, **Civic Square** names the Visitor's
Centre, Town Hall, Post Office and LIBRAiRY while its card opens Town Hall.
Primary destinations must be directly findable by their exact public names and
state what the visitor will find there. A district is useful only if it
truthfully reveals its destinations; it must not be a decorative extra click.

### 6. Important destinations and jobs are not explained

The Homepage must make LIBRAiRY, Miss Jeeves, NewsStand, KSVL, LUMINAiRY,
Episodes, tools, games and future Classes understandable through their visitor
job. Town Hall also needs a clear description if its job is to introduce
LAiDIES/SUNNYVAiLE characters and civic world-building.

The current page contains a short LUMINAiRY paragraph that names Patron Saints,
MAiVENS and Trailblazers, but this is too easy to miss and does not introduce
the people of the world. The revised hierarchy needs a meaningful, visually
legible way to meet:

- the recurring SUNNYVAiLE characters and the role each one plays;
- the Patron Saints and the qualities or lessons they represent; and
- the historical and current women in AI reached through LUMINAiRY.

This is not a demand to put every person on the Homepage. It is a clear
editorial entrance that explains why these groups matter and routes to the
complete experience.

### 6a. The Homepage Miss Jeeves artwork is outdated

The current Homepage directly loads
`/assets/library/jeeves-scene.webp`. Ali has identified the visible Miss Jeeves
as not the latest character representation. That exact Homepage use is now
rejected as stale identity evidence and may not be treated as the successor.

Several newer-named Jeeves files exist, including
`assets/library/jeeves-desk.png`, but file date, filename and prior prototype
use do not prove which one is current or approved. The visual curator must
resolve the exact canonical Miss Jeeves reference and reject superseded
variants before a Homepage candidate is built.

### 7. Resident Card and Wednesday Postcard are conflated

Creating a Resident Card is the opt-in path for supported saving, progress,
collections and continuation. Subscribing to the Wednesday Postcard is a
separate email choice for news and new releases. A visitor must be able to take
either action without the page implying that one requires the other.

### 8. The visual system feels old and disconnected

The live page uses softer pastel/candy colours where Ali expects the more
vibrant, electric evolution of the LAiDIES palette. It also lacks the pop-art
devices that can visually connect episode, editorial and town experiences.
The whole composition should feel like a polished, lively 1990s town for adult
women: bright, bold and colourful—not country/cottagecore, gothic fairy tale,
juvenile, flat, generic, overly painterly or photorealistic.

Environment and object art can use the established vibrant 1990s town language.
Pop-art shapes, callouts and graphic devices can bridge that system with the
adult graphic-novel language used for Episodes. They must organize or clarify;
they must not become decorative clutter.

### 9. Public visual coverage is visibly incomplete

The current source contains nine named held Homepage visual slots: dial-up,
Ada, Chick Flicks, Mme CLAi-O, Businesswomen's Special, Girl Talk Truth, Girl
Talk Dare, NewsStand and puffy binder. These are deliberate hold states after
asset cleanup, not proof that replacement art exists or is approved. The page
cannot be called visually complete until exact successor art passes its
destination-specific visual gate and is publicly verified.

### 10. The page wastes space and its card systems do not look authored

Ali does not want unexplained blank gaps, manufactured section height or large
areas with no visual or informational job. Peer cards within one section need
the same outer geometry and a deliberate shared composition. Internal image
placement may vary only when the set still reads as one family.

The live **What brought you to town today?** set fails that standard:

- two card accents disappear into their section background;
- only **Explore the town** has an image;
- its split treatment squeezes the copy and crops the image into an unreadable
  fragment; and
- the four cards are forced into a three-column grid at wide viewports, so the
  fourth card wraps rather than forming a deliberate set.

The activity cards also have inconsistent actions and content heights. Images
must have an owned display region, approved focal point and crop rule; no face,
object, sign or teaching detail may be lost to a generic `cover` crop. Every
peer card needs a stable title, description and action region, with text that
fits at desktop, intermediate and mobile widths.

### 11. Businesswomen's Special is not merely a drink generator

The current line, “Spin for a perfectly unnecessary happy-hour
recommendation,” hides the real product. Businesswomen's Special is a playful
way to **call a happy hour with friends, choose a cocktail or equally complete
spirit-free option, and use the BRONZE AiGE conversation menu to talk about AI
and what everyone is trying**. The name carries the *Romy and Michele's High
School Reunion* reference, but the control still has to explain its outcome to
someone who does not recognize it. Homepage copy must foreground the social AI
ritual and may add the reference as earned flavour; it must not reduce the
experience to a random recommendation.

### 12. Existing, local, public and planned capabilities are currently mixed

The Homepage cannot truthfully represent the full town until the page-by-page
walkthrough and a repository-wide destination inventory distinguish:

- publicly working and publicly verified;
- built and verified locally but not deployed;
- present in source but not end-to-end verified;
- specified or planned only;
- rejected, superseded or throwaway work; and
- missing navigation or a missing receiving page despite some supporting
  functionality existing.

This is especially important for Closet continuation, Butterfly Clips and
spending, messages/postcards, community, saving and rewards. Their concepts are
important to the Homepage story, but a concept, local prototype or browser-only
receipt cannot be advertised as a working public outcome.

## First-session entry transition

Ali wants the first Homepage arrival in a browsing session to feel like moving
from the modern world into LAiDIES: a brief dial-up/entering-SUNNYVAiLE sequence,
the LAiDIES wordmark with changing `i` symbols, then a clean handoff into the
masthead. It must not replay when the visitor navigates away and returns in the
same tab/session.

The later canonical logo family identifies
`continuous-i-evergreen-six-clean-electric-v10.mp4` (SHA-256
`05a52c003ecf0b0caad7dcdb9c056da3b77dd9ee27d9dc67ee0aa7eaf2c1ffa3`) as the
Homepage master. The release worktree contains its manifest but not the MP4
bytes, and no combined Homepage portal implementation exists. The separate
Episode transformation cue supplies related “NOW ENTERING SUNNYVAiLE / DIALING
UP” language, but it is not itself Homepage authority. A successor may compose
these already-decided ideas only after the exact media bytes are recovered and
admitted for this destination.

Required behavior:

- session-scoped suppression (`sessionStorage` or an equivalently bounded
  mechanism), surviving reload and internal navigation but ending with the tab;
- visible Skip and Pause when motion is playing;
- reduced-motion users receive the static masthead immediately;
- muted/silent playback with no audible autoplay;
- masthead, navigation and core actions are never blocked if media or storage
  fails; and
- no loop, interstitial gate, essential-content delay or repeat on every Home
  click.

## Homepage discovery model

Use a **guided town square**, not an infinite feature catalogue:

- first screen: the comprehension sequence above, one clear newcomer route and
  a small number of outcome-labelled alternatives;
- **New in town:** a finite, dated set of current admitted items plus one route
  to the full NewsStand/current archive;
- **What do you want to do?** a compact visitor-job layer for ask/find, learn,
  keep current, practise, choose a game and connect;
- **How SUNNYVAiLE works:** a concise visual explanation plus an obvious full
  welcome tour;
- **Explore the town:** the complete, directly labelled directory for the
  long tail; and
- returning value: continuation and current material may rise in prominence
  only when their state is actually known.

This is progressive disclosure, not concealment. Every hidden capability must
remain findable under a plain label, and every control must say what happens
when it is used. The town can be surprising and playful; its interface cannot
be a mystery.

## Permanent header and KSVL contract

The shared header should be a stable utility bar across the town: Home/brand,
a short priority-ordered set of task routes, current-location state,
account/Closet state when proved, one complete Explore menu and KSVL controls.
It is not a compressed sitemap.

The current implementation does not meet that contract:

- Homepage uses its own `.topbar`, so the shared header does not mount there;
- the shared **KSVL 99.9** item is a link to `/radio.html`, not an audio-start action;
- Homepage has no `data-ksvl-start-live` control;
- the current player has substantial controls after playback starts, but its
  local restoration deliberately excludes the `live` queue;
- LIBRAiRY and Visitor's Centre are among the key routes that do not load the
  shared player; and
- `ksvl-player.js` and `mini-player.js` are competing player systems.

The successor must use one canonical player and separate two outcomes:

- **Listen/Pause KSVL 99.9** starts or controls audio after an explicit user
  action; and
- **Open KSVL** navigates to the station page.

Once started, visible controls retain the same location, name and meaning on
every permitted page: listen/pause, previous/next only when the catalogue really
supports them, volume/mute, current track/status, stop and Open KSVL. Device
Media Session controls are an enhancement, not a substitute. Full-page
navigation destroys an in-page audio object, so uninterrupted audio must use a
proved shared application shell or the explicit pop-out; local state restoration
must not be called seamless continuous playback.

## Proposed Homepage hierarchy

This is the smallest structure that reconciles Ali's walkthrough without
turning the Homepage into a complete town directory.

1. **Evergreen arrival** — existing core promise, explicit LAiDIES → learning
   town → buildings comprehension sequence, stronger SUNNYVAiLE cue and one
   obvious first action.
2. **What's happening in SUNNYVAiLE** — current admitted NewsStand/Daily and
   current-release preview, with truthful quiet/empty states.
3. **What would you like to do?** — the grouped visitor jobs above, including
   prominent Miss Jeeves and direct LIBRAiRY navigation.
4. **Your Wednesday in SUNNYVAiLE** — the preserved episode ritual as a guided
   route, not the only way to use LAiDIES.
5. **Meet the women and people of LAiDIES** — a meaningful entrance to the
   SUNNYVAiLE characters, Patron Saints, MAiVENS and Trailblazers, with KSVL
   and the Rewind reference world edited into the surrounding hierarchy rather
   than another equal card grid.
6. **Explore SUNNYVAiLE** — direct named building routes or truthful district
   reveals, with each building's purpose.
7. **Save, collect and continue** — Resident Card/Closet value and exact
   supported scope.
8. **Get the Wednesday Postcard** — a separate subscription action that does
   not require residency.

## Build sequencing decision

Do not wait for every page to be fully designed before correcting the live
Homepage, and do not freeze a final Homepage before the destination inventory
and guided tour are complete.

Use two passes:

1. **Foundation correction now:** fix the town explanation, hierarchy, direct
   navigation, broken actions, layout craft, known stale visuals and truthful
   capability labels using only verified destinations.
2. **Destination-informed revision:** as each page/feature walkthrough and
   local-vs-public inventory is completed, update the Homepage destination map
   and promotional treatment. New features enter only when their receiving
   experience and status are known.

This improves the live front door without pretending the town inventory is
already settled. The Homepage remains a controlled composition with explicit
revision points, not a one-shot redesign or an endlessly appended page.
