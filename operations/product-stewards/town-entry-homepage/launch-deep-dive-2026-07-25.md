## Intent, message and hierarchy

The hero says what the product is and why its method is different: “AI fluency” through pop culture a visitor remembers. The page then offers four immediate intents and later purpose cards, a weekly/episode route, reference lookup, activities, an explainer, map/directory and more town depth. This is aligned with the product rule that a visitor need not learn the whole town before using it.

The strongest hierarchy to protect is:

1. practical AI value and a safe next step;
2. a small number of purpose-based choices;
3. current-week/story continuity for those who want it; and
4. town depth as optional discovery.

The visual system uses the candy palette, editorial type, full-bleed imagery and object-world cues rather than generic SaaS. Source-level responsive rules reorganise hero, cards, directory and forms for smaller screens. These are source positives, not a visual/accessibility approval. In particular, the number of simultaneous hero and adjacent navigation choices needs clean-user evidence rather than taste-only expansion.

## Route, discovery and service truth

The canonical town directory supplies building names/routes, but it cannot certify every building's product readiness. Entry must maintain a small manifest that joins each promoted destination to its owning champion's current status. It should offer an evergreen alternative when a current surface is held rather than putting a newcomer into a broken or misleading flow.

Newsletter is an external-service handoff. A documented hosted fallback and non-guaranteed “request sent” wording are good boundaries, but no submission/confirmation/retry behavior was tested in this run. The entry steward may require that proof; it may not call an email address subscribed based on an iframe render or button click.

## Analytics, privacy and learning

Plausible/Clarity tags appear on the homepage, but no entry-specific event dictionary, aggregate pull, baseline, review owner or experiment log is wired. After consent/privacy review, use controlled aggregates for path selection and failure only. Never record email, search/query text, identity, private prompts, form content or raw replay material in champion evidence.

Clicks alone cannot validate the entry product. Combine route-choice data with brief qualitative comprehension checks and receiving-product completion/failure evidence. A high click rate to an unavailable activity is a routing defect, not conversion.

## Launch disposition

### FIX BEFORE LAUNCH

1. Run clean first/returning visitor and mobile/keyboard entry tests through the actual promoted destinations.
2. Create and enforce an entry-route readiness manifest so primary calls never overpromise receiving products.
3. Prove current-week/latest labels and newsletter lifecycle or visibly narrow the relevant entry copy.
4. Resolve any primary nav/map/directory dead end, inaccessible control or stale destination.

### HIDE/LABEL FOR LAUNCH

- Limit claims about membership, community, account restoration, rewards and current weekly content to verified behavior.
- Label/replace any promoted path whose owning product is held or unresolved.
- Keep every social/reopening claim, channel status, asset and copy at **HOLD / NOT APPROVED** until the explicit gate passes.

### POST-LAUNCH EXPERIMENT

After the truth foundation exists, test one controlled purpose-led versus story-led first action and one returning cue. Use privacy-safe aggregates plus comprehension evidence; do not optimise toward superficial clicks.

### DECLINE: revenue before trust

The entry product must not monetize orientation, placement, referrals, account restoration or newsletter attention. Revisit only after the free first journey is accessible, honest, publicly verified and not dependent on hidden downstream gaps.
