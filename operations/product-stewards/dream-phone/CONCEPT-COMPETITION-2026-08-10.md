# Dream Phone full-game concept competition

**Status:** CONCEPTS 1–2 REJECTED; CONCEPT 3 SURVIVES FOR CLARIFICATION ONLY
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Decision boundary:** concept selection only; no visual, code, content-deck,
deployment, reward or release authority

## Governing test

The game must remain desirable when every teaching label is removed. AI
usefulness must emerge from decisions and consequences inside the game; it may
not be supplied by a lesson pasted onto a quiz, workplace assignment or
decorative phone interface.

The original 1991 game contributes these mechanics, not its dating premise:
a changing mystery, limited caller choice, private or selectively shared
information, a phone that acts as game master, surprise calls and a risky final
dial. Its luck-heavy clue access, stall cards, weak wrong-guess consequence and
adult-level simplicity are not inherited.

## Concept 1 — The Last Call

**ALI VERDICT 2026-08-10: REJECTED.** The premise does not make sense as an AI
game; its AI connection is secondary and unclear.

**Genre:** branching comic phone mystery

At 8:42 p.m., a caller from twenty minutes in the future says, “Do not let
tonight happen,” then the line dies. The player must work out what went wrong
and steer the night toward the best available ending before the future catches
up.

- **Objective:** resolve the mystery and commit to a final call before the
  countdown expires.
- **Loop:** choose one of three ringing people; decide which part of the story
  to pursue; choose whether to call back, pass information, keep it private or
  use one scarce phone effect; watch the next scene change.
- **Meaningful decisions:** whom to trust, which thread to leave unresolved,
  when to expose a secret and when enough is known to make the final call.
- **Tension:** limited time/calls, conflicting motives, surprise rings and a
  final irreversible dial.
- **Comedy:** bad discretion, overheard voicemail, caller-specific agendas and
  consequences rather than joke buttons.
- **Replay:** different hidden cause, alliances, clue paths, secret numbers and
  endings.
- **Natural AI usefulness:** the phone can help the player explore, reframe,
  rehearse or communicate inside different cases; the story shows how context
  and human judgment change the outcome. Different mysteries can expose
  different AI uses without making the game a catalogue.
- **Solo/social:** strongest solo; a later party mode can let players control
  lines or vote on speakerphone/final-call decisions.
- **Kill condition:** if the first choice does not materially change a later
  scene, it is a visual novel with decorative buttons and is parked.

## Concept 2 — Don't Lose the Plot

**ALI VERDICT 2026-08-10: REJECTED.** The premise is bizarre/confusing and its
AI connection is not legible enough to justify the game.

**Genre:** comic transformation / broken-telephone challenge

The player receives a bizarre but precise message, plan or creative brief that
must survive a chain of callers before it reaches its destination. Every caller
can improve one dimension while accidentally distorting another.

- **Objective:** deliver a final result that preserves the essential intent
  while earning style, surprise and audience fit.
- **Loop:** choose one of three callers; select a transformation such as
  shorten, dramatize, visualize, organize, challenge or adapt; lock one
  must-keep detail; receive the changed version; react to the next call.
- **Meaningful decisions:** transformation order, which detail to protect,
  when to revise versus continue and whether a funny improvement is worth the
  risk of drift.
- **Tension:** limited locks/calls, escalating distortion and a surprise caller
  who adds a late constraint.
- **Comedy:** the final output visibly mutates, callers take instructions too
  literally and preserved details collide with accumulated style choices.
- **Replay:** shuffled starting messages, must-keep details, caller hands,
  transformations and destination audiences.
- **Natural AI usefulness:** the player experiences how context, constraints,
  iteration and transformations affect an output. The useful idea is felt in
  the before/after result, not explained afterward.
- **Solo/social:** strong solo score-chase; party mode can let each player own
  a caller or secretly choose a transformation.
- **Kill condition:** if the transformations are predictable templates or the
  scoring rewards keyword preservation more than comic invention, it becomes
  a prompt worksheet and is parked.

## Concept 3 — Dream Team: Saturday Night Swap

**ALI VERDICT 2026-08-10: ONLY POSSIBLE SURVIVOR, NOT SELECTED.** The one-event
description wrongly implied a single-use experience. It may advance only after
showing how repeat missions, changing caller hands, order, complications and
outcomes create a replayable game whose AI connection is obvious in play.

**Genre:** tactical caller-combination game

A rival is about to replace a beloved SUNNYVAiLE hangout. The player has five
turns to assemble a gloriously mismatched crew and throw a counter-event that
the town cannot ignore.

- **Objective:** overcome the event's changing obstacles and create a winning
  final combination before the rival completes its plan.
- **Loop:** draft one of three callers; pair that caller's ability with the
  current obstacle; choose whether to spend a scarce Dream Phone assist; then
  resolve the personality/ability combination.
- **Meaningful decisions:** caller order, complementary versus volatile
  combinations, resource timing and which obstacle to accept rather than fix.
- **Tension:** five turns, rival counters, limited assists and a complication
  revealed after the second call.
- **Comedy:** unlikely character pairings and concrete outcomes—Miranda runs
  the bake sale, Deb takes the microphone, Miss Jeeves books the headliner.
- **Replay:** changing hand, rival, venue, obstacles, abilities and combination
  effects.
- **Natural AI usefulness:** callers embody genuinely different kinds of help
  such as generating possibilities, challenging, organizing, reframing and
  rehearsing. Selecting and sequencing help is the strategy itself.
- **Solo/social:** good solo or turn-based shared play.
- **Kill condition:** if caller abilities reduce to obvious colour matching or
  the AI relationship exists only in labels, it is a dressed-up sorting puzzle
  and is parked.

## Comparative judgment

| Concept | Fun without teaching | Dream Phone DNA | Natural AI usefulness | Main production risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Last Call | Strongest | Strongest | Medium, expandable across cases | High narrative/branch cost |
| Don't Lose the Plot | Strong | Strong | Strongest | Live variability or template predictability |
| Dream Team | Promising | Medium | Strong | Combinatorial content and shallow matching |

## Champion recommendation

Do not prototype Concepts 1 or 2. Clarify Concept 3 with one complete sample
mission showing the opening objective, available callers, exact AI actions,
three consequential choices, mid-round complication, final outcome and replay
variation. If that sample still reads as one-off helper matching, project
management or a lesson rather than a game, park the full game and return
foreground work to Just Call.
