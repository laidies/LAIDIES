# LAiDIES learning-content standard

**Status:** ACTIVE PRODUCT-OWNER DIRECTION
**Applies to:** substantial books, classes, episodes, explanations, learning
tools and educational games. NewsStand retains its separate sourcing and
editorial rules.

## Learner outcome

A LAiDIES learner should leave able to:

1. understand the idea rather than repeat a definition;
2. distinguish it from adjacent concepts and capabilities that exist today;
3. identify nuance, uncertainty and credible disagreement;
4. explain where we are now, what may happen next and possible personal/social
   implications without presenting scenarios as facts;
5. use examples and analogies that clarify without replacing the mechanism;
6. explain the concept accurately to another person;
7. apply or adapt the idea; and
8. recognize misleading claims and identify evidence that would change the
   conclusion.

Depth comes from instructional design, not word count. Begin simply when
useful, then build the correct mental model, test it, add nuance and support
transfer.

## Accuracy and uncertainty

- Separate sourced fact, definition, present capability, inference, expert
  disagreement, forecast and scenario.
- Use current credible sources when claims may have changed.
- Never preserve an outdated contrast because it makes a lesson tidier.
- Do not say current AI can only follow one narrow instruction or that only
  AGI can plan, use tools, pursue goals, work across steps or switch domains.
- For AGI, explain generality and transfer across unfamiliar intellectual
  tasks as an important proposed distinction while acknowledging that modern
  general-purpose models blur the old narrow-AI boundary.
- Represent the product-owner position that AGI does not exist today while
  acknowledging there is no universally agreed definition/test and credible
  experts disagree about thresholds and timelines.

## Analogy rule

An analogy is admitted only when it makes the mechanism easier to understand.
State its limit when the mapping could mislead. Remove it if the explanation
becomes clearer without it. Audience recognition—such as a 1990–2010
pop-culture reference—is never sufficient reason to use one.

## Format jobs

- **LIBRAiRY:** durable conceptual understanding and reference value.
- **High/classes and episodes:** sequenced instruction using narration,
  demonstrations, images and animation to show mechanisms, comparisons, time,
  scale, causal chains and alternative futures.
- **NewsStand:** timely developments and evidence under its established
  sourcing, qualification, correction and editorial rules. Do not force it
  into an evergreen lesson template.
- **Tools such as FAiRY:** solve the immediate job while teaching enough
  reasoning for transfer and limits; route deeper learning rather than
  reproducing a whole book/class.
- **Games:** practise or encourage a useful behaviour or skill. A game
  mechanic is not evidence of learning by itself.

Repetition is justified only when a different modality or retrieval context
meaningfully reinforces learning.

## Required content record

For every substantial item, record:

- topic and audience;
- learning objective;
- prerequisite and intended next experience;
- evidence and date sensitivity;
- correct mental model and adjacent distinctions;
- misconceptions addressed;
- instructional sequence and modality advantage;
- analogy and its limit, if any;
- uncertainty/disagreement;
- explanation, application and misconception-resistance checks;
- overlap, gap and recommended product home;
- quality/accuracy risk, freshness owner and correction path.

## Release gate

Content does not pass because it is readable, attractive, short, funny or
technically rendered. Independent instructional and accuracy reviewers must
show that a representative unfamiliar learner can understand, explain, apply
and qualify it without reproducing a false or outdated claim.
