## Qualifying surprise — a credible first viewport can hide an unfinished building

The incumbent's opening room is materially better than the old essay page and
passes its map/directory contracts. A full-page same-state capture showed that
the page still stops behaving like a Visitor's Centre immediately after the
room. Contract proof and a strong hero crop concealed a 3,502px/3,785px stack of
detached products.

**Prevention rule:** every building visual gate must include a full-page
desktop/mobile comparison and answer whether the building's physical mechanic
still governs the last major action. Never judge only the opening viewport.

**Behind the Build angle:** "The hero was finished. The building wasn't."

## Qualifying fix — generated environment art needs deterministic truth slots

All three direction boards turned the supplied town panorama into generated
approximations. Even visually strong approximations cannot become an operable
map or truth source. Editing the selected direction into a clean compositing
plate created a reliable slot for the exact final-v5 map and removed pseudo-text
from brochures.

**Prevention rule:** when generated art contains a factual map, product screen,
status, name or route, require a clean plate and composite the exact approved
asset/text in deterministic code.

**Behind the Build angle:** "Why the prettiest AI map was never allowed to be
the map."

## Reusable success — one object can restore product ownership

Turning the fold-out paper tourist map into the main counter surface made the
room, directory, destination reveal, tour ticket and postcard ticket one
coherent experience. It also made the product boundary clearer: the Visitor's
Centre offers the postcard object, while the Post Office/postcard route owns the
composer and handoff truth.

**Prevention rule:** before adding a section to a building page, identify the
physical object that owns the action. If no object in that building owns it,
link to the owning product rather than reproducing its full workflow.

**Behind the Build angle:** "We removed half the page by deciding who owned the
postcard desk."

## Qualifying limitation — maker-blind is not independent

The same champion could use a fixed rubric and anonymous labels, but could not
unsee the concepts or provide independent visual approval.

**Prevention rule:** maker score may qualify a candidate for owner review, but
must never close an independent-judgment gate. Record the gate as OPEN until a
separate judge rules.

**Behind the Build angle:** "A blind score is not independent just because the
rows are anonymous."

## Qualifying dependency failure — an optional tour can contradict the front desk

The isolated candidate's 79 checks passed, but the existing Visitor's Centre
source contract failed because the shared Welcome Tour invites KSVL listening
while the current contract requires public listening to remain held. The front
desk and its optional escort therefore disagree about the same destination.

**Prevention rule:** every orientation product must test the current claims of
all shared escorts, menus and directories it launches. An optional handoff is
still part of the product's trust boundary.

**Behind the Build angle:** "The map told the truth; the tour guide didn't."
