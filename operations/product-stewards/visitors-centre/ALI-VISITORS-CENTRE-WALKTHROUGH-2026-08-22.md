# Visitor’s Centre — Ali guided walkthrough

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**Status:** ALI VISION CAPTURED / CURRENT PUBLIC PAGE REJECTED / SYSTEM INVENTORY IN PROGRESS / NO CANDIDATE BUILT

## Product job

The Visitor’s Centre should feel and work like the tourist-information centre
in a real town. It is the complete welcome and orientation home for SUNNYVAiLE:
a visitor can understand how the site works, learn what every building is for,
see what she can do across the town and navigate directly to every building.
The Homepage previews this help; it does not duplicate the full Centre.

The Centre is not a generic directory page. Its map, destination information,
guided tour, trailer and postcard display are parts of one coherent
tourist-centre experience.

## Arrival and map

- The small SUNNYVAiLE map may remain an interactive way to explore. Selecting
  a building should reveal useful information and a clear route.
- The map is not the masthead and cannot carry the whole arrival composition.
- Every building remains findable by its exact public name without requiring
  precise map tapping or remembering where it sits.
- The current masthead, lobby-directory stack, large blank bands and detached
  square modules do not establish a useful Visitor’s Centre.

## Guided orientation

- Build a real optional guided tour that walks through the site, explains each
  page and says what a visitor can do there.
- The tour must help a first-time visitor without turning every destination
  into a long onboarding interruption for returning visitors.
- Direct navigation always remains available. A visitor may start, pause,
  skip, leave or resume the tour without losing access to the page she chose.
- Destination explanations should be compact and contextual. The tour may
  carry the fuller walkthrough while each page still explains its own job and
  primary action without repeating a long generic script.
- The current pop-up treatment is rejected: it uses retired plum/gold styling,
  feels disconnected from the page and does not demonstrate the intended
  experience.

### One orientation system, not two competing tours

Ali raised a second need: visitors may want help based on their goal or on the
kind of content they need, not a linear parade through all 17 buildings. A
single building-by-building sequence risks becoming overwhelming and chaotic,
especially when it jumps between NewsStand, Chick Flicks, Blend & Snap,
SUNNYVAiLE High and the LIBRAiRY without explaining how their jobs differ.

The recommended architecture is one orientation system with two entrances:

1. **Show me the town** — browse every building, its purpose and its direct
   route in any order. This is the complete tourist-centre directory/map, not a
   forced 17-stop procession.
2. **Help me do something** — choose a visitor goal or learning path and see a
   short itinerary across only the relevant buildings. Examples include learn
   something deeply, understand what changed today, follow the current Episode
   and Study Pack loop, practise/apply, create/play or connect with people.

These are two views of one maintained building/content map, not separate tour
products with duplicated descriptions and state. Exact public labels remain
open for Ali's ruling.

The orientation must also explain the distinct job of each content format:
NewsStand handles dated current information; Episodes create memorable stakes
and narrative; Study Packs support review/practice; LIBRAiRY books provide
durable connected reference; Classes demonstrate, practise, diagnose and test
transfer. A route appears only when its destination is truthfully available.

## Proposed boundary with Town Hall — Ali confirmation open

Ali identified a potential collision between the Visitor’s Centre and Town
Hall. The current records support this non-overlapping split:

- **Visitor’s Centre:** “What is SUNNYVAiLE, how does this site work, which
  learning/content path fits what I need, where is each building and how do I
  get there?” It owns orientation, navigation, tours, stable how-to-use-the-site
  FAQs and postcards/invitations.
- **Town Hall:** “Who runs and lives in this fictional town, what is its civic
  history and how can I address the town?” It owns Mayor Deb, the Regulars,
  town backstory/history, notices/civic records and the private compliment,
  complaint or suggestion function when its real intake/staff lifecycle exists.

The Centre may explain that feedback belongs at Town Hall and route there only
when the intake is truthfully available. Town Hall may route navigation/help
questions back to the Centre. Neither building creates a second FAQ store,
feedback form, support channel, character encyclopedia or public-record system.

Current Town Hall truth: Mayor Deb/history/Regular source exists; the character
roster and device-local Town Regular interaction have bounded local evidence;
the feedback form is deliberately disabled because no safe server intake or
staff lifecycle is proved; public civic records are planned only. Town Hall is
not a support hotline, emergency route, public message board or guaranteed
reply channel.

This boundary is recommended and recorded for continuity. Ali has not yet
confirmed the final public framing or exact Town Hall history/character scope.
