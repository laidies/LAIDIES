## Start with the room

The first view is the inside of a colourful, vibrant Visitor’s
Centre at No. 1 MAiN. The room is not a decorative hero above a webpage. The
room itself performs the work.

A visitor should feel that she has crossed a SUNNYVAiLE threshold: deep-plum
ink and shadow, warm lit architecture, candy pink/coral/tangerine/teal/sky/
periwinkle signals, glossy Y2K materials, dimensional signage and meaningful
objects. Colour belongs to architecture, wayfinding, light and operated
objects—not a stack of recoloured boxes.

The direct comparison target is the current colourful Homepage masthead:
full-bleed place imagery, decisive type, strong light/dark contrast, purposeful
candy actions and a clearly inhabited town. The Visitor’s Centre must be at
least as specific and alive while remaining bright enough to read as an open,
welcoming daytime interior.

## Environment is the interface

The primary operated object is the exact SUNNYVAiLE map. The visitor ritual is:

1. cross the threshold and locate “you are here” at No. 1 MAiN;
2. operate the room’s map/wayfinding system;
3. choose one of all 17 named places;
4. read the place’s current status and limitation in the same physical
   environment;
5. deliberately step through its doorway/handoff.

The equal named route is not a white form beside the art. It is a legible,
deterministic layer integrated into a real object: destination shutters,
flip-board slats, illuminated route tabs, a rotary index, an arrival ribbon or
another genuinely diegetic wayfinding device. It remains live HTML and usable
without the image.

Tour, trailer and postcard are secondary take-away objects—a tour ticket,
cinema/trailer ticket and postcard rack—not three webpage modules.

## Pre-ruling environmental hypotheses

The following hypotheses were written before Ali's sitewide-Brand-first
sequence ruling. They remain non-selective intended-experience vocabulary, not
an active owner visual program. Any bounded specimen use belongs to Brand &
Experience's controlled championship.

The exploration varies room architecture and operated-object grammar—not global
brand style.

### Welcome Arcade

A bright, glamorous tourist-arrival arcade. The exact town map occupies a
large illuminated panorama; destination names live as colourful wayfinding
marquee tabs around its architectural frame. Selecting a place lights one
route and opens a physical “departure window” below it. Tickets and postcards
live in real counter fixtures.

### Map Rotunda

A circular civic welcome room with a central compass/map table and a
wrap-around skyline/destination ribbon. The visitor stands at the centre of
SUNNYVAiLE; named routes operate like illuminated directional vanes. One route
draws forward into an in-room doorway/status threshold.

### Visitor’s Centre Dispatch Hall

A lively Y2K travel bureau/television-station hybrid. The exact map is the
dispatch wall; destination truth appears on a mechanical flip-board and one
large departure portal. Glossy luggage tags, ticket punches and route lights
make the building feel useful and specific without becoming a dashboard.
