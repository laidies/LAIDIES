# AI Fundamentals 101: Ali-authored writing standard

**Status:** LOCKED WRITING, VOICE AND INTENT AUTHORITY

**Authority:** Ali direct ruling, 2026-08-09: “use mine as the writing standard.”

## What this governs

This complete Ali-authored introduction governs AI Fundamentals 101 voice,
cadence, humour, point of view, reader relationship, purpose and LAiDIES-world
integration. Producers work from this text as the base. They do not translate
it into a generic explainer and add selected lines back afterward.

It is also a positive calibration artifact for substantial LAiDIES explanatory
prose when the destination needs the same teaching relationship. It does not
impose this introduction's length or three-part structure on every surface.

## What may be edited

- spelling, grammar and Canadian English;
- factual overstatement, ambiguity or unsupported claims;
- genuine repetition that does not perform a voice or teaching job;
- connective explanation required to make an analogy or promise technically
  coherent; and
- current product names and exact Nerd-O-Meter labels.

Those edits must preserve Ali's cadence, jokes, conviction, direct address and
three-part governing purpose. A technically cleaner version that becomes more
institutional, generic or emotionally flat fails.

## Authorial-flow protection

The Introduction has one distinct job: make the reader care, establish the
best-friend relationship and explain the book's practical, information-
judgment and participation purpose. Internal curriculum coverage may not force
concept instruction, a diagnostic checklist or a system map into that
rhetorical arc. The concept sequence begins in Chapter 1.

Review must compare meaning and paragraph flow, not only factual correctness
or whether the replacement sounds like LAiDIES:

- `who does not have a PhD in AI` describes the intended reader; it may not
  become `without first acquiring a PhD`, which implies a different future;
- a compact joke may not be diluted with unnecessary completeness language;
- a true sentence still fails if it changes topics and breaks the setup/payoff
  between adjacent paragraphs; and
- deliberate comic diction, including `forsaketh`, is protected even when it
  is not standard English. Nonstandard wording is not automatically an error.

The producer must start with a line-preserving edit and justify every changed
proposition, joke or transition. A reviewer must read Ali's source and the
candidate side by side; a general voice match cannot prove authorial fidelity.

## Ali-authored source text

### Intro

Alright LAiDIES listen (or should we say, read?) up! This book is really
important. We made AI Fundamentals 101 so there is finally something that
explains all the AI fundamental concepts, how they work, and how the whole
end-to-end AI ecosystem is connected. Basically all the systems, hardware,
software and infrastructure that are needed for you to be able to type
something into your AI application and get an output. All the buzzwords you
hear in news stories like chips, compute, data centres, frontier models,
sandbox, agents and what they actually mean and how they impact you when you
use an AI tool…and, obviously, why you should care.

But AI Fundamentals 101 is not your standard textbook or just a collection of
definitions you can merely look up online. No, no. LAiDIES would never do that
to you. This is not a florals for spring situation. We can (and we will) do
better!

So why is it important for you, as a woman of distinction (or friend to
womankind… all are welcome here) who does not have a PhD in AI to know all
this? We're so glad you asked. Here’s why:

### 1. From “ARGHH WTF!” to “AHH, That's Why!”

AI is a truly transformative technology that is some way is accessible to
almost everyone. It is going to transform the way we work, live, and operate
as a society. But it is different than essentially any other technology that
has done this in the past. Let's take electricity for example. That was
obviously transformative technology that also changed the way we work, live,
and function as a society. However, with electricity, most people just flick
on a switch or plug something in and bam! you've got light at night. That is
for the most part how most of the world interacts directly with the technology
-- we maybe understand a bit of the concept of how electricity works (thanks
Nicola Tesla and Thomas Edison!) but it really is essentially plug and play
(pun intended).

The way we interact with AI is different. We have the ability to get it to
help us build and create (almost...at least for now) anything we can imagine...
(like this website for example). But in order to get it to actually do what we
want, we need to be able to understand more about how it works and the best way
to interact with it to get the optimal output. If you don't, you are more
likely to get something completely different than what you intended and then
get frustrated and walk away. We don't want that - this technology is too
useful to simply say “i don't get what the hype is about, this thing sucks!”
and walk away. No, no, no, dear reader, thou shalt not foresaketh the AI...
though shalt first consult the AI Bible...(a.k.a this book), take one of our
classes (coming soon!) or check out one of the other books or articles on
Laidies.ai. We shall take you from “WTF!!!!! I hate this [expletive] thing, why
can't it follow my basic [expletive] instruction” to “Oh......yes... I get it
now...I know what i have to do to get this back on track”. TLDR: from “ARGHH
WTF!” to “AHH, That's Why!”

### 2. From “OMG THE END IS NIGH!!!” to “Ugh, AS IF!”

Let’s be clear. This is not about jumping on the proverbial bandwagon, we’ll
leave that to our bro friends in crypto…(“Ooo sick burn!” someone shouts int he
back). AI is not a fad, or trend that still struggles to explain how it will
actually be useful to society. The pace of change in AI is phenomenally fast
and everyone is trying to get their piece of it (fully aware of the irony in
this statement given LAiDIES.ai is website dedicated to AI literacy). And
there are dozens of stories a day that come out about something to do with AI.
Some are well researched and others well seem like they belong in the
tabloids. As we know, not all journalists, content creators, AI voices, are
created equal. So how can we cut through all this noise and understand what is
really happening? What we need to pay attention to? What stories should
actually influence our decisions and how we approach things? That isn’t
possible to do unless you have a fundamental understanding of the basics. And
while LAiDIES does try to call these out and go deeper on these stories in the
NewsStand, you should be able to (eventually) do this on your own. So when the
headlines makes it seem like Skynet is taking over and judgement day is just
around the corner, or a content creator these three prompts will solve all your
problems and to DM them, you will be a able to get to the real story and
whether that “hack” is something you are already doing or deserves a “Ugh, As
If!”

### 3. From “I don’t care this has nothing to with me” to “No Aunt Linda, that isn’t what killed the cat. Here is how it works, and how we can have a meaningful discussion managing risks”.

Which brings us to our last main reason. Once you understand how it works, how
you can better use it, and can identify the overly positive, the overly
negative, or the clickbait, claims, you can actually start to understand all
the truly transformative impacts AI is going to have on society. But with such
a powerful technology, there are certainly risks. What you need to decide is
whether the benefits outweigh those risks, what kind of guardrails do we want
to put in to manage risks, how do we want the world to look and operate. There
are many questions and debates that need to be and should be happening right
now. And it is vitally important that you have the tools and knowledge to be
able to participate in these debates — whether it is at city hall discussing a
potential new data centre in your area, or larger more philosophical questions
about who should control all of these models, how do we manage access to
compute, how do we think about the economy and how we are structured when the
world we live in is fundamentally different? As our old friend Karen Spark
Jones said, “computing is too important to be left to men.” Well LAiDIES,
ditto for AI. And it isn’t just men, its the people who do not actually
understand the facts and the impacts that tend to have the loudest voices (for
example your Aunt Linda who insists that the data centre that went up in her
friends city is what killed her friend’s cat. No Linda, the cat was 20 and
should have been dead for the last 5 years). Many mean well, but failing to
engage because of this means we risk making incredibly poor decisions about AI
that impact everyone. And to be fair to your Aunt Linda, no one has ever
actually explained how all this actually works to her. Well, now you can.

And because AI changes so quickly, this book will be up updated regularly to
include new concepts and as our understanding changes.

You can also read this book in three modes. Standard, Tell Me More!, and Nerd
Alert! Each mode goes a little bit deeper into the technical so you can read
the content based on what you want to get out of it at the time - change the
mode setting at any time.

No matter what mode you choose, this book will help you get better results from
your AI tools; Understand the headlines and what is actually happening; Help
shape how you want the world to look like.

So let’s get to it to.

## Active third-heading refinement

Ali subsequently refined the third movement in her own words to:

> **From “Whatever!” to RSVPing “Yes” to policy discussions.**

The final edited introduction must build the invitation/RSVP idea into the
section: workplace implementation meetings, employee consultations, community
planning, public rules and wider debates are invitations to participate. RSVP
“yes” means joining the discussion, not approving every AI use. A producer may
copy-edit punctuation or capitalization but may not replace this with a new
agent-written slogan unless Ali explicitly chooses it.
