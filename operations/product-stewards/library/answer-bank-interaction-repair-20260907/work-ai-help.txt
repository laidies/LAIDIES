Use AI to build the structure, then add and check the real project facts in your company’s approved document system. That gives you a useful starting point without sending confidential information to AI.

Start with a tool your employer permits. Some tools can work inside a document: Microsoft’s current Copilot in Word guidance describes drafting from an outline or reference files, then revising the draft. Availability depends on your subscription and organization’s settings. Being able to attach a file does not mean that file is permitted—use only material approved for that tool.

For an update that keeps project facts out of AI, give it safe context: who will read the update, what they need from it and how long it should be. For example: “My manager needs a short weekly update showing progress and decisions needed. Create a blank format under 200 words with questions to help me fill it in. Leave project facts as placeholders.” If you have a blank company template that is approved for sharing, use it as a reference.

Inspect the structure before filling it in. Does it make room for a delay, its consequence and the help you need? If not, ask for that specific change. Then add this week’s facts yourself in the approved work document. Keep confidential notes outside AI; changing names alone may still leave identifying details.

Before sending, compare every date, number, status and commitment with your records. Check what was left out, too: “on track” could mislead your manager if it depends on an approval that has not arrived. AI can help find gaps when you are allowed to share the material, but its reassurance is not independent verification.

Save the empty format and useful instructions for next week. Replace the facts and check them again. Reusing the structure saves work; carrying forward an old deadline creates new work.
