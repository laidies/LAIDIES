- **Intended learner:** An adult newcomer who uses, or is considering using, AI products but does not have a technical background.
- **Problem or question she brings:** “What do I need to understand about AI so I can use it effectively, make sense of important claims and discussions, and contribute something more useful than hype, fear or borrowed jargon?”
- **Why LAiDIES is teaching this:** AI now appears in ordinary work, home life, public policy, products and headlines. The reader does not need engineering training, but she does need a durable foundation for using it, questioning claims about it and applying her existing judgment where technical language can otherwise obscure the real decision.
- **Why it is important now:** Product capabilities and public claims change quickly, while the distinctions between a product, model, data, context, retrieval, tools, automation and human decisions remain essential to understanding what a claim actually means.
- **Where this appears in her life:** Using AI to draft, organise, research or plan; deciding whether an AI feature belongs in a workplace process; reading claims about what AI can, cannot or will do; and participating in decisions about its evidence, limits and consequences.
- **Observable result:** She can explain the main parts of an AI system in plain language, use that understanding to make a better-informed choice about an ordinary AI task, identify what an oversimplified claim leaves out and locate one unfamiliar term without rereading the book.
- **Concrete next use:** When she encounters an AI feature, result or headline, she can ask what system is actually involved, what evidence supports the claim, which human decision is being made and what important condition has been left out.
