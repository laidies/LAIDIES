A conservative starting point is public material or an invented example that contains no sensitive information. Being able to paste something into an AI tool does not mean you have permission to share it there.

For workplace material, check your employer’s policy and which tool and information it permits. For personal material, consider whose information it is and whether sharing it could expose them. Privacy settings do not grant someone else’s permission.

Keep passwords, access codes, account details, private records and confidential company or customer information out of a general AI prompt. Review the whole attachment, including comments, hidden notes and details visible in screenshots.

Removing names is not enough by itself. A job title, small town and unusual event may identify a person when combined. If you cannot confidently separate the sensitive details, use an invented example or ask for a blank template instead.

For example: “Show me a friendly reminder email about an overdue invoice, using invented details.” Add the real customer and invoice information later in your approved work system.

Share only what the task needs. If permission or the tool’s current handling of your information is unclear, leave the information out.
