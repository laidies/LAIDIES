A prompt (the instructions and information you give the AI) works best when it says what you need, why you need it, and what a good answer should look like. Clear context, constraints and examples make the result more reliable.

Try this formula:

“I need help with [task]. The situation is [relevant background]. Please produce [specific result] for [audience or purpose]. Keep it [length, tone or format]. Include [must-haves]. Avoid [unwanted material]. If anything important is missing, ask me before answering.”

For example:

“I need to explain our new vacation policy to employees. Write a friendly email for people who have not read the policy. Keep it under 250 words. Include the effective date and where to ask questions. Do not invent details. Ask me for anything you need.”

You do not need magic wording. Treat the AI like a capable new colleague who has zero knowledge of your situation. If the first answer misses, say exactly what went wrong: “Shorter,” “use simpler language,” or “you assumed something I did not say.”

One important wrinkle: do not paste confidential, personal or workplace information unless you know you have permission and understand how that AI service handles it.
