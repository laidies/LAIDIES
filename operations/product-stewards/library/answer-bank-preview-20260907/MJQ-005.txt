Give the AI a proper brief. “Make this better” leaves it guessing what better means to you: shorter, clearer, friendlier, or finally something your boss will read.

Your prompt—the instructions and information you give it—should name the job, the useful background and what you want back. Clear instructions and relevant examples help steer the response.

For example, instead of “write an email,” try:

“Help me explain our new vacation policy to employees who have not read it. Write a friendly email under 250 words. Include the effective date and where to ask questions. Do not invent details; ask me for anything you need.”

You can reuse that structure: help me do [job], for [audience], using [relevant context], in [format]. Include [must-haves] and avoid [unwanted material].

Then read what comes back. If it misses, give it a specific correction: “You assumed a start date. Leave a placeholder,” or “The main point is buried. Put it first.” You are directing the draft, not searching for magic words.

Keep confidential, personal and workplace details out unless you know you have permission and understand the tool’s handling of them. A useful brief does not need to contain every detail you know.
