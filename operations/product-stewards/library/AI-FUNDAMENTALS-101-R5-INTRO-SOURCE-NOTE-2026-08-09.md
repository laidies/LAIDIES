# AI Fundamentals 101 R5 introduction source note

Checked: 2026-08-09

Candidate: `LIB-AI-FUNDAMENTALS-101-V4-INTRO-R5-ALI-STYLE-PROOF`

## Ruled claims

### AI interaction and system structure

The introduction may explain that AI systems infer outputs from inputs and can
combine models with surrounding software, information, tools and human choices.
It may contrast a predictable light switch with the more participatory work of
briefing, inspecting and steering a flexible AI application. It may not imply
that electricity is simple in general, that all AI is generative, or that user
skill can overcome a model or product that is unsuitable for the job.

Primary authority:

- OECD, [updated definition of an AI system](https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/definition-)
- OECD, [How artificial intelligence works](https://oecd.ai/en/inside-artificial-intelligence)

Freshness trigger: recheck if the OECD definition changes or before a material
rewrite of the system explanation.

### Physical infrastructure and public choices

Training and running current AI models depend on physical data-centre
infrastructure including servers, storage, networking, cooling and electricity.
Local consequences vary by project and grid; the introduction must ask for the
actual proposal and evidence rather than attributing an unrelated event to a
nearby data centre. It may not dismiss real energy, affordability, planning or
environmental concerns.

Primary authority:

- International Energy Agency, [Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/)
- International Energy Agency, [Energy demand from AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai)

Freshness trigger: recheck before publication and whenever the IEA updates its
energy-and-AI assessment.

### Karen Spärck Jones quotation

The exact line “Computing is too important to be left to men” is preserved in
Oxford Internet Institute's *The Future of Computing: A Vision*, dedicated to
Karen Spärck Jones. LAiDIES may apply the sentiment to AI as its own clearly
marked extension; it may not attribute the AI wording to her.

Primary authority:

- Oxford Internet Institute, [The Future of Computing: A Vision](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/old-docs/FD11.pdf)

Freshness trigger: stable historical quotation; recheck only if attribution is
challenged or a stronger archival source is added.

## Deliberate exclusions

- No universal AI-access claim.
- No claim that every AI product or business model will succeed.
- No claim that AI can build almost anything a person imagines.
- No unsourced adoption, gender-gap, productivity or labour-market statistic.
- No equivalence between understanding AI and accepting its use.
- No claim that a data centre causes a local outcome without project-specific
  evidence.
