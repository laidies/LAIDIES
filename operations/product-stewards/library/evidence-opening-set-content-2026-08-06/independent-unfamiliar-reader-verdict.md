## Review method

For each artifact, I asked whether an unfamiliar nontechnical reader can, from the artifact itself:

1. state what it helps with before reading;
2. predict and follow the route by headings;
3. understand one concrete worked case;
4. distinguish the adjacent concepts that would cause a wrong action;
5. resume and retrieve the relevant rule;
6. retain its causal model or procedure;
7. choose a useful next action; and
8. see uncertainty, freshness and correction boundaries.

A PASS means no blocker was found for those questions. It is not a claim about visual presentation, route behavior, source freshness beyond the stated contract, or publication authority.

It makes useful distinctions between a paid plan, work email, a data-use setting, later deletion, account identity, authority and provider behavior. It also gives a clear safe action—do not paste when any answer is unknown—and has an appropriate currentness, non-legal-advice and correction boundary.

The required concrete visibility is incomplete. The artifact needs one recurring worked decision, for example a named client handover note considered in a personal account, an employer-managed workspace and a provider business account. For each condition it must show: the exact door, the material classification, the authority evidence, the current provider rule, what remains unknown, and the resulting stop/approved route. That would let a reader apply the five steps in order and distinguish *account placement* from *authorization*. Do not change this verdict by relying on the existing shortcut table; it teaches counterexamples, not the full decision procedure.
