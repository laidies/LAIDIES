## Decision first: use the smallest toolchain that preserves a usable, reviewable final file

The wrong framing is “Which AI tool is best?” **Likely:** most beginner jobs need one source-grounded research space, one editable production tool, and a human check—not seven generators. Choose by the deliverable and its risk:

* **Research or source briefing:** Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is the appropriate first candidate when the work must stay grounded in a supplied, bounded source pack and citations must lead back to it. Its generated audio/video overview is a learning aid, not publishable narration. Google says it can ingest documents, web pages, video and audio; its answers cite notebook sources, but it can make mistakes. [Google help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16164461?hl=en), [name change, 2026-07-16](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/)
* **Slides, documents, social graphics and simple videos:** Canva is the adequate editor when an editable design and conventional export matter. Check every asset’s licence badge: Free, Pro and Popular Music have materially different rules. [Canva licensing](https://www.canva.com/licensing-explained/)
* **Original image/video exploration:** Adobe Firefly and Runway are candidates to test, not established winners. Adobe's “commercially safe” positioning and Content Credentials are relevant selection factors, not an independently established safety advantage. Both still need output, likeness, typography, accessibility and use-specific rights checks. [Firefly FAQ, updated 2026-05-13](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/get-started/learn-the-basics/adobe-firefly-faq.html), [Runway pricing](https://runway.com/pricing)
* **Recorded human speech → edit/master:** use Descript for transcript-led editing and export, or Audacity when the job is local audio editing/export and no cloud AI workflow is needed. Do not generate a voice merely because narration is missing. [Descript exports](https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/10255817744653-Export-and-publish-content-with-Descript-web-links), [Audacity export manual](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/file_export_dialog.html)
* **Text → synthetic narration:** ElevenLabs is a paid-commercial candidate only after the script, voice/identity permission, language, delivery format, and listener review are defined. Free output is not commercially licensed. [ElevenLabs publishing policy](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/13313564601361-Can-I-publish-the-content-I-generate-on-the-platform)
* **Music sketch/production:** Suno is a candidate for personal sketching or paid-period production, subject to its changing download/licensing rules. Udio's official notice says downloads are disabled, so it cannot currently be recommended for an export-dependent soundtrack workflow. [Suno pricing](https://suno.com/pricing), [Udio download notice](https://help.udio.com/en/articles/12683565-changes-associated-with-the-universal-music-group-umg-partnership)

## Free-to-paid decision rules

1. **Pay for a licence or export gate, not optimism.** If Free output is non-commercial (Suno, ElevenLabs) or watermarked/low-resolution (Descript, Runway), use it to test the workflow only. Purchase only after a scoped deliverable has passed an inexpensive representative test and the exact paid-period terms cover that output.
2. **A trial is a billing event.** Udio’s current trial can renew at the full selected annual price unless cancelled before the seven-day end. Treat it as a timed evaluation with a calendar reminder, no public launch dependency, and a documented cancellation decision. [Udio trial](https://help.udio.com/en/articles/13338649-the-subscription-trial)
3. **An API is consumption, not a magic add-on.** ElevenLabs permits API use across plans, but each request consumes account credits. For a recurring product, estimate characters/minutes and set a spend ceiling before using an API key. [ElevenLabs API](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/28184926326033-How-much-does-it-cost-to-use-the-API)
4. **Prefer a local master.** Download/source-control the editable source and a final delivery file before relying on a share page, cloud project, usage-credit balance, or model version. This is especially important where plans, output formats or platforms change.
5. **Public rights unclear = HOLD.** Music, third-party media, voice identity, client transfer, a beta feature, an external model, recognisable people, and a location-specific right all require the exact terms plus a human decision. No source in this packet warrants saying “copyright-free” or “safe to publish everywhere.”

## Three end-to-end toolchain examples (proposed, not run here)

### 1. Source-backed staff briefing and live presentation

1. Put the exact approved reports, web captures and source notes in Gemini Notebook; ask for a claim/evidence table with citations and a list of contradictions.
2. Open every consequential cited source; correct or delete unsupported claims. Treat audio/video overview as a comprehension aid only.
3. Make the editable deck in Canva from the checked outline. Use only assets marked Free or, if paid, record each asset/licence context; avoid Popular Music and library material as a trademark.
4. Export PDF/PPTX where supported, run a visual/accessibility/readability check, and save both editable and exported versions.

**Why this chain:** Notebook grounding makes source checking easier but does not prevent invention; Canva is the editor. **What it does not prove:** accuracy after source changes, cross-plan export availability, or rights in user-uploaded source media.

### 2. A short educational video with a real narrator

1. Draft, fact-check and time a script. Record a consenting human narrator; save the raw WAV and take notes on any corrections.
2. Edit transcript, timing, captions and rough clips in Descript. On Free, use it for an internal test only; the public candidate needs the right export plan or another no-watermark editor.
3. Create a small number of still/motion inserts in Firefly or Runway only after the visual job, age/identity, required objects and prohibited errors are defined. Use Firefly when its non-beta commercial-use claim applies; use Runway to test one short shot before buying production credits.
4. Assemble a local master, inspect every frame/caption/audio transition, export a delivery file, and preserve raw narration/project/asset ledger.

**Why this chain:** it keeps meaning and narration human, and limits expensive generation. **What it does not prove:** that synthetic visuals or share links are legally/visually ready for release.

### 3. Personal music concept → public-use decision

1. On Suno Free, make a short non-commercial sketch only. Do not plan a public release around a download that is changing under current policy.
2. If the creative direction earns a paid test, generate only during the paid period and record account/plan/date, prompt/input provenance and output IDs. Inspect lyrics, similarity risk, unintended voice cues and factual/sensitivity issues.
3. Export stems/range only where the paid plan permits it; use Audacity or another local editor for a reversible master and delivery formats.
4. Before public use, review exact current terms, platform policy, all contributor rights and the project’s publication need. If any answer is unclear, HOLD it; use original commissioned/licensed music or silence instead.

**Why this chain:** it separates sketching from licensing and release. **What it does not prove:** copyright ownership or distributor acceptance.

## Specialist methods worth adapting — demonstrations, not our results

1. **Human rhythm → bounded generated layer → stems → timing check.** *Suno Help, “Recording in Studio,” edited 2025-10-11; Suno Studio.* The vendor demonstration records a simple tap pattern, aligns latency on the timeline, raises audio influence to create drums, then extracts the drum stem. Beginner adaptation: make a 10-second original rhythm, save the original, generate one variation, then A/B it with a metronome before retaining anything. Caveat: it is an official demonstration, not independent proof of output quality; the current Studio help itself warns some generations can land off beat. [Recording workflow](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/8640385), [timing caveat](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/13670529)

2. **Transcript-led edit → one paragraph becomes one clip → human publication sequence.** *Erin Ollila, 2026-07-29; Descript creator workflow.* Ollila’s sequence puts recording, edit, show notes, derivative clips and promotion into one repeated production flow; a recorded paragraph can become a short video composition. Beginner adaptation: make one 30–60 second clip from a real recording and check caption accuracy before producing a series. Caveat: the author writes for Descript, so this is a credible practical method rather than independent product comparison; it does not cure bad audio or make a share link private. [Source](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-create-a-personalized-podcast-workflow-that-makes-your-life-easier)

3. **AI as an instrument/idea source, not a substitute for final musical judgement.** *Alan Braxe and Fred Falke, interview reported 2026-03-13; MusicRadar.* The musicians describe using Suno within their music-making rather than as a replacement for musicianship. Beginner adaptation: name the human creative decision first (melody, lyric, rhythm, mood), generate a bounded alternative, reject aggressively, then rework/export only material that serves that decision. Caveat: their personal practice does not transfer commercial rights or prove release suitability; use it as a creative discipline, not a licence rule. [Interview](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/i-didnt-get-it-at-first-then-i-visited-my-friends-studio-the-way-he-was-using-it-opened-my-eyes-house-icons-alan-braxe-and-fred-falke-on-embracing-ai-in-the-studio)

4. **Source store → reasoning layer, only with a trust boundary.** *Kevin Pocock, 2026-06-25; Tom’s Guide hands-on account.* Pocock reports testing a connection that lets Claude reference material stored in NotebookLM, reducing repeated manual context transfer. Beginner adaptation: begin more safely with a single source folder/notebook and an explicit claim-evidence export; connect tools only after reviewing authorisation, data handling and prompt-injection risks. Caveat: a journalist’s hands-on account is not vendor documentation or a general security result. [Source](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/i-connected-notebooklm-and-claude-and-built-the-ultimate-ai-research-assistant)

5. **Test pronunciation before spending on a whole narration.** *ElevenLabs developers, living documentation checked 2026-08-31.* Spell out ambiguous numbers/dates/acronyms as they should sound; proofread the script and select a voice suited to the delivery. Test names and sentence transitions in a short passage before generating a chapter. Pause/phoneme controls are model-specific: v3 does not accept SSML break tags. Free can test a short noncommercial passage; paid changes allowance/rights, not the need to listen. Keep spoken-script substitutions separate from correctly spelled captions. This adaptation is untested here. [Product guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech), [model-specific controls](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices)

## Conflicts, evidence gaps and required tests

* **Suno is in an active download-policy transition.** Its current pricing says Free has no monthly downloads beginning 2026-09-03, while a 2026-08-10 company post describes transitional free trial downloads. Do not write a timeless “Free gives ten downloadable songs a day” claim; use a dated checked card and retest in the actual account. [Pricing](https://suno.com/pricing), [policy update](https://suno.com/blog/suno-updates-tos)
* **Udio export question resolved from a primary source.** The official help notice dated February 17 2026 says audio, video and stem downloads are disabled, consistent with the October 29 2025 CEO announcement. Public soundtrack delivery remains HOLD; current commercial-right terms are not cleared. [Help notice](https://help.udio.com/en/articles/12683565-changes-associated-with-the-universal-music-group-umg-partnership), [CEO announcement](https://www.udio.com/blog/a-new-era)
* **NotebookLM’s name is moving.** Google’s current announcement calls it Gemini Notebook, while the active prompt and Help Centre retain NotebookLM. Teach “Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)” with a checked-date note, and do not assume identical tiers/features across accounts.
* **Vendor commercial-use language is not copyright ownership.** Suno explicitly disclaims a guarantee that copyright will vest; Adobe says laws are changing; Canva limits standalone redistribution/trademark use. Each public job needs an asset/rights review.
* **No account/product output tests were performed.** Before recommendations, run a same-input mini-test for each selected lane: account/region/plan; exact model/mode/settings; cost/credits; source files; output/editability; export/watermark; rights screen; factual/visual/audio errors; repair time; final local files. For narration, never use a cloned/identifiable voice in the test without documented consent.
