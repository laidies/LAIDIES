## Invalidated assumption

An accurate definition in shorter words is not yet an explanation. A source link, clear taxonomy, named Hannah Fry benchmark or completed contents route does not prove that an unfamiliar reader can follow the cause-and-effect chain or explain it in her own words.

## Producer preflight

Every substantial concept must contain, in natural prose rather than a visible template:

1. **A reason to care:** a situation, choice or puzzle the reader can picture before terminology appears.
2. **A plain answer:** one sentence that says what changed or who does what.
3. **A causal chain:** no skipped step between familiar situation and technical result.
4. **Technical naming after meaning:** introduce the correct term only after the reader has the idea it names.
5. **A faithful example or analogy:** only where it materially clarifies the mechanism; state where the mapping stops.
6. **A return to consequence:** explain why the mechanism or relationship matters outside the definition.
7. **A limitation:** make clear what the result, relationship or analogy does not establish.
8. **A teach-back:** ask the reader to restate, sort, predict or apply the idea without copying the book’s vocabulary, then supply diagnostic feedback.

This is a Feynman-style comprehension test—plain explanation, exposed gaps and transfer—not a claim that Richard Feynman authored this exact modern checklist.

## Voice gate

The reader is a capable adult colleague, not a student being processed through a course. The prose must sound like a smart girlfriend at work: warm, specific, lightly irreverent and useful before clever.

Reject:

- paragraphs dominated by unexplained nouns such as recurrence, geometry-aware, residual, zero-shot, foundry, lithography, equity or non-exclusive licence;
- repeated `Before / Mechanism / Observed change / Boundary / Consequence` scaffolds in visitor-facing prose;
- corporate-directory entries with no memorable reason the reader should retain the name;
- lists of people that do not explain the decision they control;
- “in plain English” followed by another technical abstraction;
- analogy without a return to the real mechanism and its limit;
- a quiz that tests vocabulary recall instead of explanation or transfer.

## Hannah Fry communication gate

For each substantial chapter, the reviewer must locate the actual:

- shared starting point and live human question;
- useful curiosity gap;
- mechanism explained link by link;
- earned click or corrective distinction;
- small landing in a useful action or better next question.

For a reference chapter, proportional adaptation is allowed, but at least the human question, concrete hidden relationship and better next question must be visible.

## Exact-artifact review questions

The next independent reviewer must answer with evidence from the rendered book:

1. Can an unfamiliar non-technical reader explain the idea without using the book’s technical nouns?
2. Where would she predictably get lost, and did the prose repair that point before moving on?
3. Does the human opening earn the mechanism, or merely decorate it?
4. Does each analogy map to the real system and name its limit?
5. Does the teach-back reveal misunderstanding rather than reward copied phrasing?
6. Does the book sound like LAiDIES beside Episode 1, rather than an accurate corporate explainer?

Any `PARTIAL`, inferred comprehension or unsupported “readable” judgment is `REPAIR`, not `PASS`.
