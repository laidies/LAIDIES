## Reverse brief

- **Reader's question:** I understand the basic AI concepts; how do I make the
  tool useful and safe in real work without chasing magic prompts?
- **Payoff:** one repeatable working loop and a kit of reusable decisions,
  briefs, checks and boundaries.
- **Central mental model:** useful AI work is a managed system—task, allowed
  context, brief, tool/mode, inspection, verification, revision, authority and
  reuse—not a single prompt.
- **Daily-life connection:** email replies, document summaries, comparisons,
  research, recurring preparation and bounded delegation.
- **Surface job:** a connected practical textbook companion, not a feature
  directory, glossary or agent promotion.
- **Desired feeling:** capable, clear-eyed and ready to try one safe task.

## Known-failure preflight

- The book does not begin with a glossary or technical taxonomy; it begins with
  the reader's lived inconsistency and practical question.
- Key Terms support later mechanisms but do not carry the chapter alone.
- Pop-culture references are retained only where they explain specificity,
  persistent setup or tool labels; they are not stacked as reference confetti.
- The kit prevents generic “try AI” actions by producing named reusable
  artifacts with boundaries and definitions of done.
- Current-product claims name dates, scope or availability where those limits
  change the reader's decision.
- No claim was removed merely because a reviewer failed to find its source.
- The agent chapters retain permissions, pause/stop conditions, review burden
  and a manual pilot before scheduling; autonomy is not presented as a default
  upgrade.
