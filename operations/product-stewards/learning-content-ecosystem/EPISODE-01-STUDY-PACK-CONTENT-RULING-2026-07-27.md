## 1. One-page Cheat Sheet

### Promise

A fun, visually memorable, genuinely useful one-page summary of the lesson:
what Episode 01 says, why it matters, the key evidence, the terms a beginner
needs, the phrases worth remembering and the first practical move.

It must work both as:

- a responsive web page; and
- a real downloadable/printable US Letter and A4 page.

### Proposed information hierarchy

#### A. Episode title and big lesson

**EPISODE 01 — ON WEDNESDAYS WE DO AI**

**The lesson:** AI fluency starts with one useful, low-risk task. You do not
need a 40-hour course or a new identity as “technical” before opening the tab.

Primary phrase:

> It’s not a confidence problem. It’s a physics problem.

#### B. What generative AI is

Use one clear visual contrast:

**SEARCH** helps you find existing material.  
**GENERATIVE AI** creates new content from instructions and other inputs.

The generated result is not automatically a source or a finished answer.

#### C. The two limits Episode 01 teaches

1. **Missing context:** it cannot use the situation, rules, audience or
   information it does not have.
2. **Hallucination:** a polished answer can still be invented or wrong.

Use the episode’s short memory cue:

> It read everything. It’s lived nothing. And it never says “I don’t know.”

#### D. Key terms

- **Generative AI:** creates new text, images, code, summaries or drafts.
- **Model:** a trained system that transforms inputs into outputs. It can be
  used through an app, API, coding tool or another system; the product and the
  model are not the same thing.
- **Hallucination:** a confident, polished answer that contains invented or
  incorrect information.
- **ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini:** apps/product families the episode compares;
  the app and the underlying model are not the same thing.
- **Context:** the relevant situation and information the system needs for the
  task.
- **Your judgment:** the context, evidence, taste, responsibility and final
  decision that remain with the person using the result.

#### E. Key data points

Use no more than three, with the scope printed beside each number:

1. **25% lower adoption on average:** a 2025 HBS summary of research across
   18 studies and more than 140,000 people reported that women adopted
   generative-AI tools at an estimated 25% lower rate than men on average.
2. **About one-half versus one-third:** a February 2024 New York Fed survey of
   US residents found roughly half of men had used generative AI in the prior
   year, compared with about one-third of women.
3. **The gap is not a finish line:** BCG’s 2024 survey of more than 6,500 tech
   employees in five countries found senior women in technical functions led
   their men peers in adoption by an average of 14 percentage points. The
   sheet must label this as **adoption**, not performance.

These figures are contextual evidence from dated studies, not current global
population rates or claims about ability.

#### F. Your first move

Use one short, clearly numbered action:

1. Pick one small, low-risk task you can judge.
2. Keep confidential or restricted information out.
3. Give the same task to the tools available to you.
4. Compare what came back.
5. Decide what is useful, what needs correcting and what should not be used.

This is a recap of the Episode 01 action, not a new named framework.

## 2. Episode 01 Try-On

### Job

Make ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini feel familiar while helping the learner begin
one small, low-risk task she has been avoiding. She should see what each tool
looks like, know where to begin, give the same task to each available tool and
notice how the answers differ. The point is orientation, permission to start
and useful progress—not a finished-work guarantee or a permanent best-tool
ranking.

Recommended experience:

**THREE TABS, ONE TASK**  
*One thing you have been avoiding. Three first passes. One place to begin.*

### Activity

1. Meet the three tools through current, dated orientation cards showing each
   official interface, official link, message box and material account/access
   note.
2. Choose one small avoided task:
   - draft the email she keeps postponing;
   - turn non-confidential notes into a useful checklist; or
   - prepare for a conversation she has been putting off.
   Her own task must be low risk, reversible, non-sensitive and personally
   judgeable.
3. Copy exactly the same task into ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, or the tools
   available to the learner. Do not improve it between tabs.
4. Use an account-free guided comparison when the learner cannot or does not
   want to open the live tools.
5. Put the three example answers or short non-sensitive excerpts side by side.
6. Notice:
   - which was easiest to understand;
   - which included the most useful detail;
   - how tone or structure differed;
   - what was missing, invented or unsupported.
7. Choose the most useful first pass **for this task**, make one obvious human
   correction and save/download a **MY FIRST-PASS RECEIPT** containing the
   exact task, dated tool/model receipts, ratings and notes, selected draft,
   human change and any remaining verification flag.

### Required interaction

This is an interactive experience, not a digital worksheet:

- the avoided task is entered in a real text field and copied with a working
  control;
- every answer is pasted into an editable multiline field, with a guided
  example available;
- the learner records **MODEL / MODE SHOWN IN YOUR TOOL** for each run;
- `Other` and `Not shown in this chat` are valid model/mode answers;
- ratings are real labelled segmented controls with selected, focus and
  keyboard states;
- response-style descriptors are real range controls with visible values;
- `I liked / I didn’t like / I would change` are real text areas;
- the learner chooses one task-specific answer and edits it; and
- the completion actions **COPY MY DRAFT**, **DOWNLOAD / PRINT RECEIPT** and
  **TRY ANOTHER TASK** must work.

LAiDIES never invents an undisclosed model. The dated controlled fixture records
ChatGPT's logged-out underlying model as `not disclosed`, Claude as
`Sonnet 5 · Medium` and Gemini as `Flash`. A learner's own receipt records what
her current surface shows.

The complete state and completion contract is:
`operations/design-explorations/episode-01-three-tabs-one-task-20260727/INTERACTION-CONTRACT.md`.

### Same-task ratings

The learner rates the answers she actually received. Each tool gets the same
four five-point scales:

1. **Got what I meant**
2. **Gave me useful material**
3. **Tone and style fit**
4. **Easy to work with**

Every scale uses plain anchors:

- `1 — missed it`
- `3 — partly useful`
- `5 — strong fit for this task`

Each answer also gets three short observations:

- **I liked…**
- **I didn’t like…**
- **I would change…**

And one separate evidence prompt:

- **Is there anything factual here I need to verify?**
  `Yes / No / Not applicable / I’m not sure`

Then the learner describes—not scores—the response style on four neutral
sliding scales:

- `short ←→ detailed`
- `straight to the point ←→ conversational`
- `sticks closely to my ask ←→ adds extra ideas`
- `plain paragraphs ←→ heavily structured`

These style sliders have no “better” end. They create a visual fingerprint of
the three actual answers and make the differences easy to discuss.

Do not combine these into a permanent provider leaderboard. The learner chooses
**MY PICK FOR THIS TASK**, with the date and task type shown, then makes one
human change before generating **MY FIRST-PASS RECEIPT**.

### Tool Style File

The Try-On includes a short information card for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini:

- what the consumer tool is;
- what its current interface looks like and where to type;
- which current default surface/model was observed;
- what response controls or styles the product currently exposes;
- what LAiDIES observed in a controlled same-task test;
- what may differ because of plan, region, selected model, memory, personality,
  style or custom instructions; and
- `CHECKED [date]` plus the next refresh trigger.

The cards may describe repeated, test-backed tendencies as **WHAT YOU MAY
NOTICE**, not fixed personalities. Never publish unsupported shorthand such as
“Claude is always verbose,” “Gemini is always factual” or “ChatGPT is always
best at writing.”

Before release and after a material model/product change, LAiDIES runs the same
three Episode 01 task fixtures in fresh consumer-chat conversations using
default settings where possible:

1. avoided email;
2. notes to checklist; and
3. familiar concept explained plainly.

The receipt records date, provider, consumer surface, selected/default model,
plan/region when material, settings, exact task, outputs and reviewer
observations. Only tendencies repeated across the fixtures may enter the
reader-facing Style File.

The first avoided-email fixture completed on 2026-07-27 is recorded in
`operations/research/episode-01-three-tabs-one-task-20260727/CONTROLLED-COMPARISON.md`.
It is suitable as a dated worked example, not yet as evidence of repeated
provider tendencies. It showed three materially different answer treatments:

- ChatGPT: a concise plain-text draft without a subject line, followed by an
  offer to revise the tone;
- Gemini Flash: a subject line, warm draft and separate extra tip; and
- Claude Sonnet 5 at Medium effort: a longer draft inside a dedicated email
  composer with separate subject/body fields and an unused Gmail-send action.

The Try-On must therefore compare both **what came back** and **how the product
presented it**. Product affordances are not model personalities. Any send,
share, connector or account action remains outside the exercise unless the
learner deliberately chooses it.

Current official style/customization evidence checked 2026-07-27:

  `https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001038-characteristics-in-chatgpt`
  and
  `https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11899719-customizing-your-chatgpt-personality`
- Anthropic Claude Styles:
  `https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/10181068-configuring-and-using-styles`
- Google Gemini Instructions:
  `https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16598625`

The Try-On does not teach Episode 02’s full prompting/briefing lesson. It gives
the beginner a first tour of the tools, useful progress on something real and
visible evidence that the same task can produce different answers.

There is no fixed human percentage. The episode’s 80/20 email result remains a
personal story, not the scoring system or a general rule.

Interface images, official links, access notes and example answers are
changeable evidence. They require a checked/updated date and a refresh trigger
when any provider materially changes the relevant consumer-chat surface.

## 3. Episode 01 Trading Card Pack

### Job

Help women remember the important terms and ideas Episode 01 actually
introduces through a fun, authentic pack of 1990s-style flash/trading cards.
The deck must balance the three terms the episode explicitly defines with the
two ideas that carry its women-and-AI argument. It must not turn a memorable
line, anecdote or Try-On mechanic into a false standalone concept.

### Four-card deck

1. **GENERATIVE AI** — AI that creates new content—such as text, images,
   audio, video or code—from instructions and other inputs.
2. **MODEL** — a trained system that transforms inputs into outputs. It can be
   used through an app, API, coding tool or another system, and a product can
   use or route among several models.
3. **HALLUCINATION** — AI presenting unsupported or invented information as if
   it were reliable. The Burn Book example must say that every entry was made
   up; it was not a mixture of true and false entries.
4. **PARTICIPATION GAP** — women adopt generative AI less often on average;
   the gap can widen when practice and opportunity accumulate unevenly.

Retire **INVISIBLE LOAD** from the trading-card deck. Episode 01 uses invisible
load as a story and stakes beat. Explaining the term back to women does not
create a useful learning card and risks patronizing the audience. The card
pack's job is to preserve concepts that help the learner understand or use AI,
not define a burden she already knows.
Retire **YOUR 20%**. It incorrectly turns one email anecdote into a universal
concept.

Retire **SAME TASK, DIFFERENT DRAFTS** from the trading-card deck. That is the
Episode 01 Try-On mechanic, not a standalone concept. Retire **YOUR JUDGMENT**
from the trading-card deck. Judgment remains a core episode principle, but it
is not a standalone Episode 01 card.

Retire **PHYSICS PROBLEM** from the trading-card deck. It is a strong episode
reframe and Cheat Sheet phrase, but not a concept the lesson teaches. Retire
**START BEFORE READY** and **ONE TINY WIN** from the card deck: they are
actions/story beats, not concepts. Retire **CONTEXT**, **SEARCH** and
**AI APP** from this pack because the episode does not define them as part of
its three-term block.

Retire **WOMEN SHAPE AI**. The rejected card expanded Episode 01's
participation argument into a broader lesson about women collectively designing
AI around accessibility, justice, education and global needs. Episode 01 does
not teach that lesson. The importance of women participating is already carried
accurately by **PARTICIPATION GAP**.

The card count follows the concepts Episode 01 genuinely teaches. Four is the
correct count; do not invent a fifth concept to fill a visual quota.

Do not use “a maker, not a fact-checker” as the explanation of **GENERATIVE
AI**. It is risk guidance. The card explanation must say that generative AI
creates a new output from instructions rather than retrieving an existing item.

The **GENERATIVE AI**, **MODEL** and **HALLUCINATION** backs must use the
canonical Concepts 101 explanation and link to that one maintained entry. The
other two backs link to Episode 01 or its original evidence. Do not create
parallel definitions elsewhere.

Each card requires:

- a real image/word front;
- a short explanation back;
- episode, pack and card numbering;
- the saved 1990s reference style;
- the approved Episode 01 heroine as the identity anchor on every front;
- purples, vibrant pinks, electric/cobalt blues and sunshine yellow with
  strong black/white structure;
- no candy palette;
- a non-motion front/back view; and
- aligned printable Letter/A4 fronts and backs.
