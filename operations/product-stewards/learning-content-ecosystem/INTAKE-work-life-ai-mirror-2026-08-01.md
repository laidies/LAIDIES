# Learning intake — Work, life and the AI mirror

**Status:** CAPTURED / ROUTED — EXTEND THE MISSING MIDDLE LAB; BUILD A
CAREER-PRACTICE BANK; NO PUBLIC ITEM IS APPROVED

**Captured:** 2026-08-01

## Ali's idea

LAiDIES can make practical career guidance feel native to the learning system
by identifying the AI principle inside a real workplace situation. We already
use familiar life and pop-culture situations to explain AI. This format turns
the mirror around: the same ideas—context, memory, a good brief, asking for an
approach, verification, feedback, escalation and keeping a person responsible
for the decision—can help a reader prepare for difficult work conversations.

The useful tension is:

> **We use life to make AI make sense. Then we use what AI taught us to
> rehearse life.**

This line is a candidate, not approved public copy.

## Reader promise

Start with one recognizable workplace situation. Explain the AI concept that
makes the hidden structure easier to see. Give the reader language, a decision
brief or a small workflow she can actually use. Never imply that an AI tool can
judge motives, establish truth, replace professional judgement or solve a
serious workplace dispute.

The format assumes the reader is capable. It addresses situations women
regularly have to navigate; it does not frame women as needing to be fixed,
made confident or taught how to behave pleasantly.

## Reusable content anatomy

1. **The situation** — one specific, recognizable workplace moment.
2. **What is actually difficult** — the decision, ambiguity, power dynamic or
   missing information; not a personality diagnosis.
3. **The AI mirror** — one accurate AI/workflow principle that makes the
   structure visible.
4. **Where the analogy stops** — people are not models, conversations are not
   prompts and a workplace is not a software system.
5. **Your next move** — exact language, a preparation card or a bounded action.
6. **Where AI can help** — preparation, option generation, rehearsal or
   drafting with fictional/redacted information; never secret evidence,
   authorship proof or final responsibility.
7. **When this tip is not enough** — route material discrimination,
   harassment, retaliation, legal, safety or career risk to the appropriate
   workplace process and qualified support.
8. **Do the full practice** — the precise accepted lab, class, episode pass or
   LIBRAiRY entry that teaches the complete method.

## Daily, class and Library division of labour

### Daily / Paige

A Daily card contains one situation, one AI mirror and one next move. It fits
one screen and links to the exact parent. It does not compress a consequential
workplace issue into a slogan. A parent route that is held, missing or stale
holds the derivative.

### SUNNYVAiLE High

The class or lab provides controlled practice with several versions of the
situation, shows where the analogy fails and produces a durable artifact:

- a context handoff;
- an approach brief;
- a conversation checkpoint;
- a neutral follow-up;
- an interview story map; or
- a keep/revise/escalate decision.

The existing **What the Viral Reel Left Out / Missing Middle** lab owns the
general diagnostic. Do not create a new class for every reel. Add a distinct
lab only when the learner must practise a materially different observable
skill.

### LIBRAiRY

The Library owns durable definitions and reusable reference pages—for example
context, memory, briefing, verification, decision ownership and responsible
use at work. It must not become a pile of rewritten social tips.

### Episodes and tools

Link to an episode pass when the story already teaches the concept (for
example Episode 2 for context/briefing and Episode 3 for claims/receipts). A
FAiRY Godmother drafting tool may help rehearse or rewrite only after privacy,
professional-responsibility and unsupported-claim controls pass.

These are candidates for Brand/editorial review, not settled public names:

- **The AI Mirror** — clearest compact label;
- **Workplace Try-On** — clearest functional label for the deeper practice;
- **What Would You Brief?** — possible recurring prompt, but too narrow for the
  whole family.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate a colleague with a model or a conversation with a prompt.
- Do not recommend entering confidential, personal, legal, client or employer
  information into an unapproved tool.
- Do not make AI the judge of who is right, what someone intended or who owns
  an idea.
- Do not turn structural workplace problems into advice about being nicer,
  calmer or more confident.
- Do not offer a snappy line without explaining when it works, when it does
  not and what the reader still has to decide.
- Do not manufacture an AI tie-in. If the analogy does not improve the
  guidance, publish the item as a bounded professional note or decline it.
- Preserve internal source provenance, but make the visitor-facing artifact an
  original LAiDIES work that stands without the reel.

   analogy integrity and surface fit.
