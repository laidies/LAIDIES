# Hannah Fry communication benchmark

**Status:** ACTIVE INTERNAL EXPLANATION LENS
**Benchmark ID:** `HANNAH_FRY_COMMUNICATION_LENS_V2`
**Owner:** Learning System & Concepts Director

## Purpose and boundary

This benchmark adapts communication mechanics previously analysed from Hannah
Fry's public science communication. It is not permission to imitate her voice,
persona, mannerisms, identity or the structure of a particular talk or book.
Her name is not evidence that a draft is clear or engaging.

The LAiDIES producer must translate the useful mechanics into the cognitive job
and scale of the destination. A class can support prediction, demonstration and
transfer. A book can create curiosity, make a hidden process concrete and route
a better next question without becoming a lecture. A reference, FAQ, practice
item, interactive or NewsStand explanation uses only the moves that improve its
own job. Promotional copy and microcopy declare the lens not applicable rather
than manufacturing teaching.

## Communication dimensions

1. **Human question:** begin from a reason the learner already has to care,
   choose, notice or challenge—not from terminology for its own sake.
2. **Useful curiosity:** invite a prediction, tension, surprise or question
   only when the answer will reveal the mechanism. A hook without explanatory
   payoff fails.
3. **Invisible process made concrete:** use the smallest faithful case,
   demonstration, diagram, comparison or sequence that lets the learner see
   what otherwise happens out of view.
4. **Familiar and technical movement:** connect ordinary experience to the
   correct abstraction and then return to the real system. A familiar example
   that never reconnects to the mechanism is decoration.
5. **Limitations and consequences:** retain where the explanation stops, what
   can go wrong, uncertainty, misuse and why the distinction matters.
6. **Humour and surprise:** use them only when they sharpen attention,
   distinction or recall without making the learner or subject childish.
   `NOT_APPLICABLE` is often the right choice.
7. **Better next question:** leave the learner able to ask, test or notice
   something more precise than before.

## Default substantial-explanation arc

Ali's supplied [analysis reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DazjzEQo4gP/)
adds an ordering rule to the dimensions above. The direct Instagram fetch was
throttled during the 2026-08-08 evidence check; a public
[cross-platform transcript](https://tokscribe.com/t/cmrm19jj202oi140r806a97qv)
records the creator's observational analysis of 30 high-performing Hannah Fry
videos. Treat that analysis as a communication-pattern source, not an
experimental proof of learning effectiveness.

For an episode, class or substantial explanation, the default arc is:

1. **Shared starting point and live question:** begin with a physical,
   everyday or work experience the learner can picture, then open a precise
   gap she has a reason to resolve.
2. **Mechanism, link by link:** spend the centre and largest share of the
   explanation on cause and effect. Let the learner follow the discovery; do
   not substitute suspense, analogy or personality for the mechanism.
3. **Earned click:** after the mechanism, resolve the gap with the distinction,
   correction or consequence that makes her think “oh, I get it now.” Do not
   hide a direct safety-critical answer merely to manufacture a reveal.
4. **Small landing:** finish with a precise observation, useful action, better
   question or earned joke. Do not inflate a bounded explanation into a grand
   inspirational moral.

This is a default explanatory sequence, not a sentence template. An FAQ,
reference, breaking-news item or safety instruction may need the direct answer
first; it records a proportional adaptation rather than distorting its real
job to imitate a reel.

## Generation rule

Before drafting, the producer records which dimensions apply, why, the exact
experience or prose move planned for each, the destination-specific adaptation
and the no-imitation boundary. Full explanations apply at least six dimensions
and bind the four-part arc's shared starting point, curiosity gap, causal
sequence, earned click and small landing. Proportional formats apply at least
two dimensions and either adapt named arc moves or explain why the sequence
would obstruct their job. The record fails if it merely says "Hannah Fry
inspired."

## Review and learning-loop rule

The producer and independent reviewer inspect the exact artifact for evidence
that the applied moves improve understanding and engagement while preserving
technical truth. For a full explanation, they also locate the actual starting
point, causal sequence, earned click and landing in the artifact. They reject
name-dropping, curiosity without payoff, a familiar example with no technical
return, pastiche, a reveal before the mechanism earns it, a hook that receives
more attention than the explanation, entertainment before understanding and
an inflated ending. Any newly observed reusable failure follows the shared
learning-disposition route; feedback cannot automatically rewrite, retire or
unpublish content.
