# AI Fundamentals R7 whole-chapter false pass

**Status:** REJECT — calibration only; never copy

**Incident:** BTB-471

**Rejected source:** `content/library-books/pilots/ai-fundamentals-101-v4/introduction-and-chapter-1-r7.md`

**Rejected source SHA-256:** `563f9419eddbbc10bf4b66a9170f5702bec8a2bde40dfb16aa93ef1940aaba86`

Ali stopped reviewing near the beginning because the chapter's order and
organising idea were already confusing. The examples below are evidence of a
whole-chapter production and evaluation failure, not an exhaustive repair list.
No later R7 passage retains a semantic pass.

## Acronyms arrived before their meaning

The opening used `AGI` and `ASI` before giving the reader the full names or a
plain reason those categories matter. A zero-prerequisite reader was asked to
carry unexplained shorthand into the chapter.

## Ordinary automation was taught as rule-based AI

R7 used one threshold-and-country transaction rule as the representative
example of rule-based AI. A single explicit business rule is ordinary
automation as written. It does not demonstrate the knowledge base and linked
reasoning that make an expert system a useful example of symbolic or
knowledge-based AI.

## Internal taxonomy language replaced the reader's question

The text asked, `what job is this part of the system doing?` before establishing
which system, which part or why the reader would ask that. The next heading,
`Some labels describe the job`, exposed the producer's classification framework
instead of continuing a natural explanation.

## The teaching order did not build

The chapter moved from a broad AI definition to machine learning and then to
task labels without showing why the next distinction had become necessary. It
made the reader reorganise the producer's categories instead of following one
causal or historical progression.

## A human action was presented as though it explained AI

The video-shop clerk passage described a person classifying films and making an
implausible recommendation for the reader's mother. It neither showed an AI
system nor established a clean mapping to one. The analogy added a second story
the reader had to debug and failed the mechanism it was supposed to clarify.

## Failed-evaluator ruling

The R7 producer review and role-distinct semantic direction review are invalid.
Both judged local clarity and terminology while missing the reader's continuous
experience from one idea to the next. A successor must begin from a blank
Chapter 1, introduce every acronym before use, use actual AI systems for core
examples, distinguish expert-system reasoning from trivial automation and pass
a full-sequence review that can explain why every section follows the previous
one.
