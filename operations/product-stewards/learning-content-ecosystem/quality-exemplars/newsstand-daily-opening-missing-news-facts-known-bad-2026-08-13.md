# Known bad — Daily opening that withholds the basic news facts

## Exact rejected opening

> A new study examined records people had published to let others inspect or replay an AI task. Those records held more than the answer on screen, and some included passwords and other private details. This does not mean ordinary private chats became public. The lesson is for anyone sharing AI work: send the checked result, not the whole work file.

## Why Ali rejected it

The paragraph says “a new study” and “records people had published,” but does
not identify the researchers, the date or review status; who published which
records; where or why they published them; or how the researchers recovered
material that was not visible on screen. A newcomer reaches the advice before
she can form a concrete picture of what happened or decide whether the finding
could apply to her.

## Required prevention

Before a Daily draft may begin, its opening must bind four exact, reader-facing
facts: source identity and status; actor-object-channel-purpose sharing path;
actor-action-target-result recovery path; and affected/unaffected audience plus
the useful action. Those sentences must appear in the opening itself, which
remains capped at 90 words and jargon-free.
