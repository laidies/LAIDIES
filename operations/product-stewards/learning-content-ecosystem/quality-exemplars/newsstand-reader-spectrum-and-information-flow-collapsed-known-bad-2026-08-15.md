## Why this fails

The candidate interpreted two reader examples as the article's architecture.
`If I only use ChatGPT` turned one end of a broad audience spectrum into a
separate reader category. `What about a Markdown file?` turned one possible
file into a lesson on its format. Neither gave the reader the reusable mental
model she needed.

LAiDIES readers may ask questions in ChatGPT or Claude on a phone, paste or
upload work material, use AI throughout a professional workflow, or use tools
that can operate across a whole project. The useful distinction is not which
reader label or file extension applies. It is:

1. what the person gives the AI;
2. what the AI returns visibly;
3. what a more advanced tool may save while working; and
4. what the person later gives another person or the public.

The paper concerns hidden information in item three travelling with item four.
The rejected article never supplied that portable model, so readers still had
to infer whether their own documents, prompts, uploads or work practices were
the same thing the study examined.

## Failure families

- `readerSpectrumCollapsedToSinglePersona`
- `readerExampleLiteralizedIntoSection`
- `informationFlowModelMissing`
- `fileTypeSubstitutedForSharingBoundary`

## Forward calibration

A successor must let readers locate themselves along a continuum without
calling one group `ChatGPT-only`. It must first separate input to the AI,
visible output from the AI, additional work history saved by some tools and the
later sharing/publication action. Phone questions, work documents, shared links,
Markdown and whole projects may illustrate that model; none may replace it.

The exact rejected v4 must fail the reader-entry checker without being named as
the expected failure. A successor may pass producer checks only when the full
four-part information-flow model and at least three materially different use
levels occur in the exact prose.
