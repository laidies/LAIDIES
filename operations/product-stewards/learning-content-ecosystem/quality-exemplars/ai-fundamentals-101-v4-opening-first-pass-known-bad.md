## Missed defects

### Wit without contagious enthusiasm

The opening was consistently smart, wry and funny but sustained scepticism
about corporate AI language. It showed too little delight in the subject
itself. One late sentence saying AI becomes interesting did not establish the
canonical passionate and enthusiastic best-friend relationship across the
artifact.

Exact examples:

> The slide says **AI-powered transformation**.
>
> Of course it does.

> It is also for the moment an AI tool produces a genuinely excellent first
> draft and you want to know why it worked.

### A familiar word planted with the wrong meaning

The sentence below used `objectives` to mean desires or an agenda:

> No suggestion that the machine floated down from the cloud with its own
> objectives and a tasteful carry-on.

Chapter 3 later needs `objective` as a precise training term. The early joke
would make the reader carry the wrong meaning into the technical explanation.

### Diagnostic vocabulary without a worked anchor

The reader was asked whether a system can `recognise`, but none of the four
worked examples explicitly returned to recognition. The phone finding dog
photos appeared earlier as atmosphere and was never connected to the verb.

## Prevention

- Canonical voice evidence must separately prove sustained warmth,
  enthusiasm/passion, humour, clarity and usefulness. Wit or scepticism cannot
  stand in for excitement about learning the subject.
- Reserve a technical term before its teaching chapter; do not casually plant
  it with a conflicting meaning in an earlier joke.
- Every verb in a reusable diagnostic must have a worked anchor before the
  diagnostic is introduced, or be removed.
