# Known bad — Promptoscope whose action is hidden behind regression jargon

**Direct Ali ruling:** 2026-08-12

> **Mercury is in regression.**
>
> Today’s forecast: save one work example and one home example of a repeat task,
> plus what good must include. Change one instruction, rerun both examples and
> compare. Smoother but worse on an old case? That’s regression. Mercury is
> innocent.

## Why this fails

- It never plainly says that the reader is changing an AI prompt or reusable
  instruction.
- “Save one example” does not say whether to save the input, the old output,
  the expected result or all three.
- “What good must include” is an abstract substitute for showing the reader
  how to decide whether the answer is better.
- “Rerun both examples” assumes the reader already understands a repeatable
  prompt test.
- “Regression” is introduced as the punchline before it has been explained in
  ordinary language.
- It gives no next move when the revised instruction makes an earlier case
  worse: undo, revise, or keep testing.
- Ali's observed-reader result was: “I don't actually understand what it is
  telling me to do.” Internal editorial and teaching acceptance is invalid.

## Prevention

A Promptoscope must name the AI object and the action in ordinary language. A
cold reader must be able to answer all five questions after one read:

1. What am I changing?
2. What exact examples am I keeping?
3. What result am I comparing?
4. How do I recognize that the change made something worse?
5. What do I do next?

The horoscope joke may make the habit memorable only after those answers are
clear. The joke, headline or technical term cannot carry the instruction.
