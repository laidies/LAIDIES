# AI Fundamentals R6 Ali-flow false pass

**Status:** REJECT — calibration only; never copy

**Incident:** BTB-468

Ali rejected the following exact R6 Introduction edits after independent review
had returned `READY_FOR_ALI_DIRECTION_REVIEW`.

## Meaning drift

Ali wrote for a reader who **does not have** a PhD in AI. R6 changed that to:

> So why is it important for you, a woman of distinction (or friend to
> womankind—all are welcome here), to know this without first acquiring a PhD
> in computer science?

“Without first acquiring” implies that acquiring the degree is a possible next
step and changes the intended audience relationship.

## Joke diluted by unnecessary qualification

Ali’s compact electricity joke thanked Nikola Tesla and Thomas Edison. R6
expanded it to:

> thank you, Nikola Tesla, Thomas Edison and the many other people who built
> modern electrical systems

The added completeness clause was not needed for the teaching claim and made
the joke less funny.

## Flow-breaking topic insertion

Ali’s frustration paragraph led directly into the mock-biblical “forsaketh”
passage. R6 inserted:

> But a polished answer is not automatically a good one either, so
> understanding AI also helps us know when **not** to trust it.

The sentence is defensible elsewhere, but here it changes topics from fixing a
frustrating interaction to trust and breaks the setup/payoff flow.

R6 also normalized Ali’s intentionally nonstandard and funnier `forsaketh` to
`forsake`.

## Curriculum intruded on the Introduction’s job

The bound section map forced a new `One tiny map before we start` lesson into
Ali’s persuasive Introduction, including an email example and:

> Whenever an AI claim becomes confusing, ask three questions

The Introduction’s job is to make the reader care, establish the relationship
and explain the book’s purpose. Concept instruction and system-inquiry practice
belong in the first concept chapter, not inside the authored rhetorical arc.

## Predictive AI was named after its teaching had already started

Chapter 1 moved through classification, prediction, ranking and recommendation
before telling the reader that the section was defining the public label
`Predictive AI`. The `Pin this` box arrived at the end, so the reader could not
tell which conceptual container the preceding explanation belonged inside.

## The chapter listed labels without teaching how they fit together

The headings moved from predictive tasks to generative, multimodal, agentic,
embodied and breadth labels as though the list itself supplied the structure.
It did not first explain that these labels answer different questions and can
overlap. A later statement that several labels apply did not repair the missing
hierarchy or clearly show how one type can sit inside, combine with or describe
a different property of another.

## Higher depth exposed prompts instead of deeper teaching

`Tell Me More!` mostly relabelled one hypothetical product. `Full Nerd Alert!`
presented classification questions without explaining a deeper mechanism,
technical distinction or relationship. A depth layer must answer a natural
follow-up question; it cannot merely ask more questions or reorganize Standard
prose.

## The Concept Index claimed teaching that the chapter had not earned

Several index entries had received only a sentence, a disclaimer or a label in
the chapter. Index presence was treated as evidence of coverage. A concept may
enter the index only after the chapter explains its meaning, job or mechanism,
relationship to nearby concepts, a concrete example, a material limitation and
how the distinction helps the reader.

## An unapproved cultural reference was used as teaching shorthand

R6 compared a proposed AI-to-AGI-to-ASI progression with a Pokémon evolution
chart. Pokémon is not part of the LAiDIES voice or teaching-reference system.
More importantly, the borrowed shorthand did not teach any additional
technical relationship. A factual discussion whose actual subject is Pokémon
is different; that narrow use does not make it available as a brand analogy.

## Chapter definitions and source notes competed with the lesson

The chapter-specific definition list was labelled as a `Concept Index`, even
though the book already requires one cumulative Concept Index. Chapter-local
lookup is `Key Definitions`; the cumulative index owns book-wide routing.
The source/freshness note was also given the same visual heading prominence as
a teaching section instead of behaving like a compact textbook source note.

## The chapter began with organization before context

The chapter presented type labels, classification questions and a framework
before explaining where readers encounter the terms, why experts and products
use `type` for different jobs, why the distinctions change a real decision or
how one familiar product makes the relationships visible. Correct organization
did not become reader understanding merely because a list preceded the prose.

## Definitions used abstractions in place of teaching

The AGI definition relied on breadth, robustness and transfer without first
making those ideas concrete. It mainly stated disagreement and what current
systems are not. A reader could repeat the wording without being able to
picture AGI, distinguish it confidently from general-purpose or agentic AI or
explain what evidence an AGI claim would require.

## The book omitted normal textbook continuation

The candidate did not provide a truthful `See more at LAiDIES` route to
existing episodes, classes, NewsStand work or reference destinations. Missing
treatments were also not handed to their surface owners, so the book neither
continued the learning journey nor exposed the genuine ecosystem gap.

## The heading hierarchy did not reveal the teaching hierarchy

Readers could not reliably distinguish the chapter's organizing questions,
individual concepts, subtopics and optional depth. Headings were technically
nested but their wording and visible treatment made the route difficult to
scan. A valid document outline is necessary; it is not sufficient when the
visible page still makes unlike levels look equivalent.

## Deeper material became detached technical prose

Tell Me More and Full Nerd Alert appeared as separate panels after a concept,
but did not read as the next turns in one conversation. Their headings did not
consistently name the parent concept, their examples changed or disappeared,
and specialist terms arrived faster than their meaning. Technically correct
material still failed when a nontechnical reader had to work out both what the
paragraph meant and what earlier lesson it belonged to.

## The page layout increased reconstruction work

The page treated depth as a sequence of similarly weighted blocks instead of
containing Standard, Tell Me More and Full Nerd Alert inside one concept
module. The reader had to remember the parent explanation while crossing
visual boundaries. Long lines, weak level contrast or dense callouts compound
that problem: layout can make sound prose harder to understand.

## Successor depth and textbook variants found by cold review

The first progressive-depth successor still failed when:

- one hard concept family used a deeper heading level than peer concepts, so
  the document outline silently made AGI and ASI look less important than
  predictive or agentic AI;
- Key Definitions exposed technical terms at Standard or Tell Me More even
  though the prose taught them only at a higher Nerd-O-Meter setting;
- one Standard section withheld its only concrete example until Tell Me More;
  and
- a near-verbatim joke and example appeared twice inside the same concept
  cluster, making an otherwise careful section read as unedited duplication;
- one depth pair used H5 while peer pairs used H4, and a synthesis placed
  Standard recap and Full Nerd headings at the same rank;
- a Key Definitions entry claimed an explanation was deferred even though the
  current chapter had already supplied the bounded explanation; and
- a glossary headword was never explicitly introduced in its teaching prose.
- one type definition stated what the label meant but omitted the adjacent
  non-implication boundary needed to stop readers overreading physical
  embodiment as understanding or safety; and
- a capstone called itself Full Nerd Alert while merely restating the Standard
  classification in a table and skipping the intermediate depth.
- a type label such as `Predictive AI` and a lower-level mathematical operation
  such as next-token prediction reused the same word without explicitly
  separating their levels, allowing the reader to infer a false subtype; and
- four named breadth concepts shared one detached depth pair instead of each
  receiving its own attached scientific or technical deepening.
- four construction concepts shared one box whose middle depth omitted neural
  networks and deep learning entirely;
- technical terms were filed in a shallower Key Definitions tier than the prose
  that introduced them; and
- a technical use of a loaded everyday word such as `discrimination` was
  introduced without distinguishing the intended model-evaluation meaning.
- a deepest-tier section described a named mechanism without naming it, or
  merely repeated the Standard limitation in more technical-sounding prose;
- peer concepts received visibly uneven depth: one introduced mechanisms and
  evaluation terms while another only restated its scope boundary; and
- an optional depth layer recycled the Standard example before adding new
  teaching, wasting the reader's request to go deeper.
- source prose promised a named reading control or setting without explaining
  what the control reveals, where it appears or how Standard differs;
- the portable recap dropped a middle rung from a construction relationship
  the body had correctly taught;
- a Key Definitions entry pointed to the wrong depth and omitted the mechanism
  actually taught at the named depth; and
- a reference list omitted terms the prose explicitly introduced as named,
  load-bearing distinctions.
- a depth heading remained open across the following Standard spine, diagram or
  continuation, so lower Nerd-O-Meter settings silently lost required teaching;
- a Standard construction concept supplied a definition and caveat but no
  concrete example or practical reason the distinction mattered; and
- a term performed load-bearing work at Standard or Tell Me More while its
  definition was available only at Full Nerd Alert.
- one load-bearing capability property changed names between Standard and Full
  Nerd Alert without defining or bridging the apparent synonym; and
- a first-class breadth concept kept the concrete case inside Tell Me More,
  leaving Standard with an abstract capability list or disputed definition.
- Standard evidence contracts covered only selected concept families, allowing
  an uncovered first-class concept to repeat the same missing-example defect;
- an additive Tell Me More repeated the Standard takeaway almost verbatim
  after its useful example had been promoted; and
- a depth heading promised a named scenario that no longer existed inside its
  section after the scenario moved to Standard.
- Standard promised that the next visible section would explain a mechanism
  whose explanation existed only inside hidden optional depth;
- a Full Nerd layer repeated its Tell Me More model/product example instead of
  adding an evaluation method;
- an AGI Tell Me More consisted only of rhetorical restatements of Standard
  rather than teaching a new mechanism or distinction; and
- a bold, section-titled term was absent from Key Definitions while other terms
  were filed below the first depth where the prose relied on them.
- ASI Tell Me More repeated the Standard specialised-versus-broad distinction
  and independent-verification sentence instead of teaching how beyond-human
  claims could be checked; and
- the Machine Learning Standard contract accepted a caution about human choices
  as `why it matters` without stating where a reader or builder would actually
  intervene when a learned system is wrong.

A cumulative view is not truthful merely because the prose layers are hidden
and shown correctly. Navigation, definitions and examples must agree with the
exact depth at which the reader receives the teaching.

## Required reusable rejection

Reject an author-led successor when it:

- changes the author’s intended audience or proposition while smoothing prose;
- weakens a compact joke with unnecessary completeness language;
- inserts a true but differently scoped sentence that breaks setup/payoff flow;
- normalizes deliberate comic diction merely because it is nonstandard; or
- makes internal curriculum coverage overrule the authored surface’s distinct
  rhetorical job;
- names a concept only after its examples or subtypes have already been taught;
- lists overlapping AI labels without first teaching the questions and
  relationships that organize them;
- calls a layer deeper while adding prompts, labels or restatement instead of
  further mechanism, distinctions and worked explanation; or
- lists a concept in the index before the prose has genuinely taught it;
- uses Pokémon as a LAiDIES analogy or brand reference rather than as the
  actual factual subject; or
- presents chapter-local definitions or source notes as competing parts of the
  main teaching hierarchy;
- opens a concept chapter with labels, framework or figure before establishing
  human context, confusion, stakes and a concrete case;
- defines a concept through unexplained abstractions without an example or
  faithful analogy that enables confident explanation; or
- omits truthful continuation to existing LAiDIES learning and fails to route
  a genuinely missing treatment to its accountable surface owner; or
- uses headings whose wording or visible hierarchy does not let a reader
  distinguish chapter groups, concepts, subtopics and optional depth; or
- presents technically accurate higher-depth prose without a concrete entry,
  plain-language decoding and an explicit return to the parent concept; or
- displays depth as detached, similarly weighted blocks instead of one
  progressively disclosed concept module; or
- assigns a core peer concept a weaker heading level than comparable concepts;
  or
- exposes a definition at a shallower depth than the prose that teaches it; or
- withholds a Standard concept's only concrete example until an optional depth;
  or
- repeats a near-verbatim explanation, example or joke within one concept
  cluster without a deliberate retrieval or comparison job; or
- changes the semantic heading rank of the same depth component between
  concepts, or places Standard and optional depth at the same rank; or
- contradicts the chapter's actual teaching boundary in a continuation note;
  or
- defines a headword that the corresponding prose never explicitly introduces;
  or
- teaches a type label without the material adjacent distinction or
  non-implication that prevents a likely misunderstanding; or
- labels a recap or reformatted repetition as higher depth without adding a new
  mechanism, comparison, evidence boundary or application; or
- reuses one word for a public type and an internal operation without naming
  the level change and preventing a false nesting inference; or
- names a first-class concept without attaching its own Tell Me More and Full
  Nerd Alert teaching where the three-depth pattern applies; or
- bundles several first-class construction concepts so a depth layer silently
  skips one or more of them; or
- files a glossary entry above the shallowest prose layer that actually teaches
  it; or
- introduces a technical term with a materially different everyday meaning
  without explicitly disambiguating the intended use; or
- promises progressive depth but withholds the accepted name of the mechanism
  being explained, substitutes a noun list for explanation or simply restates
  Standard at greater length; or
- gives comparable first-class concepts materially uneven scientific depth; or
- spends optional-depth space repeating an example without a deliberate new
  comparison, mechanism or retrieval job; or
- promises a reading control, mode or setting without teaching the reader how
  it changes the exact page experience; or
- lets a recap simplify a taught relationship into a false or incomplete one;
  or
- misroutes a definition to the wrong depth or omits what that depth teaches;
  or
- presents Key Definitions as the chapter reference while omitting explicitly
  named concepts required to recover a material distinction; or
- allows a depth panel to swallow later Standard content because the source has
  no explicit peer heading boundary; or
- lets Standard promise plain answer, example and importance while omitting the
  example or importance for a first-class concept; or
- teaches or relies on a term at one depth while withholding its definition
  until a deeper setting; or
- renames one property across depth layers without explicitly preserving the
  relationship and definition; or
- withholds a breadth concept's only concrete case until optional depth.
- applies the Standard answer/example/importance contract to only a convenient
  subset of first-class concepts; or
- repeats the Standard takeaway in an immediately additive depth instead of
  adding a new mechanism, relationship, evidence boundary or application; or
- leaves a heading promising an example, analogy or question the section no
  longer delivers after content moves across depth; or
- makes a Standard-only forward promise whose payoff is hidden at an optional
  setting; or
- uses an additive depth to repeat the same example and relationship rather
  than add a method, mechanism, evidence boundary or application; or
- substitutes rhetorical questions for deeper teaching; or
- omits a bold, section-titled term from Key Definitions or files a definition
  below its first load-bearing teaching use.
