## Rejected pattern

> Public AI work logs can contain more than the chat you see
>
> An August 10 preprint found sensitive material in some publicly shared
> technical AI-task records. A clean-looking chat is not the same thing as a

The article later cycles through `task record`, `work log`, `technical export`,
`raw session`, `API session` and `agent trace`. Each phrase may be technically
defensible, but none tells an ordinary reader what object exists in her world,
who created it, why it was posted publicly or how that differs from using a
private ChatGPT or Claude chat.

## Missed defects

### `undefinedArtifactBeforeSituation`

The producer replaced one piece of jargon with several neighbouring labels. A
technical label is not explained merely because the sentence around it uses
short words. The reader first needs a complete ordinary situation: a developer
or researcher uses an AI system, the software saves a detailed behind-the-scenes
file, and the person deliberately uploads that whole file so other people can
inspect or reuse the work.

### `panicBoundaryArrivesTooLate`

The headline and standfirst lead with an unfamiliar threatening object. A later
“does not mean” sentence cannot undo the first impression. If the likely reader
fear is “my private chats and information are online,” the headline and
standfirst must say that the study did not find that.

### `wrongEditorialModeForLikelyMisread`

The pipeline selected Plain-Language Explainer because the primary paper was not
itself judged misleading. That ignored the reader's actual entry point: alarming
coverage or a headline that can be reasonably misread. When the material public
value is correcting that likely fear, Headline Reality Check is required even if
the underlying research is careful.

### `impactLadderMissing`

The prose does not quickly distinguish four reader situations:

1. using a normal private consumer chat;
2. copying and sharing only the answer she chose;
3. being asked to send a diagnostic or troubleshooting file; and
4. deliberately publishing a complete developer or research file.

Without that ladder, “this can affect anyone” expands the fear instead of
showing where the boundary actually is.

## Required successor proof

Before technical explanation, an unfamiliar reader must be able to answer:

1. What frightening conclusion might I have drawn, and is it true?
2. Who actually put information online?
3. What exactly did they put online, in ordinary words?
4. Why would they share it?
5. Which of the four reader situations applies to me?
6. Why is LAiDIES reporting this?
7. What should I do differently, if anything?

The headline and standfirst answer the likely panic. The first 120 words contain
the person, action, ordinary object, sharing path, directly affected group and
reason for coverage. Only after that may technical labels appear. A successor
that merely defines `API`, `trace`, `session`, `log` or `export` still fails.
