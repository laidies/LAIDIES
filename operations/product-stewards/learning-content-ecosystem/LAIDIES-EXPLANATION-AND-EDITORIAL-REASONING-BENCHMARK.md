# LAiDIES explanation and editorial reasoning benchmark

**Status:** ACTIVE INTERNAL PRODUCTION STANDARD
**Benchmark ID:** `LAIDIES_EXPLANATION_EDITORIAL_TRIAD_V1`
**Owner:** Learning System & Concepts with each destination's product owner
**Decision date:** 2026-08-11

## The standard

LAiDIES explanations combine three methods. None is optional for material
meaning-bearing work published on laidies.ai or through its audio, video,
newsletter or social derivatives. This includes episodes, books, classes,
study packs, FAQs, NewsStand reporting and columns, Behind the Build, games or
interactives that teach, and scripted radio/audio/video explanations.

This is a method standard, not permission to imitate Hannah Fry's or Richard
Feynman's literal voice, persona, wording or the structure of a particular
work. AIDB is a comparative editorial check, not a factual authority or a
house style to copy. LAiDIES retains its own Rewind Era voice and Episode 1
writing authority.

### 1. Hannah Fry: make the reader want and able to see the explanation

- Start with a human reason to care, choose, notice or challenge.
- Make an invisible process concrete with the smallest faithful example.
- Move from a familiar experience to the technical mechanism and back again.
- Spend the largest share on cause and effect, not the hook.
- Preserve limitations and consequences.
- Land with an earned useful action, sharper distinction, better question or
  joke that improves recall.

The maintained detail remains in
`HANNAH-FRY-COMMUNICATION-BENCHMARK.md` and is consumed by reference, not
duplicated or weakened here.

### 2. Feynman-style reasoning: reveal and repair fake understanding

- State the question in ordinary words before specialist terminology.
- Reconstruct the answer from the smallest necessary first principles.
- Explain every causal link; never bridge a gap with “the AI just does it.”
- Define a technical term only when it becomes necessary, then reconnect it
  to the mechanism.
- Ask what the reader should now be able to explain in her own words.
- Test the explanation on a different case. If it cannot transfer, the draft
  may have produced recognition rather than understanding.
- Treat the producer's own inability to explain a step plainly as a knowledge
  gap requiring more research, not a wording problem to disguise.

This is LAiDIES' first-principles and explain-back lens, influenced by
Feynman's public teaching approach. It is not a claim that one named
“Feynman technique” mechanically guarantees learning.

### 3. AIDB-style scrutiny: separate the story from the story being told

After LAiDIES independently reads the primary evidence:

- write the circulating headline or common interpretation;
- write what actually happened in plain language;
- identify the original study, data, document, product change or event;
- state exactly what it establishes and does not establish;
- separate direct evidence, interpretation, inference and opinion;
- inspect method, population, comparison, measures, incentives, missing
  context and meaningful uncertainty where applicable;
- challenge the claimed impact: who is affected, how, when and by what causal
  mechanism;
- compare AIDB's treatment when available, including agreements, omissions
  and meaningful disagreements;
- record a dated honest absence or unavailable result when AIDB did not cover
  it; and
- lead with the real takeaway, not the most alarming possible framing.

AIDB-style scrutiny is adapted proportionally outside current news: the
producer still separates the claim being taught from its evidence, limits and
real consequence even when no AIDB edition exists or the subject is not a news
story. AIDB can sharpen an investigation and reveal questions LAiDIES missed. It
cannot substitute for primary evidence, determine publication importance by
itself or receive automatic attribution.

## Required explanatory sequence

Material meaning-bearing work records and then visibly delivers:

1. **The reader's live question** — why this matters to a smart non-technical
   woman and what background she needs.
2. **The claim under inspection** — the headline, advice or common account.
3. **What actually happened** — the primary-evidence account in plain words.
4. **The mechanism** — cause and effect, link by link, with necessary terms
   introduced only after meaning.
5. **The evidence boundary** — what the evidence proves, does not prove and
   leaves unknown.
6. **The real consequence** — who is affected, what changes and what does not.
7. **The understanding test** — an explain-back prompt plus a different case
   that exposes superficial understanding.
8. **The useful landing** — a tested action, decision rule or better next
   question proportionate to the evidence.

Breaking news may answer first and compress the sequence. A compact service
column, FAQ, practice item or game interaction may apply it proportionally.
Neither brevity nor entertainment may omit the mechanism or evidence boundary
when its central teaching claim depends on them. Pure navigation, labels,
legal text and transactional microcopy declare the framework not applicable;
they may not use that exemption to carry an unreviewed teaching claim.

## Producer gate

Before drafting any material meaning-bearing content, the producer must bind
the current SHA-256 of this benchmark and the Hannah Fry benchmark, then
record:

- the planned human entry and causal mechanism;
- every link that must be explained from first principles;
- the likely false or shallow understanding to test;
- the primary-evidence establishes/does-not-establish record;
- the AIDB comparison or dated honest absence;
- the explain-back and distinct transfer case; and
- the useful reader change.

Missing any one of these fields is a production failure. Saying “Hannah Fry,”
“Feynman” or “AIDB” without locating the actual moves in the artifact fails.

## Review gate

The producer self-review and independent artifact-first reviewer reject:

- jargon before meaning;
- a hook that receives more work than the mechanism;
- unexplained causal jumps;
- a familiar example that never reconnects to the real system;
- a summary of claims without examination of the original evidence;
- “sources say” language that hides method, limits or disagreement;
- an explain-back question answerable by parroting a sentence;
- a transfer case that merely repeats the worked example;
- advice that was not tested on the problem it claims to solve;
- AIDB name-dropping, uncritical deference or copied framing; and
- imitation of another communicator instead of recognizable LAiDIES prose.

The validator can prove the production record contains the required method and
can reject calibrated omissions. It cannot prove the finished prose is clear,
investigative, accurate or enjoyable; those remain exact-artifact judgments.
