## Outcome and release disposition

### Intended outcome

Create one durable, source-qualified LIBRAiRY reference that lets a newcomer
turn an AI answer into checkable claims, choose evidence proportionate to the
stakes, inspect whether a source really supports a claim, account for scope and
freshness, make a bounded decision, and preserve uncertainty or a correction.

This is not another explanation that “AI can be wrong.” It is the reusable
method a learner can pull down after Episode 3, during real work, before Dream
Phone practice, or from a current NewsStand story.

## Correct mental model

### The model the learner should be able to draw

```text
AI output
  → split into claims and non-claim draft material
  → assign stakes, scope, jurisdiction and freshness need
  → choose the best evidence for each material claim
  → retrieve/ground (when useful)
  → open and inspect the actual source
  → test identity + relevance + date + support + method
  → corroborate independently when stakes/uncertainty require it
  → decide: supported / contradicted / unresolved / not applicable
  → use, qualify, revise, escalate or stop
  → keep the source/date/decision; correct when evidence changes
```

### Six distinctions that must survive the lesson

1. **Generation** creates the answer. Fluency is not evidence.
2. **Retrieval/search** finds material. Finding a page does not make it the
   right page.
3. **Grounding** constrains or connects generation to retrieved/provided
   material. The material can still be weak, stale, out of scope or
   misinterpreted.
4. **Citation** points to a source. It must exist, be opened and support the
   nearby claim.
5. **Provenance** records where an asset came from/how it changed. It can help
   authenticate an origin trail without proving the claim/content is true.
6. **Verification** is the decision process that connects a specific claim to
   adequate evidence under the right scope, date and stakes.

The book may call this the **Receipt Loop**:

> Frame → Split → Find → Inspect → Cross-check → Decide → Record.

The label is mnemonic. The mechanism above is canonical.

## Misconceptions and required corrections

| Misconception | Correct teaching |
| --- | --- |
| “If it has citations, it is verified.” | A citation is a pointer. Check existence, source identity, exact support, scope and date. |
| “Grounded means true.” | Grounding connects an answer to material; it does not certify the material or the model’s interpretation. |
| “Primary source means automatically trustworthy.” | Primary evidence is often closest to the claim, but methods, incentives, omissions, errors and applicability still matter. |
| “Ask the AI if it is sure / ask it twice.” | Self-checks may help detect inconsistency, but independence comes from the underlying source, a qualified owner, or a genuinely separate evidence path. |
| “The same verification effort fits everything.” | Effort is proportional to consequence, reversibility, uncertainty and freshness. |
| “Only facts need checking.” | Material assumptions, scope, definitions, recommendations and implied decisions can fail even when individual facts are accurate. |
| “A source supports the whole paragraph.” | Split compound statements and map evidence to each material clause. |
| “Old information is false.” | It may be historically accurate but inapplicable to the current date, version, jurisdiction or decision. |
| “Many sites saying it makes it corroborated.” | Repetition can trace to one weak origin. Seek independent provenance/evidence. |
| “Content Credentials prove an image is real.” | Provenance can validate an origin/edit record; it does not determine whether the depicted event or assertion is true. |
| “The prompt prevents hallucinations.” | Prompting and grounding can reduce risk; critical output still needs external validation. |
| “I must redo every word manually.” | Triage claims and stakes; preserve useful drafting while checking the parts that could change a decision or cause harm. |

## Evidence hierarchy and proportionality contract

There is no universal source ladder divorced from the claim. Teach
**claim-matched evidence**:

| Claim type | Preferred evidence | Useful secondary role | Never sufficient alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current product feature, price, plan, limit or policy | Current official provider documentation/account UI/contract, with region, plan and access date | Reputable independent comparison for context | AI memory, search snippet, undated screenshot |
| Law, regulation, public rule or jurisdictional requirement | Current legislation, regulator/court/official authority; qualified professional for consequential application | Reputable legal analysis explaining implications | Generic blog, non-jurisdictional answer, disclaimer |
| Scientific/empirical result | Original paper/dataset/method, correction/retraction status; appropriate expert synthesis | Systematic review or reputable science reporting | Press release or percentage without denominator/method |
| Quote, statement or event | Original transcript, recording, filing, document or direct statement | Reputable reporting that links/describes the original | Quote graphic, repost or citation with no accessible source |
| Internal decision or customer commitment | Approved record, named decision owner, contract or attributable follow-up | Meeting summary as a lead | Model-written recap or unattributed notes |
| Image/audio/video origin | Original asset/context plus provenance/Content Credentials where present | Reputable forensic/context reporting | Metadata/provenance treated as a truth verdict |

For every source ask:

1. **Identity:** who created/published it, and what authority or interest do they
   have?
2. **Relevance:** does it address this exact claim rather than a nearby topic?
3. **Scope:** correct person, product, plan, place, jurisdiction and conditions?
4. **Date/version:** current enough for the decision?
5. **Support:** does the source actually entail the claim, including numbers
   and qualifiers?
6. **Method:** how was the evidence produced; what is missing or uncertain?
7. **Independence:** does corroboration trace to a separate origin?

### Stakes dial

- **Low/reversible:** label as draft; a quick source inspection may be enough.
- **Material but reversible:** claim table, authoritative source, date/scope
  check and one independent corroboration where uncertain.
- **High-stakes or hard to reverse:** do not rely on the Rulebook as
  professional advice. Use authoritative records and the qualified human owner
  or professional; preserve uncertainty and stop/escalate when evidence is
  inadequate.

Correctness, sources, corrections and safety boundaries may never be
paywalled or turned into reward scarcity.

## Source, date and freshness contract

The built book requires a machine-readable or plainly structured claim ledger.
Every substantive factual/current claim gets:

- stable claim ID and exact public wording;
- claim type and risk tier;
- source URL/document and source class;
- exact supporting passage/page/section;
- publication/version date and access date;
- scope/jurisdiction/plan conditions;
- reviewer and review date;
- next review date or event trigger;
- status: `SUPPORTED`, `QUALIFIED`, `CONTRADICTED`, `UNRESOLVED`,
  `SUPERSEDED`;
- correction/supersession history.

### Default recheck rules

| Content | Default recheck | Event trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named AI product feature, model, plan, price, UI or policy | Before each publication/build and at least quarterly while visible | Provider release/deprecation/policy change or user correction |
| Legal/regulatory/jurisdictional statement | At use for consequential guidance; quarterly if retained as a public example | New law, regulator guidance, decision or jurisdiction change |
| Current statistic/benchmark | Before publication and at source’s next release | New edition, correction, retraction or methodology change |
| Research finding | Annual link/correction/retraction check unless used in a current claim | Correction, retraction, replication or material new evidence |
| Durable concept definition | Annual editorial review | Standards/field usage changes or learner confusion |
| All external links | Monthly automated health check plus semantic review at content review | Redirect, 404, ownership/domain change |

Display on the book: **Published**, **Last reviewed**, **What can change**, and
**Report a correction**. A passing link check is not semantic re-verification.

Avoid provider-name dependency in the core method. Put current provider
examples in dated, replaceable sidebars/cards so the durable chapters survive
capability changes.

## Chapter and interaction architecture

### Opening — “You do not need to redo everything”

- Promise: learn what to check, how much checking is enough, and when to stop.
- One compact work example that initially looks plausible.
- Learner commits: “draft,” “claim,” or “decision-changing claim.”
- Reveal shows why fluency did not answer the evidence question.

### Chapter 1 — Sort the output

- Draft vs factual claim vs assumption/inference vs recommendation/decision.
- Compound-claim splitting.
- Stakes/reversibility/freshness triage.
- **Interaction:** classify eight lines; feedback explains the consequence, not
  only the label.

### Chapter 2 — Know what the tool actually did

- Generation, retrieval/search, grounding, citation, provenance, verification.
- Diagram traces source → retrieval → model → answer → citation → human check.
- **Interaction:** match five artifacts to what they establish and what they
  do not establish.

### Chapter 3 — Frame the claim before searching

- Exact wording, scope, jurisdiction, plan/version/date, denominator and
  decision.
- “She doesn’t even go here” may remain as a mnemonic for scope mismatch, with
  the explicit limit that a familiar film analogy does not itself test
  evidence.
- **Interaction:** repair three vague/compound claims into checkable units.

### Chapter 4 — Choose and inspect evidence

- Claim-matched hierarchy and seven source questions.
- Open the source; locate exact support; distinguish source authority from
  applicability.
- Lateral reading and tracing repeated claims to their origin.
- **Interaction:** compare three candidate sources and explain which one is
  diagnostic and why.

### Chapter 5 — Run the Receipt Loop

- Frame → Split → Find → Inspect → Cross-check → Decide → Record.
- Verdicts are `supported`, `contradicted`, `unresolved`, `not applicable`—not
  forced true/false.
- Worked example: meeting notes → client update, preserving the useful draft
  while removing invented approval.
- **Interaction:** learner completes a claim table and chooses a verdict before
  reveal.

### Chapter 6 — Currentness, media and independent checks

- Stale-but-real information; product/version/jurisdiction traps.
- Provenance/Content Credentials versus truth.
- Same-model self-check versus independent evidence.
- **Interaction:** one freshness case and one media-provenance case.

### Chapter 7 — Use, qualify, escalate or stop

- Proportional checking by stakes.
- When a human owner/professional is required.
- How to write a qualified answer and name missing evidence.
- Correction path and personal “decision receipt.”
- **Interaction:** choose a next action for low, material and high-stakes cases.

### Pull-out reference

The copyable prompt remains, revised so it is explicitly a **risk-reduction
aid**, not a truth machine. It must request:

- source-bounded answer;
- exact quotations/locations for material claims;
- `not stated` for absent evidence;
- separation of source fact from inference;
- source/date/scope column;
- `[needs receipt]` and permission to abstain.

Immediately below it: “Now open the source yourself. This prompt cannot certify
the source, the quote or the conclusion.”

### Closing transfer challenge

Present a new domain and format not used in instruction (for example, a
current product comparison containing a plan mismatch and a percentage with no
denominator). The learner must:

1. split the material claims;
2. identify the decision-changing claim;
3. choose the best source/evidence action;
4. name the freshness/scope check;
5. give a qualified verdict and state what would change it.

Do not award completion for opening the book, copying the prompt or guessing a
binary truth label.

## Tone, analogy and brand contract

- Keep the intelligent, funny, adult LAiDIES voice and Episode 3 continuity.
- Use Elle/Chutney/Burn Book as memorable support, not as the structure of
  every chapter.
- Every analogy must map to a named mechanism and carry a limit where needed.

  it.
- “Receipts” means inspectable evidence; it must never imply that a link,

- The book contributes to the brand by making the reader harder to mislead,
  not by sounding sassier than competing explainers.

## Accessibility and responsive contract

The built reader must meet WCAG 2.2 AA and the LIBRAiRY reader contract:

- semantic landmark, one `h1`, ordered heading hierarchy and real lists/tables;
- each interaction usable with keyboard alone and without drag, hover or
  colour-only meaning;
- visible, unobscured focus; focus returns after dialogs/reveals;
- all controls have programmatic name/role/state; feedback uses an appropriate
  live region without interrupting reading;
- source links have descriptive purpose and visible URL/domain context;
- diagrams have a text equivalent that communicates the same relationships;
- examples never rely on images of text; decorative visuals have empty alt;
- no loss of content/function at 200% zoom or 320 CSS px reflow; long URLs and
  code wrap without two-dimensional page scrolling;
- minimum 24×24 CSS px targets, with 44×44 preferred for primary interaction
  controls;
- colour/non-text contrast passes; supported/contradicted/unresolved also use
  text and shape/icon labels;
- copy action works without Clipboard API and announces success/failure;
- printable/offline reference preserves headings, source URLs, dates and
  correction information;
- reduced motion removes nonessential animation; no timed learning task.

Manual acceptance includes current VoiceOver/Safari plus keyboard-only Chrome,
not automated scanning alone.

## Exact acceptance tests

### Publication binding and truth

1. A single canonical book source is named; all derived HTML/fragments declare
   it and can be reproduced.
2. The LIBRAiRY shelf status manifest has exactly one state:
   `preview/hold` before approval or `available` after every gate.
3. When `available`, selecting the cover loads the full approved body—not
   embedded teaser copy—and direct section URLs restore the exact heading.
4. Fetch/source failure displays “Book unavailable” with retry/back actions;
   it never substitutes plausible teaser copy.
5. Miss Jeeves and every cross-product link obey the same status and never
   route to a held body as though it were approved.

### Accuracy, evidence and freshness

6. Every substantive factual/current claim has a complete claim-ledger row;
   zero material claims rely only on model output, search snippets or an
   unattributed secondary summary.
7. An independent reviewer opens 100% of material claim sources and a
   risk-weighted sample of minor sources; links exist, exact passages support
   the public wording, and scope/date/qualifiers are preserved.
8. Every provider capability example is checked against current official
   documentation on the release date and isolated in a replaceable dated card.
9. The text never equates search, retrieval, grounding, citation or provenance
   with truth; six distinction probes score 100%.
10. Prompt language says “reduce risk,” not “prevent/eliminate hallucinations,”
    and instructs external validation for critical claims.
11. High-stakes cases route to authoritative records/qualified humans and do
    not present the book as legal, medical, financial, HR or safety advice.
12. `Published`, `Last reviewed`, `What can change`, correction route and
    source drawer are visible and agree with the ledger.
13. Automated link health plus semantic source review passes; a broken,
    redirected-to-unrelated or superseded source fails the build.

### Learning and transfer

14. A versioned 18-case suite covers:
    - 4 explanation/distinction probes;
    - 6 application cases across source, date, scope, denominator, compound
      claim and provenance;
    - 4 misconception-resistance cases;
    - 4 transfer cases in unseen domains/formats.
15. Cases require a reason and evidence action; answer-key keyword matching or
    binary verdict accuracy alone cannot pass.
16. Eight representative newcomers who did not write/review the content test
    the rendered candidate. At least 7/8, without hints:
    - explain why a citation/grounding/provenance is not verification;
    - split a compound claim;
    - select the most diagnostic evidence;
    - identify a scope/freshness issue;
    - produce `supported`, `contradicted` or `unresolved` with a valid reason.
17. At least 7/8 correctly state one limit of the Elle/receipts analogy and can
    explain the method without using the film reference.
18. On the closing unseen transfer task, at least 7/8 choose an evidence action
    that would materially resolve the decision; nobody reports that copying
    the prompt guarantees accuracy.

### Product, brand and accessibility

19. Independent 20-point gates each score at least 17/20 for:
    product/content quality; accuracy/safety/trust; and positive LAiDIES brand
    contribution. No score can compensate for another.
20. Zero critical factual/source errors, unsafe scope crossings, misleading
    certainty or placeholder-as-book states.
21. Keyboard, VoiceOver/Safari, 200% zoom, 320/390/430/1440 widths, reduced
    motion, contrast, focus order/visibility, live feedback and clipboard
    failure paths pass with recorded screenshots/transcript.
22. A first-time reader can state the book’s job and begin the first meaningful
    check within 30 seconds; a returning reader can deep-link to the Receipt
    Loop, evidence hierarchy and prompt.
23. Episode 3, Dream Phone, NewsStand, High and FAiRY handoffs name their
    distinct payoff and are hidden/labelled when the destination is held.
24. Exact candidate hashes are recorded before release; the deployed public
    bytes and complete new/returning journeys are rechecked separately.

Until all twenty-four tests pass, status remains **BUILDING** or
**HIDE/LABEL FOR LAUNCH**, never `VERIFIED PUBLICLY`.

## Measurement and privacy

Collect only controlled, aggregate events:

- book opened / named section viewed;
- interaction started/completed with case ID and outcome category;
- source drawer/source link opened by source ID;
- transfer challenge completed with rubric category, not free text;
- correction started/submitted with book/section ID only in analytics.

Do not send pasted source material, copied prompts, typed claims, learner
answers, correction text, employer/client identity or URLs containing private
identifiers to Plausible, Clarity or model providers.

Success is not page views, dwell time, prompt copies or completion. The primary
signal is transfer: the learner chooses and explains the evidence action on a
new claim. Pair aggregate behaviour with the newcomer study and correction
quality.

Review at 14 and 30 days after verified public release, then monthly for link
health/corrections and quarterly for provider-currentness. If transfer or trust
falls below the acceptance floor, return the book to `hold`, preserve the
correction note and repair before re-promotion.

## External capability position

No plugin, vendor or paid service is required to build this first vertical
slice. A structured source/claim ledger in the repository plus automated link
checks is lower-risk and more inspectable than adopting a new evidence tool
before the workflow is proven.

After one complete cycle, the champion may compare source-monitoring or
citation-management tools only against a concrete bottleneck. Any installation,
spend, account connection or disclosure of private material requires separate
approval. A tool may monitor changes; it cannot own the editorial verdict.

## Rollback and correction

- Before approval: keep the cover in `preview/hold` and remove direct
  promotional routes.
- After release: if a material source breaks, a claim becomes stale, an unsafe
  interpretation appears, or transfer falls below the floor, switch the
  canonical status back to `hold`; preserve a visible correction note and the
  last verified hash.
- Never silently replace the approved book with embedded fallback prose.
