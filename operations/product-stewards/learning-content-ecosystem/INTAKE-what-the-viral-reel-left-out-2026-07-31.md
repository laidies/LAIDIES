# Learning intake — What the Viral Reel Left Out

**Status:** SPECIFIED — recurring Daily format and parent lab are built locally
as governed candidates; no item, class, route or public release is approved.

**Captured:** 2026-07-31

## The idea Ali approved

**What the Viral Reel Left Out** is a recurring Daily Buzz item. It starts with
one appealing AI claim, identifies the missing operating instruction and gives
the reader one useful check she can apply immediately. It then points to the
accepted class or lab that explains how to do the work properly.

The permanent teaching line is:

> **The viral tip is not the workflow.**

This is not a creator-callout column and not a collection of generic warnings.
Creator content is a discovery lead. LAiDIES independently verifies the
capability, teaches the complete setup and credits ideas where appropriate
without copying the creator's words, visuals or performance.

## Learner problem

A short demonstration can make a result look like three clicks. The reader
tries it, gets a weaker result and reasonably concludes that either the product
does not work or she did something wrong. Frequently, neither conclusion is
fair: the demonstration omitted prerequisites, context, configuration,
judgement, testing or upkeep.

The learner does not need another slogan telling her to “be careful.” She needs
to see the missing middle and know what to do next.

## Division of labour

### Daily Buzz item

The Daily item must fit one screen and do five jobs:

1. state the exact claim without inflating it;
2. rule it **supported**, **true but incomplete**, **misleading** or
   **unsupported** from current primary evidence;
3. name the most consequential missing step;
4. give one bounded action the reader can take now; and
5. link to the most specific accepted class, lab or reference that teaches the
   complete method.

The Daily item is a derivative. It does not become the canonical explanation.

### SUNNYVAiLE High lab

The lab teaches the reusable **Missing Middle Check**:

1. **Outcome** — What result is actually being promised?
2. **Capability** — What feature, model or product behavior could produce it?
3. **Prerequisites** — What account, plan, files, data, access and permissions
   are required?
4. **Setup** — What context, instructions, connections and customization must
   be supplied?
5. **Judgement** — Where must a person decide, approve, correct or take
   responsibility?
6. **Boundaries** — What privacy, safety, accuracy and failure conditions
   change whether the workflow is appropriate?
7. **Test** — What small, realistic test would prove the setup works for the
   reader's job?
8. **Upkeep** — What must be reviewed when the product, source material or job
   changes?

The learner leaves with a completed workflow card, not a list of cautions.

## Routing rule

Each Daily record must declare one canonical destination and why it is the
right next step.

- Link directly to a specific accepted class/lab when it teaches the missing
  setup.
- Link to the Missing Middle lab when the claim spans several products or no
  narrower accepted class exists.
- Link to a LIBRAiRY reference only when the reader needs a definition or
  written lookup rather than a setup walkthrough.
- Never link to a candidate, held route, dead page or generic building front
  door.
- If the necessary destination is not accepted and publicly verified, hold the
  Daily item and open the exact build order. Do not weaken the promise to make
  the card publishable.

## Recurring intake and review flow

1. Audience or NewsStand captures a candidate reel or claim as discovery.
2. Accuracy checks current primary/official sources and records the exact
   claim boundary.
3. Learning applies the Missing Middle Check and selects the canonical owner.
4. The owner builds or updates the required class/lab/reference.
5. Editorial and Brand review the Daily copy independently from the source.
6. Platform proves the exact relationship, search ingestion and responsive
   destination.
7. Only the accepted public destination can unlock the Daily derivative for
   release.
8. Freshness changes to the parent suppress or correct every affected Daily
   record.

## Format guardrails

- Do not make the creator the story. Make the missing operating instruction
  the story.
- Do not imply that the reader failed because a compressed demonstration
  looked easier than the real setup.
- Do not use “human in the loop” as empty boilerplate. Name the exact decision
  or approval the person retains.
- Do not publish a checklist that is too general to change the reader's next
  action.
- Do not describe configuration, availability, plan access or product behavior
  without a dated primary source and recheck trigger.
- Do not turn every candidate into a class. Extend an existing canonical lesson
  when it already owns the mechanism.

## Accepted examples and boundaries

- The reusable-skills reel remains a valid discovery lead for the existing
  **Build the Skill. Keep the Job.** lab order. Its “staff a department” framing
  is not accepted as a factual capability claim.
- The edit-to-instruction reel remains a valid discovery lead for teaching the
  difference between a one-off edit and a reusable rule. One edited output is
  not enough evidence to change permanent instructions.
- Future examples need their own current evidence and destination ruling. This
  intake does not pre-approve their claims.

## Song and Promptoscope ruling

- **Promptoscope:** no. This is a practical recurring Daily item, not a comic
  horoscope premise.
- **Song:** not automatic. A class may separately propose a mnemonic if it has
  a distinct learning job and an original hook; the recurring column itself
  does not commission one.
