## Cross-surface admission rule

For any substantial teaching or explanatory artifact, structural validation
may prove only identity, completeness and required evidence presence. It may
not issue a learning-quality verdict.

Before admission, an independent artifact-first reviewer must record evidence
that an unfamiliar reader can:

1. state the human question and promised payoff;
2. reconstruct one connected causal explanation rather than repeat labels;
3. use a worked example to diagnose a failure or make a decision;
4. transfer the explanation to a different case;
5. identify what every retained analogy maps and where it stops; and
6. distinguish this surface's cognitive job from its Concept Index, class,
   episode, reference or dated-news neighbours.

The rejected Fundamentals chapter is the shared known-bad calibration. A
reviewer must reject it unaided for the observed comprehension failures even
though every required micro-field is present. This does not permit one generic
review receipt to admit multiple surfaces: each destination owner must bind its
own exact rendered/playable artifact and medium-specific learner outcome.
