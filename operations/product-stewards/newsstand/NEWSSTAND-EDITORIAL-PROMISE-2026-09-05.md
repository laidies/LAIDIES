## Reader and outcome

The NewsStand should be the place LAiDIES readers choose to get AI news and understand the headlines. Readers are smart professional women with no assumed AI or computer-science background. They should leave better able to use AI, understand what is happening, and participate in informed, constructive decisions about its future.

Women must help shape the future of AI. That requires useful conceptual understanding, practical experience, access to opportunity and a voice in decisions about benefits, harms, responsibility and guardrails. Do not frame women as deficient, assume all women share an experience, or turn that mission into predetermined optimism or dismissal of criticism. Examine evidence, distribution, power and affected people's perspectives.

## Nine editorial obligations

1. Explain what happened, what it actually means, why it matters to the reader, and the concepts needed to understand it. Anticipate likely reader questions and answer them inside the article rather than requiring prior technical knowledge or a second visit.
2. Earn trust through accurate sourcing, appropriate uncertainty, honest headlines and visible corrections. No misinformation, clickbait, exaggerated capability or unearned certainty.
3. Include practical guidance when the subject earns it. Guidance may be something to try, compare, ask, check, avoid or watch; do not invent advice or force every story into a tips checklist.
4. Cover the major relevant AI headlines readers encounter and consequential developments that mainstream coverage may overlook. Relevance, importance and evidence govern inclusion, rather than one scout's agenda or a quota.
5. When circulating coverage is misleading, identify the exact claim or framing, explain the supported account and its limits, and show why the difference matters. Continue beyond debunking to the interesting verified mechanism. Ali's OpenAI/Hugging Face and agent-message-board example identifies this desired depth; it does not establish that distinct reported events are connected. Verify that relationship before any factual treatment.
6. Seek important advances in STEM, medicine and other fields. Explain the exact contribution of AI, what became possible or materially easier, what people still do, what was demonstrated, what validation remains, and who may benefit or be excluded. Claims such as 'without AI this would be impossible' need specific comparative evidence; a demonstration is not a patient outcome or general capability.
7. Maintain accurate, current practical tips that help readers get more from their tools and discover useful applications. Bind product, surface, version, access, cost, task, evidence, limitations and recheck trigger where material. Newly published advice is not automatically current practice.
8. Develop Big Picture articles around the largest consequential questions. Explore the strongest arguments and counterevidence, explain the underlying mechanisms, and assess plausible solutions, responsibilities, trade-offs and what readers or institutions can do. The data-centre article is a positive direction, not a finished universal template.
9. Apply the women-focused mission throughout selection, sourcing, consequences and useful agency. Career opportunity, becoming effective tool users, interpreting the news and participating in constructive discourse are connected outcomes. Benefits, risks, uncertainty and guardrails receive evidence-led treatment; neither hype nor blanket opposition supplies the conclusion.

## Explanation, voice and memory

Use conversational, enjoyable, adult language. Explain necessary terms as they arise, without assuming background knowledge or replacing precise mechanisms with vague metaphors. Anticipate what a newcomer would ask about names, products, tiers, mechanisms, comparisons and consequences.

Apply the existing Hannah Fry communication benchmark as a method: a human question, useful curiosity, an invisible process made concrete, a cause-and-effect sequence, an earned moment of understanding, retained limits, and a useful or witty landing. Adapt these mechanics into LAiDIES' own voice; a mention of the benchmark is not evidence of clarity.

Rewind Era connections are learning devices. Where useful, connect a concept to a familiar memory that makes the mechanism click and remain retrievable. Ali reports remembering model-generated synthetic data through the existing Scream/'inside the house' device in the AI 101 textbook. Preserve the learning job of that association; this is not an instruction to copy the line into every article or to inherit all surrounding factual claims.

The producer must be able to explain which relationship the reference makes memorable, map it back to the actual mechanism, and state where it stops applying. Fun and recall matter alongside accuracy. Do not force jokes into harm, bereavement or another context where they would undermine the subject or the affected people.

## Distinct publication jobs

| Surface | Job and cadence |
|---|---|
| Breaking | A rare, proportionate interruption when waiting would disadvantage the reader. |
| Daily / The Latest | Understand the important dated developments, including major circulating headlines and consequential overlooked news. |
| THE FRONT PAiGE | An interest feature especially relevant to LAiDIES readers. It need not change daily or weekly. Select and replace it when an earned feature better serves the reader; do not make it merely the day's longest news item or a compulsory derivative of Big Picture. |
| Weekly | Highlight and explain the biggest stories of the actual covered week so a reader can grasp their key messages without already reading the daily articles. Preserve the established Wednesday review cadence and honest original dates; a retained earlier roundup must not masquerade as a newly produced week. |
| Big Picture | A deep, sourced exploration of a consequential question, with arguments, counterarguments, mechanisms, potential solutions and practical or civic agency. Track emerging themes over time and commission when the evidence earns the inquiry. |
| Practical and service columns | Useful, current guidance with distinct desk jobs, source/canon boundaries and truthful unavailable states. |

## Connections and trend development

Identify the core concepts and necessary terms in each article. Explain enough within the story for understanding now; then offer exact relevant links to admitted LAiDIES concepts, lessons, Library chapters and other useful destinations, with the payoff stated. Connect relevant people to LUMINAiRY when their work or perspective helps understanding. Do not invent a destination, link merely for decoration, or make an unpublished resource appear available.

Maintain a source-linked view of emerging trends, recurring questions, changed evidence, disagreements and affected groups. Use it to develop distinct Front PAiGE features and Big Picture inquiries. Weekly selection must also compare the actual week's evidence with the prior roundup; do not let an existing thesis swallow every new development.

## Preserve reported history and link later coverage — September 5 reaffirmation

DECIDED — Ali reaffirmed an existing design, not a request for a new feature:
a later news development receives its own story, stable ID/URL and publication
date. The new article explains what changed and refers readers to the earlier
reporting. Do not silently update an old news story to make it describe facts
that became known later, redate it, or lose what readers could see at the time.

Keep the original reported body, sources and publication date. Separately
maintained navigation must add links at the bottom of the old article to later
published coverage of the same story. The new article links back. Follow the
editorially identified story through further developments, rather than relying
on a generic topic/tag match or an incidental source link. Held or withdrawn
coverage must be labelled honestly and must not expose withheld prose.

An error in the original reporting needs an explicit dated correction or
retraction notice, with the previous version/history retained. That process is
not permission to use same-ID correction machinery for later news developments
or to overwrite history silently. Source rechecks alone do not create a new
publication date. This rule governs dated news reporting. Big Picture living
analysis, described below, and book/guidance maintenance have different jobs.

Current implementation is PARTIAL. The reader supports reciprocal story IDs,
but the ordinary candidate path rejects non-empty lineage, the live dataset
contains no populated lineage and the renderer places its optional links near
the top. The runbook and workflow audit record the exact production/reader gap;
no claim of working bottom-of-article navigation is made.
