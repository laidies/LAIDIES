# Send the message before you need a favour.

Reconnect with something specific, not a mystery invitation to “pick your brain”.

You liked working with someone, then six months disappeared. You do not need an elaborate reason to get back in touch.

Try: “I was reminded of our conversation about onboarding when I saw this example. The section on first-week checklists made me think of your project. How did the new process work out?” Only send the link if it is genuinely relevant; a warm check-in can stand on its own.

Dorie Clark recommends building relationships before an immediate need arises. The point is to stay interested in the person’s work, not to disguise a request. If you do need help, say what you are asking for and make it easy to decline.
