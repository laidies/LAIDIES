# Why does my AI keep getting the day wrong?

A calendar question needs a reliable date, not just a convincing sentence.

Dear Miss Jeeves, my AI can explain quantum physics, then confidently tell me Monday is Wednesday. Why is it so bad at knowing the day and time—and how do I stop it messing up my plans?

— Temporally Confused in Toronto

Dear Temporally Confused,

Explaining a calendar and checking today’s date are different tasks. A model can generate an answer about dates from the information it has, but the app must supply a current date or give it access to a suitable tool if you want it to check the time now.

Some products supply date information automatically. Some can use search or other tools. That still leaves room for mistakes: the answer might use the wrong time zone, carry forward an old date from the conversation or get the day-of-week calculation wrong. Saying “Are you sure?” does not supply the missing fact.

For a scheduling task, give it the confirmed date, place and time zone: “Today is Monday, 7 September 2026, in Vancouver. The meeting is on Thursday, 10 September at 2 p.m. Vancouver time. Draft an invitation using those details.” If you are converting between time zones, use a calendar or time-zone converter to confirm the result.

For a question about right now, ask the tool to check a current clock if it has access to one. If it cannot, check your device’s clock yourself. For flights, appointments and deadlines, confirm the final date against the booking or calendar before acting. Miss Jeeves would rather you checked twice than arrived on Wednesday for a Tuesday lunch.
