## Reader question

What changes when an AI tool can remain online, sign into other tools and carry
out work after I close my laptop—and what should I check before giving it that
access?

## Plain-language mechanism

1. A normal chat pauses until the reader returns and sends another message.
2. Grok Bot receives a cloud computer that stays online.
3. The reader signs the Bot into selected websites or apps, giving it the ability
   to act inside those services.
4. The Bot can continue a multi-step job and place results in the real tool
   while the reader is away.
5. That convenience also moves the control question earlier: before starting,
   the reader must decide which account, actions and consequences the Bot may
   reach—not merely review the prose it writes afterward.

## Evidence classification

- **Confirmed by xAI:** launch date, early-beta status, named eligible plans and
  devices, cloud-computer model, sign-in and cross-app action claims.
- **Company framing, not independent proof:** “finished work,” improving over
  time, internal examples and user testimonials.
- **LAiDIES inference:** an always-on tool with connected-account access changes
  the practical review point from output-only checking to access plus action
  design. This follows from the announced mechanism and xAI's own terms, but is
  not a quoted xAI conclusion.
- **Unknown:** Bot-specific credential isolation, permission granularity,
  connected-app retention, training treatment, reliability and regional access.

## Reader consequence

The useful action is not “never use an agent.” Before connecting one, choose a
low-consequence first job, list the exact accounts and actions it needs, remove
access it does not need, require approval before sending, paying, publishing or
deleting, and check the real destination—not only the Bot's summary.
