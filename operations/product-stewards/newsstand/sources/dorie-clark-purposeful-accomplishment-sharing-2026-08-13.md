# Source brief — sharing accomplishments with purpose

**Checked:** 2026-08-13 06:55 PDT

**Source type:** first-party practitioner guidance, not research

**Author:** Dorie Clark

**Primary URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/doriec_for-a-long-time-i-was-selective-about-when-activity-7409011943279083520-D-kB

## What the authored post supports

Clark distinguishes useful professional visibility from disconnected
self-promotion. Her practical test is relevance and contribution: share an
accomplishment when it strengthens a point, gives another person a useful
example or lets someone learn from the experience. Audience and context matter.

The post also recommends emphasizing what the experience taught rather than
offering a win with no connection to the moment.

## What it does not establish

- It is practitioner guidance, not an experiment or universal finding.
- It does not establish that every workplace rewards visibility fairly.
- It does not override confidentiality, employer policy or professional rules.
- It says nothing about AI. Any LAiDIES AI connection must be a separately
  reasoned parallel and must not be attributed to Clark.

## Permitted Career / Work-Life use

Build one career-first item about deciding when and how to share a result so
that the audience receives useful context or a lesson. The advice must remain
recognizable and useful if the later AI paragraph is removed.

An earned AI connection may explain that an evidence-rich accomplishment note
gives an AI tool better material for organizing a performance-review draft.
The human still judges confidentiality, fairness, workplace dynamics, timing
and whether sharing serves the moment.
