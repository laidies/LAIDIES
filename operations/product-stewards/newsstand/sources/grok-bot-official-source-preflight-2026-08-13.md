## Plain reader job

Explain what changes when an AI service receives its own cloud computer and
can keep acting in signed-in tools after the reader closes her laptop. The
useful question is not “is this exciting?” It is which work can be delegated,
which access the bot receives and which consequences remain the user's
responsibility.

## Current official facts

1. [xAI's Grok Bot page](https://x.ai/bot) labels the product **early beta** and
   says bots have their own computer, can sign in to apps and websites, can
   learn a demonstrated routine, can work in parallel and can continue 24/7
   even when the user's laptop is closed.
2. The same page currently lists **Cursor Ultra at USD 200/month** and
   **Cursor Premium Teams at USD 120/seat/month**. It says Grok Bot is included
   for existing Cursor Ultra or SuperGrok Heavy customers. The page offers a
   macOS Apple-silicon download and describes desktop and iOS interaction. Do
   not infer support for an unlisted device, plan or region.
3. [xAI's consumer terms](https://x.ai/legal/terms-of-service), effective
   2026-06-26, define agentic actions to include browsing, code execution,
   communications, file changes, tool use, data processing and third-party
   interactions. They place responsibility for directed agentic actions and
   their consequences on the user and do not warrant their accuracy, safety or
   legality.
4. [xAI's privacy policy](https://x.ai/legal/privacy-policy), effective
   2026-04-04, describes collection, use, disclosure, security and retention
   for the general xAI consumer service. It warns users not to put personal
   information into prompts and inputs. It does not, in the publicly readable
   text verified here, establish a Bot-specific credential-isolation,
   retention or model-training contract.
5. [xAI's pricing page](https://x.ai/pricing) links Grok Bot as a current
   product but its general plan comparison does not provide a complete
   Bot-specific feature matrix. The Bot page is the current price/access
   authority for this preflight.

## Evidence boundary

The official sources establish a real early-beta product, named access paths,
current displayed prices, a cloud-computer/always-on operating model and a
material user-responsibility boundary. They do **not** establish universal
availability, enterprise security suitability, the reliability of completed
work, exact per-tool permissions, credential isolation, Bot-specific data
retention, or whether every connected-tool interaction is excluded from model
training.

Direct command-line retrieval of all four xAI pages returned HTTP 403 on this
machine at the observation time, so no raw-HTML checksum is claimed. The pages
were readable through the public web index/browser path. Publication-day
review must reopen the live official pages; this file is not a frozen source
substitute.
