## Candidate: knowing when a task deserves a loop

AIDB's September 3 webinar supplies a useful teaching route, not a breaking-news story. Its strongest reader question is: **When should I let an AI keep working toward a finish line, and when should I keep the judgment myself?**

Possible future jobs, in priority order:

1. **Concept of the Week:** distinguish a schedule (runs when a time or trigger arrives) from a loop (runs until a checkable condition is met).
2. **Paige's Practical AI and Productivity Tip:** before using a long-running agent, write a concrete finish line, a stopping cap and the decisions that still require you.
3. **AI Fundamentals 101 link/update review:** Chapter 15 already teaches loop and graph engineering. The webinar may strengthen its treatment of verification, but it does not establish a new concept.
4. **Behind the Build/internal practice:** compare this advice with LAiDIES' existing goal, bounded-work, role separation and independent-review practices. Current disposition: **ALREADY COVERED / WATCH FOR A BOUNDED PILOT**, not an automatic agent or framework change.
