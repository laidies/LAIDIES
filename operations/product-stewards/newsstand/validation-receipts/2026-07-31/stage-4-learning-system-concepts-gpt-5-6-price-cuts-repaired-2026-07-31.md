## Reader mental-model ruling

**PASS.** The packet gives a newcomer the correct three-meter model:

1. **API token rate:** what a developer is charged per input/output token;
2. **subscription price and quota:** the recurring ChatGPT/Codex plan price
   and its budget, which this announcement says did not change; and
3. **credit consumption:** how much of the same Codex or ChatGPT Work budget a
   use consumes, which may change without making the subscription cheaper.

It then adds the decision-relevant fourth layer: completed-task cost also
depends on input/output mix, reasoning, tools, caching, retries and context,
including the long-context surcharge. Thus “Luna API rate fell 80%” does not
become either “my subscription is 80% cheaper” or “every completed task is 80%
cheaper.” The plan-access line is also exact: Free and Go receive Terra;
Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise may choose Terra or Luna in ChatGPT Work
and Codex. API availability remains a separate developer access path.

This is useful to a newcomer because it answers the likely immediate mistake,
then supplies a bounded action: test Luna on a specified reversible workflow
step and compare accepted-result quality, retries, latency and total cost; use
the no-action path when the reader uses ordinary ChatGPT chat rather than the
API, Codex or ChatGPT Work. It does not require the reader to infer model
equivalence from a vendor benchmark or from a price table.

## Coffee analogy ruling

**PASS — accurate, narrow and explicitly limited.** Coffee-club membership
maps to the monthly subscription price; wholesale beans map to developer API
token rates. The analogy earns its place by separating two charges that a
newcomer might otherwise collapse into one “ChatGPT price.” It does not map
credits one-to-one to beans, and it does not establish a completed-task price.

The draft states that limit immediately: actual API cost depends on volume,
input/output mix, reasoning, tools, caching, retries and context length. Its
separate credit paragraph supplies the missing middle case—more work can fit
within an unchanged budget—without calling that a plan-price cut. The analogy
therefore supports the mechanism without replacing it.
