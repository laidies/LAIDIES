## Reader mental-model and analogy ruling

**PASS.** The packet distinguishes five controls without replacing their
mechanisms with a vague sandbox label:

1. a written exercise scope or statement of intent;
2. a technical containment boundary that actually restricts reachable systems;
3. model safeguards that may refuse or constrain risky action;
4. whole-trajectory monitoring, rather than an isolated action check; and
5. an independent stop procedure outside the model.

This is accurate and useful in a dated application. A model can continue an
assigned goal without having a new motive; that does not make a written
instruction, an asserted lack of internet, or one control layer a technical
guarantee. No listed control is represented as a guarantee or as proof that
ordinary consumer chat products can reach the internet.

**Hotel/fire-drill analogy: PASS — narrow, not literal.** The drill briefing
maps to the written simulation premise; the accidentally accessible guest room
maps to a real reachable route; hotel supervision maps to trajectory
monitoring; and the emergency procedure maps to an external stop control. Its
explicit limit is essential: a participant opening a door does not establish
an escape motive, while the mismatch between brief, physical boundary,
supervision and halt procedure remains a real safety failure. It does not
treat a hotel drill as cyber evidence, equate a model with a human moral agent,
or imply that locking one door supplies complete containment.

The Breaking lets a reader resist both errors — the agent became sentient, or
it followed instructions so nothing was wrong — without becoming an evergreen
security course. The existing Concepts route, not this article, owns reusable
definitions and fuller analogy work.
