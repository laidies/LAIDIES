## Stage 3 outcome

**HOLD**

The central correction is legally sound: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 did not
postpone the whole AI Act, and it did move the Chapter III, Sections 1–3
application dates for the two specified high-risk categories. The exact
reader article cannot proceed, however, because it describes a new
"nudification-system ban" as part of what changed without saying that the new
Article 5 prohibitions apply from **2 December 2026**, not on the regulation's
27 July entry-into-force date. It also leaves the reader with an unspecified
"every August 2026 task" warning when the amended text contains a material,
specific adjacent calendar: Article 50(2) transparency steps for covered
pre-2-August systems are due by 2 December 2026.

Those omissions matter to the precise mental model the story promises. They
can leave a reader believing that the new prohibition is already operative or
that the only relevant next milestone is an undefined August task. This is a
terminal HOLD for this exact packet. Do not send it to Learning System,
Brand/render, champion reconciliation or release packaging.

## Legal accuracy and scope ruling

### Supported at the required level

- Article 113 now applies Chapter III, Sections 1–3 (except Article 6(5)) from
  2 December 2027 for Article 6(2)/Annex III systems and from 2 August 2028
  for Article 6(1)/Annex I systems. Calling these, respectively, broadly
  stand-alone and regulated-product high-risk systems is acceptable only if
  the article preserves the Article 6/Annex qualification.
- The regulation is directly applicable and entered into force on 27 July
  2026. It does not make every AI Act provision subject to either new
  high-risk date.
- The calendar metaphor and instruction not to self-classify a product are
  sound. The draft appropriately avoids individual legal advice.

### Claims that require correction before publication

1. **“Adds a ban on nudification systems” is legally overbroad and missing its
   commencement date.** The new Article 5 points (ba) and (bb), and Article
   5(1a)–(1b), apply from 2 December 2026. The law is not a categorical ban on
   every image-editing or "nudification" system. For a provider, the
   prohibition is limited to systems intended for the prohibited material, or
   where that outcome is reasonably foreseeable and reproducible and the
   system lacks reasonable and adequate safeguards. For a deployer, use is
   prohibited where it is for that prohibited purpose. The intimate-content
   limb is further bounded by realism, an identifiable natural person and
   specified consent conditions; the child-sexual-abuse-material limb has a
   statutory "without right" exception under national law. The Breaking and
   both companion drafts must not imply an immediate, unqualified product ban.

2. **The live calendar needs at least one accurate near-term counterexample.**
   The amended Article 113 keeps the general application structure while
   moving only defined provisions. It also gives providers of covered
   synthetic-audio/image/video/text systems placed on the market before 2
   August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to take the necessary steps to comply
   with Article 50(2). A revised Breaking piece may state this narrowly, or
   remove the vague "every August 2026 task" formulation; it cannot use a
   vague calendar warning as a substitute for the material qualification.

3. **“The rules ... now apply from” needs Article 113 precision.** It must be
   clear that the postponed dates concern the listed Chapter III Sections 1–3
   high-risk obligations, except Article 6(5), not every rule that could touch
   a system in those sectors. The current first paragraph is close, but the
   revised claim map should carry the exact provision so the shorthand cannot
   expand in later editing.

4. **Weekly and Tribune are not independently admissible.** They lack their
   own candidate envelopes and Stage 1/Stage 2 treatment. Their repeated
   "nudification-system ban" shorthand must be corrected before they become
   a new candidate identity; the Tribune's policy position also needs its
   promised named regulator, affected-organization and rights-advocate
   perspectives before it can be evaluated as a Tribune argument.

## Harm and detail-level judgment

The material is appropriate to report. It concerns a public regulation and
does not expose operational security, private information or harmful
instructions. The risk is legal and reputational: a headline correction about
several statutory clocks cannot flatten a targeted prohibition into an
immediate universal ban, or convert sector/category shorthand into a reader's
classification decision. Keep the explanation at calendar-and-scope level and
direct organization-specific classification to qualified counsel.
