# Daily newspaper format — Town Group Chat

**Status:** SPECIFIED LOCALLY — FICTIONAL FORMAT CANDIDATE; NO PUBLIC COLUMN,
CHARACTER SCRIPT OR COMMUNITY FEATURE IS APPROVED

## Reader promise

**Town Group Chat** is a short, clearly fictional text thread among established
SUNNYVAiLE characters. It builds the town, rewards regular readers and turns
small crises into memorable comedy. Some threads can reveal a useful AI lesson;
others can simply be funny town life.

Examples of the energy—not approved scripts—include:

- “OMG, you asked it to *what*?”
- Deb broke the printer again.
- Miss Jeeves has located an unsupported claim loose in aisle three.
- JoJo is trying to find out who changed the group-chat name.

## What this is not

- It is not a transcript of real resident conversation.
- It is not a disguised testimonial or fake user-generated content.
- It is not Breaking or Daily AI news.
- It is not permission to invent character speech without canon review.
- It is not required to force a lesson into every joke.

## Ownership boundary

- **NewsStand fictional SUNNYVAiLE desk:** owns the edited recurring column,
  clear fictional label, publication cadence and separation from factual news.
- **Community:** owns real resident messages, rooms, moderation, privacy and
  notification behavior. Real messages never feed this format by default.
- **Character/building owner:** approves the exact characters, relationships,
  voice and visual identity.
- **Learning:** verifies any teaching principle and exact destination.
- **Audience & Growth:** may build an original channel-native derivative only
  after the canonical thread is accepted.

## Thread anatomy

1. **Tiny crisis** — one line that is immediately legible.
2. **Three to six messages** — short enough to read without pausing a video.
3. **Character turn** — each line sounds like its established speaker and does
   one comic job.
4. **Payoff** — a final reveal, callback or reaction; not an explanatory
   paragraph.
5. **Optional receipt** — when the joke rests on an AI mistake, a small link to
   the exact lesson, lab or lookup. The chat remains funny without the link.

## Content lanes

### AI memory hook

A character forgets or ignores a specific LAiDIES practice. The mistake and
reaction make the principle memorable. The underlying teaching must already
be accepted and the chat must not distort it.

### Town continuity

Characters react to an upcoming event, a building incident or an established
relationship. This can tease an accepted announcement but may not fabricate a
real product release or promise.

### Pure town nonsense

A harmless recurring joke that makes SUNNYVAiLE feel inhabited. It does not
need an educational CTA.

## Visual and accessibility rules

- Label the surface **Fictional SUNNYVAiLE chat** or an approved equivalent.
- Use the correct approved character avatars; do not regenerate identities.
- Distinguish speakers by name and redundant visual treatment, not colour
  alone.
- Provide readable static text/captions for any animated or video version.
- Keep messages in logical reading order at mobile width; no cropped bubbles,
  rapid unreadable timing or fake operating-system notification permissions.
- Never make the column resemble the resident's private inbox or imply she has
  unread personal messages.

## Editorial and safety gate

Reject a thread when it:

- humiliates a character rather than creating affectionate town comedy;
- relies on a stereotype about women, age, competence or technology;
- presents a made-up AI claim as fact;
- teaches the wrong lesson for the sake of the punchline;
- copies a creator's wording, visual sequence or performance;
- exposes or imitates a real resident's private message;
- conflicts with current character/building canon; or
- needs a paragraph of explanation to work.

## Pilot build

Build three scripts before choosing one pilot:

1. one AI memory-hook thread;
2. one town-continuity thread; and
3. one pure town-nonsense thread.

Use only canon-stable characters and existing approved identities. Review the
three as static mobile threads before motion, audio or social production. One
accepted pilot then proves visual grammar, readability, character voice and
the optional learning-link behavior.
