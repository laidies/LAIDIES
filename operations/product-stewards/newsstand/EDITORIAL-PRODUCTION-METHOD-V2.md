**Purpose:** replace field-filling with a premise-first reporting and usefulness method.

## 0. Apply the combined explanation method

Every material NewsStand explanation, investigation and advice item binds
`operations/product-stewards/learning-content-ecosystem/LAIDIES-EXPLANATION-AND-EDITORIAL-REASONING-BENCHMARK.md`.
The producer must visibly combine:

- Hannah Fry's human-first, mechanism-visible explanatory movement;
- Feynman-style first-principles reconstruction, plain language, explain-back
  and a genuinely different transfer case; and
- AIDB-style separation of headline, original evidence, establishes/does-not-
  establish, claimed impact, real consequence and unresolved questions.

Every substantial NewsStand explanation also carries one workplace example
and one non-work/everyday-life example. Both apply the article's same mechanism
in genuinely different contexts; neither may be decorative or a relabelled
copy. A compact Breaking alert may link to the substantial paired explanation
when two examples would obstruct the alert's job.

These are methods, not borrowed personas. LAiDIES retains its own voice. A
producer cannot compensate for a missing lens with stronger performance on
the other two.

## 1. Qualify the premise before choosing prose

Before opening a new candidate, search the repository by the reader's question,
format, subject, central mechanism and known headline variants. Record every
plausible existing artifact and disposition it as `CURRENT PREDECESSOR`,
`SOURCE MATERIAL ONLY`, `REJECTED KNOWN-BAD`, `DISTINCT READER JOB` or
`DUPLICATE — DO NOT BUILD`. If a comparable candidate exists, the new work must
be a checksum-bound successor that states what it preserves and improves. It
may not call itself `FIRST`, silently restart the article or leave the older
candidate competing for release.

Every candidate begins with a one-page premise record. It must answer:

- What exact new fact, recurring question or observed problem exists?
- What does a smart non-technical woman likely already know?
- What remains confusing, consequential or difficult after ordinary coverage or advice?
- Why is LAiDIES doing this now?
- Which format performs the reader job—and why would the next-nearest format be wrong?
- What primary evidence exists, what is missing and what could overturn the premise?
- What did AIDB and other trusted analysis cover? If they did not cover it, why is LAiDIES continuing?
- What will the reader understand, decide or do that she could not before?

If those answers are weak, the disposition is `QUIET`, `WATCH`, `UPDATE EXISTING` or `DECLINE`. A blank publication slot is not a reason to continue.

## 2. Prove the format job

### The Breaking

The new information itself—not the history around it—must materially change a reader's action or available choice before the next Daily. Require:

- exact time and change;
- minimum newcomer background;
- a named LAiDIES reader consequence;
- a useful action she can understand now;
- proof that waiting matters; and
- a non-clickbait headline test.

An available packet, viral framing, serious topic or once-breaking parent event cannot supply urgency.

### The Daily

Build a real dated edition, not a schema demonstration. Before prose, create a simple reader-facing flatplan:

- front-page lead and why it leads;
- secondary news, if earned;
- recurring columns actually available that day;
- clear hierarchy and reading order;
- deliberate empty desks omitted or shown without internal status language; and
- one reason the items belong together today.

The reader must recognize a newspaper before encountering production logic.

### The Weekly

Require at least two distinct dated developments and a connection that changes understanding. The producer must show what becomes visible only when the events are read together. A chronology, bundle or enlarged Daily is insufficient.

### The Big Question

Treat this as investigation, not a thesis template. Begin with a question whose answer is not already written. The investigation plan must name:

- primary documents and datasets;
- claims that conflict or remain unproved;
- affected people or practical cases;
- responsible organizations and questions put to them;
- independent expertise needed to interpret evidence;
- incentives, money, power or accountability where relevant;
- what can be directly tested;
- what discovery would be genuinely new to the reader; and
- what remains unanswered after reporting.

Only after reporting may LAiDIES form a conclusion. A predetermined thesis plus a counterargument is analysis, not investigative journalism.

### STRAiGHT TALK

Start from a recurring question people actually ask or a decision they repeatedly mishandle. Record the reader's likely baseline so the answer does not spend half its space announcing something she already knows.

For verification questions, useful action goes beyond opening a link:

- identify the exact claim and the evidence type it needs;
- find the original study, document, data or product record;
- inspect author, publisher, incentives, funding and conflicts;
- check method, sample, comparison, measures and limitations where research is involved;
- distinguish direct evidence from interpretation and causal claims from association;
- follow the citation chain rather than stopping at a summary;
- look for correction, retraction, version and date history;
- triangulate with independent authoritative evidence;
- test whether omitted context reverses the takeaway; and
- record a claim-level decision: supported, bounded, disputed, unsupported or still unknown.

The answer must teach at least one move beyond the reader's existing common knowledge.

### Dear Miss Jeeves

Reproduce the actual failure before giving advice. For persistent instruction failure in long work, distinguish:

- prose instructions from enforced controls;
- model context from durable external state;
- an accumulated pile of corrections from one reconciled current authority;
- remembered rules from executable validators;
- acknowledgement from observed compliance;
- one-off prompting from orchestration across long jobs; and
- product limitations from user-fixable briefing problems.

Possible remedies must be tested rather than listed: smaller bounded work units, one current external brief that replaces and marks superseded instructions instead of accumulating conflicts, checkpoints, clean handoffs, automated acceptance and regression tests, tool-enforced permissions, versioned artifacts, context refresh without abandoning the job, and human takeover where the system cannot reliably comply. “Start a new chat” may be one recovery option; it is never the universal answer.

### Paige, Career/Work-Life and Promptoscope

Each must solve one real problem and pass a usefulness test independently. A small format is not a lower quality bar.

- Paige: one current action plus an observable before/after check.
- Career/Work-Life: career guidance stands without AI; the AI connection clarifies rather than hijacks it.
- Promptoscope: the playful form makes one technically accurate habit memorable; the joke cannot be the whole value.

## 3. Source and analysis order

1. Read the original signal.
2. Resolve current primary and official evidence.
3. Decode what the evidence establishes and does not establish.
4. Check independent reporting and domain expertise.
5. Compare AIDB's treatment or record a dated absence/unavailable result.
6. Investigate meaningful disagreement rather than averaging sources.
7. Requalify the premise after research; decline it if the payoff disappeared.

## 4. Advice must survive a real case

Before advice prose passes, test it on the situation it claims to solve. Record:

- starting conditions;
- action attempted;
- observable result;
- where it failed;
- product or context boundaries; and
- the recovery path when the advice does not work.

Advice that sounds sensible but has not been tested against the failure remains `HOLD`.

## 5. Reader and voice gate

The producer must be able to answer:

- Could a newcomer understand the subject without another tab?
- Does this assume a developer, security, policy or research background?
- Is the action plausible for the real LAiDIES reader?
- What sentence or move makes this recognizably LAiDIES without decorating the prose?
- What did she learn that competent mainstream coverage did not already give her?
- Would she continue because the reporting is useful and alive—not because the headings promise depth?
- Can she reconstruct the central mechanism in ordinary language rather than repeat a phrase?
- Can she apply it to a genuinely different case?
- Does the article distinguish the circulating story from what the primary evidence actually supports?

Any `no` stops the draft inside production.
