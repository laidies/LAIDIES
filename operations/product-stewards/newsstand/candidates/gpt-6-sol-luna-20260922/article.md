# GPT-6 Sol and Luna: where the price applies

OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Sol and Luna on September 22. Its release notes place them in ChatGPT Work and Codex, and say they are separate from the models in ordinary Chat.

OpenAI also lists lower API prices. An API is the developer-facing connection that lets software send work to a model and pay for the text it processes. Those figures are not a ChatGPT subscription price.

A model name can appear in more than one OpenAI service. You might use Chat personally and Work through an employer, and the lists of available models can differ.

OpenAI says model and reasoning choices depend on a plan and workspace settings. A workspace is a shared work account whose plan and settings can affect which model choices appear. Its rollout is gradual, so an announcement cannot show what a particular account can select.

OpenAI says GPT-6 Sol can take on difficult work tasks. For a routine email, summary or document draft, another available model may be a sensible alternative; compare it with Sol or Luna in your own service before treating a new name as an automatic upgrade.

First identify the service you use: Chat, Work or Codex. Then look at that service’s current model picker and your plan or workspace details before assuming Sol or Luna is an upgrade you already have.

OpenAI’s performance and cost comparisons are its own evaluation claims. They do not promise that a particular task will be faster, cheaper or better for you.

“Sol and Luna are model names, but access and price depend on the service you use. The lower numbers are API prices for developers, not a new ChatGPT subscription bill.”

A model picker is the list of AI models an account can choose. The AI Fundamentals 101 lesson on tokens explains why text processed can be a usage meter rather than a monthly subscription price.
