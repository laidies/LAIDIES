# Rehearse the uncomfortable conversation before confidence arrives

Waiting until you feel confident can keep a necessary conversation on your calendar for weeks. Dorie Clark’s useful distinction is that people who advance are not necessarily more confident; they may be more willing to choose some uncomfortable moves deliberately, such as difficult conversations or sharing ideas before they feel fully ready.

Pick one conversation with a clear purpose. Write the outcome you need and the first sentence you can honestly say. For example: “I want to understand why the deadline moved and agree on what we can still deliver. Could we walk through what changed?” Rehearse it once with a trusted person. This is practice for clarity, not a prediction of how the real person will respond.

The AI connection: if you have access to a voice-capable AI tool, it can be a low-stakes rehearsal partner. Give it no names or confidential details. Describe the other person’s role, your intended outcome and one likely objection. Ask it to raise one objection at a time, then pause while you answer. Afterward, write the sentence you wish you had said.

The comparison stops there. AI can create practice turns; it cannot know the person, power dynamics, fairness, safety or consequences. Do not use it as a substitute for human advice in a dangerous, discriminatory or legally sensitive situation. Check your workplace’s rules before using a work account or work information.

Try this today: choose one bounded conversation, write the first line and rehearse one round. Confidence is not the entry ticket; preparation is. The AI is a rehearsal partner, not the office oracle.
