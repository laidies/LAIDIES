# California started data-centre rate and reporting work. Your bill did not change today.

## The Story

California made SB 1168 and AB 1577 law on September 21. They are two of seven data-centre bills signed that day; this article covers only these two. Neither law gives households an immediate bill credit or proves that electricity rates will fall.

SB 1168 tells the California Public Utilities Commission to assess opportunities for rate structures — the rules used to divide charges among customer groups. The assessment must consider data centres’ reasonable share of transmission and distribution costs, their proportionate share of load and procurement costs, and pressure on residential customers.

## The LAiDIES Read

“Assess opportunities” is the important phrase. The law starts work inside a new or existing commission proceeding. It does not select a tariff, calculate a household saving or show how a particular utility will divide costs.

AB 1577 creates a different kind of visibility. Covered data centres — facilities with at least 10 megawatts of electrical capacity — must supply estimates when seeking a discretionary local approval. Once a facility is connected to power, owners report specified information to the state, with further reporting at least annually.

## What This Means for You

The Energy Commission must publish submitted information annually in anonymized and aggregated form: combined so the public release does not expose one customer’s protected data. Its first specified statewide data-centre load-trend assessment belongs in the 2029 integrated energy policy report.

The reporting provisions exclude facilities below 10 megawatts and named public research, public safety, national-security, publicly owned and utility facilities. For a proposed project, local records may offer planning facts. For an electricity-bill claim, look for the later commission proceeding and an approved rate — not the signing headline alone.

## The Cocktail Party Explanation

“California started a process for possible data-centre rates and new reporting. The laws are real; an approved rate, household saving and facility-level public dossier are not here yet.”
