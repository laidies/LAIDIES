19.4 — How to Read This Industry

A few patterns that help decode job titles and company structures:

"Engineer" vs "Scientist": Engineers build things that work. Scientists discover new things. The boundary is blurry — many people do both — but if a title says "engineer," the emphasis is on building reliable systems. If it says "scientist" or "researcher," the emphasis is on discovery and experimentation.

"ML" vs "AI" vs "Data": Often used interchangeably in job titles. Roughly: "Data" roles focus on data itself (cleaning, moving, analysing). "ML" roles focus on building models. "AI" is increasingly used as the umbrella term for any role touching these systems.

The seniority ladder: Junior → Mid → Senior → Staff → Principal → Distinguished. At most companies, "Staff" and above means you're setting direction for teams and systems, not just building individual components.

🔍 Concept in Practice: Why your company's "AI team" might be 3 people or 300 The range of roles described above represents the full ecosystem. A startup might have one person wearing five hats: designing prompts, building pipelines, running evals, deploying to production. A large technology company might have separate teams for each role — hundreds of people across research, engineering, operations, product, safety, and governance. Neither is wrong — the size of the team reflects the scale and complexity of what they're building.
