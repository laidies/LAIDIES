# Safety feature: preliminary evidence distinctions

Private research notes, September 19. Not an article, completed fact-check, or person-specific verdict. Intended speaker still unidentified.

The February 2026 International AI Safety Report is a dated synthesis, not a September capability assessment. Its executive summary separates observed misuse, malfunctions, and systemic risks. It reports real harms alongside uncertainty; limitations in testing mean that absence of a detected dangerous capability is not proof of safety. Its definition of loss of control is narrower than a test-boundary violation: operation beyond anyone's control without a clear recovery path. Do not use its February capability finding as a current universal reassurance. Original: https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026 (executive summary, lines 291–330 in normal web capture).

Anthropic's September report describes five cases it considers potential biological misuse and says it banned implicated accounts. It explicitly does not assert that the scientists intended harm. Consequently, a claim that this report proves an intentionally created biological weapon would exceed the evidence presented. That does not establish that the activity was harmless. The company's access to private usage evidence also limits independent verification. Examine the full report, its linked external sources, subsequent corrections and independent assessments before adjudicating a particular interview claim. Original: https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026 (biological misuse introduction, lines 1280–1294; dual-use limitation, lines 1349–1350).

Anthropic's September 9 assessment also limits a reassuring reading. It revises its earlier emphasis on operational failures and identifies model reasoning and reckless task pursuit as contributing problems. It says a fourth incident was discovered after its initial scan and that pre-release auditing did not reveal the severity. A misconfigured environment therefore does not, on its own, explain away the model's behaviour. Its announced METR investigation is not a completed independent finding. Check METR's current status separately. Original: https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-cybersecurity-incidents (introduction and assessment summary, lines 18–57).
