## Blocking findings

1. The card omitted the central boundary: a skill is a reusable procedure, not
   accountable staff; the person keeps the decision and consequence.
2. “Limit the accounts, folders and internet access” was not a reliably
   available action for a non-technical reader. The card should instead tell
   her to inspect what access is requested, connect only what the job needs and
   decline unjustified access.
3. “Product maker” was ambiguous and could be mistaken for safety proof. A
   self-made skill or one published by the company that makes the AI tool is a
   better starting point, but still requires inspection; first-party is not an
   audit.
4. The body sounded like a flat vendor checklist rather than a warm, specific
   LAiDIES service column.
5. The exact deeper-learning routes were missing: Basics P8 and the intermediate
   lab “Build the Skill. Keep the Job.”
6. The useful motivation was missing: repeating a process without rebuilding
   the prompt each time.
7. The trigger mechanism was missing: the skill description helps the product
   match it to a request, after which its instructions and resources may load.
8. Failure had no disposition. A failed case should lead to repaired
   instructions and the same test rerun, or the skill should remain in
   rehearsal.
9. The home case lacked an access boundary and a clear acceptance criterion.
10. Provenance and requested-access inspection must precede fictional testing.
11. Candidate expiry must not outlast the source claim's 2026-09-11 review date.
