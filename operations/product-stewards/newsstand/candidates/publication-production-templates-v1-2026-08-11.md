# REJECTED — LAiDIES publication production templates candidate v1

**Rejected by Ali on 11 August 2026. Do not use this file to commission or judge content.** It converted format labels into fields but did not provide a reliable editorial method for selecting consequential topics, conducting investigation, producing genuinely useful reader actions or sustaining current long-running AI work.

**Status:** REJECTED KNOWN-BAD TEMPLATE — NOT CANONICAL, ADMITTED OR PUBLIC

**Applies to:** The Breaking, The Daily, The Weekly, The Big Question, Straight Answers About AI, Dear Miss Jeeves, Paige's Practical AI Tip, Career/Work-Life Tip and Promptoscope.

**Purpose:** make the formats hard to confuse, hard to fill with generic AI prose and easy to route from verified source material.

## Rules shared by all nine formats

Before drafting, the producer must have:

1. one exact reader job and one reason this format is the right destination;
2. the original item plus current primary or official evidence for every consequential claim;
3. an explicit `ESTABLISHES / DOES NOT ESTABLISH / UNKNOWN` record;
4. the AIDB comparison when AIDB covered the topic, or a dated honest `NOT COVERED` or `UNAVAILABLE` result;
5. an immutable source-version binding, checked date, expiry or event-triggered recheck, and correction owner;
6. a declared relationship for every reused signal: `PRIMARY OUTPUT`, `CONTRIBUTING EVIDENCE`, `UPDATE EXISTING` or `RELATED READING`; and
7. a producer contract that binds the current positive and negative exemplars before public prose begins.

Every draft fails if it:

- rewrites a press release, reputable newspaper or AIDB summary without checking the underlying evidence;
- makes the headline stronger than the study, event or source permits;
- uses the same lead, argument and action in multiple formats;
- hides uncertainty in a source list at the bottom;
- adds a Rewind Era reference that performs no explanatory or memory job;
- gives “be careful,” “verify it” or “use AI responsibly” as the action;
- treats a local file, review, commit or deploy as public publication; or
- fills a quiet desk with invented content.

Internal fields such as format tests, evidence status and gate notes do not appear as reader-facing copy. They remain in the producer record and manifest.

## Template 1 — The Breaking

### Use only when

Waiting for the next Daily would materially disadvantage the reader, or a qualifying model release immediately changes the reader's available choices. The new information must itself qualify: a clarification does not become Breaking merely because the original event once was. Urgency must be evidenced; “people are talking about it” is not enough.

### Required source record

- exact confirmed event and time;
- minimum background for someone who has never heard of the story, people or products;
- what changed since the last verified state;
- primary or official confirmation;
- affected people, products, regions or decisions;
- dispute, uncertainty and remaining unknowns;
- next scheduled recheck; and
- correction and escalation owner.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Headline:** the verified change, not the most dramatic interpretation.
2. **Newcomer background:** who or what this is about and the minimum prior event needed to understand it.
3. **Two-sentence alert:** what happened and who needs to know now.
4. **Why this interrupts:** why this new information—not merely the earlier event—belongs before the next Daily.
5. **What changes now:** exact action, access, price, risk, availability or choice.
6. **What does not change:** prevent the most likely overreading.
7. **What remains unknown or disputed.**
8. **One bounded action for an actual LAiDIES reader.**
9. **Sources and next update time.**

### Length and voice

Usually 250–500 words. Calm, fast and exact. No scene-setting paragraph, generic history lesson or cocktail-party flourish before the changed fact.

### Automatic rejection

- Can wait until the next Daily without harming the reader.
- A later update whose original event was urgent but whose new information is not.
- Assumes the reader already knows the story, companies or product.
- Introduces infrastructure or specialist terminology before explaining the event in plain language.
- Gives niche organizational advice instead of an action a real LAiDIES reader can understand and use.
- Reads like a security, policy or developer memo rather than LAiDIES.
- Depends on one uncorroborated social post or secondary headline.
- Says a model “escaped,” “went rogue,” “understands” or “decided” without exact mechanism evidence.
- Announces a model version without explaining material reader consequences.

## Template 2 — The Daily

### Use when

A new dated SUNNYVAiLE newspaper is due. The sourced-news desk may be quiet; the edition still requires admitted service columns or their governed empty states.

### Required edition manifest

- publication date and editorial time zone;
- front-page lead or explicit sourced-news quiet state;
- additional consequential items, if earned;
- admitted content IDs and immutable versions for every column;
- source health and freshness for each desk;
- correction state, archive identity and related-learning routes; and
- explicit empty-state text for any unavailable desk.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Masthead, date and one-sentence morning read.**
2. **Front-page lead:** what changed, why it matters and who is affected.
3. **Also on the desk:** no more than the earned consequential secondary items.
4. **Paige's Practical AI Tip or exact empty state.**
5. **Career/Work-Life Tip or exact empty state.**
6. **Promptoscope or exact empty state.**
7. **Other admitted Daily desks:** Mme CLAi-O, Song, Did You Know, town note, curiosity or clearly labelled fiction—or their governed empty states.
8. **One useful thing to do today.**
9. **Sources, corrections, archive and related reading.**

### Length and voice

The whole edition should feel curated, varied and finishable. The lead carries reporting depth; service columns stay compact. Do not force one voice across all desks.

### Automatic rejection

- A single article presented as the entire Daily.
- Repeated versions of the same item across lead, tip and Promptoscope.
- Empty desks silently filled with generic copy.
- A Daily composed entirely of internal LAiDIES promotion.
- A page with many cards but no editorial hierarchy.

## Template 3 — The Weekly

### Use only when

At least two distinct dated developments form one useful pattern, mechanism or change across the week. A headline bundle is not a Weekly.

### Required synthesis record

- week-ending date;
- at least two development IDs, dates and source versions;
- the connection between them;
- what changed across the week;
- what remained stable or unresolved;
- affected people and practical consequences;
- AIDB comparison and source ledger; and
- watch points and correction trigger.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Front-page thesis:** the pattern the week revealed.
2. **Development one:** what changed and why it mattered.
3. **Development two:** what changed and why it altered or completed the picture.
4. **The connection:** mechanism, chronology or shared consequence.
5. **The LAiDIES read:** what the combined evidence establishes—and does not.
6. **What this means for the reader.**
7. **What did not become clear this week.**
8. **What to watch next week.**
9. **Sources, AIDB comparison and related learning.**

### Length and voice

Usually 700–1,200 words. The centre belongs to connection and mechanism. Warm and readable, but not a chatty recap or a listicle.

### Automatic rejection

- Fewer than two distinct developments.
- Two items that share only the word “AI.”
- A rewritten Daily with a longer introduction.
- “The week in AI” without an earned thesis.
- A Tip of the Week substituting for synthesis.

## Template 4 — The Big Question

### Use only when

A consequential live issue supports a distinct evidence-led LAiDIES position, a serious counterargument and a useful decision model. It is not a larger news recap.

### Required argument record

- one explicit consequential question;
- why it matters now;
- evidence chronology and claim map;
- what evidence establishes;
- what remains inference or editorial position;
- LAiDIES thesis;
- strongest credible counterargument;
- evidence that would change the conclusion;
- individual and wider implications; and
- watch points, corrections and related learning.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **The question.**
2. **The short answer.**
3. **Why this question matters now.**
4. **What the evidence shows.**
5. **The LAiDIES position.**
6. **The strongest case against that position.**
7. **Why the position still holds—or how it changes.**
8. **What would change our mind.**
9. **What this means for individual and public choices.**
10. **The better question to carry forward.**
11. **Sources, uncertainty, corrections and related learning.**

### Length and voice

Usually 1,000–1,800 words. Intelligent magazine argument: curious, transparent and willing to show its working. No unsupported hot take, false balance or self-important editorial voice.

### Automatic rejection

- The thesis merely repeats the news event.
- No counterargument could plausibly change a reasonable reader's view.
- Evidence, inference and LAiDIES opinion are blended together.
- The conclusion is grander than the evidence.
- “Both sides” are presented without evaluating their evidence.

## Template 5 — Straight Answers About AI

### Use when

The same practical AI question will recur beyond today's news and can be maintained as one living reference answer. If the answer depends mainly on today's event, route it to NewsStand first.

### Required answer record

- exact recurring reader question;
- one-sentence direct answer;
- solid, conditional, contested and unknown claims;
- decision-relevant dependencies;
- common myth or misleading shortcut;
- useful action or decision model;
- confidence, verified date and recheck trigger; and
- correction owner and related dated coverage.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Question as the headline.**
2. **Short direct answer.**
3. **What is solid.**
4. **What it depends on.**
5. **What is contested or unknown.**
6. **Myth to drop.**
7. **Decision method or practical check.**
8. **Sources, confidence and last verified date.**
9. **Related current coverage, if any.**

### Length and voice

Usually 500–1,000 words. Answer first, then earn nuance. It should feel like a smart reference editor, not an essay that makes the reader excavate the conclusion.

### Automatic rejection

- Uses a volatile number without a dated source and recheck trigger.
- Treats one study as a universal answer.
- Hides “we do not know” behind vague balance language.
- Repeats the same scaffold mechanically when the question needs a different explanation.

## Template 6 — Dear Miss Jeeves

### Use when

A recurring, frustrating AI-use problem benefits from a direct diagnosis, one plain mechanism and a practical recovery move. The question may be pre-generated until a privacy-safe visitor intake exists.

### Required answer record

- exact question and recognizable situation;
- direct answer;
- mechanism;
- common misunderstanding;
- what the reader can change;
- when the problem belongs to the product or workflow rather than the user;
- canonical continuation;
- freshness, correction and product-specific boundary; and
- optional radio adaptation notes that do not create separate factual canon.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Dear Miss Jeeves question.**
2. **Short salutation and direct answer.**
3. **Why this keeps happening.**
4. **What people commonly misunderstand.**
5. **Miss Jeeves prescribes:** three to five exact recovery steps.
6. **When it is not your fault:** product, access, context or workflow limit.
7. **One memorable landing.**
8. **Learn more / canonical continuation.**

### Length and voice

Usually 350–650 words. Wry, composed and kind. Miss Jeeves diagnoses the system; she does not scold the reader, anthropomorphize the model as malicious or turn every answer into a technical manual.

### Automatic rejection

- The joke arrives before the answer.
- The diagnosis blames the reader without checking product limits.
- Advice is only “write a better prompt.”
- Product-specific behaviour is asserted without current documentation.
- The radio version silently changes the factual answer.

## Template 7 — Paige's Practical AI Tip

### Use when

One current, source-bound action can improve a specific AI task today and the reader can check whether it worked.

### Required tip record

- specific job;
- one practical move;
- why it works;
- boundary or risk;
- observable result check;
- canonical continuation; and
- source, checked date, expiry and correction trigger.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Memorable action headline.**
2. **The job:** one sentence.
3. **Try this:** one exact move.
4. **Why it works:** one mechanism, not a slogan.
5. **Check it:** observable success or failure.
6. **Boundary:** when the tip is insufficient or unsafe.
7. **Learn more.**

### Length and voice

Usually 80–180 words. Crisp, capable and lightly playful. One tip, not a miniature class or a list of twelve prompting tricks.

### Automatic rejection

- “Use a better prompt,” “be specific” or “verify the answer” without an exact move.
- No observable check.
- Vendor-interface advice without a current product-source check.
- A general inspiration quote with AI added to the final sentence.

## Template 8 — Career/Work-Life Tip

### Use when

The career guidance is useful without AI, and a truthful AI or workflow connection improves the reader's understanding or action. The AI connection is a mirror—not the topic requirement.

### Required tip record

- specific workplace situation;
- what is genuinely difficult;
- useful career guidance and its source;
- AI or workflow mirror;
- where the comparison stops;
- one concrete next move;
- workplace and AI evidence; and
- privacy, employment, power or other safety boundary.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Career-first headline.**
2. **The situation and real difficulty.**
3. **The useful career move.**
4. **The AI connection:** only the shared principle.
5. **Where the comparison stops.**
6. **Try this at work.**
7. **Safety or power boundary, when relevant.**

### Length and voice

Usually 120–250 words. Practical colleague, not corporate coach. Never tell women to solve structural power problems by becoming more confident, agreeable or efficient.

### Automatic rejection

- Career advice that exists only to promote an AI tool.
- Generic confidence, productivity or personal-brand language.
- Treating a colleague like a prompt, model or optimization target.
- Suggesting confidential workplace material be pasted into an unapproved tool.
- Using AI to diagnose another person's motive, intent or honesty.

## Template 9 — Promptoscope

### Use when

A playful horoscope frame makes one accurate prompting, context or model-use habit easier to remember. Astrology supplies the form, never the causal claim.

### Required tip record

- playful forecast headline;
- one accurate AI-use habit;
- mechanism;
- specific try-today move;
- observable comparison or check;
- verification boundary; and
- canonical source, checked date, expiry and correction trigger.

### Reader-facing structure

1. **Forecast headline.**
2. **One playful sentence.**
3. **The real habit.**
4. **Why it changes the input or evaluation.**
5. **Try this today.**
6. **Check the result.**
7. **One closing astrology disclaimer or joke.**

### Length and voice

Usually 80–160 words. Playful, brief and technically faithful. The joke should make the habit stick, not consume the column.

### Automatic rejection

- Generic horoscope filler.
- More than one habit.
- A promise that the technique will produce a better or correct answer.
- Astrology presented as prediction or evidence.
- A pun with no mechanism or testable action.

## Multi-output routing example

One verified incident may produce:

- **The Breaking:** the newly confirmed fact and immediate reader consequence;
- **The Daily:** a concise report in the next complete edition;
- **The Weekly:** the incident plus at least one other dated development, connected into a larger mechanism or pattern;
- **The Big Question:** contributing evidence inside a broader argument;
- **Straight Answers:** an update only if the evidence changes a recurring durable answer;
- **Paige, Career or Promptoscope:** a derivative only when it has its own source-bound action and does not repeat the news article.

Each output gets its own content ID, source-version binding, producer contract, review, correction state and release identity. Shared evidence is allowed; inherited approval and duplicated treatment are not.
