# Premise — Put the name back on the idea

## The real problem

A woman makes a useful point, the room moves on, and the same point receives attention when somebody else repeats it. Generic advice to “speak up” misses both the attribution problem and the need for language she can actually use without turning the meeting into a trial about intent.

## Reader baseline and gap

The reader already knows that women can be interrupted or overlooked. She may not have a low-drama sentence for reconnecting the idea to its source, a simple way to leave a neutral contribution record, or a clear limit on what that record can prove.

## Why this format

Career/Work-Life Tip is the correct destination because the useful job is workplace action first. Paige would wrongly make AI the protagonist. Dear Miss Jeeves answers an AI-use frustration. A class could later rehearse harder power and escalation cases, but this compact item should solve only the immediate meeting-and-recap move.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Current Lean In guidance supports making women's ideas visible, attributing the person who surfaced an idea and documenting contributions and outcomes. It does not establish any individual's motive. Current OpenAI guidance separately supports the narrow AI mirror that confident wording does not establish truth and important information needs source verification.

## Reader payoff

After reading, the visitor can use one sentence in the room, send one neutral three-line recap, explain why a record helps without proving motive, and apply the same source-preservation habit to an AI answer or a forwarded claim.

## Advice test

- **Starting condition:** a contribution was overlooked and later repeated; intent is unknown.
- **Action:** reconnect the repeated point to the earlier contributor and decision, then send a factual recap naming contribution, decision and owner.
- **Observable result:** the idea's source and next responsibility are visible in the meeting record without an accusation.
- **Failure boundary:** the wording cannot repair repeated bias, a power imbalance, retaliation or disputed ownership by itself.
- **Recovery:** preserve the facts and seek a manager, sponsor or appropriate workplace channel when the pattern or consequence is material.
