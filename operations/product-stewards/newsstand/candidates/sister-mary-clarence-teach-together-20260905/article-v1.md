# Sister Mary Clarence’s useful move: pass along the method

You have found a way to use AI that saves time. Now three people want the answer. Forwarding your final draft may be generous, but it can also make you the office’s unofficial AI help desk: the next task changes, the answer no longer fits, and everyone comes back to you.

Sister Mary Clarence is a LAiDIES teaching device inspired by a fictional teacher. It frames a different move: not “be the cleverest person in the room,” but make the room stronger. With AI, that means sharing the method behind a useful answer, not just the answer itself.

Say you used AI to turn meeting notes into a client follow-up. Do not send a colleague only the finished email. Show her the job you were solving, the notes you gave the tool after removing private details, the instruction you used, the facts you checked, and the edits you made because you know the client. She now has something she can adapt for her own meeting. If a step is unclear or the check missed something, she can improve the method instead of guessing what you did.

That is why a shared method travels farther than a clever prompt. The final answer belongs to one meeting. The method shows which choices made it useful: the purpose, the information, the request, the check and the human judgment. It also leaves room for another woman’s experience to make the next version better.

Try this once this week. Pick a low-stakes task and send one colleague a four-line method note: **job; information used; request; check and edit.** Ask her to run it on her own version, then tell you one step that was confusing and one change that would make it more useful. Keep sensitive names, notes and client details out of the example. You are not handing over your judgment. You are giving someone else a way to practise hers.
