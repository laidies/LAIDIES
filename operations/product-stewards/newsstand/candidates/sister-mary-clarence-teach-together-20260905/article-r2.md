# Sister Mary Clarence: make the whole room stronger

A good AI answer can make one woman look very efficient. It can also leave everyone else waiting for her to forward the clever bit. That is not the kind of progress we are here for.

Sister Mary Clarence is our PATRON SAiNT of Teaching: a fictional character used as a LAiDIES learning frame, not a person who teaches AI. Her useful move is to bring the whole room up. Think of a chorus. It does not become fuller because one singer hands everyone a copy of her solo. It becomes fuller when people can hear the shared shape, add their own part and notice what the room still needs.

That is a better way to share an AI practice. A final answer belongs to one situation. A shared method lets another woman use her own knowledge. She may know the client history you do not, spot a promise you would not make, or find the question your first version forgot to ask. Her contribution is not a detour from the result. It is part of what makes the result stronger.

Say you used AI to turn meeting notes into a client follow-up. Do not send a colleague only the polished email. Tell her the job, the non-sensitive information you used, what you asked the tool to do, and what you checked and changed. Then let her adapt that shape for her own client. When you compare notes, she may add a check you missed; you may spot a clearer way to describe the task. The next shared version is better because neither of you had to sing the whole thing alone.

Try one small chorus this week. Choose a low-stakes task and invite one woman to adapt your method for her own version. Ask: what did your context change, what did you check, and what should we add for the next person? Keep private names and details out of the example. Bring her along by making room for her judgment—and let it raise the whole result.
