## Product, game and brand assessment

The strongest model is a curated bonus shelf, not another weekly hub. Every object must do a distinct episode-linked job—practise, retrieve, judge, delight or encourage—and name why it is optional. The activity audit found generic card-grid treatment and no sufficiently magical/object-world hierarchy; visual polish should follow truth and child admission, not conceal unresolved game quality.

New and returning readers need one obvious current action, an honest archive path and a way back. Do not force completion, duplicate the Bag’s core routes, require account state, or imply that browsing a card is practice/replayability.

## UX, accessibility, analytics and upkeep

Historical QA indicates mobile stability for the shelf but not every child/end state. Accessibility requires keyboard/card semantics, current-selected episode state, visible unavailable state, focus-safe return and no status conveyed by colour alone. No aggregate analytics/VOC packet exists; use future privacy-safe measures only for child selection, real outcome and return to the lesson. Do not collect child inputs/private prompts or equate click volume with learning.

## Launch blockers and next action

1. **FIX BEFORE LAUNCH:** gate all cards on a child registry and hide/label unresolved products.
2. **FIX BEFORE LAUNCH:** prove exact per-episode/current/archive selection plus clean-user mobile/keyboard return route.
3. **HIDE/LABEL FOR LAUNCH:** any local reward/progress and in-progress shelf content.
4. **POST-LAUNCH:** use evidence to add at most one genuinely useful extra per episode, then maintain monthly archive health.
5. **REVENUE DEFERRED:** no paywall or reward pressure before a real optional-product value/delivery contract exists.

## Evidence limits and learning scan

This review relied on source and audit evidence; it did not run a selected game, test a reward, use mobile/assistive technology, access analytics or exercise public child outcomes. No new painpoint entry is created. Reapplied: a routed card/click is not an authoritative outcome, and a hub cannot advertise a child beyond that child’s verified status.
