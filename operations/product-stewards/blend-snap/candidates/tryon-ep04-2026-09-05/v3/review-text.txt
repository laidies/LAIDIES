          Caption included
          You wanted the pension plan. The search also found the project plan, hiring plan, travel plan and marketing plan. Technically, they match. Helpful? Less so.

Karen Spärck Jones showed how counting a word’s use across a collection could help distinguish results. A word in almost every file gives little help separating them; a matching word found in only a few can give more.

In this six-file example, “plan” appears in five files and “pension” in two. Her 1972 method gives each plan match 1 point and each pension match 3. The file matching both gets 4. Pension limits gets 3, separating it from the four plan-only files on 1 point each. That is what “more weight” does: it changes a match’s contribution to the result’s score and can change the order.

These are invented files with exact word matches. Rarity does not guarantee relevance or truth; current search systems may use other signals too.
