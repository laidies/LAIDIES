## Ali's signal

Ali experiences this almost every day: she has already told a model a rule or
how she wants something handled, it ignores the guidance, and when she calls
that out it replies that she is right and it should have followed the rule—but
then it still does not change its behaviour.

Preserve the annoyance and repetition. The recognizable learner problem is not
merely “write a better prompt.” It is the false sense of resolution created by
a fluent acknowledgement that does not update the governing instruction,
project source, current brief, output or future run.

Ali also proposed a learner-facing Classes format:

> **Help! [insert issue]**

For this idea, the concrete first candidate is:

> **Help! The model keeps ignoring my rules**

Ali then named the broader version:

> **Help! I set up the agents and rules—why am I still not getting the result?**

Her concrete LAiDIES experience is important evidence: she investigated the
failures, established owners and governing rules, specified repairs, marked
images as not in use and still did not receive the experience she wanted.
This must not be reduced to “the user needed another prompt” or “the system
needed another checklist.”

## `Help!` format candidate

Preserve `Help! [insert issue]` as a problem-first diagnostic wrapper. It could
let someone enter Classes through the frustration she actually recognizes
rather than requiring her to know the underlying concept name.

Classes and the Learning System should compare two bounded uses:

1. **In-class diagnostic label:** a recurring `Help!` segment inside an
   existing class or masterclass lesson, followed by what is happening,
   diagnosis, matching fixes, controlled example and proof.
2. **Recurring micro-class collection:** short standalone troubleshooting
   classes, each with one observable repair skill and a link to the deeper

Do not approve an open-ended series from the name alone. Every `Help!` entry
must begin with a real learner signal, reconcile against existing content,
teach a distinct observable repair, identify when its mitigation does not
apply and pass the normal learning-content admission standard. The label does
not lower the evidence, transfer or accuracy bar.

The owner review should decide whether the single-model and governed-agent
scenarios form one progression or whether the latter is an advanced standalone
`Help!` entry.

## Distinct teaching value to preserve

The lesson should help a learner:

1. recognize that “you are right” is conversational acknowledgement, not
   evidence that saved instructions, current context or an artifact changed;
2. inspect where the governing rule actually lives and whether it is current,
   available, unambiguous and controlling;
3. consolidate or refresh the rule in the current brief instead of repeatedly
   arguing about the past failure;
4. rerun only the failing part and check the result against the exact rule; and
5. deliberately reset the session or split the job when repair in place keeps
   failing.

Do not teach a universal diagnosis. A rule can fail because it is unavailable,
stale, ambiguous, contradicted, lower in the instruction hierarchy, mediated
by product behaviour or simply not followed reliably by the model. The
portable principle is:

> Judge the repair by the next result, not by the apology.

A possible learner line—not yet approved copy—is:

> Do not just tell me you understand. Show the current rule, redo the failing
> part, and check the new result against it.

## Explain what is actually happening

Ali explicitly wants mechanism, mitigations and concrete examples—not only a
workflow prescription. The lesson should separate at least these plausible
cause categories:

1. **Acknowledgement is generated language, not a settings change.** The model
   can produce a socially fitting apology without updating personal
   instructions, Project sources, a durable brief or future chats.
2. **The rule may not be available in the current model input.** It may be far
   back in a long conversation, omitted by product context selection or absent
   from this new session.
3. **The rule may be available but not controlling.** Several instructions can
   conflict, differ in priority, use vague scope or leave the model to infer
   which version is current.
4. **The rule may live in the wrong layer.** A correction made in one reply
   does not necessarily change personal preferences, Project instructions,
   another chat or a reusable workflow.
5. **The task can exceed reliable free-form compliance.** Long, multi-stage or
   exacting work can drift even with a good instruction. A fluent model remains
   fallible, so hard constraints need checks rather than trust.
6. **Governance may be documented but not operational.** A registered or named
   agent may be idle; a rule file may not be loaded into its active run; a
   prepared handoff may not be accepted; or the owner may receive only a
   compressed task title instead of the current governing brief.
7. **The system may optimize process without defining the artifact.** Roles,
   routing, investigations, repair records and status files can all exist
   while the exact desired output, positive examples, taste bar, acceptance
   test or accountable final integrator remains unclear.
8. **Negative controls do not specify the wanted result.** Marking an image
   `REPLACE`, `REJECT` or “not in use” can stop one wrong input, but it does not
   generate or admit the right replacement or prove the whole experience.
9. **Activity can be mistaken for outcome evidence.** An agent saying it
   worked, writing a plan, producing a local file or passing a process
   validator does not prove that Ali receives the result she intended.

These are cause categories for owner research and accurate teaching, not final
technical claims. Current product behaviour and official documentation must be
checked before scripting or filming.

## Match mitigation to cause

The class should offer a small diagnostic ladder:

- put a durable preference in the product's actual standing-instruction
  control; put project-specific rules in the Project/source brief; put
  task-specific constraints in the current assignment;
- keep one clearly labelled current rule or brief and explicitly supersede
  stale versions;
- when a failure appears, identify the violated rule and redo only the failing
  part rather than collecting another apology;
- split substantial work into bounded stages and check each stage against the
  same rule;
- start a clean session with a concise handoff when the current conversation
  is mixed, anchored to the wrong premise or repeatedly fails repair; and
- convert exact, consequential constraints into a checklist, schema, template,
  validator or human review where possible. Do not rely on the model's
  self-report as the test;
- trace the complete governance path: current source of truth → live owner →
  exact brief received → artifact produced → independent outcome evidence;
- distinguish registered, handoff prepared, running, built and verified
  instead of treating the existence of an agent system as execution;
- pair every exclusion with a positive target and exact approved reference,
  then judge the complete artifact rather than the disposition log; and
- evaluate one representative artifact against concrete examples and
  non-compensable quality gates before adding more agents, rules or repair
  cycles.

No mitigation guarantees perfect compliance. The practical goal is to make the
instruction current and testable, reduce ambiguity, lower correction cost and
catch failures before they propagate.

## Concrete example requirement

Use low-risk, controlled examples that differ by one variable:

- **Context availability:** “Never use exclamation marks” appears only far
  back in a mixed conversation versus the same rule in the current brief.
- **Conflict/current authority:** an early “friendly and enthusiastic” request
  conflicts with a later approved “calm, no hype” rule; the repair names the
  later version as current rather than asking the model to “remember.”
- **Wrong scope:** a correction made in one chat is tested in a new chat and
  fails; the repair places the preference in the appropriate standing or
  Project instruction layer.
- **Hard constraint:** “Use only these three approved sources” is checked by a
  source ledger or validator after generation; the model saying it complied is
  not accepted as proof.
- **Governed agents:** several named agents, repair documents and `REJECT`
  image labels still yield a generic page because none of that defines and
  proves the wanted full-page experience. The controlled repair binds one live
  owner to the current positive brief and references, produces one exact
  desktop/mobile candidate and uses an independent outcome review. Adding
  another agent is not the changed variable.

At least one example should show that repeating the same correction can still
fail, followed by a visible change in setup and a separately verified rerun.
