## Ali's question and intended payoff

**Dear Miss Jeeves: How is AI contributing to scientific breakthroughs?**

The answer should connect several things that are normally presented as
unrelated headlines:

- AlphaGo's Move 37 and the danger of mistaking an unfamiliar correct approach
  for an error;
- AlphaFold and the acceleration of protein-structure prediction;
- recent AI-assisted progress on open mathematical problems;
- AI's ability to search, combine evidence or represent a problem differently
  from established human practice;
- the harder question of how people recognize and validate a genuinely new
  result; and
- what may distinguish today's systems from a more transformative form of AGI.

The intended reader change is not “AI is doing amazing science.” A reader
should understand **which part of discovery AI performed, what humans and
specialized verification systems still supplied, why novelty can resemble
error, and how a surprising result earns trust**.

## Ali's locked editorial and learning direction

LAiDIES should do a materially better job of making the scale and nature of
AI's potential for scientific advancement understandable: what it may allow
humanity to discover or accomplish that was previously unreachable. This is
not a “positive AI” position or a benefits-versus-risks scorecard. The
advancement should not be assigned a positive or negative moral label before
its evidence, consequences, risks, distribution and governance are examined.

Every treatment must therefore distinguish:

- what has been demonstrated from what merely enables later research;
- plausible potential from speculation or provider framing;
- the mechanism that produced the result and the validation still required;
- who gains capabilities or outcomes, who bears costs or harms, and how access
  is distributed;
- which risks can be tested or mitigated technically; and
- which governance choices belong to institutions, communities and society.

AI fundamentals are not background trivia. They give women the causal literacy
needed to interrogate claims, recognize trade-offs and participate meaningfully
in workplace and public decisions about how AI should be developed and used.

## Material refinement — paint the cross-domain future, not a trophy shelf

Ali wants the answer to make a larger future world imaginable across medicine,
biology, physics, mathematics, materials and engineering. A sequence of famous
model names is insufficient. The treatment must show **what part of scientific
work changes**, what that could allow people to attempt next, and what still has
to happen before a prediction or candidate becomes reliable knowledge or a
real-world outcome.

The organizing mechanism for owner review is the scientific discovery loop:

1. **Observe and represent:** extract patterns or useful representations from
   data too large, noisy or multidimensional for one person to inspect.
2. **Search and generate:** explore enormous spaces of proteins, molecules,
   materials, proofs, designs or experimental settings and propose candidates.
3. **Simulate and predict:** approximate expensive calculations or experiments
   so researchers can compare more possibilities before using scarce physical
   resources.
4. **Control and optimize:** respond to complex systems in real time or improve
   designs with many competing constraints.
5. **Experiment and learn:** connect candidate generation to laboratories,
   instruments or robots that test, measure failures and choose the next trial.
6. **Verify, explain and govern:** prove, reproduce, clinically test and
   independently evaluate results; decide which capabilities should be used,
   by whom and under what safeguards.

Every domain example should use the same four-part lens:

`what changed now → what new work that enables → what future becomes plausible
→ what evidence, safety or governance still stands between candidate and outcome`

### What AlphaFold really means for medicine

AlphaFold is not “AI cured disease” and it is not merely a faster picture of a
protein. Protein shape influences how a protein works and how it may interact
with other molecules. AlphaFold2 showed that a model could predict many protein
structures with accuracy competitive with experimental structures on the
CASP14 benchmark. AlphaFold3 extends prediction to interactions involving
proteins, DNA, RNA, small molecules, ions and modified residues.

The advancement is a changed research starting point: for many questions,
scientists can begin with a useful structural hypothesis instead of waiting for
one structure to be solved experimentally. That can help researchers choose
which disease mechanisms, mutations, molecular interactions or drug targets
are worth investigating first. It may reduce dead-end search and let more
teams ask structurally informed questions.

The plausible future is not one machine issuing medicines. It is a much tighter
cycle in which models suggest structures and interactions; researchers form
better hypotheses; laboratories test binding, mechanism and toxicity; failed
results improve the next candidates; and clinical trials establish whether a
treatment is safe and useful for people. Structure prediction does not prove
biological function, drug efficacy, safety, dosage or clinical benefit.

### Translate the horizon into one person's life

The cross-domain map remains incomplete until it answers “what could this mean
for me?” The answer must translate each material example through three seats a
woman may occupy simultaneously:

1. **As a person, patient, family member or customer:** What might she actually
   experience—an earlier warning, a new testable treatment option, a safer or
   cheaper material, a more resilient service, or no direct change yet? What
   does she still need a qualified professional or institution to verify?
2. **As a worker, expert, manager or founder:** Which part of her work could
   move from manual search to judgment, experimentation and oversight? What new
   skill or responsibility appears? Whose expertise becomes more valuable, and
   whose role, bargaining power or livelihood could be disrupted?
3. **As a citizen and community member:** What decision might be made in her
   name about research funding, public infrastructure, health data, safety,
   intellectual property, environmental cost, access or acceptable risk? What
   does she need to understand to participate rather than merely accept a
   technical claim?

Every treatment also asks **who does not receive the imagined advancement**.
A scientific capability can exist while a particular person never benefits
because the treatment is unaffordable, the infrastructure is unavailable, her
population was absent from the data, deployment is unsafe or a company retains
exclusive access. Individual relevance therefore includes both possibility and
distribution.

Examples for the eventual producer to make concrete, not promise:

- AlphaFold could mean that a researcher investigating her rare condition has
  a useful structural hypothesis sooner; it does not mean she receives a
  diagnosis or therapy sooner, and it does not remove clinical validation.
- Faster antibiotic search could enlarge the pipeline her doctors may someday
  draw from; an active laboratory compound is still many stages from a safe
  prescribed medicine.
- Better weather prediction could mean earlier, more local warning before a
  dangerous event; it still depends on reliable public communication,
  infrastructure and her ability to act on the warning.
- Better materials could eventually change the price, durability, safety or
  environmental footprint of batteries, buildings and devices she uses; a
  computational candidate is not a manufactured product or an equitable
  supply chain.
- AI-assisted engineering could remove repetitive search from her job and give
  her more design options, while increasing her responsibility to question the
  objective, inspect failure modes and refuse unsafe optimization.
- Mathematical advances may reach her indirectly through cryptography,
  logistics, computing and scientific tools, while raising direct questions
  about how humans verify systems whose reasoning exceeds one reviewer's
  capacity.

The producer's required individual translation is:

`scientific capability → possible lived change → person/worker/citizen choice
→ remaining evidence and professional check → access, harm and distribution`

## Ali's hypothesis — preserve and test, do not convert to doctrine

Ali's own working hypothesis is:

> Today's AI mostly answers questions inside fields and problem spaces humans
> have already defined. A truly transformative general intelligence might do
> more: originate important questions, representations or mathematical
> frameworks that grow the field itself rather than only contributing answers
> to existing fields.

Ali is explicitly unsure whether this is the right distinction and wants her
thinking tested. The eventual treatment must therefore present it as a
serious hypothesis, name supporting evidence, test counterexamples and avoid
using it as a settled definition of AGI.

## Preliminary validation ruling

**Substantially supported as a useful framework; not supported as a clean or
consensus AGI boundary.**

Why it is useful:

1. Independent mathematicians involved in First Proof describe research
   mathematics as asking questions, developing frameworks and solving. Their
   June 2026 assessment was that current AI had clearly demonstrated aptitude
   chiefly at the last job. That closely matches Ali's distinction.
2. Google DeepMind's own 2026 AlphaGo retrospective makes an analogous but
   explicitly company-framed claim: Move 37 was novel strategy inside an
   existing game; deeper original invention would include creating a game as
   worthy of study as Go.
3. Move 37 usefully shows that expert expectation is not a truth test. A result
   can be initially judged strange or wrong because it lies outside established
   human patterns.

Why the boundary is porous:

1. FunSearch produced previously unknown cap-set constructions and executable
   programs showing how they are built.
2. AlphaEvolve produced new algorithms and improved known mathematical bounds.
3. Co-Scientist explicitly generates, debates and refines hypotheses, with
   some hypotheses subsequently tested in laboratories.

Current systems have therefore begun parts of question formation, hypothesis
generation, representation change and new construction inside human-scoped
fields. A more defensible working distinction is the **breadth, independence,
reliability and completeness of the cycle**:

`choose a consequential problem → formulate a useful question → invent or
select a representation → produce candidates → verify → explain → survive
independent replication → transfer the method across domains`.

This is a proposed teaching model for owner review, not an approved AGI
definition.

## Claim corrections and resolved identification

- The protein system is **AlphaFold**, not “ProteinFold.” The 2024 Nobel Prize
  in Chemistry recognized Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for protein structure
  prediction using AlphaFold2; it does not mean AlphaFold independently
  completed the downstream biological experiments or drug-development cycle.
- The closest verified match to “about ten conjectures overnight for a few
  thousand dollars” is not exactly that statement. A May 2026 preprint reports
  that AlphaProof Nexus resolved **9 of 353 open Erdős problems**, proved **44
  of 492 OEIS conjectures**, and cost a few hundred dollars per attempted
  Erdős problem. Separately, First Proof tested **10** unpublished lemmas under
  a 24-hour limit; results were mixed, checking was expensive and some systems
  cost several thousand dollars. These must not be collapsed into one claim.
- The remembered points-and-lines example is the **Erdős planar unit-distance
  conjecture**, posed in 1946 and disproved by an internal OpenAI model in 2026.
  The problem asks how many pairs among `n` points in a two-dimensional plane
  can be exactly one unit apart. The result did not simply rotate the picture
  into ordinary 3D: it connected the geometric problem to algebraic number
  theory and constructed planar point sets through a high-dimensional
  Minkowski embedding. OpenAI describes the result as the first autonomous AI
  solution of a prominent open problem central to a mathematical subfield;
  that autonomy framing remains a provider claim. External mathematicians
  reviewed the proof and described it as a major result, while also explaining
  the continuing human work of improving, interpreting and exploring it.

## The verification mechanism the answer must teach

Move 37 transfers only with its limit intact. Go supplied fixed rules, a game
outcome and extensive later analysis. Science often lacks an immediate answer
key. A surprising candidate becomes knowledge through domain-appropriate
checks:

- **Mathematics:** translate the claim into precise definitions; use a formal
  proof assistant such as Lean where possible; check every dependency; seek a
  separate proof or counterexample; and obtain expert review. Machine-checked
  syntax proves only what was correctly formalized, not that the chosen theorem
  captures the intended claim or matters.
- **Biology/medicine:** distinguish predicted structure or hypothesis from
  measured mechanism and clinical benefit; reproduce computational results;
  run laboratory experiments; compare against controls; replicate
  independently; and retain uncertainty and safety gates.
- **Open scientific hypotheses:** predefine what observation could falsify the
  claim, check for leakage and already-known work, compare alternatives, and
  make data, methods and provenance inspectable.

As results exceed one person's review capacity, verification must become more
formal, distributed and reproducible—not disappear. Independent proof checkers,
formal systems, adversarial search, multiple teams, shared datasets,
replication and correction trails become part of the scientific instrument.
