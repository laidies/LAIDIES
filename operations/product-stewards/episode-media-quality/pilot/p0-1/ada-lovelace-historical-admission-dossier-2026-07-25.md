### Interpretation, not evidence

“Ada sees music in a machine” is a LAiDIES teaching interpretation of her
writing about non-numeric quantities. It must be presented through narration or
visual metaphor clearly separated from documentary fact. The enduring lesson is
the transfer from arithmetic to symbol manipulation—not magic, a sentient
machine, or an established 1843 device making music.

## Allowed visual claims (all still require independent sign-off)

- A dignified period woman **interpreted as Ada**, with a provenance caption or
  surrounding editorial context that does not pass the invented face off as an
  exact portrait.
- A mid-19th-century **generic study or writing setting** labelled as an
  interpretation, not “Ada’s documented 1843 room.”
- A manuscript / table / diagram that teaches “Note G: a procedure for
  Bernoulli numbers,” if the exact math/text is independently checked.
- A plan, partial mechanical assembly, cards, or a clearly diagrammatic
  *proposed Analytical Engine*, always visually/narratively marked unbuilt or
  hypothetical.
- A distinction between `a method/instructions` and `a complete working
  historic machine`, which reinforces the actual lesson.

## Not evidenced / prohibited until a stronger source is bound

- Ada’s exact 1843 clothing, hair, face angle, working location, room, desk,
  window/view, books, companions, or time of day.
- A completed, functioning, full-scale Analytical Engine in 1843, or an Engine
  built/operated by Ada and Babbage.
- A claim that the 1836 NPG portrait depicts Ada at work or represents 1843.
- A literal mechanism producing music/oversized glowing music notes as a
  historical occurrence.
- Modern-computer-looking punched cards/interfaces, invented labels, or a
  specific historical machinery configuration not bound to an authoritative
  drawing/model reference.
- Reuse, tracing, recolouring, style transfer, or visual imitation of NPG L274
  without documented rights clearance. The NPG rights page requires contact
  with rights holders; the source is research evidence, not an art asset.

## Language guard

Use: “Lovelace’s 1843 notes include a procedure for Bernoulli numbers,
intended for Babbage’s unbuilt Analytical Engine.”

Avoid: “Ada programmed the working machine,” “the first programmer” as an
unqualified fact, “the first algorithm” without the intended-machine context,
or “she invented a computer.” The episode canon’s narrower claim—published
algorithm / general-purpose insight, with the first-programmer label
contested—is the governing teaching guard.
