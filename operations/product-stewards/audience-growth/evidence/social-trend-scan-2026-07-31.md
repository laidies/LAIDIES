## First LAiDIES applications

These are hypotheses for production, not approved posts.

1. **Original recurring series over disconnected announcements.** Build a
   recognizable visual and editorial family for each runway—one system across
   covers, motion, captions and Stories—rather than combining unrelated
   episode art with a generic social card.
2. **A strong first-frame promise.** Use one legible hook that creates
   recognition, surprise or utility, then reveal the evidence or practical
   payoff. The first frame must make sense without the caption.
3. **Send/save jobs.** Turn current AI news into “what this means in real
   life,” practical repair cards, sourced comparisons and small demonstrations
   that are useful enough to send to one person.
4. **Conversation with substance.** Use founder questions, “why/why not?”
   prompts, town choices and response posts only when the answer will shape a
   lesson, feature or follow-up—not as empty engagement bait.
5. **Different native treatments from one source.** Instagram can use the
   story, character, motion and reveal. LinkedIn should use the professional
   implication, Ali's point of view, a useful explanation and an actionable
   takeaway.
6. **Measure the right behaviour.** Test hooks and treatments against watch,
   saves, sends, replies, qualified comments, profile actions and follows per
   reach. Do not declare a winner from likes or impressions alone.

## Full-package application

The social owner produces the media, cover, caption, alt text, channel-native
variation, first comment, supporting Stories, reply materials, timing,
moderation notes, measurement receipt and correction path. Ali receives the
finished approval package; she is not the production dependency.

The common immediate-first-comment practice is admitted only as a test. No
official platform source reviewed here says that the act itself creates a
ranking boost. A LAiDIES first comment therefore needs a useful job—such as an
exact source, a short piece of context that would overload the caption, or one
substantive question—and matched results must decide whether to keep it.

## Learning decision from the rejected Founding Mothers teaser

The exact `founding-mothers-reopening-teaser-v1.png` visual is **REJECTED**.
It placed authoritative episode title art above a generic gradient campaign
card, producing two competing visual systems. The result read as disconnected
and chaotic rather than recognizable, sophisticated or campaign-coherent.

It is retained only as rejection evidence. It is not approved for publication,
not a reusable template and must not be fed back into the runway. The caption
and source proposition remain separate working material; the visual must be
redesigned later as one complete concept.
