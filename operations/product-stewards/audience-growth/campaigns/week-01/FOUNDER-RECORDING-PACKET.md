## Script A · Better brief

**Board unit:** `W01-D1-01`  
**Target:** 35–45 seconds  
**Primary channel:** Ali’s LinkedIn  
**Secondary:** Instagram Reel

### Teleprompter copy

The Spice Girls did not say, “tell me what you vaguely want.”

And yet that is how most of us prompt AI when we are rushing.

“Make this better.” “Write the email.” “Summarize the policy.”

The tool has never met your director, your project or your office politics. It
has to guess.

Give it the audience, the context, the tone, the constraints and what good
looks like.

Prompting is not coding. It is delegation.

### Edit map

1. Cold open on the first line; no animated logo before value.
2. Cut or punch in on each vague prompt.
3. On-screen list: `AUDIENCE · CONTEXT · TONE · CONSTRAINTS · GOOD`.
4. End card: `Prompting is delegation. · LAiDIES`.

### Caption candidate

If the output sounds generic, inspect the brief before blaming the tool.
Specificity cannot guarantee truth, but it gives the draft a real job.

## Script B · Ten useful minutes

**Board unit:** `W01-D3-01`  
**Target:** 35–50 seconds  
**Primary channel:** Ali’s LinkedIn  
**Secondary:** Instagram Reel

### Teleprompter copy

You do not need a 40-hour AI course before you open the tab.

Most women I know do not have a mythical free weekend waiting to become
“technical.”

Start with one low-risk task and ten useful minutes.

Ask AI to explain a public concept, reorganize a safe draft or compare two
public options. Then review what helped, what was generic and what you would
never send.

One small try is not everything. It is a real beginning.

### Edit map

1. Cold open on “You do not need a 40-hour AI course.”
2. Keep the “mythical free weekend” line in one uninterrupted take.
3. On-screen list: `EXPLAIN · REORGANIZE · COMPARE`.
4. End card: `One low-risk task. Keep your judgment. · LAiDIES`.

### Caption candidate

Start small enough to do and useful enough to learn from. Check your workplace
policy and keep private work out of the experiment.
