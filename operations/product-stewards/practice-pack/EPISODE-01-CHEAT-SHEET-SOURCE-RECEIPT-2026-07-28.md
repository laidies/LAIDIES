# Episode 01 Cheat Sheet — claim-level source receipt

**Accessed:** 2026-07-28  
**Surface:** Episode 01 one-page Cheat Sheet local review candidate

## HBS Working Knowledge

- Original item:
  `https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/women-are-avoiding-using-artificial-intelligence-can-that-hurt-their-careers`
- Published: 2025-02-20.
- Establishes: researchers reviewed 18 studies involving more than 140,000
  college students and workers; the reported average women’s GenAI adoption
  rate was 25% lower than men’s.
- Limits: aggregated studies, populations and settings; not a current global
  population rate and not a performance or ability finding.
- Freshness trigger: revised paper, correction, later meta-analysis or a change
  to the episode’s dated evidence framing.

## Federal Reserve Bank of New York

- Original item:
  `https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2024/10/exposure-to-generative-ai-and-expectations-about-inequality/`
- Survey: February 2024 Survey of Consumer Expectations.
- Published: 2024-10-02.
- Establishes: a representative sample of US residents reported a meaningful
  gender gap in prior-year GenAI use; the associated HBS summary describes
  about one-half of men and one-third of women.
- Limits: US residents, self-reported prior-year use and a February 2024
  fielding date; not a current global adoption rate.
- Freshness trigger: a newer comparable New York Fed survey or a correction.

## Boston Consulting Group

- Original item:
  `https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/women-leaders-in-tech-are-paving-the-way-in-genai`
- Published: 2024-05-14.
- Establishes: a survey of more than 6,500 women and men at technology
  companies in five countries; senior women in technical functions led their
  men peers in GenAI adoption by an average of 14 percentage points.
- Limits: technology-company employees, selected countries, functions and
  seniority groups; adoption, not performance.
- Freshness trigger: a newer comparable BCG survey or methodological update.

## Treatment

The Cheat Sheet prints the two findings that carry the episode's argument
without turning the page into a statistics inventory: the HBS 25% average
adoption gap and BCG's +14-point senior-women result. The footer preserves the
HBS / New York Fed / BCG evidence chain, and the displayed copy labels the
numbers as scoped adoption findings rather than ability or performance. The
New York Fed result remains resolved source context but its 1/2-versus-1/3
figure is not repeated on the final sheet. The unresolved 78-per-100,
23/27/32 and Fei-Fei Li claims remain excluded.
