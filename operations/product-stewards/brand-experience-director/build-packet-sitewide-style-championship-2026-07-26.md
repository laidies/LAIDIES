## One variable and fixed invariants

### Variable allowed to change

Only the visual system may change:

- rendering language;
- line, plane, edge and texture construction;
- palette distribution within locked brand/capability constraints;
- art-to-UI boundary;
- motion grammar; and
- the transition grammar between site and episode media.

### Product and geometry invariants

Every candidate uses one shared semantic page and the same measured layout
tokens. It must preserve:

- identical viewport sizes and crop boxes;
- identical room perspective, architecture, shelf count and desk location;
- identical Miss Jeeves identity, pose, scale and placement;
- identical book count, order, labels, statuses and hit areas;
- identical masthead, promise, capability explanation and cross-links;
- identical Miss Jeeves query/result state;
- identical available, held and Puffy-saved states;
- identical reader body, reading width, hierarchy and continuation;
- identical DOM order, accessible names, landmarks and keyboard order;
- identical breakpoints, spacing scale and control dimensions;
- identical content and approved source/reference set; and
- identical motion duration budget and reduced-motion outcome.

Any candidate that changes an invariant is quarantined before scoring.

## Deterministic UI and typography contract

- All names, navigation, headings, labels, statuses, book titles, buttons,
  captions, dialogue, tooltips and results are live HTML or deterministic SVG
  with approved text.
- Environment plates contain no generated words, numbers, logos, UI panels,
  signage, book titles or data-like marks.
- Textless books use stable overlay zones for real cover labels and statuses.
- The exact same font files, weights, optical sizes, line heights and letter
  spacing are used in A/B/C. Display treatment may change only where the
  rule sheet identifies a rendering-system token, not by changing the words or
  information hierarchy.
- Body and long-form reading typography remains calm, editorial and common to
  all three systems.
- Focus, hover, selected, held, saved, error and disabled states are CSS/SVG,
  not baked into raster art.
- Art can suggest an affordance, but semantics and status never depend on
  colour, texture, lighting or illustration alone.

## Direction rule sheets

### A — one adult comic/graphic-novel world

**System promise:** the website and episodes are visibly one authored world.

| Surface | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Architecture and rooms | Decisive variable-width ink; faceted hard-edged light/shadow; adult graphic-novel depth; restrained print texture; no painterly blur |
| Characters | Episode master people-rendering language and character identity references; site and episode versions must remain recognizably identical |
| Operated objects | Same world rendering, with stronger silhouette, local contrast, live focus/status treatment and quieter surrounding detail |
| Content illustrations | Comic editorial diagrams/panels with deterministic labels |
| Reading surfaces | Calm cream/light editorial plane; minimal halftone; deep-plum ink; no panel clutter behind prose |
| Episode transition | Panel edge, gutter or page-turn carries into the room; no style swap |
| Motion | Ink reveal, panel slide, page turn, caption stamp and object-state snap; motion must explain action |

**Diagnostic minimum:** at full size, architecture, Miss Jeeves and every
operated object must show the same line/plane construction as the approved
episode environment and people references. A thin outline filter over a
painterly render fails.

**Likely advantages:** strongest cross-format continuity; simplest style rule;
clear authored identity.

**Likely costs/risks:** largest initial site-art replacement; high identity and
anatomy QA burden; excessive panel energy can harm quiet tasks; every building
may begin to feel like the same issue unless architecture and accent systems
carry real local identity.

### B — deliberate painterly town/buildings plus comic episodes

**System promise:** the visitor inhabits a dimensional storybook town and
opens comic episodes as a deliberately different published medium.

| Surface | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Architecture and rooms | Painterly dimensional depth; authored brush planes and material variation; sunny, specific place-making; no faux-comic outline |
| Site characters | Painterly dimensional site register bound to the same identity reference; not photoreal, beauty-ad or generic architectural-render people |
| Operated objects | Painterly objects supported by deterministic UI/status rings, labels and focus—not comic ink pasted onto them |
| Content illustrations | Site editorial illustrations follow the painterly register unless visibly framed as an episode/comic artifact |
| Reading surfaces | Shared calm editorial system; typography and evidence rules match A/C |
| Episode transition | A visibly framed issue, screen, page or cinema threshold moves into the locked comic medium |
| Motion | Environmental light/material movement and physical object transitions; episode threshold uses a deliberate page/panel change |

**Diagnostic minimum:** room and site character must have visible painterly
edge/material construction with no episode-style inking, while the transition
storyboard must make the switch to comic media read as intentional within one
brand.

**Likely advantages:** preserves atmospheric town value and much of the current
art estate; quieter room experiences; clear medium distinction.

**Likely costs/risks:** duplicate character-production systems; the website can
still look unrelated to episodes; painterly assets are prone to generic AI
architectural rendering; transition devices can become decorative excuses
rather than a coherent bridge.

### C — controlled functional hybrid

**System promise:** atmosphere establishes place; graphic explicitness signals
what the visitor can operate, learn from or receive.

| Surface | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Architecture and rooms | Painterly/faceted atmospheric depth with subdued passive edges and controlled material texture |
| Characters | Adult comic/graphic-novel identity register; shared light direction and palette bind them to the room |
| Operated objects | Decisive ink edge, faceted planes and slightly higher local contrast; the graphic treatment begins at the true hit-area/object boundary |
| Content illustrations/results | Comic/editorial explicitness; deterministic labels; high-energy results may use panels, bursts or halftone within bounded zones |
| Reading surfaces | Calm shared editorial plane; illustrative energy ends before sustained prose |
| Episode transition | Operated object or content surface activates from atmospheric room state into the full episode comic system |
| Motion | Ink edge/halftone resolves on focus or activation; no constant shimmer; reduced motion changes state without the reveal |

**Diagnostic minimum:** a masked comparison must show passive architecture
remaining atmospheric while the exact same operated books, Miss Jeeves,
result and continuation surfaces are unmistakably more graphic. Shared
palette, light direction, edge colour and contact shadow must prevent a pasted
sticker effect.

**Likely advantages:** strongest functional signaling; preserves atmospheric
place-making; creates a principled bridge to episodes and high-energy learning
states.

**Likely costs/risks:** highest governance and compositing burden; ambiguous
objects can expose inconsistent rules; a weak pass looks like mixed-source AI
art; responsive crops can sever the boundary between passive and operable
areas.

## Maker/judge separation

- The Image Production Director and frontend integrator are makers.
- The Brand & Experience Director owns the brief and final recommendation but
  does not self-score the candidate art.
- At least one independent judge covers each of:
  product/capability fidelity, visual/brand coherence, UX/accessibility,
  image/canon quality, media/transition continuity and implementation/
  maintainability.
- The red-team reviewer must read this packet, D-050, D-058, BTB-130, BTB-139,
  the Library brief, artwork system and episode visual/media records.
- Candidate labels are randomized for the first scoring pass. Direction names
  and cost estimates are revealed only after visual/product scoring so the
  incumbent or presumed recommendation cannot bias the first verdict.
- A maker may repair a failed candidate once against exact findings. The same
  judge rechecks the exact new artifact; a new file does not inherit the old
  score.

## Admission gates

### Hard preflight gates

A candidate is not scored if any of these fails:

- invariant manifest and geometry overlay;
- generated-text/pseudo-data inspection;
- complete desktop/mobile/reflow/failure/transition evidence;
- exact reference and asset provenance;
- functional state parity and truthful Library capability coverage;
- no overflow, concealed control or art-dependent status;
- image/canon exact-use preflight; or
- candidate manifest binds every screenshot to source/artifact hashes.

### Independent scorecard

Each category is scored out of 20 with written evidence:

| Category | Floor | What the judge must establish |
|---|---:|---|
| Product/capability fidelity | 17 | Same Library promise, geometry, states and continuation survive |
| Accuracy, safety and trust | 17 | No generated UI/data/text, false status, identity drift or misleading capability |
| Positive LAiDIES brand contribution | 17 | Distinctive, adult, warm, useful and coherent with the Rewind Era |
| Visual-system coherence | 17 | The direction is diagnostically visible and governable across all named surfaces |
| UX and accessibility | 17 | Operability, hierarchy, reading, responsive and fallback behavior pass |
| Maintainability and migration | 15 | Rules are transferable and cost/risk is explicit rather than hidden |

No averaging rescues a failed floor. A hard-gate failure or a score below a
floor produces `NOT ADMITTED`.

### Cross-candidate fairness gate

admitted as honest implementations of their direction. If one cannot clear its
own floor after one bounded repair:
