# LAiDIES site and episode visual-continuity audit

Date: 2026-07-23

## Scope

Visual review of the current homepage, its main content system, the Episode 04 page, the locked Heroine episode-style reference, and the Visitor Centre building page. This is a screenshot-based design audit; no production files were changed.

## Verdict

Yes: the current site imagery feels materially disconnected from the episode world.

The problem is not simply that painterly artwork is wrong. The site currently combines four different visual registers without an intentional hierarchy:

1. Cinematic or painterly town renders.
2. Stained-glass or fine-art portraits.
3. Crisp, dimensional graphic-novel episode artwork.
4. Clean, flat editorial interface design.

The locked Heroine image is the strongest and most ownable visual language. It should become the master style for characters, environments, buildings, episode covers, and major homepage storytelling. Painterly and stained-glass work can remain only as clearly framed in-world artifacts, such as a postcard, gallery piece, tourist poster, or shrine window.

## Audit steps

### 1. Homepage hero — needs redesign

Evidence: `01-homepage-viewport.png`

The dark, cinematic nighttime town establishes a prestige-streaming or retro-noir tone. It conflicts with the intended bright, daytime, sunny, colourful LAiDIES world and does not visually promise the graphic-novel episode experience.

### 2. Homepage content system — promising but inconsistent

Evidence: `04-homepage-method.png` and `06-homepage-later.png`

The light gradients, spacious layout, clear hierarchy, and large plum typography are a strong foundation. The imagery shifts between stained glass, cinematic landscape, and modern flat UI, so the page feels curated from different brands rather than authored as one world.

### 3. Episode art — strong and canonical

Evidence: `07-episode-four-page.png` and `08-locked-episode-style.png`

The locked Heroine image has the clearest identity: confident ink lines, detailed hair strokes, faceted highlights and shadows, restrained halftone and paper texture, and adult graphic-novel proportions. This should lead the site system.

### 4. Visitor Centre and building imagery — good structure, wrong rendering language

Evidence: `09-visitors-centre.png`

The page concept and sunny environment are directionally right. The image still reads as a polished AI architectural render, and the teal/magenta bands repeat the colour combination too heavily. Buildings should be redrawn as crisp, authored comic environments, each with a distinct lead accent.

## Recommended master visual system

### Canonical image tiers

1. **Character and episode heroes:** locked Heroine graphic-novel language.
2. **Town and building environments:** the same ink weight, faceted lighting, controlled halftone, and paper grain, with no character required.
3. **Graphic devices:** caption blocks, speech balloons, issue labels, starbursts, objects, sound effects, and repeat patterns.
4. **In-world artifacts:** painterly, photographic, or stained-glass images are allowed only when visibly framed as objects within the world.

### Colour

- Preserve the existing light background gradients.
- Use deep plum as the principal ink/UI colour.
- Use bright 1990s accents with one lead colour per section or building.
- Avoid defaulting every building to teal plus magenta.
- Avoid electric hot-pink-on-black as a global identity.
- Keep halftone and high-energy colour away from body-copy regions.

### Typography

- Keep the clean UI typeface for navigation and body copy.
- Reserve comic display lettering for the LAiDIES title, issue numbers, transitions, section labels, and occasional word art.
- Create contrast through scale, outline, shadow, and halftone rather than using many unrelated fonts.

### Motion

- Prefer panel slides, page turns, ink reveals, halftone wipes, caption stamps, and lettering that switches or lights on.
- Avoid generic floating, slow parallax, and repeated fades as the principal motion language.
- Provide reduced-motion alternatives.

## Homepage direction

### 1. Hero: a bright LAiDIES cover splash

A sunny daytime view of Sunnyvaile, rendered in the locked graphic-novel language. Use a clear focal point, strong sky, graphic shadows, and restrained 1990s colour. Place the LAiDIES title and core promise in a cream comic-caption block. The first viewport should feel like opening the cover of the same world viewers encounter in the episodes.

### 2. This week in LAiDIES

Use an approved episode image as a genuine issue-cover or wide comic panel. Include episode number, title, runtime, and one direct watch action.

### 3. How LAiDIES works

Turn the current method section into three or four clean sequential panels: plot, analogy, practice, and soundtrack. Keep ample whitespace and avoid novelty-comic clutter.

### 4. Explore Sunnyvaile

Present the town as a clean illustrated map spread. Each building receives a full-colour inked storefront or interior panel and one distinctive accent colour. The buildings should feel related without being colour clones.

### 5. Women built this

Use a gallery or archive-wall composition with portraits in the same dimensional graphic-novel language. Stained glass can appear only as an intentional artifact inside one specific location.

### 6. News, social, and trailers

Use a controlled newsstand or strip format with sticker-like labels, issue dates, and compact comic devices. Keep the surface bright and legible.

## Highest-impact next move

Stop producing additional painterly hero and building images. First create and approve one bright daytime Sunnyvaile environment in the locked Heroine rendering language. That image becomes the environmental style lock for the homepage and every building. Then prototype the homepage with that system before replacing imagery at scale.

## Evidence limits

This audit is based on rendered screenshots and visual comparison. It does not establish keyboard behaviour, focus order, screen-reader semantics, responsive behaviour at every breakpoint, or WCAG contrast compliance. Those require code and interaction testing.
