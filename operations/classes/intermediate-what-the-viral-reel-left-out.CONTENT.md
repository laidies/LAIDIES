# SUNNYVAiLE High lab — What the Viral Reel Left Out

**Status:** BUILT LOCALLY — instructional content candidate; not independently
accepted, filmed, admitted, routed or public.

**Teaching line:** The viral tip is not the workflow.

## Learner and objective

**Learner:** A woman who has seen an AI demonstration she would genuinely like
to use, but does not yet know what the demonstration assumed or whether the
setup will work for her job.

**Level:** Mixed-entry intermediate. A newer learner can complete the guided
card; an experienced learner can use the same method to audit a more
consequential workflow.

**Observable objective:** Given one current AI claim and its primary evidence,
the learner can:

1. classify the claim as supported, true but incomplete, misleading or
   unsupported;
2. identify the missing prerequisite, setup, judgement, risk, test and upkeep;
3. design one bounded, realistic test using safe material;
4. name the human decision and evidence required before use; and
5. choose build, revise, investigate, decline or monitor.

**Take-home artifact:** One completed **Missing Middle workflow card** that can
be used to set up the workflow or explain exactly why it should not proceed.

## Correct mental model

A feature is a capability. A workflow is the complete path from an intended
outcome through inputs, configuration, actions, judgement, testing and
maintenance.

Seeing the feature produce one polished result does not establish that the
workflow is ready for another person's data, account, job or consequence.

This is an expectation gap, not a character test. The useful question is not
“Why couldn't I make it work?” It is “Which part of the operating setup was not
shown?”

## The Missing Middle Check

| Pass | Question | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | What exact result is promised, for whom and under what conditions? | One bounded outcome sentence |
| Capability | Which actual feature or behavior could produce it? | Current primary product source |
| Prerequisites | Which account, plan, files, data, access and permissions are required? | Dated access and dependency list |
| Setup | What context, instructions, connections and customization must be supplied? | Reproducible setup steps |
| Judgement | Which decision, approval or correction still belongs to a person? | Named owner and checkpoint |
| Boundaries | What privacy, accuracy, safety or failure condition changes the decision? | Stop conditions and prohibited inputs |
| Test | What small test would prove the setup works for this job? | Normal, edge and failure examples plus acceptance criteria |
| Upkeep | What must be checked when the product, data or job changes? | Review owner, trigger and rollback path |

## Instructional sequence

### 1. Watch for the jump cut

Start from a short claim that moves directly from “turn this on” to a polished
outcome. Write the promised outcome without repeating promotional adjectives.

### 2. Separate capability from workflow

Confirm what the feature actually does from a current primary source. Then
list every condition the demonstration supplied, assumed or hid.

### 3. Build the missing middle

Complete the eight-pass card. Do not accept “human in the loop” as an answer;
name the exact decision and the person who remains responsible for it.

### 4. Test a small version

Use fictional or otherwise authorized material. Test one normal case, one edge
case and one case that should stop. Decide what would count as success before
running the test.

### 5. Make an evidence-based decision

Choose one:

- **Build** — the setup and boundaries are supported and the test passed.
- **Revise** — the capability is real, but the setup or test needs work.
- **Investigate** — evidence is missing or conflicting.
- **Decline** — the claim is unsupported, the risk is inappropriate or the
  required control is unavailable.
- **Monitor** — it is not useful now, but a named product or evidence change
  could alter the decision.

## Controlled practice

Use the admitted reusable-skills discovery lead as the first case:

> Install a reusable AI skill and it can handle a department's work.

The learner should reach **misleading as stated**. A skill can package a
repeatable procedure, but it is not accountable staff. The full setup still
needs a bounded task, trusted files, minimal permissions, behavior tests,
review and maintenance. The specialized follow-on destination is **Build the
Skill. Keep the Job.**

Use the admitted edit-to-instruction discovery lead as the transfer case:

> Compare an AI draft with the final version and write every improvement back
> into the permanent instructions.

The learner should reach **true but incomplete** for the feedback-loop idea and
reject automatic persistence. She must distinguish a factual correction, a
one-off choice and a recurring pattern; only a recurring candidate proceeds to
multi-example testing, versioning and rollback.

## Before and after demonstration

**Same claim, same product, same low-risk task. One changed variable:** define
the operating setup and acceptance evidence before relying on the result.

- **Before:** enable the capability, use one favorable example and infer that
  the workflow works.
- **Why:** one polished output does not reveal prerequisites, boundaries or
  failure behavior.
- **After:** complete the Missing Middle card and run normal, edge and stop
  cases against declared acceptance criteria.

The demonstration must show the result honestly. If the after case still
fails, the learner records **revise**, **investigate** or **decline** rather
than tidying the output for camera.

## Work-life transfer cases

These cases turn the method back toward real work. They are not instructions
to treat people like models. The AI mirror makes the hidden workflow visible;
the learner still owns the conversation, judgement and consequence.

### Give the next move enough context

**Compressed claim:** “It already knows the project. Ask it to finish the
handoff.”

The learner should classify this as **true only under stated conditions**.
Useful project context must have been captured, retained, permitted and made
available in the current tool, account and conversation. A colleague’s memory,
a private inbox, an unshared file and a decision that was never written down do
not become available because the requester remembers them.

**Artifact:** a context handoff containing:

- the exact next job;
- the relevant background—not the whole archive;
- decisions already made and their authoritative records;
- open questions and named owners;
- information the tool is not permitted to receive; and
- the check that proves the recipient received the intended context.

The transfer is complete only when the learner can distinguish **I remember
it**, **we recorded it**, **this tool can access it** and **this is the current
source**.

### Do not answer the bad brief yet

**Compressed claim:** “Give the perfect response to this loaded question.”

The learner should classify the magic-comeback promise as **misleading**. A
tool can generate wording, but it cannot establish the other person’s motive,
choose the learner’s risk tolerance or know the organizational consequence.

Before drafting a response, complete an **approach brief**:

1. What outcome matters here?
2. Which assumption or frame should not be accepted without question?
3. What facts are known, missing or unsafe to paste?
4. What are three reasonable response approaches and their trade-offs?
5. Which decision remains human-owned?
6. What would make this a stop/escalate situation rather than a writing job?

**Artifact:** one short response the learner can stand behind, plus the reason
she selected it and the condition that would make her choose another route.

These cases originate in the governed Work-Life AI Mirror bank. They may not
become Daily cards until the lab, exact parent links and independent workplace
review pass.

## Misconceptions to resist

- If the feature exists, the workflow exists.
- If the demonstration worked once, my setup must be wrong.
- “Human in the loop” is a sufficient control without naming the decision.
- Customization means adding more personal data.
- A creator's successful result proves current plan access, product behavior
  or suitability for another use.
- Testing only the happy path is enough.
- Once configured, the workflow will stay correct without review.

## Analogy and limit

The reel is the movie trailer. It can show you that the premise is interesting;
it cannot contain the call sheet, permissions, continuity notes, safety plan,
editing decisions and quality check that produced the finished scene.

**Limit:** A software workflow is not a film production, and some simple
low-risk features genuinely require little setup. The analogy explains why a
short demonstration can omit operating conditions; it does not prove that
every claim is misleading.

## Current evidence and recheck

This lab is a framework candidate, not evidence for any product-specific
claim. Every worked case must be rebound to current primary/official sources
before script acceptance and again before filming.

Recheck when:

- a named product changes feature behavior, access, plans, permissions or
  interface paths;
- a parent claim enters correction, hold or retraction;
- a worked case is replaced;
- privacy, safety or professional-responsibility guidance materially changes;
  or
- learner testing shows the card does not produce a usable next decision.
