# PART 1 — THE CONCEPT

## The one idea
**The tool keeps a note about you. It is separate from your conversations, you can read it, and
you can change it.**

That is the whole class. Everything below serves that sentence.

## Why she should care
Without it she re-supplies who she is at the start of every task, and when she doesn't, she gets
the generic answer and assumes the tool is mediocre. With it, the same short request comes back
usable. **The difference she feels as "it finally gets me" is a screen she filled in once.**

## What she will be able to do afterwards
1. Find what the tool has stored about her.
2. Correct one entry that is wrong.
3. Delete one entry she doesn't want kept.
4. Know that deleting a conversation does **not** delete the note it produced.

## The one mistake this class exists to prevent
**She deletes the chat and assumes the record is gone.** It is not. The conversation and the
stored note live in two different places, and each has to be cleared on its own.
*(Documented — OpenAI's memory FAQ states the chats, archived chats, files and connected-app
content are separate sources that must each be deleted individually.)*

## The analogy — already LAiDIES canon, not newly invented
**The Blend & Snap.** A brand-new café cannot give you "the usual" — it has never met you, so
you spell out the order every single time. Your regular spot can, because you built that over a
hundred Tuesdays. This screen is how the new café becomes your regular.
*(Canon: `coffee-relationship-ai-memory-metaphor`. This class is the payoff it was saved for.)*

## The two nouns, used precisely throughout
- **the tool** — the thing you click. Memory belongs to the tool.
- **the model** — the part that produces the words. It does not remember you; it is *handed*
  what the tool stored.
⚠ This distinction is load-bearing here. If she leaves thinking the thing that writes is also
the thing that remembers her, the class has failed.

## Deliberately NOT in this class
- **How to write a better prompt** — that is Episode 2.
- **How to check whether an answer is true** — that is Episode 3.
- **What a model is, at length** — one sentence only; the concept class covers it.
- Every other tool's memory screen. This class is ChatGPT. Claude and Gemini get their own.

# PART 2 — THE NARRATION SCRIPT

> Format note: this is the spoken track plus what is on screen. Screenshots and capture come

## CHAPTER ONE — Why you care · 0:00–3:05

**0:00–0:08** · Lesson slide, held. No voiceover.
The overhead transparency: *ChatGPT · Class One · What It Has Stored About You.* Under TODAY YOU
WILL — find what it has kept about you · correct one line · delete one line · learn why deleting
the chat is not enough.

**0:08–0:24**
> Before we go anywhere near a settings menu, I want you to try something. Open whatever you
> use, start a new chat, and type this: what do you know about me. Four words. Then come back.

*On screen: a blank chat. The words typed slowly, in full view. Enter.*

**0:24–0:50**
> Some of what comes back, you told it. Some of it you did not. It worked that out from things
> you mentioned in passing, weeks ago, and it wrote them down.

*On screen: the answer fills. No narration over the reading — let her read it. Cursor traces two
or three lines.*

**0:50–1:04**
> That one. You never sat down and typed that.

*On screen: slow zoom on one line that is clearly inferred rather than stated.*

**1:04–1:40**
> Here is why that matters, and I want to show you rather than tell you. This is a second
> account. It has never met me. Watch what the same eight words get me.
>
> Write a note to my team about the delay.

*On screen: second window, clean account. The eight words typed. The answer streams.*

**1:40–2:10**
> Dear Team. I wanted to take a moment. There is nothing wrong with it. There is nothing of mine
> in it either. And the model is not being lazy — it was handed eight words and nothing else, so
> it filled every gap I left with whatever was most likely. A generic team. A generic delay. A
> generic voice.

*On screen: hold on the bland output. Do not cut away.*

**2:10–2:32**
> Now the same eight words, on the account that knows me.

*On screen: back to the first account. The identical eight words. The answer streams — short,
direct, specific.*

**2:32–3:05**
> Same eight words. Same tool. Same minute. The only thing that changed is that this one had
> something to go on — because the tool had kept a note, and handed it over. I did not write a
> better prompt. I filled in a screen, once, weeks ago.
>
> So let me show you that screen.

*On screen: the two answers side by side. Cursor traces the second.*

## CHAPTER TWO — Where it lives · 3:05–6:00

**3:05–3:30** ⚠ *path to be confirmed on screen before capture*
> Settings. Then Personalization. Then Memory. Say those three words to yourself, because every
> tool has a screen like this and they all bury it somewhere slightly different. That is the
> only genuinely hard part of any of this.

*On screen: each menu highlighted before it is clicked, held long enough to find on her own
screen. Breadcrumb builds across the bottom: Settings → Memory.*

**3:30–4:05**
> There it is. Everything it has decided is worth keeping about you. Read it. All of it. Most
> people never look at this once, and it has been filling up the whole time.

*On screen: the memory screen. Scroll slowly, top to bottom. No talking over it.*

**4:05–4:45** ⚠ *editing mechanism to be confirmed on screen*
> When something in here is wrong — and something will be — you do not argue with the model
> about it in a chat. That does nothing. You come to this screen and you fix the note itself.

*On screen: one entry read aloud, then corrected. Show the entry change.*

**4:45–5:15**
> And you can take things out. One line at a time. You do not have to wipe everything to remove
> the one thing you would rather it forgot.

*On screen: one entry deleted. Watch it go.*

**5:15–6:00**
> Two controls in here look similar and are not. One stops the tool writing anything new while
> keeping what it already has. The other wipes the lot, and that does not come back. People
> reach for the wipe when they wanted the pause — usually over one odd conversation. For that,
> just edit the single line, the way we did a minute ago.

*On screen: the two controls side by side. Hover each. Click neither.*

## CHAPTER THREE — The traps, and what to do · 6:00–8:00

**6:00–6:35**
> Now the part that catches people, and it is the reason for this class. Deleting the
> conversation does not delete this. They are two separate places. The chat is gone, and the
> note it made is still sitting right here.

*On screen: delete a whole conversation. Return to the memory screen. The entry is still there.
Hold on it.*

**6:35–7:05**
> One more thing worth knowing. This summary is not a complete list of everything held. The help
> page says so outright — some detail is deliberately left out of what it shows you. So read
> this screen. Just do not read it and conclude that is everything there is.

*On screen: the summary, scrolled.*

**7:05–7:30**
> And there is a temporary chat mode. In there this screen does not apply — it is not read from,
> and nothing is added to it. That is what you reach for when you want to ask something without
> it going on the record.

*On screen: open a temporary chat. Ask something. Return to memory — nothing new appeared.*

**7:30–7:50** · Recap
> So. Settings, Personalization, Memory. Read what is there. Type into the box to correct a line.
> Delete a line you do not want. And remember that clearing your chats does not clear this.

*On screen: the breadcrumb again, then each step as a still.*

**7:50–8:12**
> Think about the Blend & Snap. A new café cannot give you the usual — it has never met you. Your
> regular spot can, because you built that over a hundred Tuesdays. This screen is how the new
> café becomes your regular.

*On screen: the memory screen, settled.*

**8:12–8:25**
> So go and read yours. Today. It takes four minutes, and you will find something in there that
> is wrong.

*On screen: final card — Go and read yours.*
