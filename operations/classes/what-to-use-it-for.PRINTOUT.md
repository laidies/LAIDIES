# What to actually use it for
*A LAiDIES take-home. Real, researched uses of a chat AI (ChatGPT · Claude · Gemini) — the range most people never find because they only ever use it like Google. Companion to the "What these tools are for" class. Each line is a USE, not a script.*

---

## At work

**Get it written**
- Turn a messy brain-dump into a clear, sendable message — *five bullets → a polished email*
- Draft the message you're dreading — a no, a pushback, bad news — and get the tone right
- Recut the same message for a different audience — *your boss vs. your team*
- Have it read your draft as the recipient and flag what could be misread — *before you hit send*

**Make sense of the pile**
- Boil a long report or contract down to one plain-English page
- Turn a forty-message thread into what was decided and what's still open
- Turn scrappy meeting notes into a clean recap with action items
- See what actually changed between two versions of a document
- Pull scattered details from several documents into one clean table

**Think it through**
- Line your options up side by side against what actually matters to you
- Make your call — then have it argue the opposite and find the holes
- Build yourself a meeting briefing from your own notes and docs
- Rehearse a high-stakes conversation or Q&A — it plays the other side, then critiques how you did

**The tedious admin**
- Draft your self-review from a list of your wins
- Study for something by having it quiz you — hints, not the answers

**Check its work before it becomes yours.** At work, only feed it information you're allowed to share — client details and anything confidential, check your workplace's rules first — and verify its facts, figures, and quotes against the source before you pass them on. It's a sharp assistant, not a signed-off authority.

## At home

**Understand what you're handed**
- Decode a medical bill, insurance statement, or EOB — *what they actually charged, and what to ask*
- Read a lease or contract before you sign — *and flag the clauses worth questioning*
- Get a plain-English read of any dense letter or policy you have to act on

**Run the week**
- Cook dinner from what's already in the fridge
- Get a week of dinners plus a grocery list sorted by aisle
- Draft a day-by-day trip plan that isn't overpacked
- Hosting — work out how much food to make and when to start cooking

**Decide & sort**
- Turn a fuzzy product or plan choice into a clear pros-and-cons table
- Paste your recurring charges → a keep / cancel / investigate list
- Talk through keep-or-let-go and get a room-by-room declutter plan

**Family & the people you love**
- Turn homework help into tutoring — *hints, not answers*
- Explain a hard topic to a child at exactly their age
- Reach a relative across a language barrier
- Rehearse a hard conversation before you actually have it

**Say the thing**
- Start a heartfelt message when you know the feeling but not the words — *a toast, a thank-you, condolences*
- Get gift ideas that actually fit a specific person you describe

**Learn & prepare**
- Have it explain a hard topic at exactly your level, then go deeper as it clicks
- Walk into a doctor's visit prepared — understand the terms, bring the right questions *(to understand and prepare — never for a diagnosis)*

**Share what you're comfortable with.** Whatever you type goes to a company's computers, so use the same judgment you would on any website: skip passwords and full account numbers, and black out names and other people's details where it doesn't need them. No need to be nervous — just know you can check the tool's settings for how your chats are used, and turn off training on your data if you'd rather. *(What's kept and what you're granting is its own class.)*

## A few habits that make all of it work better
- **Give it the context.** Who it's for, what you need, the constraints. Vague in, average out.
- **Treat the first answer as a draft.** Tell it what to change — "shorter," "warmer," "now for my situation." Steering it is the point.
- **Paste in your own material** — the letter, the notes, the thread. It's far better working from your stuff than guessing at it.
- **For anything big, have it argue the other side** before you commit.
- **Ask it to show where something came from** so you can check it.

**One honest line:** it drafts, digests, explains, and organizes — often brilliantly. It is *not* the last word. It can be confidently wrong, so anything that matters — a number, a date, a name, a medical, legal, or money detail — you check against the real thing.
