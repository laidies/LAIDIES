**Target runtime 9:00.** Narrator: **The Heroine.** Kit: screen capture + voiceover + the
classroom title card. No characters, no drawn art, nothing to go off-model.

## ⚠ Read this before you hit record

**The closing beat asks the viewer to paste in a real prompt of theirs.** For the shoot, do not
paste an actual prompt of yours that names a real client, a real coworker, or real numbers —
write a stand-in that looks like something you'd really type (a real team size, a real kind of
delay) but isn't tied to an actual person or deal. The lesson works exactly as well on a
placeholder, and it keeps a real name or a real figure off the export.

## The one thing to go and do
**Take one message you were about to send today and add the three things it cannot guess** —
who it's for, what actually happened, how you talk. Send that version instead of the first draft.

## The one mistake, stated plainly
**Leaving the field open and blaming the answer for being generic.** It did not fail to write
something good — it correctly wrote the most likely thing for a request that could describe a
thousand situations. The fix is narrower input, not a different tool.

## Quotable pull (for the clip / social)
> "You did not get a bad answer. You got the average of every note like this ever written."

## Production notes
- **Let the bland output stream all the way in at 0:24.** Do not jump-cut past the boring parts —
  the boredom is the point of the beat.
- **The chair card at 0:50 is text only.** No drawn chair, no stock photo — an illustration would
  pull focus from the line and risks going off-model for no reason.
- **The gift-task beats (2:34–3:30) move faster than the email beats on purpose.** The mechanism
  is already taught; this section only has to prove it generalizes, not re-teach it.
- **The over-narrow beat at 3:30 needs the input and output visibly identical.** If the tool adds
  even a pleasantry ("Sure, here you go:") around the sentence, pick a different exact-repeat
  example before filming rather than narrating around the mismatch.
- **FAiRY Godmother section: use a stand-in prompt, not a real one** — see the warning at the top.
- Captions render **below** the picture, never over it.

## Re-shoot triggers
Any of these and this tape needs re-cutting — nothing else does:
- The FAiRY Godmother Prompt Glow-Up changes what it shows for "what was missing" (the panel
  referenced at 5:40).
- The exact-repeat prompt at 3:30 stops coming back as a clean echo on whatever tool is filmed.
