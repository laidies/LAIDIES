## 02 — The jump cut

[knowing, not smug]

The viral demonstration gives us a beginning and a polished ending. The middle
has vanished in the edit. That can prove a capability is interesting. It
cannot prove that the complete workflow is ready for your account, your data,
your job or your consequences.

## 03 — Capability and workflow

[plain]

A capability is one behaviour the product can perform. A workflow is the
complete path from an intended outcome through inputs, setup, action, human
judgement, testing and upkeep. The missing work is not evidence that you are
bad at AI. It is evidence that the demonstration did not show the operating
setup.

## 04 — The claim

[storytelling pace]

Our fictional team sees this claim: install one reusable AI Skill and it can
handle the department's work. The useful part is real. A Skill can package
instructions, examples, resources and code for a repeatable task. The leap to
accountable department work is misleading as stated.

## 05 — The source boundary

[precise, not promotional]

OpenAI describes Skills as reusable workflows that can contain instructions,
examples and code. It also says availability depends on the product and
account, workspace administrators control access, and uploaded Skills still
require your own review and judgement. That is our evidence boundary—not the
reel's confident caption.

## 06 — The missing middle

[let the checklist feel useful]

Before relying on the result, fill in the missing middle. Name the outcome and
actual capability. Check prerequisites and setup. Name the human judgement and
boundaries. Design a test. Decide who will maintain it. Eight checks turn a
dazzling demonstration into something you can evaluate.

## 07 — Prerequisites

[conversational]

Ask what must already be true. Which account and plan? Which files and data?
Which permissions? Who owns the task? For our fictional community-events team,
the Skill gets only approved event-copy examples and a small test folder—not
the shared drive and certainly not everybody's inbox.

## 08 — Setup and judgement

[firm but friendly]

Customization is not an invitation to paste more personal information. Supply
the smallest useful context, approved instructions and representative
examples. Then name the decision that remains human-owned. Our Skill may draft
event listings. A person still confirms the facts, tone, accessibility wording
and permission to publish.

## 09 — Boundaries and test

[give the three test cases distinct beats]

Do not test only the flattering example. Run one normal case, one edge case and
one case that should stop. Decide what success means before the test. A
complete public event brief should produce a usable draft. A missing
accessibility detail should trigger a question. A private attendee list should
never enter this workflow.

## 10 — Upkeep

[measured]

A working setup can go stale. Name an owner, a review trigger, a version and a
rollback. Recheck when the product changes, the task changes or the team finds
a new failure. Reusable does not mean permanently correct.

## 11 — Before and after

[emphasise the single changed variable]

The product and low-risk task stay the same. We change one variable: the
operating setup. Before, the team installs the Skill, tries one favorable
example and assumes the workflow works. After, they bound the job, limit the
inputs, run three tests, name the approval and schedule the next check.

## 12 — Decide

[clear choices, no sales pitch]

Choose the next move the evidence supports. Build when the setup is supported
and the test passes. Revise when the capability is useful but the workflow is
weak. Investigate missing evidence. Decline an unsupported or inappropriate
use. Monitor a named change that could make it useful later.

## 13 — Transfer

[practical]

The same method helps with ordinary work. It already knows the project may hide
four different claims: I remember it, we recorded it, this tool can access it,
and this is the current source. They are not interchangeable. A useful handoff
names the next job, current decisions, allowed context, open questions and the
check that proves the intended context arrived.

## 14 — Recap

[warm]

Do not throw away a useful idea because the demonstration oversold it. Check
the claim, build the missing middle, test a safe version and choose the next
move on evidence. The viral tip is not the workflow. It can still be the
beginning of one.

## 15 — Take-home

[inviting close]

Bring one current claim you would genuinely like to use. Complete the Missing
Middle card before you copy the workflow.
