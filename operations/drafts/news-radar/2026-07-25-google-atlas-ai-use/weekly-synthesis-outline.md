# THE WEEKLY — synthesis outline

## AI is not only doing tasks. It is moving the handoff line.

### Development one: parts of tasks

Google's ATLAS study finds sampled Gemini use concentrated in collaboration,
information retrieval, learning and ideation, with less than 10% of work
interactions classified as full task automation. This supports “AI inside the
workflow,” not “AI replaced the whole job.”

### Development two: tasks crossing roles

OpenAI's Work at the Frontier study finds many occupation-specific ChatGPT
messages concern work historically associated with another occupation. This
supports a second change: the person who encounters a task may attempt it with
AI instead of handing it to a specialist.

### The LAiDIES mental model

Think of a coffee shop. AI can help the barista make one step faster, but it
can also move a task across the counter: the person taking orders may now draft
the menu, inspect sales data or troubleshoot the website. That does not make
her a marketer, analyst or engineer. It changes what she can attempt before
calling one.

### The real takeaway

The near-term question is not only “Which jobs disappear?” It is “Which tasks
move, who is allowed to attempt them, and where must specialist judgment still
return?” The reports do not show completed work, productivity, quality,
employment effects or adequate review. Those limits are part of the story.

### Reporting still required

Independent labour-economist or methods review; comparison with non-vendor
behavioural evidence; examples of organizations changing review/accountability
rules; and a reader action box on testing one new task without mistaking an AI
draft for expert approval.
