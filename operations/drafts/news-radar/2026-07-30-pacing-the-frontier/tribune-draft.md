# The Tribune: A brake controlled only by the racers is not public safety

## Evidence

The Pacing the Frontier statement asks the U.S. government to support an
international effort to build tools for deliberately pacing automated
frontier-AI development. Senior people from competing labs signed, and OpenAI
and Anthropic publicly support the initiative. No binding company pact,
government decision or concrete mechanism exists.

## Inference

The industry's collective-action diagnosis is credible: a company that slows
alone can lose to one that does not. But the same companies also have
commercial power, technical information and incentives that the public cannot
independently inspect.

## Position

A workable brake cannot be a private dashboard whose definitions, evidence and
release switch belong only to the racers.

Labs should help design measurement and provide evidence, but public
institutions and genuinely independent experts must set the minimum disclosure,
verification and accountability rules. Any proposal should state who benefits,
who bears the delay, how international participation works and how advanced
open weights are treated after release.

The right question is not simply whether AI should slow down. It is who can
justify pulling the brake, who can verify the reason and who can release it
again.

This is a new editorial position and remains held for independent safety,
policy and editorial review.
