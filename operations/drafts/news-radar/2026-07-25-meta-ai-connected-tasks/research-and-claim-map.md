# Meta AI connected tasks — research and claim map

**Status:** PRIVATE

## Release details

- Company/product/model: Meta / Meta AI / Muse Spark 1.1.
- Feature: planning, recurring tasks, web research, slide creation, and
  connections to email and calendar apps; Reuters and Axios identify Gmail and
  Google Calendar.
- Access: rollout began July 24 in select markets in the Meta AI app and
  meta.ai; more countries and WhatsApp are promised later.
- Plans, exact markets, usage limits and separate feature pricing: not specified
  in Meta's announcement.
- Predecessor change: Muse Spark 1.1 existed before this launch; the material
  change is its deployment into connected, recurring and action-taking consumer
  workflows.
- Defaults/permissions: the announcement describes user setup and optional
  Incognito chats, but does not provide a complete field-by-field permission,
  retention, revocation or action-confirmation account.

## Confirmed

Meta says the updated assistant can connect to email and calendar apps, prepare
daily briefings, notice calendar conflicts, perform recurring tasks, research
across the web and create slides. It is rolling out in select markets.

## What this shows

Meta AI is moving from answer generation toward an assistant that retains an
instruction, consults connected context and returns later with work. That
changes both usefulness and the permission surface.

## What it does not show

- It does not establish reliability across high-stakes tasks or long periods.
- It does not show exactly which data fields are read, retained or used for
  product improvement in every market and account state.
- “Handle tasks on your behalf” does not prove unrestricted external action.
  The examples primarily describe planning, monitoring and content creation.
- Incognito chat does not by itself answer what happens to data pulled from
  connected services during ordinary use.

## Reader tests and unanswered questions

1. Which exact Gmail and Calendar permissions are requested?
2. Can users restrict the account, calendar, folder, label or date range?
3. Which actions require confirmation, and which can run unattended?
4. Where are recurring tasks listed, paused and deleted?
5. What data is retained after a connection or task is removed?
6. Can an output show which source event or message caused each conclusion?
7. How does it behave with sensitive mail, shared calendars and conflicting
   instructions?
