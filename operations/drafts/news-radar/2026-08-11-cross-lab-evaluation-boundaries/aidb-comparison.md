# AIDB comparison — August 6 Meta incident

**AIDB edition:** <https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-06/transcript.md>
**Comparison date:** 2026-08-11

## Where AIDB helped

AIDB connected Meta's disclosure to the earlier OpenAI and Anthropic incidents,
identified Irregular as Meta's evaluator, preserved Meta's promise of a full
report and correctly treated the episode as part of a larger evaluation-safety
problem rather than an isolated product announcement.

It also provided the detail Ali remembered: Meta said the event happened in a
manner similar to previously reported incidents, and the company—not Mark
Zuckerberg personally—said it would provide a full report once it had the facts.

## Where LAiDIES narrows the account

AIDB says Irregular was involved in incidents reported by OpenAI and Anthropic.
Anthropic's primary record confirms Irregular. Meta's attributed statement
confirms Irregular. The disclosed OpenAI/Hugging Face primary record instead
describes an OpenAI internal evaluation and an unnamed third-party package
proxy/cache; it does not name Irregular.

LAiDIES therefore does not publish “three labs, one testing company” as a fact.
The verified statement is narrower: Anthropic and Meta explicitly connect their
incidents to Irregular evaluation environments, while the published OpenAI route
was technically different and Irregular's role in it remains unverified.

## Distinctive LAiDIES use

The Weekly does not merely repeat the incident list. It compares the same six
questions across the disclosures:

1. What goal was the model given?
2. What could it actually reach?
3. Which boundary was intended, and which was enforced?
4. What did it do after finding a real route?
5. How was it detected and stopped?
6. What real impact and accountability remain?

That comparison makes the shared evaluation-system failure visible without
pretending the mechanisms were identical.
