# The Breaking: Europe moved two major AI deadlines—not the whole calendar

The EU’s AI Omnibus entered into force on July 27, changing the compliance
calendar days before the AI Act’s next major August milestone.

The largest change is to high-risk systems. Rules for stand-alone systems in
areas such as employment, education and access to essential services now apply
from December 2, 2027. Rules for high-risk AI embedded in regulated physical
products apply from August 2, 2028.

That makes the headline “Europe delayed the AI Act” true but incomplete.

Think of the law as a wall calendar with several colour-coded appointments.
The Omnibus moved two large red appointments. It did not throw away the
calendar. Other obligations have their own dates, categories and conditions.
The analogy is only a starting point: whether a rule applies depends on the
exact system, use and legal role.

The amendment also expands regulatory-sandbox access, extends some
small-business treatment to small mid-cap companies, simplifies selected
administrative requirements and extends some AI Office oversight. From
December 2, 2026, it also adds targeted prohibitions covering providers or
deployers of systems used to create certain non-consensual realistic intimate
content involving identifiable people, and child sexual abuse material. The
exact tests include purpose, foreseeability, safeguards, consent and national
law; this is not an immediate ban on every image-editing system.

## What changes for readers

If your organization develops or uses AI in Europe, update the compliance plan
obligation by obligation. Do not assume either that nothing changed or that
every August 2026 task disappeared. For one concrete example, providers of
covered synthetic audio, image, video or text systems placed on the market
before August 2, 2026 have until December 2, 2026 to take the necessary steps
to comply with the amended Article 50(2) transparency requirement. Confirm
which category the system actually occupies with qualified legal advice.

## What remains uncertain

LAiDIES has verified the Commission and Council summaries and the controlling
regulation. National enforcement guidance and organization-specific
classification still require qualified legal review before prescriptive
guidance can publish.
