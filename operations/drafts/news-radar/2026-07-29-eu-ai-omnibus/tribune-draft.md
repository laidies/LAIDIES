# The Tribune: A simpler AI law still has to protect people on time

## Evidence

The AI Omnibus delays major high-risk-system rules while expanding sandboxes,
extending proportional treatment to more companies and simplifying selected
administrative obligations. It also adds or clarifies protections, including
targeted Article 5 prohibitions beginning December 2, 2026 and broader
oversight in some areas. Those prohibitions are conditional, not an immediate
universal “nudification-system ban.”

## Inference

The amendment reflects a real tradeoff. Rules that arrive before standards and
guidance can produce costly confusion. Rules delayed too far leave people
exposed while high-risk systems spread into work, education and essential
services.

## Position

“Simpler” should be judged by whether an organization can understand and meet
the duty—not merely by whether the duty arrives later. The EU should use the
extra time to publish usable standards, operate sandboxes transparently and
show what enforcement readiness looks like. A delay without that
implementation evidence is not simplification; it is postponed uncertainty.

This argument remains held for legal and editorial review. It must include
named perspectives from regulators, affected organizations and rights
advocates before publication.
