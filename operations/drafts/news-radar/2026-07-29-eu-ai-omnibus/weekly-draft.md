# The Weekly: The AI Act has several clocks

The EU’s AI Omnibus is easy to misread because one large delay can sound like a
delay to the entire law.

The better mental model is a train station departure board. Different rules
leave from different platforms: prohibited practices, transparency,
general-purpose-model duties, stand-alone high-risk systems and AI embedded in
regulated products do not share one departure time. The Omnibus moved the
high-risk departures to December 2027 and August 2028. It did not cancel the
rest of the board.

This distinction matters for two opposite audiences. Companies should not
spend against a deadline that moved, but they also should not stand down from
obligations that did not. People protected by the law should not be told that
all safeguards disappeared merely because significant controls were delayed.

The amendment combines simplification with new or clarified protections:
expanded sandboxes and proportionality for smaller companies, less
administrative burden, targeted Article 5 prohibitions that apply from
December 2, 2026, and expanded AI Office oversight. The new prohibitions are
not an immediate universal ban on image editing; their scope turns on purpose,
foreseeable reproducible outcomes, safeguards, consent and other statutory
conditions.

What comes next is implementation: consolidated legal text, standards,
Commission guidance, national enforcement and evidence about whether
additional time improves compliance or merely postpones accountability.
