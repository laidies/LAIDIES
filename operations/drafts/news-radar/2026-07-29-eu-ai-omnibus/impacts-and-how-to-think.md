# EU AI Omnibus — impacts and how to think

**Status:** PRIVATE

## The mechanism in plain language

Think of the AI Act as a wall calendar with several colour-coded deadlines,
not one launch date. The Omnibus moved two large red appointments—the
high-risk-system compliance dates—but it did not erase every other appointment
on the calendar.

The analogy stops there: legal obligations depend on the exact system,
provider/deployer role, use, jurisdiction and article. A reader should use the
calendar to ask the right question, not to self-classify a product.

## Who is affected

- Providers and deployers of AI systems in the EU.
- Non-EU companies placing systems on the EU market or serving covered uses.
- Employers, schools, essential-service providers and public bodies whose
  systems may fall into high-risk categories.
- Makers of regulated products containing AI.
- Smaller and mid-cap companies eligible for proportionality or sandbox
  support.

## Reader decision

Update the compliance map by obligation and system category. Do not stop
current transparency, governance or documentation work because a headline says
“delay.” Obtain qualified legal advice for the actual product and use case.

## What to watch

The complete consolidated text, Commission FAQs, national-enforcer guidance,
standards and the practical operation of the EU-level sandbox.
