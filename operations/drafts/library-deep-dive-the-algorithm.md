## Start with the one thing a computer can't do

A computer cannot decide anything. Not one thing. It has no opinions, no taste, no hunches. The
only thing it can do — the only thing it has ever been able to do — is follow steps it was given,
exactly, very fast. That was Ada Lovelace's whole insight in 1843: it "only ever does what we know
how to order it to do."

## So everything it does is a list of steps

Which means every single thing your phone does is a written list of steps someone gave it.
Splitting the dinner bill: steps. Finding the fastest route home: steps (check every road, add up
the minutes, pick the smallest number). Autocorrect: steps. A list of steps like that has a name:
an **algorithm**. That's the entire word. Not intelligence — instructions.

## "THE algorithm" is just the famous one

The one everyone talks about — the thing deciding your feed — is one of these lists. What makes
it feel different is its goal. The company wrote its steps to do one job: **keep her here longer.**
So the steps are, roughly:

1. Watch what she pauses on.
2. Find more posts like that.
3. Show those first.
4. Watch what she does with THOSE.
5. Repeat, forever.

You paused two extra seconds on an engagement photo, and the list ran. That's why the wedding
content arrived before you'd told a soul.

## It's not reading your mind. It's reading your pauses.

That's the sentence to keep. The feed isn't psychic and it isn't listening through your
microphone — it doesn't need to. Your pauses, taps, and rewatches are the input; the steps do the
rest. And notice what the goal means: it isn't showing you what's *good*. It's showing you what
*keeps you*. Those are not the same list.

## Why it misfires

Because steps don't understand anything. The machine doesn't know that was your ex's engagement.
It doesn't know you already bought the boots. It doesn't know the topic hurts. The steps ran
perfectly; the steps just don't know things. Every algorithm misfire you've ever seen is this one
sentence: followed exactly, understood never.

## It was ours from the start

A knitting pattern is an algorithm — knit two, purl two, repeat to end of row; any knitter, any
decade, the same sweater out — and early computers literally took their punch cards from the looms
that wove fabric patterns. Ada saw it coming: her machine, she wrote, "weaves algebraical patterns
just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves." Women had been writing and following
algorithms for centuries. They just called them patterns.

## The takeaway

"The algorithm did it" really means: someone wrote steps with a goal, and your behaviour feeds
them. So the useful question is never "what does it know about me?" — it's "what am I feeding
it?" Pause on better things, get a better feed. The checklist can't tell the difference.
