## The motion grammar (this is what stops it reading like a slideshow)
1. **Every still moves.** No static frames, ever. A ~6-sec shot zooms ~8–12% — *slow*. Fast = cheesy.
2. **Alternate the move.** Never let two shots in a row do the same thing. Push-in → then a pan or a pull-out. That variation is 90% of what makes it feel like a show, not slides.
3. **Match the move to the beat:**
   - **PUSH IN** → tension / focus / a single word landing (cold open, "wall of text," a definition).
   - **PULL OUT** → reveal / relief / opening up (town vista, the good summary, the try-on).
   - **PAN L→R / R→L** → a place you're *moving through* (café, library, the bar).
   - **PARALLAX** → reserve for the ~3 hero shots only (opening vista, cold-open desk, the payoff).
4. **Text cards** (quotes / stats / the definition) = the beats with no photo. Give them a *slow drift*
   (tiny push or pan) so they match the motion of the photo shots. In CapCut/iMovie these are title
   cards on the plum background; the on-site cue file already has the exact wording.
5. **Speed = calm.** When in doubt, slower and smaller. The narration carries it.
