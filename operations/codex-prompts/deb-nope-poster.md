# Codex prompt — Mayor Deb "NOPE" campaign poster

**Canon:** Deb's official 2008-era campaign poster. Made by the intern (still the same intern from 1999) when everyone else in SUNNYVAiLE was putting up Obama HOPE posters. Deb saw it and said "sure, whatever." It has been her official poster ever since. She has not commissioned another. See [`deb-mayor-origin-story.md`](../../../.claude/projects/-Users-alisoneakin-Library-Mobile-Documents-com-apple-CloudDocs-LAIDIES/memory/deb-mayor-origin-story.md).

**Purpose:** Hangs framed in the Mayor's office (visible on `town-hall.html` down the line). Purchasable print at The Mall eventually. Iconic LAiDIES asset.

---

## Reference

Shepard Fairey's 2008 Obama "HOPE" poster — the iconic red / cream / navy stencil-portrait rendered in bold flat shapes, with the word beneath. That composition, that palette register, that stencil rendering. Pastiche, not copy.

## Subject

Deb — SUNNYVAiLE's mayor. Middle-aged white woman with an aggressively unbothered expression. Blonde bob that has not been restyled since the mid-90s. Reading glasses low on the nose OR pushed up on top of the head — pick whichever reads more Deb. Slight sneer / eyebrow raise. She is not looking at you. She is looking slightly off-camera at whoever just asked her to review their proposal.

Chest-up 3/4 view, same framing as the Obama poster.

## Word beneath

**NOPE** — same weight, same font register, same all-caps stencil authority as "HOPE" on the original. Not italic. Not softened. Flat and final.

## Palette (LAiDIES-brand-shifted)

Do NOT use the original Obama red/blue palette — recolor into SUNNYVAiLE tokens:

- **Shadows / darkest tone:** `--plum` (#4b2148) — deep plum instead of navy
- **Mid tone:** `--rose` (#9b3f5f) — dusty rose instead of red
- **Highlight tone:** `--cream` (#fffdfb) — off-white
- **Optional accent:** `--gold` (#c9a227) — sparingly, only if it adds punch (e.g., single star, sash trim)

Three-tone posterization. No gradients. Hard flat shapes.

## Byline strip

Below "NOPE," a thin banner in the same Fairey byline style. **Generate ONE render for each of the three canon bylines below:**

1. **`MAYOR DEB · SUNNYVAiLE · SINCE 1999`** — the civic edition
2. **`LOOP ME OUT`** — the saint edition
3. **`DEB-FLECTION™`** — the trademark edition (peak Deb; the ™ mark MUST render legibly at the end of the word)

Set in a bold condensed sans (Gotham/Futura register). Small caps or all-caps. Wide letterspacing. Match the byline style across all three so they read as a matched set of prints from the same print run.

## Style specs

- Vector-flat stencil illustration — Shepard Fairey / propaganda-poster idiom
- Portrait treatment: three-tone posterization only; no gradients, no photorealism, no soft edges
- Slight halftone dot texture on mid-tone shadow only (subtle, don't overdo)
- Background: solid `--cream` OR the classic pattern of thin rays/stripes radiating behind subject (choose the cleaner comp)
- Zero digital polish — this looks screen-printed, slightly imperfect, like the intern ran it off at Kinko's in 2008

## Output

- **3x portrait-orientation renders** — 2:3 ratio (poster proportions)
- One with byline `MAYOR DEB · SUNNYVAiLE · SINCE 1999`
- One with byline `LOOP ME OUT`
- One with byline `DEB-FLECTION™` (verify the ™ mark renders sharp and legible)
- Transparent OR cream background (specify per render)
- 2048px on long edge minimum
- Deb's portrait, palette, and framing must be **identical across all three** — only the byline strip changes. Matched-set energy.

## Do NOT

- Do NOT make her smile
- Do NOT make her look inspirational
- Do NOT use the real Obama poster colors (red/blue) — must be plum/rose/cream
- Do NOT add halos, stars-and-stripes, or explicit US political iconography — this is SUNNYVAiLE, not the United States
- Do NOT sharpen her face beyond stencil-level detail — she should look like she was traced from a bad ID photo by a bored intern

## Voice note

The joke lands because Deb didn't ask for it, didn't approve it, didn't reject it. She said "sure, whatever." The poster should feel like it was made with more enthusiasm than the subject deserves. Slightly too committed. Just a little too polished for the caption underneath.
