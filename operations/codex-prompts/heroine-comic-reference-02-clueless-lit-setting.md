**Why:** every clean heroine render on a plain white model-sheet went "plasticy" (smooth 3D sheen). Grace
holds the inked graphic-novel look because she's in a detailed, directionally-lit scene — the render has
light/shadow and texture to ink against. So render the heroine the SAME way: in a simple LIT room with
directional light, matching the Grace anchor's treatment. (We can neutralize the background to a model
sheet afterward if wanted.)

## STYLE — match the GRACE ANCHOR exactly (this is the whole point)
`assets/episodes/ep-04/pixel/ep04-scene-05a-grace-navy-office-v3-application-review-comic-barsetter-v2-more-angular-shadows-nophoto.png`
Copy its rendering: **bold black comic INK outlines, hard-edged ANGULAR shadow PLANES, grey/neutral
shadow tone, printed graphic-novel grit, high-res.** NOT smooth, NOT glossy, NOT plasticy, NOT a clean
vector cel.
- **FACE shading = like the OFFICER'S FACE in that Grace image:** multiple angular tonal planes (a lit
  plane + a midtone + a shadow plane) that model the face — NOT one flat shadow color, NOT soft airbrush.
  Fine facial linework (delicate on features, bold ink on the outer contour). Moderate depth, readable.

invented storefronts, signage, or SUNNYVAiLE-specific detail; just enough light + surface texture to give
the render something to ink. Daytime, bright overall, but with real directional modeling (not flat even light).

Single **full-body** figure, standing, clear, centered-ish. Her the clear subject.

## FORMAT
High-res (≥1920 wide). One image. Inked graphic-novel — bold ink, angular grey planes, multi-tone face
