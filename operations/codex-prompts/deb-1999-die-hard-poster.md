# Codex prompt — Deb's 1999 campaign poster (Die Hard, HR-redacted)

**Canon:** The ORIGINAL 1999 campaign poster. Deb couldn't be bothered to make one, so she used the Die Hard movie poster — unedited — and sent the intern to hang it around SUNNYVAiLE. After the election, HR went around with a Sharpie and redacted "MOTHERFUCKER" from the tagline on every poster in town. Deb has not forgiven them. Firing them would be too much work. The posters are still up. Kinko's still has a pile from the print order she never called back to cancel.

**Where it lives on the site:** hangs alongside the NOPE trilogy in the Mayor's Office print gallery at [`town-hall.html`](../../town-hall.html). This is the origin artifact — the one that started it all.

## Reference idiom

Late-80s American action-movie theatrical one-sheet. The "lone exhausted man in a dirty white tank top against an urban catastrophe" idiom that defined the genre — think the visual grammar of that era's biggest action-hero-versus-terrorists movie, the one everyone quotes. Pastiche, not copy — this is homage to the era's visual language, not a reproduction of a specific studio poster.

## Composition

- **Hero figure, centered lower half:** white male, mid-30s, buzzed hairline receding at the temples, sweaty, dirt-streaked face, small bloody scrape on cheekbone, exhausted stare. Dirty white cotton tank top / undershirt. Holds a Beretta pistol muzzle-down at his hip. Looking off-camera at something we can't see.
- **Backdrop:** night-time downtown American city skyline. One massive skyscraper dominates the vertical axis — 40+ stories, glass facade — with orange fire glow blooming from three or four upper floors, tiny silhouettes of falling glass or bodies. Low orange horizon smoke haze.
- **Print quality:** slight offset registration, film-grain texture, small dust flecks. Like a real 1988 lithograph one-sheet, not a digital render.

## Title area

- **DO NOT title it "Die Hard."** Leave the top title area either (a) obscured by a curled/torn corner of the poster, OR (b) covered by tape at the top edge from where the intern stuck it to the wall in 1999.
- The joke works because the tagline identifies the film — the top title is unnecessary and best hidden.

## Bottom tagline block — THE FOCAL JOKE

Below the hero figure, at the bottom of the composition, in massive white sans-serif ALL-CAPS block letters spanning the full poster width:

`YIPPEE KI-YAY MOTHERFUCKER`

The word **MOTHERFUCKER** is crossed out by a Sharpie — but lightly. **The word is still fully readable underneath.** That's the whole joke.

- **Two or three quick horizontal Sharpie strokes** across the word — think how a teacher crosses out a wrong answer, not how a censor blacks out a classified document
- The strokes are slightly wavy / slightly diagonal, done fast without a ruler
- Maybe one extra return-stroke where HR doubled back to make sure they got it
- **The letters are all still legible under the marker lines** — the strokes are thick permanent marker, but you can absolutely still read what it says
- Sharpie sits on top of the glossy poster print as a real physical ink layer — slight shine, slight bleed at the tips of the strokes
- The whole redaction takes up maybe 60% of the vertical height of the word — it's a crossout, not a blackout

**The tone is professionally disapproving, not angry.** HR did this quickly on the way to lunch, standing on a chair, and moved on. They fulfilled the compliance requirement — the word is technically "redacted" — and everyone in town has been reading it clearly every day since 1999. That's the joke.

## Credit strip

Below the tagline, a tiny 1988-style movie-credit block strip — the tall thin condensed caps that filled the bottom of every one-sheet from that era. Fake cast names, fake studio credits, fake ratings box (an "R" rating box on the far right). This whole strip is illegible at normal viewing distance but present as texture.

## Aging (this poster has hung in SUNNYVAiLE since 1999)

- Slight overall yellowing / warm color cast
- Top-right corner is curled and folded down slightly
- One strip of aged masking tape at the top center where it was stuck to the wall — the tape is yellowed and starting to peel
- One faint horizontal crease across the middle from being folded once for transport
- Faded slightly on the left edge where sun hits it every afternoon
- Small ballpoint pen scuff mark near the bottom right corner — nothing was written, just a pen touched it

## Palette

- **Original poster tones:** navy blue night sky, deep orange fire glow, cream/off-white for the tagline and tank top, dirt-brown and blood-red on the hero's face, matte black for shadows
- **The Sharpie redaction:** pure jet black permanent marker — visually distinct from the printed blacks in the poster; sits ON TOP as a separate physical layer

## Style specs

- Photographic-realism 1988 movie-poster illustration idiom (not a 2020s digital painting). Airbrushed shading, hand-painted hero figure over photo-composited backdrop.
- The Sharpie redaction: solid, matte, hand-drawn, sits on top of the printed art as a real ink layer with slight shine
- Overall poster: subtle aging texture — this artifact has been on a wall for 25+ years
- Portrait 2:3 ratio (theatrical one-sheet proportions)

## Output

- **2x renders**
  - **Variant A:** Flat "scan-of-the-poster" view — poster fills the frame, minimal perspective, feels like a museum plate archival scan
  - **Variant B:** Slight 3D framing — the poster is hanging on a wall (visible plum or cream wall behind it, small shadow beneath), tilted very slightly — the vibe of a poster you found still on the wall in a municipal office in 2026 that nobody has taken down since 1999
- Both: 2:3 portrait ratio, 2048px minimum on long edge, transparent OR wall-colored background per variant

## Do NOT

- Do NOT show the movie title "DIE HARD" anywhere. The top title area is obscured/covered by tape.
- Do NOT show any actual studio logos (20th Century Fox, etc.). Fake studio in the credit block is fine.
- Do NOT make the Sharpie redaction heavy, angry, or fully obliterating. The word must still be readable — it's a crossout, not a blackout.
- Do NOT scribble back-and-forth or make it look like someone lost their temper. Two or three quick strokes, done and done.
- Do NOT add "VOTE DEB" or any campaign copy overlay. Canon: she used the movie poster UNEDITED. The comedy is that nothing marks it as a campaign poster — you just knew.
- Do NOT show the actor's face too specifically — this is pastiche of a genre archetype, not a portrait of a specific actor. Generic tough-guy features.

## Voice note

The comedy relies on three things landing simultaneously:
1. The poster is a Die Hard poster. Everyone knows it's a Die Hard poster.
2. She used it as a campaign poster with no modifications.
3. HR "redacted" the swear after the fact — technically compliant, functionally readable.

The joke isn't that HR blacked it out. The joke is that HR crossed it out with two Sharpie strokes on the way to lunch, and everyone in town has read the word clearly every day for 27 years. Compliance theater. That's the whole punchline.
