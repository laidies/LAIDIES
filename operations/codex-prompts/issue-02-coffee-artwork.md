## Register / art direction (must match the sibling issue02 images)
- Y2K-honest, warm, a little wry — a real café counter, not a fantasy set.
- Brand palette: plum, rose, gold, teal, cream. **No neon, no pure black/white.**
- Letter **all** text in-generation, clean and legible (Codex does this well). This is how
  David Rose ("A GENERAL STORE BUT MAKE IT SPECIFIC") and Cher ("KNOW THESE, TRUST ME") read.

> **Colour note (v1 → v2 — the fix):** the first render read **bland** (Ali, 2026-07-07).
> **Do NOT make it warmer** — it's already warm; more warmth just makes it muddier. The problem
> is it's **dim, low-contrast, and monochrome** — everything drowned in the same sepia-brown, so
> the brand colours never actually read as colour. **Less bland means:**
> - **Brighter, cleaner light** — lift it out of the murk; crisp café lighting, not dim/moody.
> - **Real colour separation, not sepia** — the plum tag, the rose underlines, the **teal** cup
>   band and "MADE TO ORDER" chip should read as *distinct saturated colours*, not brown-on-brown.
> - **Cooler, punchier brand palette present** — bring the plum/rose/teal forward against the
>   warm counter so the frame has range, not one muddy tone.
> - **More contrast and pop in the hero objects** — the latte, the cups, the hand-lettered tag
>   should feel vivid and crisp.
> - Match the **liveliness and saturation of the `pieces-of-flair` / `the-food-court` storefront
>   renders** (bright, colourful, punchy) — that's the target energy. Still no neon.
>
> **Kill the browns (Ali, 2026-07-07 — "too many muted colours and browns"):** the setting
> itself is too brown — a dark brown counter against a brown backdrop, so the whole frame is
> brown-on-brown. **Change the set, not just the grade:**
> - **NO dark-brown wood counter, NO brown/sepia backdrop.** Put it on a **light, colourful
>   surface** — cream / pale marble, or a **plum or teal painted counter**, or a checkerboard
>   tile like the mall floors — and a **brighter, more colourful back wall.**
> - **Brand colours should DOMINATE the frame** — plum, rose, teal, cream, gold as the actual
>   surfaces and props, not tiny accents. Brown is minimal (just the coffee itself).
> - Think **bright, saturated Y2K café** — colour-forward and cheerful, not dim/moody/vintage-brown.
