## Owner findings

- The art is dark, dingy, off-palette and not a vibrant LAiDIES 1990s room.
- A stronger prior Library/aisle image already exists and must be considered
  before generating replacement art.
- Page identity overlaps the room image.
- Books appear generated twice instead of once as live catalogue objects.
- Collection names are acceptable, but their rendered CSS treatment does not
  look like credible physical aisle or shelf wayfinding.
- A newcomer cannot quickly understand what is available, how to find it, what
  each object contains or what opening it will do.
- A static masthead followed by cards or sections is not a building experience.

## Successor cutline

Preserve the incumbent while rebuilding one representative proof. The room or
aisle itself must be the intuitive interface: live shelves, desk or stations
expose the Library's real features without hidden hotspots or gimmicks. Each
book is shown once, explains coverage, topics, depth, availability/currentness
and opening outcome before activation, then opens into a book-like continuous
reader with contents and section navigation rather than page-turn chores.

The catalogue must grow through reusable bays/aisles and support new
collections or genuinely new Library stations without degrading into an
ever-longer card page. Desktop and compact mobile need purpose-built,
understandable compositions. No successor may enter owner review until an
independent browser review verifies those objective jobs and a separate visual
review confirms the exact rendered palette, room credibility and 1990s
LAiDIES character.

No existing art, route, asset registry entry, deployment or public release is
approved by this rejection record.
