## Visible review

The challenger preserves the luminous dusk masthead exactly, and its cyan/pink/coral section rule, deeper weekly band, dark activity field, and stronger editorial headline treatment improve separation and make the page more recognisably a single night-town system.

However, the prominent **“WHY LAiDIES EXISTS”** panel is a hard failure: its multi-paragraph body copy is dark ink on the dark plum/navy gradient. At full resolution it is visibly near-black and not reasonably readable on desktop or mobile, while the white quote and coral buttons below remain readable. This is a local visual defect in an important explanatory module, not a theoretical contrast concern. It directly repeats the Cycle 6 rejection condition: a more vivid treatment may not reduce readability.

The treatment also overuses identical thick dark borders, squared shadow offsets, and the four-colour rule at nearly every section. That creates a consistent system but flattens hierarchy into a CSS surface treatment. It has not resolved the image-lock issue that the existing FAiRY Godmother scene is explicitly `REPLACE` everywhere: the sealed visual-only candidate retains it unchanged. Per BTB-209, saturation/tint and component chrome are not a substitute for complete authored visual direction or asset judgment.

No veto was found for masthead/copy/IA preservation, missing imagery, white/cream-page redesign, status-card grid, Pearl Plum, purple/yellow-gold system, CSS-substitute artwork, or newly introduced internal operational vocabulary. Existing locked copy remains zero-delta; it is not treated as newly introduced candidate language.
