# Visitor's Centre building championship — direction competition

**Status:** MAKER RED-TEAM COMPLETE; INDEPENDENT VISUAL JUDGMENT OPEN.  
**Required floor:** 17/20 in product quality, accuracy/trust and LAiDIES
brand. UX/accessibility and technical feasibility are scored separately.

## Competition set

The comparison contained exactly the requested incumbent and two materially
different challengers:

- **Anonymous I:** current production-source incumbent;
- **Anonymous II:** room entered across a broad physical map counter, with the
  fold-out map and A–Z destination file as the operating surface;
- **Anonymous III:** room-scale panorama screen and vertical dispatch board,
  with destination reveal emerging from a wall slot.

The separately generated Wall Map Welcome board was retained as a control. It
was excluded before scoring because it was an incremental incumbent refinement,
not one of the two materially different challengers.

## Scoring rubric

- **Product quality:** can a first-time visitor name the place, understand the
  map/directory/tour choices and act without reading an essay?
- **Accuracy/trust:** exact map and route parity, current status visible before
  navigation, no downstream completion claim, honest failures.
- **LAiDIES brand:** whole authored room, adult comic rendering, correct palette,
  1990s/Y2K specificity, no generic card grid or unapproved character.
- **UX/accessibility:** equivalent named route, keyboard/focus behavior,
  newcomer/returning clarity, 320/390 reflow.
- **Technical feasibility:** stable deterministic overlays, source boundaries,
  failure recovery and integration risk.

## Red-team findings before reveal

### Anonymous I

- Strength: exact 17-route contract, failure truth and current room/map opening
  are already credible.
- Failure: the room ends at the first viewport. The rest becomes detached
  select, tour panels, first-15-minutes panel, postcard workspace and founder
  accordions.
- Failure: mobile turns the room into a decorative strip and requires a
  3,785px journey.
- Failure: optional postcard work visually outranks orientation.

### Anonymous II

- Strength: the visitor arrives across the signature object, with wall overview,
  take-away map and named recovery file visible in one physical composition.
- Strength: selected destination, optional tour/trailer and optional postcard
  can all remain on the same fold-out desk surface.
- Strength: exact town content can be composited into a clean framed slot;
  generated approximation is not needed.
- Risk: live copy can cover too much art on mobile. The candidate therefore
  keeps the exact wall map visible before the welcome sheet and uses the full
  named route below.
- Risk: the physical A–Z object is not itself readable; the HTML select/full
  directory must remain the semantic interface.
- Dependency risk: the current shared Welcome Tour source says visitors can
  listen at KSVL while the Visitor's Centre contract suite requires public
  listening to remain held. This source mismatch is outside the candidate but
  must be repaired before integration.

### Anonymous III

- Strength: most architectural integration; map, named dispatch board and
  reveal slot belong to one wall.
- Strength: maintains strong negative space and whole-room authorship.
- Risk: the vertical slats are difficult to align with deterministic 17-name
  controls at desktop and fragile at mobile.
- Risk: the locked fold-out map signature becomes a secondary bench prop.
- Risk: a smaller visual directory could suggest color/position memory instead
  of an equal named route.

## Reveal and decision

## Independence boundary

directions. It is not independent approval. The candidate may be shown to the
owner because it clears the maker's required floors and its DOM/browser
evidence passes; integration remains prohibited until a separate visual judge
and Ali both rule.
