## What the reference actually does

1. **0.00–0.18 s · explosive arrival**
   - The centre symbol appears first inside a full-frame radial light rush.
   - The adjacent letters arrive as stretched light trails rather than simply
     fading on.

2. **0.18–0.42 s · partial wordmark assembly**
   - Only part of the word is initially stable.
   - Small magenta symbols pulse at the sides while the missing letters resolve.

3. **0.42–1.32 s · living centre character**
   - The same centre position changes through eye, mouth and geometric states.
   - Each state has its own internal action: blink, close/open, rotate or pulse.
   - Transitions retain luminous smears and echo shapes, so the motion never
     feels like a static icon being swapped.

4. **1.32–2.70 s · hierarchy changes**
   - The logo settles.
   - Supporting copy fades in above and below only after the animated logo beat
     has completed.

5. **2.70–3.19 s · exit**
   - The branded lockup fades or drops away as the live-action image returns.

## Highest-value enhancements

### 1. Give every symbol one internal micro-action

The reference symbols are not static holds. Apply a tiny readable action during
the 0.24-second LAiDIES hold:

- VHS: reels turn a few degrees.
- CRT: prompt cursor blinks once.
- Cassette: reels nudge in opposite directions.
- Instant camera: photo advances a few pixels and settles.
- Floppy: metal shutter clicks down and back.
- Wand: one pixel travels off the tip.
- Context windows: the stack offsets once.
- Refresh: arrows rotate through a short arc.

This is the strongest next improvement because it adds character without
changing the approved collapse grammar.

### 2. Animate the cyan/yellow offsets as motion echoes

During the fastest part of a transformation, let the cyan shadow lag behind and
the yellow highlight lead by several pixels. Both return to their normal
one-position offset when the symbol settles. This translates the reference's
light trails into LAiDIES' brush language.

### 3. Add one authored entrance before the cycles

Do not begin on a fully static wordmark. A LAiDIES entrance could be:

- comic rays contract toward the lowercase `i`;
- `LA`, `DIES` arrive as two quick brush swipes;
- the underline draws on;
- the dot lands last and immediately triggers the first collapse.

The wordmark itself remains the selected brush design.

### 4. Give each symbol a different final formation

Keep the shared `dot → stem → compact orb` cause-and-effect. After the orb:

- VHS unfolds horizontally;
- CRT expands like a screen switching on;
- cassette reels split apart inside the case;
- camera develops outward from the photo slot.

The reference feels authored because its eye, mouth and geometric state do not
all use one identical reveal recipe.

### 5. Let the background answer each transformation

Use one brief local response, not a full-frame flash:

- halftone dots expand one step;
- nearby rays flex outward;
- one AI term brightens, then recedes;
- a small comic burst appears behind the symbol and disappears on reset.

Keep the wordmark quiet zone intact.

### 6. End with an episode lockup

For episode versions, use the settled wordmark as the hierarchy shift:

- episode number or title fades in below after the final `i` reset;
- the final symbol family determines the episode background terms;
- hold the complete lockup long enough to read.

The evergreen master can end on the wordmark alone.

## What not to copy

- The rounded futuristic outline font.
- The electric-blue cinema glow as the main LAiDIES style.
- Side symbols that do not carry clear meaning.
- Full-frame white flashes.
- Removing the complete `i` reset between symbols.
- Reducing the symbol holds until they become unreadable.

## Accessibility and evidence limits

- Avoid repeated full-frame flashes and strong luminance jumps.
- Web use needs a static or reduced-motion fallback.
- This is a visual frame audit. Audio and the original master file's intended
  colour/brightness could not be judged independently from the supplied
  recorded-video source.
