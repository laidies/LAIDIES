# Object-led café research and transfer notes

**Accessed:** 2026-07-26  
**Method:** bounded official/primary-source review; concepts transferred, no visual imitation

## Evidence

- [Starbucks coffeehouse designs enter a new era](https://about.starbucks.com/stories/2025/starbucks-coffeehouse-designs-enter-a-new-era-take-a-look/)
  describes comfort, more seating, local character, texture, art and familiarity
  as deliberate third-place features.
- [Starbucks: rediscover the power of third places](https://stories.starbucks.com/emea/stories/2024/reclaiming-third-places-our-bold-vision-for-community-stores/)
  frames a third place as welcoming, inclusive, community-connected and distinct
  from home/work.
- [W3C WCAG 2.2 target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
  specifies a 24×24 CSS-pixel minimum or sufficient spacing; the candidate sets
  a stronger 44–50px working target for primary actions.
- [W3C WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow)
  requires content/function without two-dimensional scrolling at a 320 CSS-pixel
  width equivalent.

## Transfer, not imitation

Third-place research supports hospitality, local identity, comfort and a reason
to linger. It does not justify copying a chain café's aesthetic or loyalty
mechanics. Blend & Snap's unique translation is:

- hospitality = optional usual and JoJo's precise welcome;
- local character = SUNNYVAiLE street, community board and episode-world ink;
- familiar ritual = menu → order ticket → pickup receipt;
- lingering = secondary noticeboard and music, never compulsory productivity;
- inclusion = the object-world composition always has a complete semantic text
  equivalent and no interaction depends on a tiny hotspot.

## Design implication

The LIBRAiRY transfer is a grammar, not a room template. The visitor operates a
native object. Shelves work in a library; menu board, ticket rail and receipt
work in a café. Reusing shelves, generic cards or a long central dashboard would
erase the building's product identity.
