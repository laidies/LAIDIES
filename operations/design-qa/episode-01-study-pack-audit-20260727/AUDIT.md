## Product verdict

Ali’s concern is correct: the current Try-On does not offer enough value for
the effort it asks from a beginner. It should not be repaired by adding more
instructions or more decorative comic elements.

The redesign should promise and deliver:

> Leave with one useful piece of work, one clear reason you chose it and one
> thing you corrected before using it.

## Proposed three-item experience system

### Cheat Sheet — the episode in one glance

**Interaction job:** quick understanding and later reference.

- Compose it like a controlled comic cover/page: one dominant lesson, three
  supporting panels and clear reading order.
- Use one visual contrast for Search versus Generative AI, three scoped stat
  bursts, compact illustrated term cues and one phrase strip.
- Keep dense comic energy at the edges and in emphasis moments; preserve quiet
  reading areas for the lesson and definitions.
- Web actions: plain-language term reveals and direct `Try It` / `Open Cards`
  / `Pop Quiz at SUNNYVAiLE High` links.
- Print: complete without interaction on one Letter/A4 page, with sources and
  checked/updated date.

### Try-On — a guided result, not homework

**Interaction job:** make ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini feel familiar while
helping the learner begin one small, low-risk task she has been avoiding.

Recommended experience name:

**THREE TABS, ONE TASK**  
*One thing you have been avoiding. Three first passes. One place to begin.*

The first screen states the payoff:

> In ten minutes, you will have started one thing you were avoiding, seen how
> ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini answer the same task and chosen the first pass you
> want to keep working with.

Offer two valid entry paths:

1. **Guided preview:** use the episode’s avoided-email example to inspect
   current, dated views of all three interfaces and compare three answers to
   the identical task. No account required.
2. **Open the real tabs:** follow current official links, use the same supplied
   task in the tools available to the learner and bring back a short,
   non-sensitive observation.

The working sequence:

1. **Meet the three tools:** show a current, clearly labelled orientation card
   for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini: official link, current interface image,
   where the message box is and any account/access note.
2. **Choose the small avoided task:** offer:
   - `Draft the email I keep postponing.`
   - `Turn these non-confidential notes into a useful checklist.`
   - `Help me prepare for a conversation I have been putting off.`
   The learner may use her own task only when it is low risk, reversible,
   non-sensitive and something she can personally judge.
3. **Ask exactly the same thing:** copy one task into each available tool.
   Do not improve it between tabs and do not turn this into Episode 02’s
   briefing lesson.
4. **Put the answers side by side:** use the current example answers or paste
   short non-sensitive excerpts.
5. **Spot the differences:** choose plain observations:
   - easiest to understand;
   - most useful detail;
   - different tone or structure;
   - something missing or unsupported.
6. **Choose the place to begin:** select the most useful first pass **for this
   task**, make one obvious human correction and record
   `ChatGPT felt… / Claude felt… / Gemini felt… / I am continuing with…`.

The side-by-side comparison uses the same meaningful rating card for every
answer:

- **Got what I meant**
- **Gave me useful material**
- **Tone and style fit**
- **Easy to work with**
- **I liked…**
- **I didn’t like…**
- **I would change…**
- **Anything factual I need to verify?**

Ratings use `1 — missed it`, `3 — partly useful` and
`5 — strong fit for this task`. They produce **MY PICK FOR THIS TASK**, not a
permanent model leaderboard.

Four neutral sliders create a visual style fingerprint:

- `short ←→ detailed`
- `straight to the point ←→ conversational`
- `sticks closely to my ask ←→ adds extra ideas`
- `plain paragraphs ←→ heavily structured`

Neither end is presented as better.

Each tool also receives a compact, dated **TOOL STYLE FILE** showing its current
consumer interface, available response-style controls and repeated tendencies
from controlled LAiDIES same-task tests. These are labelled
`WHAT YOU MAY NOTICE`, never fixed personality claims. The card explains that
plan, selected model, memory, personality/style and custom instructions may
change the result.

The completed 2026-07-27 avoided-email fixture confirms that the comparison
cannot be reduced to prose length or tone. ChatGPT returned a plain draft,
Gemini Flash added a subject and a separate tip, and Claude Sonnet 5/Medium
opened a structured email composer. The Try-On must help the learner notice
both answer differences and product-surface differences without implying that
one run establishes a permanent provider personality. Evidence:
`operations/research/episode-01-three-tabs-one-task-20260727/CONTROLLED-COMPARISON.md`.

Remove the butterfly-clip score as the primary result. Fun can live in reaction
copy, scene transitions and the Episode 01 treatment; the actual outcome must
remain a clear orientation and comparison, not a novelty score.

The replacement must also pass the interaction floor. Empty circles, lines and
feedback boxes in a static render are visual shorthand only and cannot be
accepted as the product. The built Try-On requires labelled segmented buttons,
range inputs, editable text areas, visible focus/selected states, an honest
model/mode recorder with `Not shown in this chat`, a chosen editable draft and
a completed **My First-Pass Receipt** with working copy, download/print and
restart actions. Contract:
`operations/design-explorations/episode-01-three-tabs-one-task-20260727/INTERACTION-CONTRACT.md`.

Use episode art as an active scene:

- a 1990s magazine-rack or three-cover composition drawn from the episode’s
  magazines/editors-in-chief analogy;
- three clearly separate, equally weighted tool panels;
- current interface views embedded as factual orientation material, not
  redrawn from memory;
- one comic split-screen showing the same avoided task entering all three;
- one `SAME TASK. DIFFERENT ANSWERS.` reveal;
- pop-word punctuation for short feedback;
- no generic form dashboard and no full-page busy collage behind body copy.

### Trading Card Pack — collectable memory

**Interaction job:** recall six important terms and distinctions.

- Show an actual wrapper/pack opening into six numbered cards.
- Each card has a dominant image or phrase front and a short explanation back.
- Support flip, next, previous, shuffle and view-all without requiring
  ownership or sign-in.
- Show pack progress as `1 of 6`, not false mastery.
- Add an accessible non-motion front/back view.
- Provide aligned printable Letter/A4 fronts and backs.
- Use the saved card reference construction with Episode 01’s purple, pink,
  cobalt/electric blue and sunshine-yellow palette, strong black/white
  structure and halftone.

## Shared visual rules

The three items belong together without looking identical:

- **Cheat Sheet:** controlled comic-page hierarchy.
- **Try-On:** interactive three-tool orientation using one real avoided task.
- **Trading Cards:** authentic pack, card fronts and backs.

Shared anchors:

- exact Episode 01 imagery and objects;
- comic gutters, halftone, bursts and editable dimensional lettering;
- purple, vibrant pink, electric/cobalt blue and sunshine yellow;
- strong quiet zones for reading;
- one dominant action at a time;
- no candy palette, generic white dashboard, arbitrary boxes or placeholder
  art.

## Evidence limits

This audit inspected the current desktop flow and saved state. It did not prove
mobile behavior, keyboard behavior, screen-reader behavior, print output or a
replacement design. Those remain required admission evidence.
