# Episode 6 title shortlist

**Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Status:** DECIDED — PUBLIC TITLE LOCKED  
**Capability:** choose the model/mode that fits the job instead of defaulting
to the one perceived as “best”  
**Core example:** 200 badly named files versus work where error/depth genuinely
matters  
**Core line:** You do not wear couture to the beach.

## Ruling

## Episode Six: Strike a Mode

Why it won:

- **Pop-culture memory hook:** it turns “strike a pose” from Madonna’s
  *Vogue* into the episode’s actual decision: after choosing a model, choose
  how much reasoning work that model should do.
- **Technical precision:** it teaches that **model** and **mode** are separate
  choices. A capable model can still be run in a needlessly expensive or slow
  mode.
- **Fashion continuity:** Episode 5 introduces *The Super Models*; Episode 6
  moves from meeting the lineup to styling the right model for the occasion.
- **Story engine:** the heroine discovers that using the most intensive mode
  for every task is like commissioning couture for a two-hundred-file cleanup.
- **LAiDIES voice:** short, funny and recognizable without making the lesson
  sound childish or turning the reference into decoration.

Reference validation:

- Madonna’s official archive dates *Vogue* to March 21, 1990 and lists a
  “Strike-A-Pose Dub”:
  <https://www.madonna.com/products/vogue>

## Title test

The title must:

1. point toward the actual lesson rather than merely decorate it;
2. carry a recognizable Rewind Era hook;
3. make a clear Episode 5 outro possible;
4. leave room for speed, depth, cost and risk—not just brand comparison; and
5. avoid implying that one model is permanently “hers.”

## Decision history

### The Top Model Trap — not selected

It stated the “top is not always right” mistake, but the *Parent Trap* echo
felt younger than the intended tone and the title did not teach the separate
model/mode choice as precisely as *Strike a Mode*.

### What Model to Wear / What Model Not to Wear — not selected

The occasion metaphor fit, but “wearing a model” was awkward and the result
sounded more like a lesson heading than a LAiDIES episode.

### Model Behavior — not selected

It described observation better than selection and did not naturally carry
cost, risk or reasoning effort.

### Overdressed for the Job — not selected

The line accurately described the real mistake, but it did not have enough
pop-culture or comedy baked in to carry a LAiDIES episode title. Preserve it as
an explanatory line inside the episode.

### Make It Work / One Model Fits All? As If! — not selected

Recognizable references, but they read as lesson headings and did not feel
distinctive enough for the episode.

### Groundbreaking — not selected

The Miranda Priestly reference explains novelty, taste or empty launch
hype—not how to select a model/mode for a job.

## Real teaching case discovered during production

Ali had GPT-5.6 Sol at Extra High selected for all LAiDIES work. Read-only
supporting agents inherited that expensive parent setup, so routine scans and
deep architecture received the same reasoning budget. That is the Episode 6
lesson happening in real life:

> **The most capable setup was not wrong. It was overdressed for part of the
> work.**

Use this case only with Ali's approval and without publishing private credit
balances or account limits. The 200-files scene can remain the main
task-selection example; this case supplies the modern, immediately practical
cold open.
