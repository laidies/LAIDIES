[tv announcer] Previously, on ladies: our heroine finally stopped putting it off. She took the email she'd been dreading for four days, told the machine the truth about who it was for and what she couldn't say out loud — and hit send in eleven minutes. [playful] And on this episode: a week later, she goes back to the well, asks for something simple... and gets a wall of confident oatmeal. This is Episode Two: Tell Me What You Want.

It's nine-forty on a Tuesday, and I have exactly twenty minutes to walk into a room and sound like I know what I'm going to say.

Cross-functional sync at ten. I need three talking points — clear, mine, defensible. And I'm not sweating it, because it's week two of the new me: I have a machine now. So I open one of the AI tools and I type the thing you type when you're already half out of your chair — "give me talking points for my ten o'clock" — and I hit enter with the confidence of a woman who solved this last Tuesday.

And back comes... [dry] a lot. A tidy, beautifully formatted wall of absolutely nothing. "Align on key priorities." "Ensure stakeholder buy-in." "Drive actionable next steps." [dry] It reads like a motivational poster that went to business school and came back worse. Nothing in it is technically wrong. Nothing in it is remotely usable. I cannot stand up at ten o'clock and say "let's align on key priorities" to a room of people who have watched me do this job for six years.

And here's the part that actually got me. Because the afternoon before — Monday, four o'clock, same tool, same laptop, same me — I'd asked it for an update to send my director, and it handed me something I barely had to touch. Nearly right. Sounded like me on a decent day. I sent it and thought, [warm] oh, we're friends now.

[thoughtful] So I pulled them both back up and set them side by side. Monday's update, the one that landed. Tuesday's talking points, the word salad. Same machine. One day apart. And I couldn't help but wonder... why does this thing read my mind some days, and completely ignore me on others?

Welcome back to ladies — where smart, busy women learn AI one Wednesday at a time, from a little internet town called Sunnyvale. Last week, I found out the machine is the most talented new hire I'll ever manage: brilliant, fast, and clueless about my actual job until I tell it. This week, I found out the hard way what happens when I don't. [dry] Turns out "give me talking points" is the professional equivalent of walking up to someone and saying "make it nice." Willing? Absolutely. Helpful? Not even a little.

So I did what this town has taught me to do when something isn't adding up — I shut the laptop and took it somewhere I could think. Sunday morning at the Blend and Snap: my corner table, an oat latte going cold, the radio on low, and the two drafts open side by side. Fifteen quiet minutes to stare at the thing I'd been blaming. [dry] And the tool had an alibi. Same tool, both days. It hadn't changed a thing.

[pointed] It wasn't the tool that changed between the two. It was me — what I'd typed to get each one. Monday, unhurried, in a good mood, I'd told it everything: who the update was for, what my director actually cares about, what to leave out. Tuesday, out of breath, I'd handed it six words and a deadline. The machine didn't get dumber overnight. [dry] I got vaguer.

And here's the punchline, and it took me an embarrassingly long time to see it: AI can't read your mind. It only knows what you hand it. That Tuesday I'd handed it almost nothing — and then felt personally betrayed when it gave me almost nothing back.

Think of it like this. Your regular coffee spot knows you. You walk in, you say "the usual," and before you've found your wallet there's an oat latte waiting, because they have a whole history with you — a hundred mornings on file. AI is not your regular spot. It's the brand-new café that just opened across town, and if you breeze in and say "the usual," you get a blank look. [dry] Polite. Willing. Completely lost. Not because it's bad at its job — because it has never once met you. Every new chat, you walk in a stranger, and the order starts from nothing.
