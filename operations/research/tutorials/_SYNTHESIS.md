# Tutorial research — synthesis of six passes · 2026-07-22

Six researchers: ChatGPT · Claude · Gemini · how-to-teach · courses-and-curricula ·
community-signals. This is what they agree on, what they contradict, and what still cannot be
known without opening the app.

⚠ **Read this before acting on anything below.** The previous research pass read vendor help
centres only, and everything built on it had to be blocked from filming. Treat every menu path
here as a lead until it is seen on a live screen.

---

## 1. What the evidence says about our SEQUENCE — and it backs Ali's instinct

Ali (2026-07-22): *"shouldn't it be what is it, how does it work"* — then the elements of the tools.

**Every serious course opens the same way.** Google AI Essentials, Anthropic's AI Fluency track,
and DeepLearning.AI's Generative AI for Everyone all open with a short "what is this / how does
it work" module, under ~15 minutes. The one outlier — Vanderbilt's Prompt Engineering course —
opens with two worked demos and still reaches "what is an LLM" fourth, inside the first fifteen
minutes.

**Concept always comes early, never late — but always brief.** No course found delays theory to
the end, and none spends more than one short segment on it before moving to hands-on work in the
same session.

→ **Lead with concept, briefly, then do.** That settles the sequencing question, and it is what
Ali said before the research came back.

## 2. 🔴 The gap — this is our position, and it is now evidenced

Two independent passes found the same hole from different directions.

**From the courses pass:** *no course found — vendor, university or professional-training —
sequences memory, custom instructions, projects and connectors together as a deliberate beginner
progression. And none walk the settings screen as a **location-based tour** rather than a
task-based one.*

**From the how-to-teach pass:** *nothing found combines all of — short (5–10 min), chaptered,
screen-accurate "here is exactly where this setting lives", adapted for a non-technical woman,
and grounded in the segmenting/signalling/recap evidence.*

⚠ **"AI training for women" is NOT empty space** — Anthropic × Advancing Women in Tech launched
November 2025; Founderz × Microsoft exists. But those are multi-week strategy and prompting
curricula. **The gap is the combination, not the audience.** Do not claim the audience is
unserved; claim the format is.

## 3. What every serious course includes that WE DO NOT

- **A responsible-use / ethical-use section.** Placement varies; presence is universal. We have
  no equivalent and should decide whether that is deliberate.
- **A certificate.** Ours is arguably the Report Card, the Yearbook and the merit sash — that is
  a better version of the same instinct, and worth saying out loud.

## 4. What the format evidence changed in our spec
| Finding | Source | What it changed |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement drops sharply past ~6 min | Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014, ACM L@S — 6.9M viewing sessions | Chapter every class into sub-6-minute segments even inside a 9-minute total |
| Preview the task in plain language before any screen | Van der Meij 2013/2015, peer-reviewed, software-specific | The lesson slide already does this — keep it |
| Signal the clickable target every time | Mayer + Van der Meij, independently | Now a hard requirement, not decoration |
| **Spoken recap of steps in order** | Van der Meij, replicated across two papers — the most corroborated finding in the set | **Reversed my rule** "end with one thing to do, not a summary". Do both. |
| Demonstrated errors: genuinely unresolved | BJEP 2025 special issue | **Removed my "show it going wrong" rule.** Brief trap-naming is defensible; staged failure is not supported. |

## 5. What people actually ask, over and over
From the community pass. **This is a better basis for the class list than anything invented.**
1. Why doesn't it remember me?
2. Projects vs Skills, Custom GPT vs Skill — what is the difference?
3. **Do I even have this feature?** (tier gating)
4. Where did that setting go?
5. Connector permission errors after the Google-app consolidation (2026-06-15)

→ **#3 is the one to act on.** ⚠ Note carefully what the evidence does and does not say: it
establishes that people **ask this repeatedly**. It does NOT establish that nobody answers it —
a question asked constantly probably has many answers that are not landing or not being found.
A likelier explanation is that **tier gating changes often**, so any answer goes stale fast,
which is checkable and is a reason to date ours. Our "Who gets it" field is the right instinct
and should be far more prominent.
