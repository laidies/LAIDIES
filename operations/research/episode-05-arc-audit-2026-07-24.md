## Recommendation

Episode 5 keeps its promised public title:

> **Episode 5 — The Super Models**

“Read the label” is the internal practical capability—not a replacement title.
The episode should be the **orientation episode**, not the full model-selection
episode.

> **By the end, she can look at an AI model picker or launch headline and know
> exactly what each label means—and which choices are actually hers.**

The practical capability is to distinguish:

- company/provider;
- app/product;
- model family/name;
- numbered version/release;
- reasoning mode/effort;
- subscription plan/access; and
- freshness method (built-in knowledge versus current search/retrieval).

This fulfils Episode 4’s explicit promise:

> “She learns the difference between the brand on the door and the brains
> behind it. This is Episode Five: The Super Models.”

That hook (`content/episodes/episode-04.canon.md:27`) already introduces the
recommended lesson accurately and does **not** need to be renamed or rewritten.
The narrower capability prevents model choice, specialists, pricing, stale
knowledge and the whole industry from being forced into one lesson.

The strong 200-badly-named-files example teaches a different verb—**choose the
right model/mode for the job**—so it moves intact to Episode 6.

## Teaching order

1. **The label nobody explained.** The heroine sees one recording-day-verified
   label containing an app name, model name, number, mode and plan. Are these
   five products or five settings?
2. **Why “model” feels like a 1990s word.** Use the Super Models obsession,
   launches, campaigns and new It-girl attention as the memory hook.
3. **Read the store directory.** Fashion house = company/provider;
   boutique/store = app/product; supermodel name = public model family/name;
   season/campaign = release/version.
4. **Read the two labels beside the model.** Shopping service/time =
   reasoning mode/effort; membership/budget = subscription plan/access.
   Identify them here; save the choosing lesson for Episode 6/13.
5. **The poster does not update after the shoot.** Trained knowledge is a
   snapshot. A newer release may have newer training; current search/retrieval
   can bring fresh material into the present task.
6. **The sentence the analogy must not blur:** search gives the model current
   material to work with; it does not secretly retrain the model.
7. **Stores that work differently.** Department store = a multi-provider
   product/router; specialist shop = a product/model focused on images, voice,
   music, research or another medium. Preview only.
8. **Decode the opening label.** Show which field changes when the user
   changes model, mode or plan—and which fields do not.
9. **Cocktail explanation + Try-On.**

## Analogy map and limits

| Fashion world | AI concept | Boundary that must be taught |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion house | AI company/provider | A company may also operate products that carry other providers’ models. |
| Boutique/store | App/product | The app also supplies history, files, tools and settings; it is more than a display window. |
| Supermodel name | Public model family/name | Each numbered checkpoint is technically a distinct model, not literally one unchanged person. |
| Season/campaign | Version/release | A release can change training, weights, tools and behaviour—not just styling. |
| Couture / ready-to-wear / resort | Occasion/capability fit | Use as a fit lens, not a universal vendor taxonomy or fixed price ladder. |
| Personal shopper versus grab-and-go | Reasoning effort/mode | Usually more inference-time compute, time and cost—not a different human degree of care. |
| Shopping membership/budget | Subscription plan | Controls access, limits and features; it does not make the underlying model smarter. |
| Department store | Multi-provider product/router | The user may choose a provider/model, the product may route automatically, or both. |
| Specialist shop | Specialist model/product | A specialist app may own its model or wrap a general provider. |
| Campaign photo/poster | Frozen training snapshot | There may not be one perfectly clean cutoff; products can add current sources without changing the model. |
| Live inventory check | Search/retrieval/tool call | Adds current information to working context; it is not retraining. |
| New It girl / September issue | Launch hype and attention | Attention is not proof of practical superiority; best as a social/Tribune derivative. |
| Designer changes houses | Researchers/leaders change employers | Models are made by large teams, not one auteur; useful industry context, not core Episode 5. |

The present canon alternates between model-as-supermodel, model-as-line and
model-as-item-being-shopped (`content/episodes/episode-05.canon.md:147-205`).
Use one stable vocabulary:

- the famous name is the star readers recognize;
- the number is that star line’s current release/season;
- couture/ready-to-wear/resort describes occasion or capability fit;
- the app is where the reader accesses it.

Say plainly that the analogy bends here. It is a mental model, not a technical
ontology.

Annotate the receipt:

- **Star:** model family/name

## Try-On — “Read your receipt”

Open the AI she already uses and record:

1. Who made the product?
2. What app/product is open?
3. What model name is displayed, if any?
4. Is a version displayed?
5. Is there a speed/depth/reasoning choice?
6. What plan controls access?
7. Can the product search or cite current sources?

If the model label is hidden, write **“the store chooses for me.”** That is a
useful discovery, not a failed exercise.

Do not use the current “same task, similar answer” exercise as proof that two
products use the same model. Similar tone cannot establish model identity;
explicit labels or verified product documentation can.

## What moves

### Episode 6 — choose the model/mode for the occasion

Use “You don’t wear couture to the beach” and preserve the 200 badly named
files versus board-paper example. Teach speed, depth, cost, task risk, model
retirement and what a new release may change.

### Episode 7 — privacy before file-heavy use

Move **No Scrubs, No Sensitive Data** ahead of memory, files and workplace
workflow lessons. The existing 24-episode arc leaves privacy at Episode 11,
after the reader is already being asked to use work context and files.

### Episode 8 — memory, projects and persistent context

Use **The Skeleton Key** for what the product can see, retain or retrieve.
Episode 2 remains the prompting/briefing lesson; Episode 8 is about system
state and context boundaries.

### Episode 13 — subscription/upgrade decisions

Shopping budget, “trust fund” tier, quotas, paid access and whether an upgrade
earns its cost already belong here in the existing arc.

### Episode 14 — specialist/multimodal tools

Images, voice, video, music and other specialist shops already belong here in
the existing arc.
