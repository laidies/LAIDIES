## lesson
How to tell the big general assistants apart **and** why they overlap — anchored on the one distinction
that unlocks everything: **model ≠ tool.** The company makes the model (the brain); the app is the store;
some stores (Copilot) resell other companies' models. So you stop hunting for "the best AI," pick by the
job, and know your work tool is often a familiar model in a work badge.
**One-liner:** The company makes the model; the app is just where you shop it — so stop looking for the
best one and learn which store you're in.
**Scope (locked with Ali):** Ep 5 covers the **4 general all-rounders only** (ChatGPT · Claude · Gemini ·
Copilot). It **hints** that a back shelf of **specialists** exists (research-with-sources, images,
document tools, meeting notetakers) and promises a later episode — it does NOT teach them here.

## concepts[]
*(→ glossary · LIBRAiRY · concept cards · SUNNYVAiLE High 101)*
- **Model** — the actual AI brain, trained by one company, version-numbered (new one each season).
  **Analogy (canon):** the house's **supermodel** — the named star it sends down the runway; the *same*
  supermodel gets booked by many stores (the 90s-supermodel plug). It's what got smarter; the app around it
  is just the storefront.
- **Tool / app** — the product you open and type into (ChatGPT, Claude, the Gemini app, Copilot).
  **Analogy (canon):** the house's **boutique**. "ChatGPT vs Claude" compares two stores, not two brains.
- **Multi-model tool** — an app with no single house that resells several houses' models.
  **Analogy (canon):** the **department store** (Copilot) — carries OpenAI's + Anthropic's lines (+
  Microsoft's own), which is *why the work app feels familiar: same models, different storefront.*

## comparison
*(flat way vs fluent way → article prompt-lab · FAiRY Godmother.)*
- **The setup:** three all-rounder-shaped jobs in one afternoon — a careful reply to a board member, pull
  three dates out of a week of Gmail threads, and a first draft of a deck in PowerPoint.
- **The flat way:** open your one usual app and force all three through it — then blame yourself when it's
  mediocre at two. **RULE:** the failure isn't a bad prompt, it's the *wrong store* — a perfect brief still
  loses at the counter that doesn't stock that job.
- **The fluent way:** send each to the store built for it — the delicate reply to **Claude**, the
  pull-from-my-inbox to **Gemini** (it can see your mail), the deck to **Copilot** (already in
  PowerPoint). Same hour, three right counters.
- **The result:** not "AI got smarter" — you did; you stopped asking one boutique to be the whole house.
  **DRAFT RULE:** keep it to the 4 all-rounders + plausibly-your-own-week jobs; never imply you must own
  all four — the skill is knowing *which* you'd reach for.

## try_on
*(→ try-on.html · study pack. ~10 min, not homework.)*
Two moves. **One:** open the AI your *company* gave you and one you use on your *own*, hand both the exact
same task, and read the two answers side by side — notice how alike (or not) they are. That's the
model-vs-store thing, live. **Two:** take one real job and, before you type, ask the Cher question — is
this an all-rounder job, or is it really a *specialist* job in disguise (research with sources, an image,
a stack of documents)? You're not finding the best AI. You're learning to read the floor.
