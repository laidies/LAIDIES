## 2 · THE PLAIN TEACHING SENTENCE — before any analogy

> **The one at the top of the list isn't the best one. It's the most expensive one, and it's built
> for the hardest work you have.**

Then the structure, still plain:

Every house builds more than one AI. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google — each runs its own store, and each
builds several models, with names. They aren't versions of one thing the way a phone update is.
Some are built for slow, careful, difficult thinking. Some are built to be fast and cheap and do the
same small job two hundred times without complaining. Both are real. Neither is the best one.

## 3 · THE WORKED EXAMPLE — biggest block in the episode

**Locked by Ali 2026-07-22: the two hundred badly named files.** This is the HEROINE's task, told
inside the episode — not something the listener needs to have. (The try-on is the universal one; see
below.)

**The task.** The shared drive. Two hundred files, named by whoever happened to save them:
`final.doc` · `final_v2.doc` · `FINAL USE THIS ONE.doc` · `Copy of budget (1).xls`. They all have to
match the convention before anyone else can find anything. Nobody has ever volunteered for this.

**The reach for couture.** She opens the good one — the top of the list, the expensive one, because
obviously you'd want the best. And it is *excellent*. It considers each filename. It explains its
reasoning. It asks a thoughtful clarifying question about file number six. Two hundred files, and
it wants to have a conversation about each one.

**The switch.** She gives the identical job to the fast cheap one. It doesn't think about it. It
just renames them.

**The payoff detail** *(the load-bearing bit — must be something generic writing couldn't produce)*:
🔴 draft candidate — the expensive model was not *worse*. It was doing exactly what it's built for,
which is care, on a job that needed none. The thing she notices is that "better" isn't a property
of the model, it's a property of the fit.

**Still owed before this can be written:** the ask quoted verbatim · the honest number (how right
was it?) · the time on both sides · what stayed hers. These come out of drafting, and Ali judges
them in the script.

## 4 · THE DECISION RULE — named trigger, named answer

The thing a table can never contain. Ali's own shape: *"Two-line email → ChatGPT. The contract where
one missed clause costs you → Claude."*

> **Two hundred rows of the same tidy-up → the fast one. The board paper where one wrong number
> costs you → the careful one.**

⛔ Never "the careful one, the fast one" *without names*. That's the beige table with the data
withheld — the exact defect that killed the last draft.

## 5 · THE ANALOGY — and its strip test

> **You don't wear haute couture to the beach.**

- **Funny/familiar?** Yes.
- **Makes it clearer?** Yes — overdressed is a *cost*, not a virtue. Heavy, slow, expensive, wrong
  for a Tuesday.
- **Can you reason FROM it?** Yes, and this is what the old fashion-house analogy couldn't do.
  *Why is the top model slower?* Couture is made by hand, to measure. *What if I use the cheap one
  for the hard thing?* You turned up to the gala in a swimsuit. *Is the expensive one better?*
  Better for the occasion it was cut for. It answers questions the script never asked — correctly.
- **Strip test:** delete the analogy and you still have "houses build several models, some for hard
  slow work, some fast and cheap; match the model to the job." That's a **structure**, not one
  sentence. So the analogy is a handle, not a costume. **It stays.**

*(For contrast: strip the old supermodel metaphor and one sentence was left. That's why it failed.)*
