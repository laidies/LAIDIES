# The AI Dictionary

*Derived lookup text from the current maintained term registry.*

## A

### A2A (Agent-to-Agent)

A standard for letting AI agents talk to other AI agents, rather than to tools. You are unlikely to meet this one; it is included because it appears in industry writing.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-14](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-14)

### Agent

An AI system that doesn't just answer once. It decides, acts, looks at what happened, and goes round again until the task is done or it gets stuck. 'Agentic AI' is the same idea used as a category label.

**See also:** **Agent loop**, **Tool calling**, **Autonomy**, **Irreversibility**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-14](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-14)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-10](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-10)

**Also covered in:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-2)

### Agent loop

The repeating cycle: perceive → reason → plan → act → observe → decide whether to continue or stop

**See also:** **Agent**, **Planning**, **Multi-agent system**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-14](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-14)

### AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)

An AI system that can perform any intellectual task a human can — not just one specialised skill, but the full range of reasoning, learning, and problem-solving across all domains

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-20](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-20)

### AI slop

Output that's technically correct but sounds like it was written by nobody for nobody. Generic, predictable rhythm. No perspective. You can feel it instantly even if you can't articulate why.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-5)

### AI/ML product manager

Decides what to build and why. Translates between business needs and technical capabilities.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-19](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-19)

### Algorithm

A set of instructions a machine follows — like a recipe

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-1](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-1)

### Alignment

The problem of ensuring a highly capable AI system actually does what humans want and intend

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-20](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-20)

### Allowed-Information Check

Deciding what you are permitted to put into an AI tool before you decide how to ask. Client data, salary information, anything under an agreement — the first question, not the last.

### API (Application Programming Interface)

A way for one piece of software to call another. How applications send prompts to models and receive responses.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-10](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-10)

### Architecture

The design of the network — how layers are arranged, how many, what type. The blueprint.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-5)

### Artificial Intelligence (AI)

A system that figured out its own rules by learning from examples

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-1](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-1)

### ASI (Artificial Superintelligence)

A hypothetical AI that significantly exceeds the best human intelligence in every domain — science, strategy, creativity, social skills

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-20](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-20)

### ASR (automatic speech recognition)

Converting spoken audio into text

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### Attention

The mechanism that lets the system figure out which parts of the input are relevant to which other parts

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-5)

### Authority line

One sentence stating what an AI may do on its own, what it must ask about first, and when it must stop. The core of delegating safely. See also **Irreversibility**.

### Automation

Software that follows rules a human wrote in advance

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-1](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-1)

### Autonomy

How much an AI does without asking. It is a spectrum, not a switch, and it has four useful stops: fully sandboxed (can't touch anything real), human-in-the-loop (asks before each action), supervised (acts alone on small things, asks on big ones), and fully autonomous (doesn't ask).

**See also:** **Irreversibility**, **Human-in-the-loop**, **Sandbox**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-10](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-10)

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-13](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-13)

## B

### Backpropagation

The technique that figures out which specific weights caused the error, so they can be fixed

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-5)

### Ban list

Your standing list of words and constructions to keep out of AI output. Useful, and limited — a ban list removes tells, it does not create a voice.

### Batching

Processing multiple users' requests together for efficiency

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-7](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-7)

### Benchmark

A standardised test given to every model the same way, so they can be compared. Many are fixed questions with known answers; others check whether generated code runs, or which of two answers people prefer.

**See also:** **Evals**, **Contamination**, **MMLU**, **SWE-bench**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-12)

### Bias (in AI)

When the training data over- or under-represents certain groups, perspectives, or scenarios — causing the system to produce skewed results

**See also:** **Training data**, **Labelling**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-3](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-3)

### Brief

A structured description of what you need: the outcome, the audience, the constraints, and what "done" looks like. Not a magic phrase. A specification.

**See also:** **Prompting**, **Delegation brief**, **Definition of done**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-4)

## C

### Capacity auction

The yearly market where an electricity grid pays generators to guarantee they can meet peak demand. Costs there flow into bills slowly and indirectly, which is why 'auction costs rose 174%' does not mean bills rose 174%.

### Chain-of-thought

Getting a model to work through its reasoning step by step before answering, rather than jumping straight to a conclusion. Often improves accuracy on anything with several stages, and it is what 'reasoning models' do automatically.

### Chatbot Arena

A human-evaluation platform where users chat with two anonymous models and vote for the better response

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-12)

### Cognitive offloading

Letting a tool do thinking you would otherwise have done. Not automatically bad — writing is offloading — but it has a cost when the skill was the point. See also **Cognitive surrender**.

### Cognitive surrender

The documented tendency to accept AI output without scrutiny, adopting its conclusions as your own. Not the same as using a tool. It's the moment you stop applying your own judgment.

**See also:** **The wizard problem**, **Task fit**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-8](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-8)

### Colocation

Renting space in someone else's data centre rather than building your own

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-17](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-17)

### Common Crawl

A nonprofit that has crawled and archived over 9.5 petabytes of web data since 2008, freely available — the single biggest source of training data for AI

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-3](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-3)

### Confounding

Something you didn't measure explaining your result. The reason 'AI users score lower' does not establish that AI made them score lower.

### Constitutional AI

Anthropic's approach: giving the model a written set of principles and having it judge its own outputs against them

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-9)

### Contamination

When the questions used to test a model turn out to have been in its training data — the equivalent of a student having seen the exam paper. It can inflate or distort a score, so a suspiciously good result is a reason to investigate rather than to celebrate.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-12)

### Content filter

A classifier (often itself an AI model) that scans text for policy violations — violence, hate speech, PII, etc.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-11)

### Context

Everything the model can "see" right now — your message, the conversation history, system instructions, retrieved documents. All in one window.

**See also:** **Context window**, **Context budget**, **Context rot**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-8](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-8)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-2)

### Context budget

The AI's attention is finite. Everything you put in the conversation competes for that attention. Think of it as a budget: every token you spend on background is a token not available for your actual task.

**See also:** **Context window**, **Context stuffing**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-4)

### Context clash

Two things in the conversation contradict each other, and the AI picks one without telling you. One of four ways context fails.

### Context compaction

What happens when the AI's working memory fills up: the tool silently compresses older messages into a shorter summary to make room. Details get lost without warning. You'll notice it as the AI "forgetting" things you told it earlier.

**See also:** **Context rot**, **Context window**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-2)

### Context confusion

Irrelevant material in the conversation pulls the answer off course. One of four ways context fails.

### Context distraction

So much material is in the conversation that the important part stops standing out. One of four ways context fails.

### Context engineering

Designing everything the model can see when it responds — documents, memories, tools, instructions. Prompt is a subset. (mid-2025)

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-15](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-15)

### Context poisoning

Something wrong gets into the conversation early — a bad fact, a misread instruction — and the AI keeps building on it. One of four ways context fails.

### Context rot

The measurable decline in AI output quality as a conversation gets longer. The model starts forgetting instructions, drifting, and contradicting itself. Not a bug. Not your fault. A documented property of how these systems work.

**See also:** **Context window**, **Context compaction**, **Lost in the middle**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-2)

### Context stuffing

The antipattern of dumping everything into the conversation "just in case." More context often makes output worse because the AI can't tell what matters and what's noise.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-4)

### Context window

The maximum number of tokens a system can "see" at once — its working memory for a single conversation

**See also:** **Context**, **Token**, **Context rot**, **Context compaction**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-4)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-2)

### Correction spiral

When each round of feedback makes the output slightly worse instead of better, but you keep going because it feels like you're almost there. You never get there.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-6)

### CPU (Central Processing Unit)

The general-purpose brain of a computer — good at doing many different things, one at a time, very fast

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-16](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-16)

### Cross-attention

The mechanism that connects your text prompt to the image being generated — how words steer pixels

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### CUDA

NVIDIA's software toolkit, released in 2006. Two decades of AI tools were built on top of it, so switching away from NVIDIA's chips now means rewriting a great deal of working code. That, more than the chips themselves, is why NVIDIA is hard to displace.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-16](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-16)

## D

### Data centre

A building (or campus of buildings) filled with servers — the physical home of cloud computing and AI

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-17](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-17)

### Data engineer

Builds and maintains the pipelines that collect, clean, and deliver data. The plumbing.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-19](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-19)

### Data flywheel

When using a product generates more data, which improves the product, which attracts more users, which generates more data...

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-3](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-3)

### Data scientist

Analyses data to find patterns and insights. Builds models for specific business questions.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-19](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-19)

### Decode

The writing half of answering you: the model produces one chunk at a time, re-reading everything it has written so far before each new one. This is why replies stream, and why long answers cost more than long questions.

**See also:** **Prefill**, **Inference**, **Output tokens**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-7](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-7)

### Deep learning

Machine learning that can handle complex, messy things — images, speech, language — not just neat rows of numbers

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-2)

### Definition of done

What the finished output looks like, stated BEFORE work begins. Not "I'll know it when I see it" but specific, checkable criteria.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-12)

### Degree of change

How much you want adjusted: a tweak (5-10% different), a rework (50% different), or a complete redo. If you don't specify, the AI guesses, and it usually guesses wrong.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-6)

### Delegation brief

A self-contained description of a task that gives the executor (AI or human) everything needed to produce the right output without asking questions. Not a prompt — a specification.

**See also:** **Brief**, **Definition of done**, **Autonomy**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-12)

### Delusional spiraling

Repeated agreement reinforcing a false belief rather than correcting it. Modelled formally by MIT-affiliated researchers in 2026. See also **Sycophancy**.

### Descriptive study

Research that establishes a pattern is there without establishing what caused it. Useful and honest; frequently reported as though it proved causation.

### Diffusion model

A model that generates images by starting from noise and gradually removing it

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### Distillation

Training a small, fast model to mimic a large, powerful one

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-9)

### DPO

Direct Preference Optimization — a cheaper, simpler way of doing the same job as **RLHF**: teaching a model which kind of answer people prefer. It skips the middle step of building a separate scoring model.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-9)

## E

### EBIT

Earnings before interest and tax — the standard measure of what a business actually makes from operating. When surveys ask whether AI has had 'EBIT impact', they are asking whether it showed up in the profits.

### Effort level

A setting (increasingly available in 2026) that controls how much reasoning the AI applies to your request. Low effort = fast, shallow, cheap. High effort = slow, thorough, expensive. You're trading speed for depth.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-7](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-7)

### Embedding

A list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text — arranged so that similar meanings sit near each other

**See also:** **Vector**, **Vector database**, **Semantic search**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-8](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-8)

### Emergence

Capabilities that appear unexpectedly as models get larger — abilities no one programmed in

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-20](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-20)

### Endpoint

A specific URL where you send requests to use a model via API — like an address for the model's "office"

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-10](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-10)

### Evals

Custom evaluation tests a company builds for its specific use case — "does our model get THIS right?"

**See also:** **Evaluation**, **Benchmark**, **Regression testing**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-12)

### Evaluation

Judging whether one piece of output is actually good. A rubric is just your checklist for doing it the same way twice. Not to be confused with **Evals**, which is what companies do to whole models.

**See also:** **Evals**, **Benchmark**, **Definition of done**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-1](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-1)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-11)

### Export controls

Government restrictions on selling advanced technology to specific countries — the US restricts AI chip sales to China

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-18](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-18)

## F

### Fabrication plant (fab)

The physical factory where chips are manufactured. Costs $10–20 billion to build

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-18](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-18)

### Failure mode

A specific way the output could be wrong that you'd reject. Stating these upfront prevents the AI from going in a direction you'll discard.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-12](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-12)

### Few-shot examples

Showing the AI two or three examples of what you want, instead of describing it. Also called few-shot prompting. Usually beats an instruction, because a good example carries the tone, format and level of detail all at once.

**See also:** **Prompting**, **Brief**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-4)

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-9)

### Fine-tuning

Additional training on a smaller, specific dataset — adjusting the model's weights for a particular domain or behaviour

**See also:** **RLHF**, **DPO**, **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**, **Prompting**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-9)

### FLOPS

Floating-point operations per second — a measure of raw processing power

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-16](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-16)

### Forward diffusion

The training process of adding noise to an image until it's pure static

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### Foundry

A factory that manufactures chips designed by other companies. TSMC doesn't design chips — it builds them for NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, and others

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-18](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-18)

## G

### Generative AI

AI that creates new things (text, images, code, audio) rather than just sorting or recognising

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-2](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-2)

### GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)

Originally designed for rendering graphics; turns out to be perfect for AI because it does thousands of simple calculations simultaneously

**See also:** **CUDA**, **Parallel processing**, **VRAM**, **TPU (Tensor Processing Unit)**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-16](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-16)

### Gradient descent

The method for adjusting weights in the right direction — like rolling downhill toward the lowest error

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-5)

### Graph engineering

Designing how several AI agents connect and hand work to each other — who does what, in what order, and what happens when one fails. A 2026 term, still settling. Note this is a different 'node' from the chip-manufacturing one.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-15](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-15)

### Grounding

When AI's response is based on actual source material (a document, a search result, your data) rather than generated from its training. Grounded answers are more trustworthy. Ungrounded answers may be hallucinated.

**See also:** **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**, **Hallucination**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-9](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-9)

### Guardrails

Systems that check what goes into a model and what comes out, then allow, refuse, limit, redact or escalate it. Coverage varies a lot between products, and they both miss things and over-block things.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-11)

## H

### Hallucination

When a model states something untrue with complete confidence. Not lying and not a glitch: the same machinery that produces true sentences produces plausible ones, and nothing inside it can tell the difference. Structural, not a bug to be patched.

**See also:** **Grounding**, **Guardrails**, **Evaluation**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-11)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-11)

### Handover

Deliberately ending a long conversation and starting a fresh one, carrying across a short written summary of what matters. The fix for **Context rot**.

### Harness

The operating layer around the model — manages context assembly, tool access, memory, quality gates, and the control loop

**See also:** **Wrapper**, **Context window**, **Orchestration**, **Tool calling**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-10](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-10)

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-7](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-7)

### HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)

Specialised memory stacked on top of GPUs — essential for AI, manufactured primarily in South Korea (Samsung, SK Hynix)

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-18](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-18)

### Hedging

When the AI refuses to commit to an answer: "It depends," "There are many factors," "This is a complex issue." Sometimes appropriate. Usually a crutch.

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-5)

### Human-in-the-loop

The system pauses before acting and asks a human for approval — a safety checkpoint

**See also:** **Autonomy**, **Irreversibility**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-13](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-13)

### Hyperscaler

A company that operates data centres at massive scale: Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), Google (GCP), Meta

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-17](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-17)

## I

### img2img (image-to-image)

Generating a new image using an existing image as the starting point instead of pure noise

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### Inference

Using a trained model to generate output — applying the learned patterns to new input. The "job" after "school."

**See also:** **Prefill**, **Decode**, **Latency**, **Throughput**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-7](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-7)

### Inpainting

Editing only a specific region of an image while leaving the rest untouched

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-6](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-6)

### Input tokens

The tokens in what you send (your question, your document, the conversation history)

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-4](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-4)

### Irreversibility

Whether an action can be undone. A draft can be deleted; a sent email cannot. This is the single most useful question to ask before letting an AI act on your behalf, and it matters more than how capable the model is. See also: **Autonomy spectrum**, **Sandbox**, **Human-in-the-loop**.

**See also:** **Autonomy**, **Sandbox**, **Human-in-the-loop**

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-13](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-13)

## J

### Jagged frontier

AI's capability boundary isn't a smooth line where hard tasks are on one side and easy tasks are on the other. It's ragged: AI is brilliant at some hard things and terrible at some easy things, in patterns you can't always predict in advance.

**See also:** **Task fit**, **Evaluation**

**Put it into practice:** [Working with AI 101 — chapter-8](https://laidies.ai/library#working-with-ai-101::%40chapter-8)

### Jailbreaking

Techniques that trick the model into bypassing its safety training — getting it to produce content it was trained to refuse

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-11](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-11)

### JSON

A plain-text way of writing structured information so a program can read it — labelled fields inside curly brackets, like `{"day": "Friday", "time": "7pm"}`. You will see it when an AI talks to a tool. You never need to write it; you only need to not be startled by it.

## K

### Knowledge cutoff

The date at which training data was locked — the model doesn't know anything that happened after this point

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-5](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-5)

## L

### Memory

Two different things share this word, and the difference matters. **Mechanically**, a model has no memory at all: every request runs fresh, and anything it 'remembers' was re-sent to it invisibly. **In a product**, 'memory' is the feature that does that re-sending — storing facts about you between chats and slipping them back into the context window. When a company says its AI remembers you, they mean the second thing.

**See also:** **Context window**, **Persistent instructions**, **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**

## S

### Sandbox

A walled-off space where an AI's actions are kept away from anything real — code runs but can't touch your files, an email is drafted but not sent. How well walled off depends entirely on the permissions it was given: a sandbox shrinks the blast radius, it doesn't guarantee there isn't one.

### Scaling hypothesis

The bet that you get more capable AI mainly by making models bigger and feeding them more, rather than by inventing something new. It has held since about 2018. Nobody has a complete theory for why, or for how long it will keep holding.

**Full explanation:** [AI Fundamentals 101 — chapter-20](https://laidies.ai/library#ai-fundamentals-101::%40chapter-20)

### Sideways move

### Stateless

Having no memory between requests. Every time you send a message, the model starts from nothing and reads the whole conversation again. Anything that looks like memory was re-sent to it invisibly. See also **Memory**.

### Structural constraint

A rule that gives the AI something concrete to DO (or not do) rather than a vague prohibition. "Use only information from this document" works. "Don't make things up" doesn't.

### Supervised learning

A benchmark testing coding ability — gives the model real GitHub issues and checks if its code actually passes the tests

### Sycophancy

The AI's tendency to agree with everything you say, validate your framing, and avoid disagreeing even when you're wrong. A documented, measured property of how these models are trained.
