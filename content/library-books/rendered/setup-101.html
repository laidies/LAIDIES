THE 101s · SETUP 101

Put the right context in the right place.

Before you expect an AI tool to remember you, decide what belongs in standing instructions, optional memory, one project—or nowhere. The sorting method travels between tools. Your settings and data do not.

What stays true

Standing instructions are for stable preferences and recurring output rules: Canadian spelling, a plainspoken tone, a standard structure, or a boundary that should apply broadly. A current product may call these custom instructions, profile preferences or something else.

Useful example.

“Use Canadian spelling. Write in direct, warm prose. Keep sourced facts separate from inference.” That is a reusable working preference—not a biography, password or project brief.

Check the actual product before relying on this. Some modes, specialist assistants, shared workspaces or third-party tools may ignore, replace or isolate account-level instructions.

What may change

Optional memory is for a fact or preference that may help across conversations. It is not the same as the current chat, a project file or a guaranteed record. A remembered role, location or preference can become stale, so the useful habit is not merely saving it—it is being able to review, correct or remove it.

Memory is product-, plan-, account-, setting- and rollout-dependent. OpenAI currently documents custom instructions, memory and Projects; Anthropic documents profile preferences and project instructions. Do not assume that a different tool—or even a different mode in the same tool—carries the same information.

What belongs with one job

A project or workspace is for the instructions, chats and permitted files that belong to one continuing job. A board update and a newsletter should not share one giant context pile merely because both are yours.

A project can help keep relevant material together. It does not automatically prove isolation, confidentiality, retention, sharing boundaries or cross-device behavior. Those depend on the exact product, account, workspace and settings.

Keep the binder honest.

Give one job a clear name, include only the material that job needs, and remove expired context. A project labelled “Everything” is a junk drawer, not a system.

What never goes here

Passwords and access tokens go nowhere in AI instructions, memory, projects or chats. Confidential, NDA-restricted, personal or employer-controlled material stays out until the governing agreement, policy and approved account explicitly allow it.

This book cannot decide whether an upload is authorized. Accounts 101 owns account, training, retention, sharing and sensitive-data boundaries. Maintained tool cards own current menu paths and plan-specific controls.

The check. If you cannot explain why an item belongs in that location—and how you will review or remove it—do not save it yet. Placement is not permission to upload.

The analogy—and its limit

Think of three labelled places: standing instructions are the note you reuse; memory is a pencil note that may need correcting; a project is the labelled binder for one job. The labels explain the different jobs only. They do not prove that a product offers the feature, applies it everywhere, isolates the data or keeps it private.
