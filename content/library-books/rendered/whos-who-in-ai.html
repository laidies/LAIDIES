How to read this map

Open a chatbot and you see one product name. The operational system may depend on a model lab, cloud capacity, chips, a foundry, memory and specialised manufacturing equipment. Around it, investors, distributors and public bodies can change capacity, access, ownership, rights or rules.

A conventional “top AI companies” list goes stale quickly and teaches the wrong model. AI is a dependency stack, not a leaderboard. This book includes an entity only when it controls a material gateway in model development, distribution, cloud and compute, semiconductor supply, data or platform access, funding, a consequential partnership or enforceable rules.

Every connection needs a verb: owns, holds equity in, funds, supplies, hosts, licenses, distributes, regulates, evaluates or depends on. “Partners with” is the corporate equivalent of “it’s complicated”: sometimes true, rarely enough.

This is a global working atlas, not a complete directory or ranking. Dates matter. An announcement proves what was announced; it does not prove future capacity was delivered.

Company, lab, model and product are not the same thing

OpenAI is an organisation. GPT names a model family. ChatGPT is a product through which people use models and tools. The same sorting applies elsewhere: Anthropic is a company and Claude names its models and products; Google DeepMind is a research organisation within Alphabet, while Gemini can name model families and products depending on context; Microsoft Copilot is a family of Microsoft experiences, not an independent model lab.

Before asking who is “winning,” ask what category you are comparing. App users, model benchmark scores, cloud revenue and semiconductor capacity do not fit one honest scoreboard.

People enter this book when their current role locates a decision: chief executives, research leaders, infrastructure decision-makers and public officials. Biography is not the job here; LUMINAiRY owns deeper lives and contributions.

The model labs: a selective global field

This first edition uses a disclosed boundary: it profiles organisations with a material model family, research programme or distribution gateway, plus one sourced relationship explaining why they matter. It is selective, not a claim that every region has equal infrastructure or representation.

North America: OpenAI (GPT and ChatGPT), Anthropic (Claude), Google DeepMind (Gemini and scientific research), Meta (Llama and Meta AI), xAI (Grok) and Cohere (enterprise models). Europe: Mistral AI. Asia: DeepSeek, plus the TSMC, Samsung and SK hynix infrastructure roles in the chip-chain chapter. Product and model names orient the reader; they are not treated as separate companies.

The organisations differ in ownership, business model, openness, distribution and compute. DeepSeek’s official R1 release describes weights and code released under the MIT License; that supports a relationship with developers, not every claim about cost, independence or market power.

Geographic boundary: major organisations in China, South Korea, Japan, India, Africa, Latin America and the Middle East remain outside the model-lab index until a local official relationship source is bound. That is a first-edition evidence gap, not a claim they are unimportant. Update trigger: add or remove a lab when a sourced material control point is established or lost.

TSMC and Samsung — semiconductor manufacturers. They fabricate wafers for designers including NVIDIA. SK hynix, Micron and Samsung — memory suppliers. ASML — lithography-equipment supplier. Their power comes from a supply-chain position, not from owning the model labs that depend on the chain.

Cloud, capital and alliances

Model development and service require enormous compute. Microsoft Azure, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Oracle and specialist providers such as CoreWeave sit between labs and usable capacity. Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud and Huawei Cloud matter in China and international markets.

OpenAI–Microsoft. OpenAI’s 27 April 2026 statement describes Microsoft as a major shareholder and primary cloud partner. It says supported OpenAI products are offered first on Azure, subject to an exception; OpenAI may serve products across any cloud; and Microsoft’s specified intellectual-property licence through 2032 is non-exclusive. None means “Microsoft owns OpenAI outright.” Source.

Anthropic–Amazon. Anthropic’s 20 April 2026 statement says AWS remains its primary training and cloud provider for mission-critical workloads while Claude is available on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. It also announces future capacity and investment. Those are company-reported commitments until delivery is independently established. Source.

The invisible companies in the chip chain

AI chips do not come from one kind of “chip company.” NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Intel and Huawei design chips and systems. TSMC, Samsung, Intel Foundry and SMIC manufacture semiconductor wafers. SK hynix, Samsung and Micron supply memory. ASML supplies specialised lithography systems used by chipmakers. Packaging, networking, power and data-centre construction add further constraints.

NVIDIA’s fiscal 2026 Form 10-K provides a concrete chain: NVIDIA says it uses TSMC in Taiwan and Samsung in South Korea to produce wafers, and buys memory from SK hynix, Micron and Samsung. The designer depends on foundry and memory suppliers; the suppliers do not become NVIDIA merely because its products cannot exist without them.

ASML’s equipment role makes it a material chokepoint in this book—our inference from the manufacturing dependency, not its own label. ASML’s 2025 annual report separately reports an approximately 11% fully diluted stake in Mistral AI. Equipment supply and equity are two relationships; merging them would hide rather than reveal power.

Data, platforms and the route to users

A strong model without a route to users can remain a research artefact. Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta, Apple, ByteDance and other platforms can distribute AI through search, operating systems, workplace software, social networks and devices. App stores, enterprise procurement and cloud marketplaces are gates too.

Data relationships require special care. A platform may own content, hold a licence, receive permission, rely on a legal exception, purchase access or face a dispute. “Trained on the internet” is not a sufficient relationship description. Reddit licensing, Stack Overflow arrangements and data-labelling providers such as Scale AI belong only where a dated source identifies what access or service actually exists.

For readers, the practical question is: which organisation can change my access, price, privacy terms or default route to the model? The answer may be the product company, but it may instead be an employer, cloud marketplace, operating-system provider or regulator.

Public power: rules, export controls and evaluation

Public institutions do different jobs. The EU AI Office supports implementation and enforcement of the EU AI Act at Union level. Commission guidance says obligations for general-purpose AI model providers entered into application on 2 August 2025; a separate Commission release says the AI Office and national authorities begin enforcing the Act on 2 August 2026.

The US Bureau of Industry and Security administers export controls through the Export Administration Regulations. That can affect access to controlled technology; BIS is not a general AI safety evaluator or competition regulator. The US Federal Trade Commission occupies a consumer-protection and competition lane. The UK AI Security Institute performs technical evaluation and research; that does not make it the legislature. China’s Cyberspace Administration occupies a regulatory role under China’s rules; a full CAC card remains pending official source binding.

Legal dates and mandates are volatile. Every public-power profile must name the authority, jurisdiction, operative date and official source. “The government regulates AI” is not a usable map.

How to read the next AI announcement

Translate the headline into one sentence: [Entity A] [precise verb] [thing] to or from [Entity B], according to [dated source]. This changes [capacity, access, control, rights, distribution or rules]. It does not by itself establish [common overclaim].

Then attach confidence. Certain means directly documented and in force or observable. Likely means strong evidence supports a partly inferred conclusion. Company-reported means an organisation announced future capacity, spending or performance that is not yet independently established as delivered.

This is how the living book updates: not by adding every logo, but by changing the map when control, dependence or authority materially changes.
