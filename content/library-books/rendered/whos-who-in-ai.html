One answer, more companies than you can see

You open a chatbot and ask it to tidy a difficult email. A reply appears in seconds. It feels like one product did one thing.

Behind that neat little box may be an organisation that trained the model, another company renting out the enormous computers that run it, a chip designer, factories that manufacture those chips, memory suppliers, equipment makers, investors, distributors and public bodies deciding which rules apply.

Not every organisation touches your individual question. Some built the system years earlier; some provide the ongoing machinery; some can change the commercial or legal conditions around it. The point is that the name on your screen is the front door, not the whole building.

A restaurant gives us a useful first picture. The app is the dining room you enter. The model lab develops the recipe and kitchen method. Cloud companies provide rented kitchen space and power. Chip designers plan the chips; semiconductor factories manufacture them; and separate equipment makers supply some of the extraordinary machinery those factories need. Investors help finance the operation. Public authorities set particular rules.

The analogy has limits. AI companies are not arranged in one tidy restaurant hierarchy, and one company may occupy several roles. But it reveals the hidden dependency: your answer can rely on organisations you never see.

This book helps you ask one very useful question: who gives whom what—and what can each party actually change?

Company, model and product: sort the names first

AI headlines often use a company name, model name and product name as if they were interchangeable. That is how perfectly sensible people end up comparing a chatbot’s user count with a chip company’s manufacturing supply.

OpenAI is an organisation. GPT names a family of models it develops. A model is the trained pattern-making system behind an answer. ChatGPT is a product: the service you open to use models and tools.

The same sorting works elsewhere. Anthropic is a company; Claude names its models and products. Google DeepMind is an AI research organisation within Alphabet; Gemini can refer to model families or products depending on the sentence. Microsoft Copilot is a family of Microsoft products, not an independent model laboratory.

Brands reuse names because ordinary customers do not arrive clutching an organisational chart. So look at the job being described. Did something train the underlying system? That is the model or its developer. Did someone open an app or workplace tool? That is the product. Did a company sign an agreement? That is the organisation.

Explain the correction

A colleague says, “Claude released a new company.” What would you fix? A useful answer is: Anthropic is the company; Claude is the name used for its models and products. If you answer only “Claude is AI,” you have not yet sorted the jobs.

The organisations building the engines

A model lab develops the trained system underneath an AI product. It may also make the product, publish research, release model weights or sell access to other companies. It does not necessarily own the cloud computers or factories it depends on.

This first edition includes a selective set with an evidence-backed reason to remember each one:

OpenAI: remember it for the GPT model family and ChatGPT, plus its consequential Microsoft relationship. Anthropic: remember it for Claude and its Amazon/AWS relationship. Google DeepMind: remember it for Gemini and scientific work including AlphaFold. Meta: remember it because it develops Llama models and can distribute Meta AI through enormous social platforms. xAI: remember it for Grok and its connection with X distribution. Cohere: remember it for an enterprise and workplace focus. Mistral AI: remember it as a France-based model company with a material ASML investment relationship. DeepSeek: remember it as a China-based model lab whose official R1 release made model weights and code available under an MIT licence.

A licence is permission to use something under stated terms. It is not ownership. “Open weights” means people can obtain the trained numerical parameters under the licence; it does not automatically mean the training data, all system components or every possible use is open.

DeepSeek’s R1 release supports that specific availability claim. It does not prove every claim made about cost, independence or market power.

Geographic boundary: this is not a complete world directory. Major organisations across China, South Korea, Japan, India, Africa, Latin America and the Middle East still need locally sourced relationship profiles. Their absence is an evidence gap in this edition, not evidence that they are unimportant.

Remember the inclusion rule

Why include a lab: because it controls a consequential model, research or distribution route—not because it had a loud week online. Fame gets you recognised. A control point gets you onto this map.

Does Microsoft own OpenAI? Open the word “partner”

The short answer is: the cited agreement does not establish that Microsoft owns OpenAI outright. It describes several different relationships that headlines often compress into the word partner.

Equity is a financial slice of a company. OpenAI’s 27 April 2026 statement describes Microsoft as a major shareholder, meaning Microsoft holds equity in OpenAI. A financial stake can influence incentives and may come with particular rights, but it is not the same as owning every part of the company or making every decision.

Cloud means rented computing infrastructure elsewhere—the vast collections of computers used to train or operate systems. The same statement calls Microsoft OpenAI’s primary cloud partner and says supported OpenAI products are offered first on Microsoft Azure, subject to an exception. Azure is Microsoft’s cloud platform.

A licence is permission to use specified intellectual property. The statement says Microsoft’s licence to specified OpenAI intellectual property through 2032 is non-exclusive. Non-exclusive means Microsoft has permission, but not sole permission.

The relationship therefore contains at least four different facts: Microsoft holds equity; Microsoft is the primary cloud partner; Azure receives a first-offer route for supported products under the stated condition; and Microsoft has specified licensing rights. None of those sentences can be safely replaced with “Microsoft owns OpenAI.” Read OpenAI’s statement.

Anthropic and Amazon give us a useful comparison. Anthropic’s 20 April 2026 statement says AWS remains its primary training and cloud provider for mission-critical workloads, while Claude is also available through Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. The same announcement describes investment and future capacity. Announced capacity is a plan until delivery is established. Read Anthropic’s statement.

Say it without “partner”

Explain what Microsoft has, what Azure does and what neither fact proves. If you have to use “they work together,” open the envelope again. Is the sentence about money, computers, permission, distribution or decision-making?

The logo on the chip is not the whole factory

A company can design an advanced AI chip without owning the factory that makes it. This is one of the least visible and most important distinctions in the industry.

NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Intel and Huawei design chips or computing systems. A design specifies what the chip should do and how its circuitry should be arranged.

A foundry is a semiconductor factory. It manufactures chips for designers. The circuits are built on thin discs of material called wafers, which are later cut into individual chips. TSMC, Samsung, Intel Foundry and SMIC occupy manufacturing roles.

Memory holds the information that processors need to use quickly while doing their work. SK hynix, Samsung and Micron are major memory suppliers.

Lithography is the extraordinarily precise process used to place tiny circuit patterns onto a wafer during manufacturing. ASML supplies specialised lithography equipment to chipmakers. Think of precision printing at a scale where an ordinary printer has sensibly left the group chat. The analogy stops at printing: chip fabrication requires many complex physical and chemical steps.

NVIDIA’s fiscal 2026 filing makes the dependency concrete. NVIDIA says it uses TSMC in Taiwan and Samsung in South Korea to produce semiconductor wafers, and purchases memory from SK hynix, Micron and Samsung. NVIDIA designs the system; other companies manufacture necessary physical parts. Read NVIDIA’s filing.

ASML’s 2025 annual report separately reports an approximately 11 per cent fully diluted stake in Mistral AI. That is an equity relationship. ASML’s lithography business is an equipment-supply relationship. One company can appear on the map twice for two completely different reasons. Read ASML’s report.

Teach the chain

Why can NVIDIA depend on TSMC without becoming TSMC? A sound answer distinguishes design from manufacturing. Dependence means a necessary input comes from another organisation; it does not mean ownership, weakness or one corporate family.

Who controls the route to you?

A brilliant model that nobody can reach is still not the assistant on your phone or the tool in your workplace.

Distribution is the route through which a product reaches people. Search engines, social apps, operating systems, workplace software, cloud marketplaces and app stores can all become distribution gates. Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta, Apple, ByteDance and other platform companies may therefore shape AI access even when another organisation developed the underlying model.

This distinction becomes practical very quickly. Your employer can disable an AI feature in its workplace software. The underlying model has not vanished; your access route changed. An operating-system maker can make one assistant the default. A cloud marketplace can make a model easier for businesses to buy. Distribution power often hides inside a button that was simply there when you arrived.

Data relationships also need exact verbs. A company may own content, license access, purchase a dataset, receive permission, rely on a legal exception or face a dispute. These are not interchangeable. “Trained on the internet” is a foggy shortcut that hides the very relationship we need to inspect.

The reader question is not only “who built the model?” It is also: who can change my access, price, privacy terms or default route to it?

Check the route

Your employer removes an AI assistant from its office suite. Did the model disappear? No. The organisation controlling your workplace route changed your access. That is distribution power, not necessarily model ownership.

“The government” is not one job

An AI system may exist but still face different rules, evaluations or sales restrictions in different places. Saying “the government regulates AI” tells us almost nothing about which body can do what.

The EU AI Office supports implementation and enforcement of the EU AI Act at Union level. European Commission guidance says obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models entered into application on 2 August 2025. A separate Commission release says the AI Office and national authorities begin enforcing the Act on 2 August 2026. An obligation starting and authorities beginning enforcement are different events.

The US Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, administers export controls through the Export Administration Regulations. An export control limits where specified technology can be sold or transferred. That can affect access to advanced chips. BIS is not the general referee for every consumer AI claim.

The US Federal Trade Commission occupies consumer-protection and competition roles. The UK AI Security Institute conducts technical evaluation and research; evaluating a system does not make the institute the legislature. China’s Cyberspace Administration occupies a regulatory role under China’s rules, but a full sourced card remains open for a later edition.

The useful sorting question is: does this body make or enforce a rule, restrict technology transfers, protect competition or consumers, or evaluate technical behaviour? Different job, different power.

Choose the right door

If the question is whether an advanced chip can be exported, which role matters first: export-control authority or technical evaluator? The export-control authority. If the issue is a misleading consumer claim, you would look for a body with consumer-protection authority. “A government agency” is not precise enough to repeat safely.

People matter—but a CEO is not the whole company

Lists of famous founders can make AI look as though a handful of men personally invented every model, chip and policy before breakfast. That is both dull and false.

This book includes a person when her or his current role helps locate a consequential decision. Inclusion is not personal endorsement, sole credit or ownership of everyone else’s work.

Demis Hassabis’s CEO role locates Google DeepMind’s combined frontier-model and scientific-research direction. Mark Zuckerberg’s chair and CEO roles locate Meta’s decision to combine model development with distribution through its platforms. These descriptions do not mean a CEO personally chooses every training example, writes every model or owns every employee’s contribution. They tell us where high-level organisational choices are accountable.

C.C. Wei’s chair and CEO roles locate TSMC’s semiconductor-manufacturing strategy and capacity. Christophe Fouquet’s president and CEO role locates ASML’s lithography-equipment direction. Explain the boundary

Why can a research leader matter here without being called the sole inventor? Because the map records where consequential choices and contributions sit. It does not turn collaborative work into a celebrity biography. If your explanation is simply “important person,” you still need to name the decision or operating layer her role helps locate.

Read the next AI deal without the corporate fog

Try this fictional headline: “BrightMind partners with CloudCastle in a landmark $4 billion AI deal.” It sounds important and tells us almost nothing.

The useful questions are ordinary. Who is paying whom? Is the money an investment, a purchase or promised future spending? Who will supply the computers? Is the arrangement exclusive? Does either party receive ownership, licensing rights or a new distribution route? Has the capacity already been delivered?

Suppose the detailed announcement says CloudCastle will invest in BrightMind and rent it computing capacity over five years. Now we can translate it: CloudCastle supplies capital and cloud computers to BrightMind. BrightMind receives funding and a route to computing capacity. The announcement does not by itself prove CloudCastle owns BrightMind, that all $4 billion has been spent, or that the promised computers are already operating.

Certain means the relationship is directly documented and already in force or observable. Likely means strong evidence supports a conclusion that still includes an inference. Company-reported means the organisation announced a plan, forecast or future capacity that has not yet been independently established as delivered.

Those labels describe the evidence, not whether the deal is clever or good.

Your turn

“NorthStar takes a stake in PromptWorks and will make PromptWorks available through its marketplace.” Explain the two relationships without saying “partner.” The answer is: NorthStar acquires equity in PromptWorks, and NorthStar supplies a distribution route through its marketplace. Neither sentence proves NorthStar runs PromptWorks or built its model.

The pocket version is: who gives whom what; what changes; what remains unproved?

How this living map stays honest

This book will age. Companies change leaders, investments, licences, suppliers and products. The honest response is not pretending otherwise; it is showing where the facts can move.

Relationship claims link to company statements, regulatory pages or filings beside the relevant passage. A company announcement proves what the company announced. It does not prove a forecast was delivered. A filing or official page can also be corrected, limited or superseded.
