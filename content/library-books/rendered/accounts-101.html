Know the account before you paste.

A paid plan, work email or reassuring toggle does not answer the real question: are you authorized to put this exact material into this exact product, account and workspace? If any part is unknown, stop before the real material goes in.

Before you paste

  1. Name the exact door. Which product, account and workspace are you using? Does a connected app, custom action or third-party service receive any part of the input?
  2. Name the material. Does it contain credentials, personal data, confidential work, client information, regulated data or contract-restricted content?
  3. Check your authority. Do the employer, client, contract and data-classification rules permit this exact use in this exact account?
  4. Check the current provider rule. Read the official privacy, data-use, retention, sharing and connected-service documentation for the exact product and account—not a memory of how it worked last year.
  5. Stop when unknown. Do not paste the real material until the authorized owner resolves the missing answer. Minimize, anonymize or use an approved route only when that is genuinely permitted.

What “approved” actually means

Approved means someone with authority has allowed this tool, account and use for this kind of material under the applicable policy or agreement. It does not mean the app is on your work laptop, colleagues use it, you signed in with a work email, the plan is paid, or a privacy setting sounds reassuring.

Provider behavior can differ by product, account, plan, workspace, setting, region and date. Payment alone does not prove data-use, retention, sharing, security or contractual permission. A provider control cannot override an NDA, client restriction, employer policy or your lack of authority.

The stop rule.

If you cannot identify the exact account, your authority and the rule for this material, do not paste the real material. “Unknown” is a stop sign, not a request to guess.

Why deletion is not the plan

A product may offer deletion controls. Use them when appropriate, but do not treat them as a remedy you can promise in advance. Deletion does not make an unauthorized disclosure authorized, and connected services, retention obligations or organizational records may follow different rules.

Where the exact rule lives
