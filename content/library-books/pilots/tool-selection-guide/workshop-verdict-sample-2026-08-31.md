# ChatGPT or Claude? Choose the job before the brand

**Representative manuscript sample — checked 31 August 2026**

For the fictional workshop pack we tested, **do not buy a subscription first**.

An observed ChatGPT Free account produced useful initial files and completed a targeted correction after its data-analysis limit appeared. Paid Claude Code provided a controlled file-writing environment and effort settings, but it also introduced unsupported source claims. Neither route earned a universal quality crown.

The practical answer is conditional:

- choose **ChatGPT Free Chat** when you can supply a small permitted source pack, accept file or complete-text output, and inspect the result yourself;
- choose **Claude Chat Free** as an untested but documented no-cost alternative when its available file/Artifact route fits the same job;
- choose **Claude Code** when the job belongs in a real folder or repository and needs explicit file, command, test or version-control access;
- choose **Cowork** when a paid, multi-step computer job needs supported cloud execution or deliberately granted local/browser access; and
- choose **ChatGPT Work or Codex** when their supported deliverable or local implementation environment removes a specific bottleneck.

“I want the best AI” is not a usable requirement. “I need two editable Markdown files built only from these six sources, with checked arithmetic and no external actions” is.

## The seven questions that make the choice

### 1. What must exist at the end?

Name the usable artifact, not the activity.

For the workshop: `proposal.md` and `evidence.md`, not “help me plan an event.” A slide outline is not an editable deck. A generated song that cannot be downloaded is not a deliverable. A waveform preview is not a narration master.

### 2. Where are the permitted inputs?

Pasted text, uploaded files, a connected service and a local folder are different access routes. A web chat cannot see a folder merely because it is on your computer. A connector can carry the permissions of the connected account. A local agent may see more than you intended if you grant a broad folder.

Use the narrowest route that can reach the material it genuinely needs.

### 3. What actions are allowed?

Drafting an email and sending one are different jobs. Recommending a folder structure and moving files are different jobs. For the workshop test, every route was forbidden from booking, purchasing, contacting, sending or publishing.

Check the product’s actual permissions. A sentence saying “do not send” is useful context, but it is not a security control if the tool already has standing permission to act.

### 4. Must the result remain editable?

Choose the format before the generator. Markdown, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, SVG, layered design files, multitrack audio and source code offer different kinds of control. If you need to revise a budget formula next month, a screenshot is a dead end wearing nice typography.

### 5. Can you legally use and export it?

Plan price does not settle commercial rights, copyright, voice consent, stock licensing or export restrictions.

This matters sharply for media:

- Suno’s current Free download policy is changing on 3 September 2026;
- Udio’s official notice says audio, video and stem downloads are disabled;
- ElevenLabs distinguishes Free and paid publication rights, and voice cloning requires consent;
- Canva separates Free, Pro and Popular Music licensing; and
- Adobe’s commercial-use position does not guarantee copyright ownership in every jurisdiction.

Those are dated research findings, not hands-on output verdicts. Check the current rights screen for the exact asset before publication.

### 6. What will it cost in money and attention?

Count the whole job: subscription, credits, API usage, retries, manual repair, export, editing and review. A cheap generation that takes three hours to rescue may be expensive. A paid tool that removes no actual bottleneck is simply a new monthly relationship.

### 7. How will you know it worked?

Define checks that inspect the artifact:

- formulas recalculate when an input changes;
- agenda durations total 90 minutes;
- invitation contains no more than 120 words;
- every factual claim maps to the supplied source;
- the exported audio plays outside the generator;
- captions spell names correctly; and
- a website works on a phone, not only in a screenshot.

If you cannot observe success, the comparison will collapse into vibes.

## The workshop comparison

The same fictional source pack asked for a venue recommendation, budget, agenda, invitation and evidence file.

| Route actually observed | What it gave us | What failed | What the route establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT Free Chat; tooltip showed GPT-5.6 Luna | Initial downloadable files; correct venue and budget; later complete text revision and repair | A 22-person revision left 20-person catering and four dependent figures in the proposal; a data-analysis limit appeared | A real no-cost route completed the job after targeted correction in this account. It does not promise identical limits or tools for everyone. |
| Paid Claude Code; Sonnet 5 Medium | Actual files; correct harder-case venue and budget; explicit file-writing environment | Changed “dietary needs not collected” into an invented decision that collection was not planned or budgeted | Paid file execution did not guarantee that every explanation stayed inside the supplied facts. |
| Paid Claude Code; Sonnet 5 High | Actual files; same correct venue and budget | Approximate word-count claim; no material superiority established | High used 8,177 versus Medium’s 6,400 reported Sonnet output tokens and took 76.7 versus 71.8 seconds in one pair. This is not a general benchmark. |

The setups were not fully equivalent. ChatGPT continued one web conversation; Claude revisions used fresh Code sessions with previous files pasted into context. ChatGPT had its own web product tools; Claude was limited to one Write tool. Only Claude Medium versus High held the prompt and tool settings sufficiently constant for a bounded effort comparison.

So the verdict is not “ChatGPT beat Claude” or “Claude is better for files.” The verdict is:

> The Free route was adequate after one targeted repair. The paid route offered a different working environment, not immunity from errors.

## The switch trigger

Choose an adequate route, then write the condition that would make it inadequate.

For this workshop:

```text
Start with an available Free chat route using fictional sources. Switch only
if repeated tool limits prevent delivery of the complete editable documents,
the source set exceeds the supported context, or the job requires authorized
local file operations that Chat cannot perform. Do not switch merely because
the first draft needs a targeted correction.
```

That sentence protects you from two bad habits: buying before finding the bottleneck, and staying with a tool after it has become the bottleneck.

## When no AI is the right answer

Use an ordinary spreadsheet when you already know the rows and formulas. Use a word processor when the job is precise formatting rather than drafting. Use Audacity when the narration exists and you need deterministic audio editing. Use licensed stock music when exact rights and timing matter more than generating something novel.

AI earns a place when it reduces the work of interpreting, drafting, transforming, comparing or building—and when you can still verify the result.

## Media choices: researched, not yet tested

The workshop comparison cannot tell us which music or narration tool wins. Those lanes need their own same-input trials.

### Music

Decide first:

- Is this private experimentation or public/commercial use?
- Do you need a downloadable master, stems or precise duration?
- Must the track remain editable?
- Can you document the licence attached to the exported asset?

Suno, Udio, Canva music, licensed stock and a human composer are not interchangeable. Udio’s disabled downloads alone can remove it from a finished soundtrack job, however appealing the preview may be.

### Narration

Decide first:

- Is the voice fictional, stock, your own or another identifiable person’s?
- Do you have consent for cloning?
- Do you need commercial publication rights?
- Can you control pronunciation, pauses and revisions?
- Will you listen to the complete exported file against the approved script?

ElevenLabs, Descript, built-in product voices and a human narrator solve different versions of that job. A convincing voice is not permission to use someone’s identity.

### Research

Use ordinary web search when the question is small and you can open the sources. Use a source-grounded notebook when a defined document collection is the authority. Use an agentic research mode when the question needs multi-step discovery and the extra usage is justified. Citations make checking easier; they do not make a claim true.

## Your one-page decision card

Copy and complete this before choosing a tool:

```text
Final usable artifact:
Allowed inputs and where they live:
Actions allowed:
Actions prohibited:
Required editable/export format:
Privacy, consent and rights boundary:
Maximum money/credits/time:
Checks that inspect the final artifact:

```

You do not need a lifelong allegiance. You need a defensible choice for this job, with a clean exit when the facts change.
