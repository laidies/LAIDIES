# The ChatGPT Guide

## How to set it up, choose the right model and turn a conversation into finished work

**Working manuscript — checked September 6, 2026**

ChatGPT changes quickly. The durable method in this book is to give it a clear job, the right source material, sensible boundaries and a result you can inspect. Model names, effort choices, plan access and button locations are dated because they can change.

This guide is written for capable women who do not want a computer-science lecture before they can get something useful done. You do not need a paid plan, a model picker or a library of clever prompts to begin. Where a paid account adds capacity or an action-taking tool, the difference is called out. Paying can make a workflow faster or larger. It cannot make a weak source true, grant permission you do not have or replace your judgement.

# 1. Your first ten useful minutes

The quickest way to understand ChatGPT is to give it one job you already know how to judge.

Do not begin by asking what it can do. That question tends to produce a brochure. Begin with a real piece of work: turn rough notes into a clean email, compare three options against your criteria, explain a document you have to act on, or organize the first draft of a plan.

Try this with material you are allowed to use:

```text
I need to turn the notes below into an email to my director.

The email should:
- state the decision needed in the first paragraph;
- explain the two options accurately;
- recommend Option B and give the reason;
- stay under 300 words;
- sound warm, direct and professional;
- keep any missing fact in [square brackets] instead of inventing it.

Notes:
[paste the notes]

Before the email, list any contradiction or missing fact that could change the
recommendation. Then draft the complete email.
```

That prompt works because it answers the questions ChatGPT would otherwise have to guess: What are we making? Who is it for? What information governs it? What does good look like? What should happen when the information is incomplete?

Now inspect the answer. Check the decision, names, dates, numbers and anything presented as fact. Ask yourself whether the recommendation follows from the notes or merely sounds confident. If it is wrong in one place, correct that place. You do not need to begin a new chat and deliver a speech about disappointment.

```text
The second paragraph says Option B starts in October. The notes say the pilot
starts in November. Correct the month everywhere it appears, preserve the rest
of the email and return the complete revised email. Stop after the email.
```

This is the operating loop for the whole book:

**Give the job → supply the context → set the boundaries → inspect the result → repair the exact defect.**

You can make that loop more sophisticated. You can add files, search, a Project, a stronger model or a tool that creates the actual document. The loop does not change.

## What ChatGPT is doing in that exchange

It is using your instructions and the material in the conversation to generate a likely useful response. If it has tools available, it may also search, calculate, read files, create files or act in another application. Its fluent wording is not evidence that it read the right source, performed the calculation or changed the real artifact. Those are separate things you can check.

This distinction matters because the most dangerous result is rarely nonsense. It is a polished answer with one quiet wrong detail—the wrong date in four coordinated outputs, an old number carried into a new deck, or an action summary beside a file that was never changed.

Treat its completion message as a colleague saying, “I’ve finished.” Useful. Now look at the work.

## Applies to both Free and paid plans

The core loop, ordinary chat, clear briefs, pasted context, targeted corrections and human review apply to both. If your account does not show a model or effort picker, use the model provided and make the task smaller when necessary.

**Free route or limit:** Work in one bounded chunk. Paste only the material needed, request the complete text, copy it into your own document and inspect it there. Limits and available tools can vary.

**Paid difference or extension:** An eligible paid account may offer more model choices, higher usage, larger jobs, Projects, Work, connectors or file-making tools. Exact access varies by plan, account, administrator, device, region and rollout.

**No equivalent:** If a paid surface can operate a local application or continue a cloud job while your computer is off, ordinary Free Chat is not the same capability. The Free route is to have ChatGPT prepare the content or instructions while you perform the real action yourself.

# 2. Chat, Projects, Work and Codex

Four labels can appear near one another and still answer different questions. Choose the place where the job should happen before worrying about the strongest model.

## Chat: think, ask, draft and refine

Use Chat when the useful result is mainly the conversation: an explanation, a decision aid, a draft, a comparison or a set of instructions you will apply yourself.

Chat is usually the cleanest place to begin because you can see the source material, the request and the answer together. It is also the honest Free route for most jobs in this book.

Good Chat jobs include:

- turning notes into a clear message;
- explaining a policy in ordinary language;
- comparing options against criteria you provide;
- preparing interview questions;
- developing a first outline;
- reviewing text for a named problem;
- troubleshooting a formula or process.

Chat can sometimes use files, search and other tools. The defining feature is still that you are working through a conversation. It does not automatically mean ChatGPT can reach a folder on your computer or take action in an external account.

## Projects: keep related work together

A Project gives continuing work a home. It can group related chats, files, sources and project instructions so you do not have to rebuild the entire context every Monday morning.

Use a Project when the same facts and preferences will govern several conversations: a quarterly planning cycle, a job search, a research topic, a recurring newsletter or a home renovation.

A Project is not a magical window into every file on your computer. Upload or connect the material you want it to use. Remove or replace stale files. If the project instruction says the 2025 policy is authoritative while you have uploaded a 2026 revision, you have built a tiny argument into the walls.

A useful Project instruction is short:

```text
This Project supports the 2027 customer-retention plan.

Audience: the executive team.
Source authority: approved-plan.md wins; current-metrics.xlsx supplies all
numbers; brainstorm-notes.md may suggest ideas but may not create commitments.
Writing: Canadian English with -ize spellings. Warm, direct, no inflated claims.
Missing facts: mark [NEEDS DECISION].
Never send, publish or change connected data without approval.
```

Put changing task details in the current prompt. Put stable, genuinely repeated guidance in the Project instructions. Do not make the instructions a storage cupboard for every preference you have ever had.

## Work: delegate a reviewable result

Use Work when you want ChatGPT to complete a task that ends in something you can review: a brief, analysis, presentation, workflow or file. Depending on the product and account, Work can use supplied files, plugins and approved tools. In the desktop app, local work can use available computer files, applications or browser context. Cloud work can continue away from the computer and later be continued from web or mobile.

The practical difference is not “Work thinks harder.” Work changes the execution surface. You are asking for a completed result, possibly across several steps or tools, instead of only an answer in the chat.

Use it when file handling is the bottleneck. If you need four coordinated outputs from three source files, Work may be helpful. If you need one paragraph, it may be ceremony.

An action-capable surface needs a clearer permission boundary:

```text
You may read the three supplied files and create drafts in the output folder.
You may calculate and cross-check the figures.
Do not email anyone, publish, overwrite the source files, change account
settings, spend money or connect another service. Ask before any external action.
```

## Codex: work on the actual codebase

Codex is the developer-oriented surface for code, files, commands and tests. You do not have to be a developer to ask Codex for help, but you do need a real code project or technical workspace for the distinction to be useful.

Use Codex when the task is the website, app, automation or repository itself: fix the mobile menu, add a filter to an existing page, trace an error, update a data file and its tests, or review a proposed code change.

Do not force Codex into a job that ends with an email draft. It can write an email, just as a cordless drill can stir paint. The question is whether its working environment solves the bottleneck you actually have.

## And where does Cowork fit?

Cowork belongs to Claude, made by Anthropic. It is not a ChatGPT setting. Claude Chat, Cowork and Claude Code are covered properly in the companion Claude Guide.

The broad comparison is straightforward:

- ChatGPT Chat and Claude Chat are conversational surfaces.
- ChatGPT Work and Claude Cowork are action-oriented work surfaces, with different features and access.
- Codex and Claude Code are developer-oriented environments for real files, commands and code.

These pairings help you orient yourself. They do not prove equal capabilities, plans, privacy terms or performance.

## The same goal, routed honestly

Suppose you have rough notes, an approved brief and a spreadsheet and need a leadership proposal.

In **Chat**, ask for the complete content, then copy it into your own presentation and document.

In a **Project**, keep the approved brief, current figures and stable writing rules together while you develop several versions over time.

In **Work**, ask it to create the presentation, speaker notes, one-page summary and email draft as reviewable files, if your account supports that workflow.

In **Codex**, do nothing—unless the proposal is specifically about changing a real website or app and you want it to inspect or implement that code. Codex does not need a cameo in every example.

That is the useful distinction. Surface choice is about where the work happens and what it can touch. Model choice comes later.

# 3. Set up ChatGPT without over-setting it up

Setup should remove repeated friction. It should not take longer than your first three useful jobs.

Open Settings from the application menu. On the desktop app, the documented shortcut is Command-comma on macOS and Control-comma on Windows. Exact sections and controls can vary by account and surface, so use the labels you actually see rather than assuming a screenshot in this book is your destiny.

## Personality changes the manner, not the mind

The current personalization guide includes choices such as Friendly, Pragmatic and None. A personality setting changes how ChatGPT communicates. It does not upgrade the model, improve factual accuracy or unlock tools.

Choose the style that makes answers easiest for you to use. If Friendly becomes too chatty for a particular job, say what you want in that prompt:

```text
Answer in three short paragraphs. Lead with the recommendation. No preamble,
encouragement or recap.
```

That instruction is more useful than switching your entire account personality for one board memo.

## Custom instructions hold stable preferences

Use custom instructions for things that are true across many conversations:

```text
Use Canadian English with -ize and -ization spellings.
Lead with the answer.
Explain unfamiliar technical terms when they first become useful.
If a fact may have changed, say what date it was checked.
Separate verified facts, inferences and recommendations.
Do not treat a polished draft as proof that a file was changed or a check ran.
```

Do not put confidential client data, a temporary deadline or the details of one project there. Stable preferences belong in custom instructions. Current project facts belong with the project.

After adding an instruction, test it in a new chat. Ask for a short sample and check whether the behaviour changed. A saved sentence that the product does not apply where you expect is not doing useful work.

## Memory is background, not your records system

Memory can make recurring conversations easier by retaining useful background. Treat it as convenience, not as the authoritative home for a policy, number, deadline or approval.

If a fact must control the answer, provide it in the current prompt or maintain it in a named Project file. You should be able to point to the source. “I think ChatGPT remembered it” is not a source-management plan.

Review memory controls and saved items periodically. Remove material that is wrong, too personal or no longer useful. If you do not want a conversation to contribute to future personalization, use the privacy controls available in your account and verify what the selected mode actually does.

## Appearance and accessibility are work settings

Choose text size, theme, voice or accessibility options that reduce effort. A setting does not have to improve the model to improve your work. If you are reviewing a dense table on a phone, moving the task to a larger screen may be a more effective upgrade than raising the reasoning effort.

For voice use, remember that speaking a request does not make private surroundings private. Check what the microphone can hear, especially around names, health information, client details and workplace conversations.

## Data and privacy controls deserve an actual look

Before using personal, client or workplace material, open the current data controls and your organization's policy. Consumer controls, business terms, enterprise settings, temporary chats and API retention are not interchangeable.

Use this three-question test:

1. Am I allowed to put this information into this product?
2. Does the account or workspace apply the privacy and retention terms I think it does?
3. Does the task require the real information, or would a redacted copy do?

Redact names, account numbers, addresses, medical details and confidential figures when they do not change the task. Do not upload a full mailbox because you need one thread. Do not connect an entire drive because you need one folder.

## Applies to both Free and paid plans

Everyone benefits from setting a usable communication style, checking data controls and keeping authoritative facts in the current context.

**Free route or limit:** Use short custom instructions where available, maintain a small rules note you can paste, and work with redacted excerpts. Available memory and privacy controls can vary.

**Paid difference or extension:** Paid or managed workspaces may add model, Project, retention, connector and administrator options. Your organization may need to enable them.

**No equivalent:** A personal account cannot imitate an enterprise legal agreement or administrator policy by adding a sentence to a prompt. If your workplace has not approved the product for the data, use non-sensitive sample material or the approved workplace tool.

# 4. Give it context it can actually use

Context is the information available to ChatGPT while it works: your current request, earlier messages, attached files, Project instructions, retrieved sources and sometimes saved preferences. More context can help. More context can also hide the instruction that matters under forty pages of old meeting notes.

The useful target is not maximum context. It is enough current, authoritative context to do the job.

## Build a small source pack

For a substantial task, label each source by its job:

```text
SOURCE 1 — APPROVED BRIEF
Status: controls audience, scope, promises and required outputs.
If another source conflicts with it, follow this source.

SOURCE 2 — CURRENT FIGURES
Status: controls all numbers. Use only the rows marked Final.

SOURCE 3 — ROUGH NOTES
Status: ideas and background only. Do not turn an idea into a commitment unless
the approved brief also includes it.
```

This prevents a common failure. The model does not know that the colourful brainstorming note is less authoritative than the dry signed brief unless you tell it. Without an authority order, it may blend both into a confident third version that nobody approved.

## Name the file, not just “the attachment”

If you have several files, refer to each by name and purpose:

```text
Use approved-brief.pdf for scope, metrics-final.xlsx for all figures and
director-notes.md for tone cues only. Ignore proposal-old.docx; it is supplied
only so you can identify language that must not be carried forward.
```

If a crucial fact is buried in a large file, point to the section or sheet. If the product can search the file, ask it to quote or map the relevant passage before relying on it. If the file is scanned, password-protected, damaged or visually complex, confirm that the text and tables were actually read.

## Put the question after the material when the pack is long

For a short conversation, ordinary order is fine. For a long source pack, provide the material first, clearly separate it from your instructions and put the final question or job after it. This reduces the chance that your request disappears above a wall of documents.

Headings work:

```text
## Sources
[source material]

## Job
[what to produce]

## Boundaries
[what not to do]

## Done when
[observable finish line]
```

You do not need angle brackets, pseudo-code or a theatrical “role.” Structure is useful when it separates different kinds of information. It is decoration when the request is already obvious.

## Examples teach taste better than adjectives

“Make it professional” can mean spare and direct, warm and diplomatic, formal and cautious, or a beige paragraph that has been through twelve committees.

If style matters, provide one short approved example and say what to copy:

```text
Use the attached approved-email.md as a style example. Copy its direct opening,
short paragraphs and calm tone. Do not copy its facts, names or structure when
they do not fit this message.
```

One accurate example is usually better than six adjectives. Several diverse examples can help when a pattern genuinely has range. A pile of nearly identical examples can cause the model to imitate accidents instead of the rule.

## Context files, rules and Skills answer different needs

A **context file** supplies information for the job: the brief, spreadsheet, policy, approved copy or research notes.

A **rule** states something that should remain true: which source wins, where outputs go, what may never be overwritten, or what “finished” means.

A **Skill** packages a repeated workflow or specialized body of guidance so an action-capable system can load it when relevant. Build one only after the workflow has worked manually. A Skill does not grant permission, make a source accurate or guarantee execution.

For ordinary Chat, a saved recipe note may do the whole job:

```text
PROPOSAL RECIPE
Required inputs: approved brief, current figures, rough notes.
Outputs: six-slide outline, speaker notes, one-page summary, email draft.
Authority: approved brief > current figures > rough notes.
Checks: every number maps to a Final spreadsheet row; every commitment appears
in the approved brief; the same recommendation and dates appear in all outputs.
Stop: return the four complete drafts and the source-check table. Do not send.
```

Run that recipe twice. Notice what goes wrong. Then decide whether it deserves a Project instruction or Skill.

## The disposable-camera rule

A 24-exposure disposable camera made each frame a choice. You did not photograph the entire room continuously and promise to find the useful moment later. Context works similarly: select the material that can change the answer.

The comparison stops there. A model can sometimes search or summarize a large collection, and a camera cannot. The useful lesson is selection. Uploading everything transfers the organization problem into the prompt; it does not solve it.

# 5. A prompt is a brief, not an incantation

There is no required magic syntax. Good prompting is mostly good briefing followed by intelligent correction.

For a small job, one natural sentence may be enough:

```text
Rewrite this paragraph so the decision appears first and the tone stays warm.
Keep every fact and stay under 120 words.
```

For a larger job, use five parts:

1. **Goal:** what useful result should exist?
2. **Context:** what does ChatGPT need to know or read?
3. **Authority:** which source wins if information conflicts?
4. **Boundaries:** what must it preserve, avoid or ask before doing?
5. **Done when:** what observable evidence proves the job is complete?

Here is the shape:

```text
Goal: [the finished result]

Use: [the relevant sources and what each controls]

Authority: [the order when sources conflict]

Boundaries: [privacy, scope, permissions, exclusions, length, tone]

Done when: [the checks and deliverables]
```

## Ask for evidence of the check

“Check your work carefully” describes an activity. It does not tell you what happened.

Ask for evidence you can inspect:

```text
For every number in the summary, return a table with:
- the exact number used;
- the spreadsheet sheet and row;
- the output or slide where it appears;
- PASS if they match, or the exact discrepancy.
```

For a 90-minute mixtape, “make sure it fits” is weak. Ask for the arithmetic for Side A and Side B, with neither exceeding 45 minutes. The calculation makes the invisible check visible. It also gives you somewhere specific to look when the cassette eats your closing track.

## Separate content from action

Drafting an email and sending it are different permissions. Preparing a purchase comparison and placing the order are different permissions. Writing a social post and publishing it are different permissions.

Say so:

```text
Draft the email and address line. Do not send it. Stop with the complete draft
and a list of any detail I must confirm.
```

An existing connector or account permission can make an action technically possible. That does not mean your conversational request authorized it.

## Tell it when to continue and when to ask

For reversible work, you may want steady progress:

```text
Make reasonable assumptions for formatting and wording and list them at the end.
Continue through reversible drafting and file creation without pausing. Ask one
question only if the answer would change the approved scope, external action,
spend or irreversible result.
```

For a decision-heavy job, ask it to stop after a plan:

```text
Inspect the source pack and propose the output structure, source authority,
risks and checks. Do not draft or change files yet. Stop with the plan and the
one decision that would most change it.
```

Planning earns its keep when a wrong direction would create meaningful rework. A seven-stage plan for fixing a heading is paperwork wearing a tiny hard hat.

## Ask for a complete result

If you need to copy the answer into a document, say “return the complete revised text,” not “tell me what to change.” If you need a file changed, ask to inspect the actual file after the change, not only to summarize it.

When a job has several outputs, name all of them and the consistency rule:

```text
The presentation, speaker notes, one-page summary and email must use the same
recommendation, date, budget and approved commitments. If you correct one of
those facts, update every dependent output.
```

## What a strong prompt cannot do

It cannot create missing evidence. It cannot make an unapproved promise approved. It cannot guarantee that a changing fact is current without a current source. It cannot override a connector's enforced permissions or your workplace policy. It cannot turn a plausible sentence into proof.

Prompting improves the conditions for good work. Inspection decides whether the work is usable.

# 6. Choose Luna, Terra, Sol or Astra

As of September 6, 2026, the practical OpenAI model ladder in the source guidance has four names. Your account may show some, all or none of them. Use the labels you actually have. If there is no picker, skip this chapter's shopping trip and use the supplied model.

## Luna: quick, clear, repeated work

Choose Luna for narrow jobs with a stable pattern and an answer you can check cheaply: classify a clean list, extract named fields, reformat approved text, produce many short variations under firm rules or summarize a bounded source.

Good Luna brief:

```text
For each row below, return Department, Owner and Due Date exactly as written.
Use UNKNOWN when a field is missing. Do not infer. Return CSV only.
```

Luna is a poor choice when the real job is deciding which policy applies across conflicting sources or maintaining several linked arguments through a long document. You may save time on the first answer and spend it again in repair.

Prompt it with a crisp schema, examples of edge cases and an explicit no-inference rule. Do not ask for a philosophical review of the extraction method.

## Terra: capable everyday work

Choose Terra for bounded professional work that needs judgement: drafting from a small source pack, comparing options, organizing a proposal, explaining a document, analyzing a modest spreadsheet or coordinating several clear outputs.

The leadership-proposal example in this book is a Terra/Medium-sized job: three bounded sources, a clear authority order and four outputs whose facts can be checked against one another.

Prompt Terra with the goal, source roles, audience, finish line and evidence check. Give it room to make ordinary wording choices. Do not bury it under instructions for hypothetical failures you have never seen.

## Sol: demanding professional work

Choose Sol when the job is more open-ended and quality depends on depth, synthesis or polish: a substantial strategy, a nuanced research synthesis, a difficult editorial rewrite, a complex decision with several trade-offs or a long professional deliverable.

Sol becomes worth the extra capacity when Terra misses something you can name: it flattens meaningful disagreement, loses the argument over a long report, or produces technically correct sections that do not form one coherent recommendation.

Prompt Sol with the decision the work must support, the evidence standard, the tensions it must preserve and the shape of the final deliverable. Ask it to distinguish fact, inference and recommendation. Stronger does not mean unbounded; give it a stopping condition.

## Astra: the hardest end-to-end work

OpenAI describes GPT-6 Astra as its strongest current model for complex reasoning, coding, computer use, research and document creation. Use it when the job crosses domains, applications or many linked sources and stronger synthesis can materially change the result.

A genuine Astra-sized job might require reconciling policies from six departments, analyzing several workbooks, researching changing regulations, preparing coordinated documents and applying approved changes through tools. It is not “write a nicer email.”

Official guidance notes several behaviours worth controlling. Astra can be sensitive to long instruction stacks. It may pause for a focused question where you expected it to proceed. It may delegate less often than your workflow needs. In coding work, it may test a small change more broadly than necessary.

Add the controls that fit the job:

```text
Authority: signed-policy.pdf wins over departmental notes; current legislation
wins over every internal source. Preserve conflicts in the evidence table.

Autonomy: continue through reversible research, analysis and drafting. Ask only
if a missing answer changes legal scope, external action, spend or an approved
commitment.

Delegation: investigate Finance, Operations and Legal as separate read-only
workstreams where that reduces context mixing. Reconcile contradictions before
drafting the recommendation.

Verification: run the named formula reconciliation and source-link check once.
After they pass, do not add broader reviews unless a failure or new change
justifies them. Do not create new tests for unchanged material.

Finish: deliver the three named files, the source map and unresolved decisions.
Do not publish, send or expand into implementation.
```

These instructions reduce wasted work. They do not guarantee correctness.

## This is casting, not a ranking

The newest model is not automatically the right model. Luna can be the better choice for 2,000 clean extractions because it is quick and economical. Terra can produce the bounded proposal perfectly well. Sol may earn its place on a nuanced strategy. Astra earns its place when the larger system requires it.

Do not compare models by giving each a different prompt, different tools and different finish line. Run the same harmless representative task in a fresh session. Record whether it completed the job, what was wrong, how much repair you did, how long it took and any usage the product exposes. That is evidence for your work—not a universal model league table.

## App choices and API choices are different

In eligible ChatGPT Power accounts, current documented choices may include Terra Light, Sol Light, Sol Medium, Astra Light, Astra Medium and Astra Extra High. Options vary by plan, account, client and rollout.

The Astra API uses a different list: `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh` and `max`, and it does not support `none`. API access and billing are separate from a ChatGPT subscription. “Pro” execution in an API is not the same thing as a ChatGPT Pro plan.

If you do not build software with the API, you do not need to memorize that paragraph. You only need to avoid assuming a developer screenshot shows a control your ChatGPT account should have.

# 7. Choose an effort level without putting a tiara on it

The model answers **which capability** you are using. Effort answers **how much reasoning time or work** that model should apply, where the surface offers the choice. Your subscription answers **what access and capacity** the account has. These are separate controls.

Start with the normal or lowest setting likely to complete the job. Raise it for an observable reason:

- the lower setting missed a named dependency or contradiction;
- several connected changes must remain consistent;
- evidence conflicts and the source hierarchy must be resolved;
- a long multi-file plan has consequences you cannot check one piece at a time.

“This matters to me” is not evidence that Extra High will improve it. Importance may justify more careful human review even when the AI task is simple.

## Light or Low

Use the lightest setting for a well-scoped task with a short path and cheap verification: extraction, formatting, a direct rewrite, a clean summary or a small code explanation.

Make the prompt lean. Name the output format and edge cases. If a changing fact matters, explicitly request current search where the surface supports it. Do not compensate for low effort with five pages of anxious instructions.

Stop or step up when it misses a dependency that the prompt clearly exposed.

## Medium

Medium is the sensible working setting for many professional tasks. Use it when the job contains a few connected sources, requires judgement or ends in several coordinated sections.

The proposal case uses Medium because the model must distinguish approved commitments from brainstormed ideas, carry spreadsheet figures into four outputs and keep the recommendation consistent. Every element is bounded and inspectable.

Stop when the source table and outputs agree. Do not escalate because the draft is not yet in your voice; first give a targeted style correction or approved example.

## High

Use High when the mechanism requires deeper synthesis: several interacting constraints, a long argument, conflicting evidence or a lower effort's demonstrated failure.

Before raising effort, name what must improve:

```text
The Medium run summarized each department separately but did not resolve the
conflict between the staffing plan and budget cap. At High, use the authority
order below, trace that dependency and return one reconciled recommendation.
Keep the sources, tools and finish line unchanged so I can compare the result.
```

Now you are buying more work for a reason you can judge.

High effort is a setting, not a tiara. It does not make the answer important, prestigious or automatically right. Without a bounded finish line, higher effort can produce more commentary and more review instead of a better result.

## Extra High

Use Extra High for an unusually demanding synthesis where High still misses a named connection, or when one long end-to-end result must preserve many linked constraints. It may take longer and consume more usage.

Do not use Extra High merely to avoid writing a clear brief. It cannot resolve an authority conflict you never described. It cannot know that a note was aspirational rather than approved.

With Astra, be especially clear about authority, autonomy, delegation and verification. Tell it how much testing is enough and what it must not expand into.

## Max

Max gives an eligible model more room for one especially hard task and may require enabling. Current guidance says most work does not need it.

Use it after a lower run exposes a consequential reasoning gap that you can reproduce. Preserve the same prompt and evaluation. Give the output enough room to finish. Stop after the named deliverables and checks.

If the job is really five independent jobs, splitting them may be clearer and cheaper than asking one Max run to hold everything at once.

## Ultra

Ultra is a specialist route for separable workstreams coordinated through subagents where the product supports it. It is unnecessary for most people and most work.

Use it when parallel investigation materially helps: separate teams can inspect different evidence sets, then one coordinator reconciles them. Do not create five workstreams for a three-page memo. Delegation has overhead and can spread inconsistent assumptions unless the final reconciliation is explicit.

## If your account does not show effort

Nothing is wrong. Use the model provided. Improve the brief, reduce the source pack, split a large job into inspectable stages and apply the same checks. Typing “use Max effort” into the chat does not change a product control that is not present.

## Applies to both Free and paid plans

Everyone can scale the **job**: make it smaller, clearer and easier to check. Everyone can compare an initial result with a targeted repair.

**Free route or limit:** Use the provided model and default effort. Break large work into source review, outline, draft and check. Carry forward an explicit handoff instead of assuming a new chat knows the old one.

**Paid difference or extension:** Eligible accounts may show model and effort controls. Compare settings on the same representative job before adopting an expensive default.

**No equivalent:** A prompt cannot unlock an effort setting, model or specialist orchestration mode that your account does not provide.

# 8. The complete job: build a leadership-ready proposal

Here is a job people actually have: your director needs a recommendation for Monday's leadership meeting. You have rough notes from several conversations, an approved brief and a spreadsheet with the current figures. She wants a short presentation, speaker notes, a one-page summary and an email draft.

The problem is not getting ChatGPT to write four polished things. The problem is keeping the same approved facts in all four.

This is a constructed teaching case, not a transcript of a consumer-account test. Its purpose is to make source authority and cross-output checking visible.

## The starting material

**File 1: `approved-brief.md`**

```text
Decision needed
Approve a three-month client-response pilot for the Western region.

Audience
Executive leadership team.

Recommendation
Start October 15 with the Customer Success team. Review results on January 20.

Approved scope
- 12 participating employees
- two existing workflow tools
- weekly manager check-in
- no change to customer-facing service hours

Approved success measures
- median first-response time
- reopened cases
- employee-reported administrative time

Budget ceiling
$18,000 CAD.

Do not promise
Head-count reductions, guaranteed savings or expansion beyond the Western region.
```

**File 2: `current-figures.csv`**

```text
Metric,Current,Target,Status
Median first-response time,19.4 hours,12 hours,Final
Reopened cases,14.8%,12%,Final
Administrative time,9.5 hours/week,7 hours/week,Final
Estimated pilot cost,$16,750 CAD,,Final
Possible annual saving,$210,000 CAD,,Draft
```

**File 3: `rough-notes.md`**

```text
Leadership dislikes long decks. Six slides maximum.
Finance asked whether this could save 30% next year.
Someone suggested a monthly live clinic for every region.
Marta wants us to emphasize customer experience, not cost cutting.
Could potentially reduce two roles later? Very preliminary. Do not circulate.
Need a clear go/no-go decision.
```

The source order is deliberate. The approved brief controls promises and scope. The spreadsheet controls current figures, but only rows marked Final. The notes can influence emphasis; they cannot create a commitment.

## Pick the surface, model and effort

Use **Chat** if you want complete content to copy into your own files. Use **Work** if your eligible account can create the reviewable presentation and document files. A **Project** helps if this proposal will continue through revisions. **Codex is not needed.**

Choose **Terra at Medium** where available. This is a bounded professional job with three sources and four connected outputs. Luna may lose nuance across the source hierarchy. Sol is reasonable if the narrative remains weak after a targeted revision. Astra is unnecessary unless the real version spans many departments, changing external research and action-capable tools.

If your account offers no choice, use what it provides.

## The full initial brief

```text
Goal
Turn the three supplied sources into a leadership-ready proposal for the
Western client-response pilot.

Outputs
1. A six-slide presentation outline with slide titles and complete slide copy.
2. Speaker notes for each slide, no more than 120 words per slide.
3. A one-page executive summary, no more than 550 words.
4. An email draft from the project lead to the executive leadership team asking
   for the decision. Do not send it.

Source authority
- approved-brief.md controls the decision, recommendation, scope, dates,
  promises and exclusions.
- current-figures.csv controls numbers, but use only rows marked Final.
- rough-notes.md may guide emphasis and questions. It may not create a promise,
  scope item or fact.
- If sources conflict, follow the higher source and record the conflict. Do not
  blend them.

Audience and style
Busy executive leaders. Lead with the decision. Use Canadian English with
-ize spellings. Be direct, calm and specific. Explain the customer and employee
reason before the operational detail. Do not exaggerate certainty.

Boundaries
- Do not browse.
- Do not use the Draft annual-saving figure.
- Do not promise 30% savings, head-count changes, a live clinic or expansion.
- Do not send, publish or alter the source files.
- Mark a material missing fact as [NEEDS DECISION].

Checks
After drafting, return a source-check table for every number, date, commitment
and success measure. Name the source line or CSV row and every output where it
appears. Confirm that the recommendation and decision request agree across all
four outputs.

Done when
Return the four complete outputs and source-check table. Stop there.
```

Notice what this prompt does not include: “You are a world-renowned management consultant.” The job, audience, sources and finish line do more useful work than imaginary awards.

## Inspect the response in the right order

Do not begin by debating whether Slide 2 should be punchier. Inspect the facts first.

1. Does every output ask for the same decision?
2. Are October 15 and January 20 correct everywhere?
3. Are the 12 employees, three measures and $18,000 ceiling consistent?
4. Is the estimated cost $16,750 CAD and clearly an estimate?
5. Did any Draft number or rough-note idea become a promise?
6. Does the email remain a draft?

Then inspect usefulness. Can a leader understand why the pilot exists, what approval means and what will be measured? Do the slides stand on their own? Do the speaker notes add explanation instead of reading the slide aloud? Does the summary fit one page in the actual document?

## A plausible failure

Suppose the presentation says:

> The pilot will save 30% next year and will expand through monthly clinics in every region.

That sentence did not come from nowhere. Both ideas appeared in the rough notes. The failure is that ChatGPT treated ideas as approved commitments, ignored the authority order and repeated them in a leadership artifact. That could cause leaders to approve a proposal they were not actually offered—or reject it because the unapproved promise feels reckless.

“Be more careful” is not the repair. Use the exact evidence:

```text
The presentation turns two rough-note ideas into commitments: 30% savings and
monthly clinics in every region. approved-brief.md prohibits guaranteed savings
and expansion beyond the Western region. Remove both claims from the
presentation, speaker notes, one-page summary and email.

Preserve the approved Western three-month pilot, October 15 start, January 20
review, 12 employees, three success measures and $16,750 CAD estimated cost.
Rerun the source-check table for every commitment and number. Return the four
complete corrected outputs. Stop there.
```

The correction names the defect, supplies the governing fact, identifies every dependent output and protects the good work. It also asks for the complete result, so you are not left assembling four patches by hand.

## What the final deliverable should contain

A sound six-slide structure might be:

1. **Decision requested** — approve the bounded Western pilot.
2. **Why this matters now** — current response time, reopened cases and administrative burden.
3. **What the pilot changes** — 12 employees, two existing tools, weekly check-in, unchanged service hours.
4. **How success will be judged** — the three approved measures and targets.
5. **Cost, timing and boundaries** — $16,750 estimate, $18,000 ceiling, October 15 to January 20, no expansion promise.
6. **The decision and next checkpoint** — go/no-go now and results review on January 20.

The speaker notes should explain the logic and uncertainty. The one-page summary should let a leader make the decision without the deck. The email should state the request, link or attach the materials and leave sending to the human owner.

## Free and paid routes

**Applies to both:** Use the same sources, authority order, brief, check and targeted repair.

**Free route or limit:** Paste the three short sources into Chat. Request one output at a time if the full job hits a usage or length limit. Carry the same source authority into each prompt. Ask for a final reconciliation table, then copy the content into your presentation and document software. Check the actual files after formatting.

**Paid difference or extension:** In an eligible Project or Work task, supply the files once and ask for coordinated, reviewable artifacts. Specify file types and output names. Inspect each actual file, not only the summary.

**No equivalent:** If Work can create files or operate an application in your environment, Free Chat does not become that tool because you asked assertively. Its honest route is complete content plus manual file creation.

## What travels to your work

Replace the fictional sources with your permitted material. Keep the roles:

- one source controls approval and scope;
- one source controls current facts or numbers;
- lower-authority notes provide ideas without becoming promises;
- one brief defines every output and check;
- one targeted correction repairs every dependent place.

That is the useful move. The presentation format is incidental. Source authority is the mechanism.

# 9. Research that survives a source check

ChatGPT can help search, organize evidence and explain disagreement. It can also give you a fluent summary of an article that does not support the sentence you are about to publish.

Begin by defining the research decision:

```text
Research whether Canadian employers should change their internal AI-use policy
this quarter. I need current primary sources, the date each was updated, the
exact claim it supports, important uncertainty and the practical policy
decision it changes. Separate law, regulator guidance, vendor policy and expert
opinion. Do not treat a search snippet or another AI summary as a source.
```

Ask for source type, date and a direct link beside each material claim. Then open the links. Check that the page exists, the date is current, the quoted or paraphrased passage says what the summary claims and the jurisdiction fits.

When sources disagree, keep the disagreement:

```text
Do not average these positions. Create a table with the exact point of
disagreement, each source's evidence, jurisdiction and date, and what additional
evidence would change the decision. Then recommend the most defensible action
and label it as a recommendation.
```

Use official documents for plan access, pricing, retention, laws and product controls. A respected practitioner's workflow can suggest a method to test. It cannot establish your plan entitlement or legal duty.

For current events, ask ChatGPT to search when that feature is available and check the sources yourself. A model's training knowledge has a cutoff; a web tool can retrieve newer material, but retrieval still does not guarantee correct interpretation.

**Applies to both:** define the decision, request direct sources, open them and separate fact from inference.

**Free route or limit:** Search in smaller questions, keep a manual source table and verify every material link. If current search is unavailable, use your browser and give ChatGPT the authoritative excerpts.

**Paid difference or extension:** Deep research or action-capable tools may inspect more material and produce a larger evidence set. They still need a source hierarchy, date boundary and human check.

**No equivalent:** A summary without accessible sources is not equivalent to source-backed research.

# 10. Documents, spreadsheets, presentations and images

“Make me a deck” describes a file type. It does not describe the decision the deck must support.

For any artifact, name four things:

- source material;
- output type and structure;
- facts and design elements that must remain consistent;
- how you will inspect the real result.

## Documents

State audience, length, required sections, source authority and editable format. Ask for headings that predict what each section contains. If the result must fit two pages, inspect the rendered document; a word count is not page proof.

## Spreadsheets

Name sheets, columns, formulas, currency, date format and error handling. Require a formula map and reconciliation:

```text
Create a budget with Inputs, Monthly View and Summary sheets. Keep assumptions
only on Inputs. Every calculated cell must use a formula, not a typed total.
Return the grand-total formula, the three subtotal formulas and a reconciliation
showing that subtotals equal the grand total. Use CAD.
```

Open the workbook. Change one harmless input and confirm dependent totals update. A screenshot of a spreadsheet can look correct while containing typed numbers where formulas belong.

## Presentations

Give each slide one job. Ask for complete speaker notes when the presenter needs them. Check for tiny text, clipped content, inaccessible colour contrast, invented logos and decorative images that contradict the message.

## Images

Describe the purpose, format, composition, style, palette, required objects and forbidden changes. Generated text in images is still a common inspection point; use editable text layers when exact wording matters. Check hands, faces, object relationships, signage and whether the image actually teaches or supports the content.

## The actual artifact wins

ChatGPT may say the workbook formulas were checked or the presentation opens correctly. Verify the file. Preview it, download it, reopen it and exercise the part that matters. If the product cannot preview that format, open it in the intended application.

Do not confuse an integrity check with a quality review. A file can open successfully and still make a terrible recommendation.

# 11. Tools, permissions and Skills

A model generates and reasons. A tool lets it do something: search the web, read a drive, create an image, calculate, operate a browser or work with files. A permission defines what that tool may reach or change. A Skill supplies reusable instructions or knowledge. These are separate layers.

Before connecting anything, ask whether the job requires it. If you need one public webpage, you may not need your whole browser history. If you need one folder, do not grant an entire drive by reflex.

Write permissions by consequence:

```text
You may read the folder named Proposal Sources and create new drafts in Output.
Do not overwrite originals. Ask before sending a message, publishing, deleting,
moving source files, spending money, changing permissions or connecting another
service.
```

Inspect standing permissions as well as the prompt. Your sentence does not necessarily narrow a connector that already has broad technical access.

## When a Skill is worth making

Create a Skill after a workflow has succeeded manually more than once and the inputs, steps, checks and stop condition are stable.

A useful Skill description is narrow:

```text
Use when turning an approved internal brief and current metrics workbook into a
leadership proposal. Require both sources. Create the four named draft outputs
and a source-check table. Never send, publish or use Draft rows. Stop when dates,
figures and commitments agree.
```

Test it on harmless sample data. Check that it triggers for the intended job and stays quiet elsewhere. A collection of overlapping Skills can create conflicting instructions and consume attention.

## When a saved note is better

If you use ordinary Chat, keep the recipe in a note. Paste only the part needed. This is not a consolation prize; it is transparent and easy to edit. Automatic loading is convenient only when the loaded instruction is current and relevant.

# 12. Codex when you have a real website, app or repository

Codex belongs in this book because some readers own a website, internal tool or app. It does not belong in the leadership proposal simply to prove we know it exists.

Suppose your organization's real website has a Resources page. On mobile, the topic filter opens but choosing a topic does not update the results. You have the code and authorization to fix it.

A useful Codex brief is:

```text
Goal
Fix the Resources topic filter so selecting a topic updates the visible results
on mobile without breaking desktop behaviour.

Context
Inspect the existing implementation before proposing a new component. The
approved behaviour is documented in docs/resources-filter.md. Existing visual
styles must remain.

Constraints
Work only in the copied project. Preserve unrelated changes. Do not deploy,
publish, change analytics or add a dependency without asking. Make the smallest
complete fix.

Verification
First reproduce the failure. After the fix, run the existing relevant tests and
exercise All, Guides and Templates at mobile and desktop widths. Report what
changed and the observed results. Do not broaden testing unless a failure or
dependency justifies it.

Done when
The filter updates results in the real interface at both widths, relevant tests
pass and the exact changed files are ready for review. Stop before deployment.
```

Why this belongs in Codex: the job requires inspecting and changing the code, running tests and exercising the actual interface. Chat can help you write the brief or understand an error, but it does not automatically have the project environment.

## Put stable project rules in `AGENTS.md`

Codex can use an `AGENTS.md` file for always-true project instructions:

```markdown
# Project rules
- docs/resources-filter.md is the approved behaviour.
- Preserve existing visual tokens and accessibility labels.
- Never overwrite files in originals/.
- Run the relevant existing test before reporting completion.
- Deployment requires separate approval.
```

Keep it short and current. Add a rule after a repeated real failure, not for every hypothetical concern. Conflicting or stale rule files can make Astra pause or take the wrong route, because it follows instruction stacks closely.

## Model and effort for this case

Terra or Sol at Medium may be sufficient for a bounded, familiar bug. Astra becomes reasonable if the issue crosses the interface, data layer, browser tools, several packages and a long instruction stack. Raise effort when the lower run misses a named dependency, not because the website is important.

For Astra, state whether it should delegate, how far testing should go and when it may continue without asking. A tiny CSS correction does not require an archaeological survey of the entire repository.

## Free and paid boundary

**Applies to both:** A Free Chat user can prepare the brief, paste a small code excerpt and ask for an explanation or proposed patch, while keeping the same safety and verification logic.

**Free route or limit:** Apply and test the change in your authorized development process. Never paste secrets, customer data or proprietary code contrary to policy.

**Paid difference or extension:** Where Codex is included and configured, it can work in the real project environment, edit files and run allowed commands or tests.

**No equivalent:** Conversational advice about code is not equivalent to changing and testing the real application.

# 13. Fix the result without starting over

When most of a result is good, protect it. A vague “try again” gives the model permission to make new choices everywhere.

Use this repair pattern:

```text
Defect: [the exact wrong result]
Correct evidence: [the source fact, calculation or observed behaviour]
Dependent changes: [every place that must change with it]
Preserve: [the useful work that must stay]
Recheck: [the smallest evidence that proves the repair]
Stop: [the boundary]
```

Example:

```text
Defect: The summary and Slides 2 and 5 call $16,750 the approved budget.
Correct evidence: approved-brief.md says $18,000 is the budget ceiling;
current-figures.csv says $16,750 is the estimated pilot cost.
Dependent changes: Correct the summary, Slides 2 and 5, their speaker notes and
the source table.
Preserve: Keep the recommendation, dates, scope, other figures and visual order.
Recheck: Quote the two source lines and list every corrected location.
Stop: Return the complete corrected outputs only. Do not redesign the deck.
```

If the failure is structural rather than local, say so. A report with no coherent recommendation may need a new outline. A spreadsheet with typed totals instead of formulas may need rebuilding. Targeted repair does not mean pretending a broken foundation is a typo.

Start a fresh chat when old instructions are contaminating the result, the job has changed, the context is full of obsolete material or you can no longer tell which rule applies. Carry forward a compact handoff with the current goal, sources, authority, decisions, open questions and finish line.

# 14. Troubleshooting the irritating bits

## “It ignored my instruction”

Check whether the instruction was specific, current and higher priority than another rule. Quote the exact missed instruction and ask what conflicting instruction it followed. Remove stale Project material. For Astra, audit accessible rule and Skill files if it pauses or changes course unexpectedly.

## “It keeps asking questions instead of working”

Tell it which assumptions are safe and what would genuinely change the outcome:

```text
Continue through reversible drafting decisions. Use the supplied style example
for ordinary wording choices. Ask only if missing information changes approved
scope, a factual claim, external action, spend or an irreversible result. List
minor assumptions at the end.
```

Do not suppress a question whose answer really does change the decision.

## “It did far more than I asked”

Add exclusions and a finish line. With action-taking models, specify folders, tools and consequences. With Astra, say exactly how much testing is enough and when not to expand into adjacent improvements.

## “The answer is too long and over-formatted”

Specify the shape:

```text
Lead with the recommendation. Use six short paragraphs and one comparison
table. No preamble, recap, FAQ or repeated conclusion. Define technical terms
in the sentence where they become useful.
```

## “It says it changed the file, but I cannot see the change”

Open the actual artifact. Ask for the exact output path, changed section or diff. Download and reopen the file. A correct chat message beside an unchanged file is still an unfinished job.

## “It invented a source or number”

Remove the unsupported claim, provide or request the authoritative source and ask for a source map. Open every important link. If the fact is unavailable, mark it unresolved instead of encouraging a second guess.

## “The file will not upload or read properly”

Check size, format, password protection, scan quality and whether tables or images carry the meaning. Try a smaller relevant excerpt or export to a supported format. Never assume that an attachment icon proves the contents were parsed.

## “I hit a usage limit”

Preserve a handoff before starting elsewhere:

```text
Current goal:
Authoritative sources:
Decisions made:
Completed outputs:
Open defects:
Next exact action:
Done when:
```

Then continue in a fresh chat or after the limit resets. Do not ask a new chat to “continue” without the material it needs.

## “The stronger setting made it worse”

Return to the lowest setting that completed the job. Compare the same task. More effort can create extra analysis, broader review and a different writing style. Keep the setting that produces the best completed result after repair, not the longest answer.

# 15. Free and paid: the honest field guide

Free is a real route through this book. It is not a promise of identical models, quotas, tools or execution.

## What both routes require

- a clear job;
- permitted, relevant context;
- an authority order when sources can conflict;
- boundaries around privacy and action;
- an observable finish line;
- inspection of facts and artifacts;
- targeted repair;
- human approval before consequential sending, publishing, purchasing or deletion.

## What paying may change

Depending on the plan, account and rollout, paying may provide more usage, additional models and effort controls, larger or longer tasks, Projects, Work, Codex, file tools, connectors, local or cloud execution and administrative controls.

Those are meaningful differences when they remove your actual bottleneck. They are wasted if you upgrade before knowing whether the problem is capacity, access, context or a vague brief.

## Start free when

- you are learning the operating loop;
- the job fits into a small source pack;
- the useful result is text you can copy;
- you can perform the real-world action yourself;
- you do not need a specific paid model or execution surface.

## Consider paid when

- limits repeatedly interrupt valuable work even after you scope it sensibly;
- a larger context or stronger model fixes a demonstrated quality gap;
- creating and revising the actual files removes substantial manual work;
- a Project or recurring workflow saves repeated setup;
- your approved workplace plan provides needed privacy, administration or connectors;
- Codex or Work can operate in the real environment you are authorized to use.

## Do not upgrade because

- a model name sounds important;
- someone else's screenshot shows more controls;
- you hope payment will make weak sources reliable;
- you have not identified the bottleneck;
- the task requires organizational approval you still do not have.

Current ChatGPT plan inclusion, usage and model access can change. Check the current account display and official pricing page before paying. ChatGPT access does not include API credits by default, and API pricing is separate.

# 16. Copyable briefs and context files

## The everyday job

```text
Goal: [what I need to use at the end]
Audience: [who it is for and what they need]
Use: [the source material]
Preserve: [facts, wording or structure that must stay]
Avoid: [specific failure or boundary]
Done when: [length, format and check]

Return the complete result. Mark missing facts instead of inventing them.
```

## The conflicting-source job

```text
Goal: [deliverable]

Source authority:
1. [highest source] controls [facts]
2. [next source] controls [facts]
3. [notes] may suggest [ideas] but cannot create [commitments]

If sources conflict, follow the higher source and show the conflict in a table.
Do not blend them.

Checks: Map every material claim to its source and list unresolved facts.
Done when: [complete outputs and evidence].
```

## The action-capable job

```text
You may:
- read [exact files/folders];
- create new drafts in [output location];
- run [named checks].

Ask before:
- sending or publishing;
- spending money;
- deleting or overwriting originals;
- changing account settings or permissions;
- connecting another service;
- expanding beyond [scope].

Continue through reversible work. Stop after [deliverables and checks].
```

## The current-research job

```text
Decision this research supports: [decision]
Current through: [date]
Preferred evidence: official primary sources first, then current independent
reporting. Keep expert opinion separate.

For each material claim, give the direct source, publication or update date,
the exact point it supports and any jurisdiction or plan limit. Preserve
credible disagreement. Do not use search snippets as evidence.

Finish with: verified facts, uncertainties, recommendation and what evidence
would change it.
```

## The targeted repair

```text
The exact defect is: [problem].
The correct evidence is: [source or observed result].
Change it in: [all dependent locations].
Preserve: [unaffected work].
Recheck by: [observable evidence].
Return: [complete corrected result].
Stop there.
```

## A compact Project instruction

```text
Purpose: [continuing body of work].
Audience: [primary audience].
Authority: [source order].
Stable style: [few specific rules].
Privacy: [what must not enter].
Actions requiring approval: [external or consequential actions].
Missing information: mark [NEEDS DECISION].
Current task details belong in the task prompt, not here.
```

## A clean handoff

```text
Goal:
Current sources and authority:
Decisions already made:
What is complete:
What has been verified:
Open defect or decision:
Next exact action:
Done when:
```

Save the templates you actually use. Delete the ones you do not. A personal prompt library should feel like a working drawer, not the bin of tangled charging cables everyone kept from 2004.

## The useful ending

You do not need the fanciest model to become good at ChatGPT. You need to know what job you are giving it, what information is allowed to win, what it may do and what evidence will prove the result.

That is how ChatGPT stops being a clever conversation you occasionally visit and becomes a tool you can direct.
