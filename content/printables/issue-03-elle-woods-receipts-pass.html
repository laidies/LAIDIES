The pass

Claim. Timeline. Domain rule. Contradiction. Receipt.

AI can give you a paragraph that sounds composed, useful, and very pleased with itself. That is not the same thing as an alibi.

2Timeline
3Domain rule

Ask this first

  • What claim am I relying on?
  • What detail would make this fall apart?
  • What timeline, source, or domain rule checks it?
  • Do I have the receipt?

Do not count as receipts

  • The same answer repeated.
  • A source link you did not open.
  • A quote that is "basically" right.
  • A policy, date, or rule from last year.
Same story, said three ways, is still not a solid alibi.

Copy-paste prompt

Prompt Like Elle.

Open your approved AI tool in another tab or app. Paste this prompt first, then paste your task or notes under the line that says "Paste what you want AI to answer here."
Prompt Like Elle. I am using this for a low-risk work task. Help me reduce unsupported or hallucinated claims before I use it. By "Prompt Like Elle," I mean: do not just make the answer sound polished. Put it on the stand. Separate what is supported from what is assumed, identify the fragile detail that could make the answer fall apart, and tell me exactly what receipt to check. Paste what you want AI to answer here: [paste what you want AI to answer here] Rules: - Use only the information I provided unless I explicitly included a source to check. - If the provided material does not support a claim, mark it [needs receipt]. - If a detail may be stale, jurisdiction-specific, company-specific, customer-specific, or context-dependent, mark it [verify]. - Do not invent sources, links, quotes, dates, numbers, policies, people, or confidence. - Do not say a claim is verified unless the provided material includes the receipt. - If you are unsure, say what is uncertain and what would be needed to resolve it. - If there is not enough information, ask up to three clarifying questions instead of guessing. - Do not smooth over uncertainty to make the answer sound more finished. Return: 1. A concise usable draft or safer rewrite. 2. A receipt tray table with: - Claim or detail - What supports it from the provided material - What is missing or uncertain - Receipt to check next - Risk if wrong 3. Assumptions or inferences you made. 4. The ordinary-looking detail most likely to make the answer fall apart if wrong. 5. Three human checks I should do before using this. 6. A final version with unsupported claims removed, softened, or marked [needs receipt].

Prevent some nonsense first

  • Bring in the source or turn on approved search/grounding.
  • Tell AI what it is allowed to use.
  • Make uncertainty acceptable.
  • Ask it to mark anything inferred.
  • Ask for claim, source, date, confidence, and what would change the answer.
  • Ask for the exact line it relied on. No line, no receipt.

Then still check

The prompt lowers the odds of nonsense. It is not truth serum.

Use the actual source, a human owner, or a different tool with access to the right material.
