The Founding Mothers

Almost two hundred years, and every real leap has a woman's name on it — whether or not the textbook wrote it down. Pin this up. Say their names.

  • 1843Ada Lovelace · The Idea

    Everyone saw an adding machine. She saw that if a machine can follow instructions, numbers are only the beginning — and wrote the first algorithm. The credit went to the man whose machine it was.

  • 1942Hedy Lamarr · The Signal

    The most beautiful woman in the world, inventing between takes: a signal that hops frequencies too fast to jam. It's in the family tree of your Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. She never made a dime.

  • 1945The ENIAC Six · The First Programmers

    Six women programmed the first general-purpose electronic computer by hand, before programming languages existed. They're in the famous photos — uncaptioned for about forty years.

  • 1952Grace Hopper · The Language

    Told a computer could never understand words, she called that a failure of imagination and built the compiler — so we talk to it in our words. Also taped the first actual bug into a logbook.

  • 1956Dartmouth · The Naming (then the winters)

    A summer room of men christens it "artificial intelligence" and promises the world. The promises outran the results, the money froze — roughly two AI winters. The work went quiet, not away.

  • 1972Karen Spärck Jones · The Finding

    The rare words carry the meaning — the arithmetic under every search box since. Her line: "Computing is too important to be left to men."

  • 2012Fei-Fei Li · The Sight

    The brain was never the problem — the machine had just never seen enough examples. Her millions of labeled images (training data) started the boom. The Godmother of AI.

  • NowJoy Buolamwini · Timnit Gebru · Emily Bender · Kate Crawford

    The mirror, the room, the parrot, the map — the women who check the whole machine, so the thing you trust is worth trusting.

🍸 Say it at happy hour

"Is AI brand new? It's almost two hundred years old and about three years old at the same time. The science has been building for centuries — your access to it is brand new."

Four words, defined

The Study Pack vocabulary — enough to hold your own in any meeting where someone says "the algorithm."

Ada, 1843

Algorithm

A precise, repeatable set of steps for solving a problem — the recipe a machine follows, in order, every time. Not magic, not thinking: very careful instructions, followed exactly.

Grace, 1952

Compiler

A translator that turns instructions written in something close to plain language into the raw code a machine actually runs. Every app you tap sits on top of that one idea.

Roughly two of them

AI winter

A long stretch when AI's big promises outran the results and the funding froze. The work didn't stop; it went quiet, until the thaw.

Fei-Fei, 2012

Training data

The examples a model learns from — the more it sees, the more it can do. It only knows what it's been shown, which is why representation in the data matters.
