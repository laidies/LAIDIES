## 01 · What the Viral Reel Left Out

[No narration. On screen: THE TIP IS NOT THE WORKFLOW]

## 02 · Turn it on. Ta-da. Apparently.

The viral demonstration gives us a beginning and a polished ending. The middle has vanished in the edit. That can prove a capability is interesting. It cannot prove that the complete workflow is ready for your account, your data, your job or your consequences.

## 03 · A feature is not a finished workflow.

A capability is one behaviour the product can perform. A workflow is the complete path from an intended outcome through inputs, setup, action, human judgement, testing and upkeep. The missing work is not evidence that you are bad at AI. It is evidence that the demonstration did not show the operating setup.

## 04 · “Install one Skill. It can handle the department.”

Our fictional team sees this claim: install one reusable AI Skill and it can handle the department's work. The useful part is real. A Skill can package instructions, examples, resources and code for a repeatable task. The leap to accountable department work is misleading as stated.

## 05 · What does the official source actually support?

OpenAI describes Skills as reusable workflows that can contain instructions, examples and code. It also says availability depends on the product and account, workspace administrators control access, and uploaded Skills still require your own review and judgement. That is our evidence boundary—not the reel's confident caption.

## 06 · Eight checks between “can” and “should.”

Before relying on the result, fill in the missing middle. Name the outcome and actual capability. Check prerequisites and setup. Name the human judgement and boundaries. Design a test. Decide who will maintain it. Eight checks turn a dazzling demonstration into something you can evaluate.

## 07 · The invisible shopping list.

Ask what must already be true. Which account and plan? Which files and data? Which permissions? Who owns the task? For our fictional community-events team, the Skill gets only approved event-copy examples and a small test folder—not the shared drive and certainly not everybody's inbox.

## 08 · Customize the process—not the privacy risk.

Customization is not an invitation to paste more personal information. Supply the smallest useful context, approved instructions and representative examples. Then name the decision that remains human-owned. Our Skill may draft event listings. A person still confirms the facts, tone, accessibility wording and permission to publish.

## 09 · Happy path. Weird edge. Hard stop.

Do not test only the flattering example. Run one normal case, one edge case and one case that should stop. Decide what success means before the test. A complete public event brief should produce a usable draft. A missing accessibility detail should trigger a question. A private attendee list should never enter this workflow.

## 10 · Set it and forget it belongs on a rotisserie.

A working setup can go stale. Name an owner, a review trigger, a version and a rollback. Recheck when the product changes, the task changes or the team finds a new failure. Reusable does not mean permanently correct.

## 11 · Same capability. Complete operating setup.

The product and low-risk task stay the same. We change one variable: the operating setup. Before, the team installs the Skill, tries one favorable example and assumes the workflow works. After, they bound the job, limit the inputs, run three tests, name the approval and schedule the next check.

## 12 · The answer does not have to be yes or no.

Choose the next move the evidence supports. Build when the setup is supported and the test passes. Revise when the capability is useful but the workflow is weak. Investigate missing evidence. Decline an unsupported or inappropriate use. Monitor a named change that could make it useful later.

## 13 · “It already knows the project.” Does it?

The same method helps with ordinary work. It already knows the project may hide four different claims: I remember it, we recorded it, this tool can access it, and this is the current source. They are not interchangeable. A useful handoff names the next job, current decisions, allowed context, open questions and the check that proves the intended context arrived.

## 14 · Keep the useful promise. Build the part the reel skipped.

Do not throw away a useful idea because the demonstration oversold it. Check the claim, build the missing middle, test a safe version and choose the next move on evidence. The viral tip is not the workflow. It can still be the beginning of one.

## 15 · Bring one tempting AI claim.

Bring one current claim you would genuinely like to use. Complete the Missing Middle card before you copy the workflow.
